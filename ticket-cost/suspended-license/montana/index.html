How much does driving on a suspended license cost in Montana?

Nationally, the cost is commonly $200–$2,500+. That is a national range, not a Montana price. In Montana, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Montana Department of Justice — Motor Vehicle Division).

Nationally commonly $200–$2,500+; a criminal offense with possible jail; in Montana the exact amount is set by the court on the citation.

We do not list an exact Montana dollar figure because the amount is not fixed on a public sticker price. It depends on the court handling your case and on what the citation says.

The fine is also not the whole bill. Court surcharges are added to the statutory fine, so the total you owe will be higher than the fine line alone.

Read your citation closely. It is the document that tells you which court has your case and how to find the amount owed.

How is the fine set for driving on a suspended license in Montana?

The exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The code is published in the Montana statutes (Montana statutes via Justia). The number on your citation, or the one the judge sets, is the one that applies to you.

Because this is a criminal offense and not a simple civil infraction, you should not assume you can pay a standard amount and close the case. The court may require an appearance, and the citation is the place to confirm that.

Court surcharges are then added to the statutory fine. These are separate from the fine itself, so ask the court clerk for the total amount due, not just the fine.

  • The fine: set by the court within the state's traffic code.
  • Surcharges: added on top of the statutory fine.
  • Possible jail: part of the penalty range for a criminal offense.

Which court you appear in, and the facts of your case, can change the outcome. We cannot predict a specific amount for you.

How many points does driving on a suspended license add in Montana?

Convictions carry conviction points toward Montana's habitual-offender threshold. That threshold is 30 points in 3 years, which results in revocation (Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws).

We have not published a point value for this specific offense, because we only state figures we can source. To see how many points apply to you, check your driving record with the Montana Motor Vehicle Division (Montana Department of Justice — Motor Vehicle Division).

The points matter because they accumulate. A conviction for this offense sits alongside any other convictions in the same 3-year window.

The habitual-offender rule is the number to watch: 30 points in 3 years = revocation. If you already have other convictions, ask the MVD where your record stands.

Does driving on a suspended license raise insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Montana-specific one.

Your own change could be smaller or larger. It depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the conviction is recorded. We do not recommend or rank insurers.

Can a suspended license charge be dismissed or reduced?

It can happen, but we cannot say whether it will in your case. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice.

Because this is a criminal offense with possible jail, it is worth taking seriously. A court may treat it differently than a basic speeding ticket, and the outcome depends on the facts and the court.

Your practical options usually include:

  1. Reading the citation for the court, date and any required appearance.
  2. Asking the court clerk how to respond and what the total amount due would be, including surcharges.
  3. Checking your license status with the Montana Motor Vehicle Division (Montana Department of Justice — Motor Vehicle Division).
  4. Speaking with a licensed attorney of your own choosing if you want advice on dismissal or reduction.

Ask the court directly what documents or proof it wants. Rules differ between courts, so confirm before you assume anything.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the citation?

Ignoring a citation is rarely a good idea, and with a criminal offense it carries more risk. The citation gives a court and a response requirement, and missing it can make matters worse.

We have no sourced figure for late fees or for specific penalties tied to non-payment in Montana, so we do not state any. Ask the court clerk what applies to your case.

There is also a license consequence to watch. Convictions add points toward Montana's habitual-offender threshold of 30 points in 3 years, which leads to revocation. More convictions in that window raise that risk.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court before the deadline. Courts often have their own process for questions about payment, and the clerk can explain it.

What should I do after getting a suspended license citation in Montana?

Start with the paperwork. Find the court name, the date and the response instructions on the citation.

Then confirm your license status. The Motor Vehicle Division is the official place to check why a license is suspended and what is required for reinstatement (Montana Department of Justice — Motor Vehicle Division).

Do not drive until you know your license status. Another stop while suspended can add to the problem.

Keep records of anything you send or pay. Write down the date, the amount and who you spoke with at the court.

If the stakes feel high because of the possible jail exposure, consider a licensed attorney of your own choosing. We do not recommend any specific one.
