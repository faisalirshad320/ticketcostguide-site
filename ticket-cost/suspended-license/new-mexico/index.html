How much does driving on a suspended license cost in New Mexico?

Nationally, driving on a suspended license commonly costs $200–$2,500+. It is treated as a criminal offense with possible jail, not a simple fee-only ticket (New Mexico Motor Vehicle Division).

In New Mexico, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single New Mexico dollar figure because we cannot source one, and any number you see elsewhere may not match your case.

The national range is a national range. Your New Mexico amount is whatever the court sets on your citation, plus any fees added to it.

The wide national spread reflects how much depends on the court, the facts of the stop, and the reason the license was suspended. The citation and any court paperwork are the documents that tell you what you actually owe.

How is the fine set for a suspended license charge?

In New Mexico, the court sets the amount within the state's traffic code. That differs from a simple penalty assessment, where an amount is printed for you to pay.

Because this is a criminal offense with possible jail, a court appearance is more likely to be part of the process than for a minor moving violation. The judge or court sets the outcome, and the result can vary from one court to another.

Things that commonly matter in cases like this, based on general court practice rather than a New Mexico schedule:

  • The reason the license was suspended in the first place.
  • Whether you have prior driving-record issues.
  • Whether you can show the suspension has been cleared or the underlying matter resolved.
  • How the court handles similar cases locally.

You can read the state's traffic code through New Mexico statutes via Justia. We do not cite a specific section number here because we have not verified one for this page.

What extra court costs and fees get added?

New Mexico adds fees to the penalty assessment. Court cases can exceed the assessment amounts, which matters here because a suspended license charge is handled as a court matter.

That means the total you pay can be higher than the fine itself. When you receive paperwork, look for the fine and any added fees as separate lines, and ask the court clerk for the total due before you pay.

Do not budget only for the fine. Fees are added on top, and the final amount comes from the court.

We do not list individual fee amounts because they are not part of the figures we verified. The court clerk or the citation will show the real numbers.

How many points does driving on a suspended license add in New Mexico?

The New Mexico points system we reviewed lists 3–8 points by mph over the limit. That range is tied to speed, so it does not tell you the point value for a suspended license charge.

We have no verified point value for this specific offense. Check your citation or ask the New Mexico Motor Vehicle Division for the figure that applies to your case (New Mexico Motor Vehicle Division).

The thresholds are general and worth knowing:

  • 7–10 points in 12 months risks suspension.
  • 12 points means revocation.

If your license is already suspended, additional points or a new conviction can make it harder to get driving privileges back. The MVD can tell you what applies to your record.

Does driving on a suspended license raise insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a New Mexico quote.

Whether a suspended license conviction is rated like other moving violations depends on your insurer and its underwriting rules. A criminal conviction tied to your license status may be weighed differently than a speeding ticket.

We do not recommend any insurer. If you are worried about your policy, ask your own insurer how a conviction would be treated, and read your policy terms and renewal notices carefully.

Can a suspended license charge be dismissed or reduced?

It may be possible in some cases, but we cannot promise any outcome. The court decides, and outcomes depend on the facts and the local court.

General steps people take, none of which is legal advice:

  1. Read the citation and note the court, the charge and any date to appear.
  2. Find out why the license was suspended and whether that has been resolved.
  3. Get your driving record from the MVD to check its status (New Mexico Motor Vehicle Division).
  4. Contact the court clerk with procedural questions.
  5. Consider speaking with a licensed attorney, since this is a criminal offense with possible jail.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not recommend specific attorneys. Because jail is possible, it is worth taking the charge seriously before deciding how to respond.

What happens if I don't pay or don't go to court?

Ignoring a citation for a criminal offense with possible jail is risky. Courts generally have tools to respond to missed appearances and unpaid amounts, and the specific consequences come from the court and the state traffic code.

Unpaid or ignored matters can also complicate your license status, which may already be suspended. Check with the court and the MVD about your record.

If you are unsure what your citation requires, contact the court clerk before the deadline on the paperwork.
