How much is a driving on a suspended license ticket in North Dakota?

Nationally, this offense commonly runs $200–$2,500+, and it is treated as a criminal offense with possible jail (North Dakota Department of Transportation). That is a national range, not a North Dakota price.

In North Dakota, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single state dollar figure because the court decides it within the state's traffic code.

Read your citation first. The amount, the court and any appearance requirement are printed on it or listed with the court that issued it.

The range is wide for a reason. The final number depends on the court, the facts of the stop and the reason the license was suspended in the first place.

How is the fine set in North Dakota?

The court sets the amount on the citation, within the state's traffic code. North Dakota statutes are available through North Dakota statutes via Justia, and you can read the traffic provisions there yourself.

Because this is a criminal offense rather than a simple infraction, you should not assume it works like a standard fine-by-mail ticket. A court may need to see you, and the result can include more than a payment.

  • The fine is decided by the court, not by the officer.
  • The outcome can involve possible jail, as with criminal offenses generally.
  • The statutory fee is usually the whole amount, with no large cost stack.

If anything on the citation is unclear, the court clerk can tell you what the court requires. The clerk cannot give legal advice.

Are there extra court costs or surcharges?

Based on the state context we use, the statutory fee is usually the whole amount. There is no large stack of added costs on top of it.

That does not mean the total is small. The national range of $200–$2,500+ already shows how high a criminal-offense outcome can go (North Dakota Department of Transportation).

Costs outside the court amount can still matter, such as time away from work for a hearing or the price of getting your license valid again. We do not state dollar figures for those because they are not in our locked facts.

How many points does it add to my North Dakota record?

North Dakota assigns points by mph over on its schedule, and 12+ points = suspension. That is the locked point rule we can state.

We do not list a specific point value for driving on a suspended license. The schedule we cite is organized by mph over, which is a speeding measure and does not fit this offense neatly.

For the point value, if any, for your exact charge, check with the North Dakota Department of Transportation at dot.nd.gov or ask the court clerk.

The practical risk is cumulative. If you are already near the 12-point threshold, any added violation could matter, and a further suspension is the outcome that threshold describes.

Does it raise my car insurance?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a North Dakota quote.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the conviction is recorded. Some drivers see more, some see less, and a few see little change.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend insurers or give insurance advice. If you want to know what a conviction would do to your rate, ask your insurer or an independent agent before you decide how to respond to the citation.

Also consider that a driver with a suspended license may have had coverage problems before the stop. Those can affect rates separately from the ticket itself.

Can it be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes outcomes change, but we cannot promise any result. Because the offense is criminal with possible jail, the stakes are higher than for a basic speeding ticket, and many people want legal advice before they plead.

Issues that people commonly raise with a court include:

  • Whether the suspension was valid when you were stopped.
  • Whether you were properly notified of the suspension.
  • Whether your license status changed before the citation date.

We cannot tell you whether any of these apply to you. Records of your license status come from the state, and the North Dakota Department of Transportation (dot.nd.gov) is the place to start for your driving record.

The court sets the amount, so any request to reduce it goes to the court named on your citation. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not recommend attorneys.

What happens if I don't pay or don't show up?

Ignoring the citation is rarely a good option, particularly for a criminal offense with possible jail. Courts generally treat a missed appearance or unpaid amount as a new problem on top of the original one.

The specific consequences depend on the court and the citation. Look at the paperwork for deadlines and appearance instructions, then contact the clerk if you are unsure.

  • Check the date and court on the citation.
  • Ask the clerk what the court requires before that date.
  • Keep copies of anything you file or pay.

We do not state specific late penalties because they are not in our locked facts.
