How much does driving on a suspended license cost in Florida?

Nationally, this charge is commonly $200–$2,500+, and it is a criminal offense with possible jail. In Florida the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Florida Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles).

We do not publish a single Florida dollar figure, because the sources we rely on do not give a fixed one. Anyone quoting an exact number without seeing your citation is guessing.

The national range is a national range. Your Florida amount is whatever the court sets on your citation, plus county costs and fees.

The fine is only part of the bill. Court costs, fees, and any other costs tied to getting your license valid again can add to the total. Those extras differ from one county to another.

How is the fine set for a suspended license charge in Florida?

The exact fine for this violation in Florida is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The statutes themselves are published online, and a free copy is available through Florida statutes via Justia.

Because the court sets the amount, two drivers with similar charges can see different results. The court looks at the charge as written on the citation and the case in front of it.

County courts also add costs and fees on top of the statutory penalty. That is why the same offense can total differently in different counties.

  • The citation lists the charge and tells you how to respond.
  • The clerk of the county court can confirm what is owed on your case.
  • Any amount you see online that is not tied to your citation is only an estimate.

Is driving on a suspended license a criminal offense in Florida?

Yes. It is treated as a criminal offense with possible jail, not as a simple civil infraction like many routine traffic tickets (Florida Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles).

"Possible jail" means jail is a legal possibility. It does not mean every case ends that way. What happens depends on the charge and the court.

A criminal charge can also create a court record. If you are unsure how that could affect you, a licensed attorney or the court clerk can explain the process. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot advise you on your case.

How many points does a suspended license add in Florida?

Florida uses a points system. The locked figures we rely on are 3 points (15 or less over) or 4 points (16+ over), and 12 points in 12 months = 30-day suspension (Florida Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles).

Those point values are written around speeding. Our sources do not give a separate point value for driving on a suspended license. Check your citation and your driver record for the points tied to your charge.

Points matter because they add up. Reaching 12 points in 12 months means a 30-day suspension.

If your license is already suspended, new points can make the situation harder to resolve. The state licensing agency can confirm your current status and point total.

Does a suspended license conviction raise insurance in Florida?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums ~20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Florida quote.

Your own change could be lower or higher. It depends on your insurer, your driving history, and how the charge is recorded.

We do not recommend any insurer. If you want to know how a conviction may affect your rate, ask your insurer directly or compare quotes yourself.

  • The national ~20% figure is an average, not a promise.
  • Premiums are set by each insurer, not by the court.
  • The effect can last as long as the conviction stays on your record.

Can a suspended license charge be dismissed or reduced?

It can depend on the facts of your case and on the court. Our sources do not list guaranteed outcomes, so we cannot say a charge will be reduced or dismissed.

Because this is a criminal offense, it is worth understanding your options before you respond to the citation. A licensed attorney can review your situation. TicketCostGuide does not recommend a specific attorney.

  • Read the citation closely for the charge and the response instructions.
  • Contact the county court clerk to confirm the process and any deadline.
  • Check your license status with the state licensing agency, FLHSMV.

Do not assume a charge will go away because the license was later reinstated. Ask the court how your specific case is handled.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the citation?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. For a criminal offense with possible jail, missing a court date or not responding can create further problems with the court.

Our sources do not spell out the exact steps the court takes for unpaid amounts, so we will not guess at them. The court clerk is the best place to confirm what applies to your case.

If you cannot pay in full, ask the clerk what options exist. Do this before any deadline passes, not after.

Unpaid costs and fees add to the problem. County courts add costs and fees on top of the statutory penalty, so the amount owed can be higher than the fine alone.
