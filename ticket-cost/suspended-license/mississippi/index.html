How much does driving on a suspended license cost in Mississippi?

Nationally, the fine for driving on a suspended license is commonly $200–$2,500+. It is treated as a criminal offense with possible jail, not a simple traffic infraction (Mississippi Department of Public Safety).

That range is a national one. In Mississippi, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation, so the number printed on your paperwork is the one that matters.

The fine is not the whole bill. Mandatory state assessments are added to every fine and often exceed it, so the total you owe can be well above the base fine.

We do not publish an exact Mississippi dollar figure for this offense. The court sets it, and it can differ from one court to the next.

Other costs can follow, such as what it takes to get your license reinstated and any change to your insurance. Those depend on your own record and are covered below.

How is the fine set in Mississippi?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The citation or the court's notice is where you find the amount that applies to you.

Several things can shape what a court does with a case like this:

  • The reason your license was suspended in the first place.
  • Whether this is a first charge or part of a pattern of earlier convictions.
  • The court that hears the case, since amounts are set court by court.

Because it is a criminal offense with possible jail, a judge generally has more room than with an ordinary moving violation. The national range of $200–$2,500+ shows how wide that room can be.

Mississippi statutes are published online, and you can read the traffic code through Mississippi statutes via Justia. This guide does not interpret them for your case.

What are the court costs and surcharges on top of the fine?

Mandatory state assessments are added to every fine in Mississippi, and they often exceed the fine itself. That means the base fine understates what most people actually pay.

We do not list individual assessment amounts, because they are not part of the figures we can source for this page. The court clerk or the citation will show the full amount due.

When you compare quotes or estimates from anywhere, check whether the number is the fine alone or the fine plus assessments. The difference can be large.

Ask the court clerk for the total due, not just the fine. The total is the number you would pay.

How many points does driving on a suspended license add in Mississippi?

None, because Mississippi has no point system. There is no point total to build up or watch for this offense.

Suspensions in Mississippi follow repeat convictions and specific offenses instead. So the risk is less about a running score and more about what your record shows and which offenses are on it.

This matters because driving while already suspended is a sign that a license problem exists. A new conviction on top of the original issue can complicate getting your license back.

For current license status and reinstatement information, the state agency is the Mississippi Department of Public Safety. For a comparison of state laws, the Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a state laws resource.

Does driving on a suspended license raise insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums ~20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Mississippi-specific one.

What your insurer does with a suspended-license conviction depends on the company, your policy and your record. Some insurers treat a license suspension and a related conviction more seriously than a routine ticket, but we do not have a sourced figure for that, so we do not give one.

A few practical points:

  • Premium changes usually show up at renewal.
  • The effect can depend on how long the conviction stays on your record with the insurer.
  • Your insurer or agent is the one who can tell you what applies to your policy.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend insurers, and this is not insurance advice.

Can a suspended license charge be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes outcomes differ from the original charge, but this guide cannot say whether that applies to you. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice.

Because this is a criminal offense with possible jail, it is worth taking seriously. These are general steps people commonly consider:

  1. Read the citation closely for the court, the date and the stated charge.
  2. Confirm your license status with the Mississippi Department of Public Safety, including why it was suspended.
  3. Contact the court clerk to ask about your court date and how payment or appearance works.
  4. Consider speaking with a licensed attorney in Mississippi about your specific facts.

We do not recommend any specific attorney. A local lawyer or the court can explain which options are available in your case.

What happens if I don't pay or miss my court date?

Ignoring a citation for a criminal offense can make things worse. Because possible jail is part of this offense, missing a court date is a real risk, not just a paperwork issue.

The exact consequences are set by the court and the state's traffic code, and we do not list specific penalties here. The citation and the court's notices explain what is required and by when.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court clerk before the due date to ask what is possible. Do not assume the court will extend a deadline on its own.

If a license problem is the reason for the suspension, resolving it with the state is a separate step from dealing with the court. Both may be needed.
