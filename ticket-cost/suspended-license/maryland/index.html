How much is a driving on a suspended license ticket in Maryland?

Nationally, driving on a suspended license commonly runs $200–$2,500+. It is a criminal offense with possible jail, not a simple fine-only ticket (Maryland Motor Vehicle Administration).

In Maryland, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Maryland dollar figure because the amount depends on the court and the facts of the case.

The national range is a national range. It is not a Maryland price list. Your citation and the court that handles it control the amount you actually owe.

The wide national spread exists because states, and courts within them, treat these cases differently. A first charge usually sits at the low end of that spread, while repeat charges or an underlying serious suspension reason can push the result higher.

Because jail is possible, the cost is not only the fine. Court-related costs, time off work, and the effect on your driving record all add to the real price.

How is the fine set, and what is the prepay amount?

The exact fine for this violation in Maryland is set by the court within the state's traffic code. You can read the code itself through Maryland statutes via Justia.

Some Maryland citations list a prepay amount, which lets you pay without going to court. Requesting a court date replaces that prepay amount with a judge-set fine up to $500.

  • Prepay: if your citation offers it, you pay the listed amount and the matter is generally closed.
  • Court date: a judge sets the fine, up to $500 under the figure we have on file.

Because this offense is criminal and carries possible jail, read the citation closely. Look at whether it says you must appear in court or whether payment is allowed. If it is unclear, the court clerk named on the citation can tell you which applies.

Do not assume that paying is always the best choice. Paying usually counts as a conviction, and a conviction can affect your record and your insurance.

How many points does driving on a suspended license add in Maryland?

Maryland uses a points system with set thresholds: 8 points = suspension and 12 points = revocation. The point tiers we have on file are 1 point (9 or less over), 2 points (10–29 over), and 5 points (30+ over).

Those tiers are written by how far over a limit a driver was, so they describe speeding-style violations. Our sourced data does not give a separate point value for driving on a suspended license, and we will not guess one.

To find the point value for your charge, check your citation and your driving record with the Maryland Motor Vehicle Administration.

Points matter most if you are already close to a threshold. At 8 points the license is suspended, and at 12 points it is revoked.

If your license is already suspended for point accumulation, a new conviction adds to a record that is already under pressure. That is one reason to understand the charge before you decide how to respond.

Does driving on a suspended license raise insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Maryland quote.

Your own change could be higher or lower. Insurers weigh your history, your policy, and how they classify the offense. A suspended-license conviction can be viewed as more serious than a minor ticket, but we have no sourced figure for that difference.

  • The ~20% figure is an average across the US.
  • It applies to moving-violation convictions in general.
  • Your insurer sets your actual rate.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer or give insurance advice. If you want to know the effect on your policy, ask your insurer or agent directly. The time to ask is before you pay, since paying can create a conviction.

Can a suspended license charge be dismissed or reduced?

It can happen, but outcomes depend on the court and the facts. We cannot predict whether yours would be dismissed or reduced.

Common questions that affect the outcome include whether the suspension was valid, whether you received proper notice, and whether the suspension has since been cleared. Those are legal questions, and the answers sit with the court.

  • Read the citation for the exact charge and court.
  • Check your license status with the Maryland Motor Vehicle Administration.
  • Collect any paperwork showing the suspension was lifted or resolved.

Because jail is possible, many people talk to a licensed Maryland attorney before appearing. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not recommend a specific attorney. A court clerk or a local bar referral service can point you to options.

Requesting a court date means a judge sets the fine, up to $500 under our data. It also means you give up the prepay option.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the citation?

Ignoring a criminal citation is risky. Because the offense carries possible jail, a missed court date can create further problems on top of the original charge.

The specific consequences depend on the court and on what your citation says. We do not have a sourced list of Maryland late fees or penalties, so we do not state any.

In general terms, you have three options:

  1. Pay the prepay amount, if your citation offers one.
  2. Request a court date, where a judge sets the fine up to $500.
  3. Contact the court clerk listed on the citation if you are unsure which applies.

Whatever you choose, act before the date on the citation. Questions about deadlines are best answered by the court itself, since it handles your case.
