How much does driving on a suspended license cost in Wyoming?

Nationally, the fine for driving on a suspended license is commonly $200–$2,500+. It is also a criminal offense with possible jail, which sets it apart from an ordinary speeding ticket (Wyoming Department of Transportation).

In Wyoming, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Wyoming dollar figure here, because the amount depends on the court and the facts of your case.

The national range is a reference point only. Your own citation, and the court that handles it, decide what you actually owe.

Court fees are added per the local schedule, so two people with the same charge can see different totals depending on where the case is heard.

The fine is only part of the cost. Court fees, any time spent dealing with the court, and the effect on your insurance can all add to the total.

How is the fine set for driving on a suspended license in Wyoming?

The fine is set by the court, within the limits of the state's traffic code. The citation you receive, or the court notice that follows it, is where the amount for your case is stated.

Wyoming statutes are available through Wyoming statutes via Justia. You can read the traffic title there to see how the offense is defined and what range of penalties the legislature has allowed.

Several things can shape the outcome in practice:

  • The court that hears the case and its local schedule.
  • Why the license was suspended in the first place.
  • Whether the court treats the matter as a first or repeat offense.
  • The details written on the citation itself.

Because this is a criminal offense with possible jail, a judge generally has more discretion here than with a simple infraction. Read the citation closely and note any court date or deadline.

What court costs and surcharges are added?

Court fees are added per the local schedule. That means the final amount you pay can be higher than the fine alone.

We do not list specific Wyoming fee amounts, because they depend on the court. The clerk of the court that issued your citation can tell you what applies to your case.

When you compare quotes or estimates from other sites, check whether they include court fees. A figure that shows only the base fine may understate the real total.

Keep every receipt and the court's written notice. If a payment is ever disputed, those records show what you paid and when.

How many points does driving on a suspended license add in Wyoming?

Wyoming has no demerit-point system. A conviction for this offense does not add points to your record in the way it would in states that count them.

That does not mean the offense has no license consequences. In Wyoming, suspensions follow repeat convictions and court action rather than a running point total.

In practice, that makes the sequence of events important. A conviction can lead to further action against your driving privilege, especially if it is not your first.

For how Wyoming compares with other states on license sanctions, see the Governors Highway Safety Association state laws page. For your own license status, the Wyoming Department of Transportation is the official source.

Does driving on a suspended license raise my insurance?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Wyoming-specific one.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your driving history, and how the insurer classifies the conviction. Some insurers treat a license-status offense more seriously than a minor moving violation.

Over several years, a premium increase can cost more than the fine itself. It is worth weighing that when you decide how to handle the citation.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend insurers or predict your premium. Your insurer or a licensed agent can tell you how a conviction would be treated on your policy.

Is driving on a suspended license a crime in Wyoming?

Yes. It is treated as a criminal offense with possible jail, not only a fine. That changes how seriously the court takes it and how much a conviction can affect you.

A criminal conviction can show up on background checks, which can matter for employment, housing, and professional licensing. A simple traffic infraction usually does not carry that weight.

Whether jail is actually imposed is up to the court. We do not state a jail term here, because the locked sources do not give one for Wyoming.

If you are facing a charge, you may want to speak with a licensed attorney about your situation. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot give legal advice.

Can the charge be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome is up to the court and the prosecutor. We cannot promise a result, and we do not give legal advice.

People in this situation commonly look at questions such as these:

  • Was the suspension valid, and was notice properly given?
  • Has the underlying issue that caused the suspension since been resolved?
  • Does the citation have errors in the details it lists?
  • Does the court offer any option other than a plain conviction?

Ask the court clerk what choices are available on your citation and what the deadlines are. Missing a deadline can remove options you would otherwise have had.

Fixing the underlying suspension matters too. Check your status with the Wyoming Department of Transportation before you drive again.

What happens if I don't pay or don't appear?

Ignoring the citation usually makes things worse. Because this is a criminal matter, a missed court date can lead to further court action.

Court fees are added per the local schedule, and unpaid amounts can grow or lead to additional consequences. Suspensions in Wyoming follow repeat convictions and court action, so an unresolved case can deepen your license problems.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court clerk before the due date. Courts often have ways to handle this, but you generally need to ask in advance.

If you cannot appear on the date given, ask the court what to do. Do not simply skip the date.
