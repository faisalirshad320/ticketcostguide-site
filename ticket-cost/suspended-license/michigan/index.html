How much does driving on a suspended license cost in Michigan?

Nationally, a driving on a suspended license charge commonly runs $200–$2,500+. It is treated as a criminal offense with possible jail (Michigan Secretary of State). That is a national range, not a Michigan price tag.

In Michigan, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not print a single Michigan fine, because the total depends on the court handling the case and what the citation says.

District court costs vary by court and make up much of the total. Two drivers charged with the same offense in different courts can owe different amounts.

Read your citation and any court notice first. The figure the court assigns to your case is the one that counts, not a national average.

The fine and court costs are only part of the expense. Reinstating a license, if you are eligible, can involve separate steps and charges through the Secretary of State. Check current requirements with the agency directly.

How is the fine for driving on a suspended license set in Michigan?

The fine is set by the court within the state's traffic code. It is not a fixed amount printed in a statewide schedule that applies the same way everywhere. The court notes the amount on the citation or in its paperwork.

Several things can shape what you see on that paperwork:

  • The specific charge written on the citation.
  • The court that hears the case, since district court costs vary by court.
  • The outcome of the case, whether a conviction, a reduced charge, or a dismissal.
  • Any additional charges that came with the stop.

Michigan traffic and licensing rules appear in the state's compiled statutes, which you can read through Michigan statutes via Justia. The Secretary of State is the agency that handles licenses and driver records (Michigan Secretary of State).

Because the exact amount depends on the court, the most reliable way to learn your total is to contact the court clerk named on the citation. Ask what the fine and the court costs add up to.

Is driving on a suspended license a criminal offense in Michigan?

The national picture is that driving on a suspended license is a criminal offense with possible jail (Michigan Secretary of State). That makes it different from a typical speeding ticket, which is usually a civil infraction.

That difference matters for a few reasons. A criminal charge can mean a court appearance is expected rather than optional. It can also mean the outcome shows up differently on records than a simple fine.

We do not state a specific jail term for Michigan here. Any possible penalty is set by the statute that applies and by the court. If jail is a possibility on your citation, treat it as a serious matter and read the paperwork carefully.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice. If you are facing a criminal charge, consider speaking with a licensed attorney in your area about your own situation.

How many points does driving on a suspended license add in Michigan?

Michigan uses a points system under §257.320a. The locked state summary reads: 2–4 points by mph over under §257.320a. Those mph tiers are written around speeding, so they do not by themselves tell you how many points a suspended license charge adds.

To find out how your charge is scored, check the citation, the court's disposition, and your driver record with the Michigan Secretary of State. Do not assume the speeding tiers apply to your case.

The threshold that matters for any driver is this: 12 points in 2 years = driver reexamination. Points from different violations can add up on one record, so a new conviction matters more if you already have points.

  • Points are tied to convictions on your record, not just to the ticket being issued.
  • The mph tiers apply to speeding and should not be applied to other charges.
  • Reaching 12 points in 2 years leads to a driver reexamination.

For a national comparison of state traffic laws, the Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws page is a useful reference.

Does driving on a suspended license raise insurance in Michigan?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Michigan rate, and it describes moving violations in general.

We do not have a sourced Michigan-specific increase for this offense. Your own change could be higher or lower. It depends on your insurer, your record, and how the conviction is reported.

A few practical points follow from that:

  • Treat the ~20% figure as a rough national benchmark only.
  • Ask your insurer how a conviction would be treated at renewal.
  • Compare the possible premium change against the cost of the court case itself.

Can a suspended license charge be dismissed or reduced in Michigan?

Sometimes charges are dismissed or reduced, but the outcome depends on the facts, the court, and the prosecutor. We cannot predict it, and the locked sources do not give a rate or a standard process.

Some questions are worth checking before you respond to the citation:

  • Was the suspension actually in effect on the date of the stop?
  • Was the suspension properly noticed and recorded on your driver record?
  • Has the underlying reason for the suspension been resolved?

Your driver record is held by the Michigan Secretary of State. Getting a copy shows the status of your license and the reason for any suspension.

Because this is a criminal offense with possible jail, consider getting advice from a licensed attorney before deciding how to respond. TicketCostGuide does not recommend specific attorneys.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the citation?

Ignoring a citation usually makes the situation worse. Courts generally expect a response by the date listed on the paperwork, and unpaid court obligations can lead to additional consequences.

Because this is a criminal offense with possible jail, a missed court date is a more serious risk than for a minor infraction. We do not state specific penalties for Michigan here, because the locked sources do not give them.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court clerk before the deadline. Ask what options exist for your case. Courts set their own procedures, and district court costs vary by court.

Also keep in mind that more points or convictions raise the risk of reaching 12 points in 2 years, which means a driver reexamination.
