How much is a driving on a suspended license ticket in Tennessee?

Nationally, this charge commonly runs $200–$2,500+. It is a criminal offense with possible jail, so it is treated more seriously than a typical moving violation (Tennessee Department of Safety and Homeland Security).

In Tennessee the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single statewide dollar figure, because the total depends on the court that hears your case.

The national range is a national range. It is not a Tennessee fine schedule, and your own total can differ.

The number printed on your citation or court notice is the one that matters. If you cannot find it, the clerk of the court named on the citation can tell you the amount owed.

How is the fine set, and what are court costs?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The statutes are published online through Tennessee statutes via Justia.

County court costs and state litigation taxes make up most of the total. That means two people with the same charge can owe different amounts depending on the county and court.

  • Fine: set by the court on the citation.
  • County court costs: vary by county and court.
  • State litigation taxes: added on top and part of the bulk of the total.

When you compare quotes or stories from other drivers, check whether they are quoting the fine alone or the full amount with costs and taxes. The difference can be large.

Is driving on a suspended license a criminal offense?

Yes. Nationally it is treated as a criminal offense with possible jail, not just a civil traffic infraction. Tennessee follows this general approach, and the details sit in the state's traffic code.

That has practical effects. A criminal charge can mean a court appearance, and it can leave a record that is different from a simple speeding ticket.

We cannot tell you what sentence a court might impose in your case. The locked sources we use give a national range and say the court sets the amount, and they do not list jail terms for Tennessee.

This page is not legal advice. For questions about a specific charge, read the citation closely and contact the court clerk or a licensed attorney of your choosing.

How many points does it add to my Tennessee record?

Tennessee's points system runs from 1–8 points by mph over the limit. For example, 3 points apply at 6–15 over, and 4 points at 16–25 over (Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws).

That scale is built around speeding. The sources we use do not give a point value for driving on a suspended license, so we will not guess one.

The threshold to know is 12 points in 12 months, which results in a suspension notice. If your license is already suspended, additional problems on your record can make the situation harder to resolve.

To see how your own record is counted, check with the Tennessee Department of Safety and Homeland Security.

Does it raise my insurance?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Tennessee-specific one.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the conviction is recorded. Some drivers see no change at renewal, and others see more than the average.

We do not recommend any insurer. If you are worried about your rate, ask your own insurer how a conviction would be treated at renewal.

  • The ~20% figure is an average across the US.
  • A suspended license can also affect whether you can keep coverage, depending on the insurer.
  • Check your policy terms rather than relying on a general estimate.

Can the charge be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes charges are amended or dismissed, but the outcome depends on the facts, the court and the prosecutor. Our sources do not list specific dismissal rules for Tennessee, so we cannot say how often it happens.

Things that commonly matter in any state include whether the suspension was valid, whether you had proper notice, and whether the suspension has since been cleared.

These are general points, not advice. If you want guidance on your own case, the court clerk can explain procedure, and a licensed attorney can advise on strategy.

Do not ignore the citation while you decide what to do. Missed court dates usually create new problems.

What happens if I don't pay or don't show up?

Because this is a criminal offense with possible jail, skipping a court date or ignoring the citation can make things worse. The exact consequences are set by Tennessee law and the court handling your case.

Common results in many states include added costs, further license problems or a warrant. We cannot confirm which of these apply in your county from the sources we use.

The safest step is to read the citation, note the court and date, and contact the clerk with questions. The clerk can confirm what is owed and what options the court offers, such as payment arrangements.

Remember that county court costs and state litigation taxes make up most of the total, so ask for the full amount rather than just the fine.
