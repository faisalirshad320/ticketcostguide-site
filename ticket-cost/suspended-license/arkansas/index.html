How much is a driving on a suspended license ticket in Arkansas?

Nationally, a driving on a suspended license charge commonly costs $200–$2,500+. It is a criminal offense with possible jail, not just a fine (Arkansas DFA Driver Services).

In Arkansas, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not state an exact Arkansas dollar figure, because the amount depends on the court and the facts of your case.

The national range is wide for a reason. Fines vary by state, by court, and often by whether this is a first or repeat offense. The top of the range is open-ended, which is why it ends with a plus sign.

Your citation and the paperwork from the court are the only reliable source for what you owe. Treat any national range as a rough guide only.

How is the fine set, and what are court costs?

The court decides the exact fine for this violation within the state's traffic code. The figure is not fixed by a single published number that we can quote for every case.

Court costs are added by the district court and vary by county. Two people with the same charge in different counties can therefore pay different totals.

When you read your paperwork, look for these separate pieces:

  • The fine itself, set by the court.
  • Court costs and any surcharges added by the district court.
  • Any other amounts the court orders, such as costs tied to a sentence.

The state statutes are available through Justia's Arkansas code. Reading the traffic code can show the framework, but the court still sets the amount in your case.

If the citation lists a total, ask the court clerk what it includes. Clerks can explain how the amount is made up, though they cannot give legal advice.

Is driving on a suspended license a crime in Arkansas?

Yes, it is treated as a criminal offense, with possible jail. That sets it apart from a typical speeding ticket, which is usually handled as a civil-style traffic matter.

The words "possible jail" mean jail is within the court's range of options. They do not mean everyone receives it. What a judge actually orders depends on the court and the circumstances.

A criminal charge can matter beyond the fine. It may appear on your record, and the court process can be more formal than paying a simple ticket.

Because of this, it is worth reading the citation closely for a court date. Missing a date on a criminal matter can add problems on top of the original charge.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice. If jail is a possibility in your case, consider speaking with a licensed attorney.

How many points does it add to my Arkansas record?

Arkansas uses a points system where points are assigned by violation. The figure we have locked is 3–8 points by speed, and 14+ points can trigger suspension.

Those numbers come from the speeding side of the system. We do not have a locked point value for driving on a suspended license, so we will not guess one.

What the 14+ threshold tells you is that points matter most when they pile up. A driver already near that level has more to lose from any new conviction.

To see exactly how your record is treated, check with Arkansas DFA Driver Services. They keep the driving record and can tell you your status.

The Governors Highway Safety Association also keeps a general overview of state traffic laws at GHSA State Laws.

Does it raise my insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. This is a national figure, not an Arkansas-specific one.

Your own change could be higher or lower. Insurers set rates using their own formulas, your history, and how they view the offense.

A suspended license charge can also lead to questions at renewal. We cannot say how any specific insurer will react, and we do not recommend insurers.

If you want a realistic picture, ask your insurer how a conviction would affect your policy. Compare that with how long the effect might last under their rules.

  • The national 20% figure is an average, not a promise.
  • Your driving history changes the result.
  • Different insurers treat the same conviction differently.

Can it be dismissed or reduced?

It may be possible in some cases, but that is up to the court. We cannot predict whether a charge will be dismissed or reduced.

A few practical steps can help you understand your position:

  1. Read the citation and note the court, the charge, and any date to appear.
  2. Find out why the license is suspended. Arkansas DFA Driver Services can tell you the status of your license.
  3. Ask what is needed to reinstate the license, and whether anything is outstanding.
  4. Contact the court clerk about how to respond and what your options are.
  5. Consider consulting a licensed attorney, especially because jail is possible.

Whether you were aware of the suspension, and whether the license has since been reinstated, can matter to a court. Bring any paperwork you have, since it may help explain your situation.

Getting the license reinstated does not by itself erase a charge already issued. It does stop the problem from continuing.

What happens if I don't pay or don't show up?

Ignoring the citation usually makes things worse. The exact steps depend on the court, but unresolved cases can lead to added costs and further action.

Because this is a criminal offense with possible jail, a missed court date can carry more weight than a missed payment on a simple ticket.

Court costs are added by the district court and vary by county. Late handling may change what you owe, so ask the clerk where you stand.

Also remember that the points system has a suspension threshold of 14+ points. Adding new problems to an already suspended record is a poor position to be in.

If you cannot pay in full, ask the court whether it offers any arrangement. We do not know what a particular court allows, so ask directly.
