How much is a driving on a suspended license ticket in Georgia?

Nationally, the cost is commonly $200–$2,500+, and it is a criminal offense with possible jail. In Georgia, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Georgia Department of Driver Services, last reviewed Oct 2026).

We do not publish a single Georgia dollar figure for this charge. That is deliberate: the number on your citation or court paperwork is the one that matters, and it can differ from court to court.

The national range is a reference point only. It is not a Georgia fine schedule and it is not a prediction for your case.

The range is wide because the amount depends on what the court imposes, and on how the case is classified. A charge that is a criminal offense is handled differently from a simple parking or equipment ticket.

How is the fine set in Georgia?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The citation or the court clerk is where the actual number comes from.

You can read the underlying law through the Georgia statutes on Justia (Georgia statutes via Justia). Look in the traffic title for the provisions on driving while a license is suspended or revoked.

Things that can shape the outcome, in general terms:

  • The court that hears the case, since amounts vary by court.
  • The reason the license was suspended in the first place.
  • Whether the case is treated as a criminal matter with possible jail.
  • Any additional state fees that attach to a related charge.

We do not list specific tiers here because we have no locked figures for them. Ask the clerk what applies to your citation.

Is driving on a suspended license a criminal offense?

Yes, nationally it is commonly treated as a criminal offense with possible jail, and our Georgia source summary reflects that (Georgia Department of Driver Services). That makes it more serious than most ordinary traffic tickets.

A criminal charge can mean a mandatory court appearance rather than a simple mail-in payment. It can also mean the matter appears on records beyond your driving history.

We cannot say what sentence a particular court might impose, because we do not have a locked figure or rule for it. For questions about your own exposure, a licensed attorney is the right person to ask. We do not recommend any specific one.

How many points does it add to my Georgia record?

We do not have a locked point value for this specific offense, so we will not state one. Check your citation and your record with the Georgia Department of Driver Services (Georgia DDS).

What we can give you is the state's speeding point scale, which shows how the system works: 2 points for 15–18 mph over, 3 points for 19–23 over, 4 points for 24–33 over, and 6 points for 34 or more over.

Fifteen points in 24 months means a suspension. That matters here because a suspension is how many drivers end up facing this charge in the first place.

The speeding scale above is not the point value for driving on a suspended license. It is included to explain the 15-point threshold.

Does it raise my insurance?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Georgia rate and not a quote.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the conviction is recorded. Some insurers also react to a lapse or suspension on its own, separate from the ticket.

If you want a real number, ask your insurer or an independent agent how a conviction would affect your policy. We do not recommend a specific insurer.

What happens if I don't pay, and what extra fees apply?

Georgia adds a $200 Super Speeder state fee on top of the court fine for the speeding offense it applies to. If that fee is not paid within 120 days, the license is suspended (Georgia DDS).

That fee is tied to Super Speeder cases, so it will not apply to every driving-on-a-suspended-license citation. It is listed here because it shows how an unpaid state fee can create a new suspension.

For any citation, unpaid amounts and missed court dates can cause further problems. A new suspension can in turn lead to another suspended-license charge if you drive.

  • Read the citation for the payment deadline and court date.
  • Ask the clerk which fees are added to the fine.
  • Do not drive while the license is suspended.

Can it be dismissed or reduced?

Courts have discretion, and the amount is set by the court on the citation. Whether a charge can be dismissed or reduced depends on the facts and on the court, and we cannot predict it.

Practical steps people commonly take:

  1. Confirm the exact reason and dates of the suspension with Georgia DDS (Georgia DDS).
  2. Check that you have the correct court and deadline.
  3. Ask the clerk about the options available for your charge.
  4. Consider speaking with a licensed attorney, because this is a criminal matter with possible jail.
