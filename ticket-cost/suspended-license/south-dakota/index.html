How much is a driving on a suspended license ticket in South Dakota?

The lead figure is a national one: nationally commonly $200–$2,500+; a criminal offense with possible jail; in South Dakota the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (South Dakota Department of Public Safety). That is the range we can support, and we do not print a single South Dakota dollar amount.

The wide spread reflects how much outcomes differ. The amount can depend on the court, the details of the citation and the driving record behind the suspension.

Read your citation first. It is the document that tells you whether the court has set an amount, whether you must appear, and what the charge is.

This page is an information guide, not legal advice. For questions about your own case, contact the court named on the citation or a licensed attorney of your choosing.

How is the fine set in South Dakota?

The exact fine for this violation in South Dakota is set by the court within the state's traffic code. It is not a flat statewide price that every driver pays. The state's statutes are published online through South Dakota statutes via Justia, and that is where the offense language lives.

Court costs are included in the standard scheduled amounts. If your citation shows a scheduled amount, that number is meant to be the all-in figure rather than a base fine with costs stacked on top. If the court sets the amount itself, ask the clerk what the total is and what it covers.

Because this is a criminal offense rather than a simple infraction, the court may have more discretion than it does for a minor moving violation. That is one reason the national range runs so wide.

  • The court on your citation decides the amount.
  • The traffic code frames what the court may do.
  • The clerk can confirm the total owed and the payment options.

Is driving on a suspended license a crime in South Dakota?

Our locked source describes it as a criminal offense with possible jail. That sets it apart from a standard speeding or equipment ticket, which is usually a civil-style penalty only.

"Possible jail" means jail is one outcome a court may consider. It does not mean every driver receives it. We do not state a jail length for South Dakota because it is not in our locked facts.

A criminal charge can also carry consequences beyond the fine. It can show up on a record in ways a simple traffic infraction does not, so it is worth taking the citation seriously and reading the charge language closely.

If your citation lists a court date, treat it as mandatory. Missing it can make things worse than the original charge.

How many points does driving on a suspended license add in South Dakota?

South Dakota assigns points per conviction toward suspension thresholds, and the threshold is 15 points in 12 months. Our locked facts do not give a point value for this specific offense, so we will not guess one.

What matters is the way points work. Each conviction adds to your total, and reaching the threshold within the 12-month window can lead to a suspension. A driver who is already suspended is by definition close to the system's limits, so another conviction is a real concern.

To find the point value that applies to your charge, check the South Dakota Department of Public Safety at dps.sd.gov or ask the court clerk. Your driving record will show the points currently counted.

Does a suspended license conviction raise insurance?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure and not a South Dakota rate or a quote from any insurer.

A conviction for driving on a suspended license is more serious than a typical moving violation, so the effect on your own premium could be different from that average. We cannot say by how much, and we do not recommend any insurer.

  • The ~20% figure is a national average for moving-violation convictions.
  • Your actual change depends on your insurer, your record and your policy.
  • Ask your insurer how it treats a conviction like this before your renewal.

The fine is a one-time cost. Insurance changes can last across several renewals, so they are worth weighing when you compare options.

Can a suspended license charge be dismissed or reduced?

It may be possible in some cases, but we cannot promise it. Whether a charge is dismissed, reduced or resolved another way is up to the court and the prosecutor, and the facts of your case matter a great deal.

Common questions people raise with the court or a licensed attorney include whether the suspension was valid, whether you received proper notice of it, and whether the suspension has since been cleared. These are questions to ask, not outcomes we can predict.

  1. Read the citation and note the charge and the court date.
  2. Check your license status with the South Dakota Department of Public Safety.
  3. Contact the court clerk about your options and the total amount owed.
  4. Consider speaking with a licensed attorney, especially because jail is possible.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend a specific attorney. Choose one based on your own research.

What happens if I don't pay or don't show up?

Ignoring a citation tends to make a problem bigger. For a charge that is a criminal offense with possible jail, a missed court date can lead to further action by the court. We do not list the specific penalties for South Dakota because they are not in our locked facts.

If you cannot pay the amount in full, contact the clerk before the due date and ask what options exist. Courts handle payment questions differently, and asking early is usually better than waiting.

If your license is suspended, you generally need to resolve the underlying suspension as well. Paying a fine does not by itself end a suspension.
