How much is a driving on a suspended license ticket in Alabama?

Nationally, this offense commonly runs $200–$2,500+, and it is treated as a criminal offense with possible jail (Alabama Law Enforcement Agency (ALEA)). That is a national range, not an Alabama price tag.

In Alabama, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. This guide does not state a specific Alabama dollar figure, because we could not verify one from the sources listed below.

The base fine is only part of the bill. Municipal and district court costs vary by jurisdiction and often exceed the base fine. Two drivers charged with the same offense in different places can owe very different totals.

Where to look: your citation and the clerk of the court named on it are the only reliable places to find the amount you owe.

Because the offense can carry possible jail, the financial penalty is not the whole picture. The court outcome, not just the fine, is what matters most.

How is the fine set for driving on a suspended license in Alabama?

The exact fine is set by the court within the state's traffic code. There is no single sticker price printed on the citation by the officer in the way a parking fine might be.

Several things can shape the final number:

  • The court that hears the case (municipal or district court).
  • Court costs and surcharges added on top of the base fine.
  • Whether the case ends in a conviction, a reduction, or a dismissal.
  • Any other charges on the same citation.

You can read the underlying state law through Alabama statutes via Justia. We do not quote specific section numbers here because the locked data for this page does not include them. If you rely on a statute, check that the version you read is current.

The takeaway is that the court, not a statewide chart, decides. Read the citation carefully for the court name, the charge description and any listed date or instructions.

Is driving on a suspended license a criminal offense in Alabama?

The locked national data describes this violation as a criminal offense with possible jail (Alabama Law Enforcement Agency (ALEA)). That separates it from a routine speeding or equipment ticket.

A criminal charge can mean a court appearance is expected rather than optional. It can also mean the outcome goes on a record in a way a simple infraction might not.

We cannot tell you what sentence a particular court will impose. The amount of any fine, and whether jail is actually ordered, is decided case by case.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice. If you face a criminal charge, consider speaking with a licensed Alabama attorney about your own situation.

How many points does driving on a suspended license add in Alabama?

Alabama's points system, as recorded in our locked facts, is 2 points (25 or less over) or 5 points (more than 25 over). Those tiers are written around speeding, so they do not tell you a point value for a suspended-license charge.

We are not stating a point value for this offense, because the locked data does not give one. Check with ALEA through Alabama Law Enforcement Agency (ALEA) or your court paperwork to see how your specific conviction is scored.

The threshold that matters for point accumulation is this: 12–14 points in 2 years = 60-day suspension. A driver already under suspension who adds more points may face further trouble on a license they are trying to restore.

For a broader comparison of how states handle points and traffic penalties, see Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws.

Does driving on a suspended license raise insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Alabama-specific one, and it is not specific to this offense.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the conviction is recorded. Some drivers see a larger increase, and some see little change.

A suspended license can also create separate insurance questions, such as whether a policy stays active or how a company views a lapse. We do not have locked data on those points, so we do not make claims about them.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend specific insurers. If you want to know how a conviction would affect your policy, ask your own insurer or agent directly.

Can a suspended license charge be dismissed or reduced in Alabama?

It can be possible, but outcomes depend on the facts and the court. We have no locked data on how often charges are reduced or dismissed in Alabama, so we will not give a percentage.

Things people commonly look into include:

  • Whether the suspension was actually in effect on the date of the stop.
  • Whether proper notice of the suspension was given.
  • Whether reinstatement paperwork or fees were already completed.
  • Whether the court offers any alternative resolution for the charge.

Because this is a criminal offense with possible jail, getting individual advice matters more than it would for a minor ticket. A licensed attorney can review your record and the citation.

Fixing the underlying license problem is a separate step from resolving the ticket. ALEA is the state agency to check for license status: Alabama Law Enforcement Agency (ALEA).

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the citation?

Ignoring a citation for a criminal offense is risky. The court sets the consequences, and they can include additional penalties on top of the original amount.

Since court costs often exceed the base fine, an unpaid balance may grow in ways that surprise people. The citation or the clerk of the court is the place to confirm what is owed and by when.

Do not assume that a missed date simply passes. Contact the court named on the citation as soon as you can to understand your options.

Remember that the amount you owe is set by the court on the citation. If a payment website or third party quotes you a different figure, check it against the court's own records.
