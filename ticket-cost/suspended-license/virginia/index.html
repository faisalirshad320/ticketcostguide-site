How much does driving on a suspended license cost in Virginia?

Nationally, a driving on a suspended license charge commonly costs $200–$2,500+, and it is a criminal offense with possible jail (Virginia Department of Motor Vehicles). That is a national range, not a Virginia price.

In Virginia, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Virginia dollar figure, because the amount depends on the court and the facts of the case.

The fine is not the only cost. Fixed processing costs apply to every prepaid ticket, and reckless cases add court-set fines and fees.

Your citation and the court that issued it are the only reliable places to find your actual amount.

When you read your paperwork, look for these items:

  • The exact charge listed on the citation.
  • The name of the court and its contact details.
  • Whether the ticket can be prepaid or you must appear.
  • Any court costs or fees listed separately from the fine.

Is driving on a suspended license a crime in Virginia?

Our sourced figures describe this violation as a criminal offense with possible jail. That sets it apart from many ordinary traffic tickets, which people often handle by mail or online.

Because it can be a criminal matter, the stakes are higher than the fine alone. A conviction can affect your record, your license status and your insurance.

Whether your citation can be prepaid or requires a court appearance is shown on the citation or available from the court clerk. Do not assume you can simply pay and move on.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot tell you how the charge applies to your situation. A licensed attorney of your choice can do that.

How is the fine set in Virginia?

The exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. You can read the code itself through Virginia statutes via Justia.

This is why two drivers with similar tickets can see different totals. The court, the circumstances and any added charges all play a role.

To find your own total, work through these steps:

  1. Read the charge and any code section printed on your citation.
  2. Contact the court named on the citation and ask for the total amount due.
  3. Ask which processing costs and fees are included in that total.
  4. Ask whether a court date is required before any payment is accepted.

Get any figure in writing or from the court's official channels, not from a third-party estimate.

How many points does driving on a suspended license add in Virginia?

Virginia uses a demerit point system. Points run from 3–6 demerit points by offense, with 6 for reckless offenses.

We do not state a specific point value for this charge, because the value depends on the offense as recorded. The Virginia DMV can confirm the points tied to your conviction.

Points matter because the DMV suspends at point thresholds. A driver who is already suspended and picks up more points may face a longer road back to a valid license.

The system also rewards clean driving. Drivers earn +1 safe point per clean year.

Points are separate from the fine. You can owe court money and also carry demerit points from the same case.

Does a suspended license conviction raise insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Virginia-specific rate.

Your own change depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the conviction is recorded. We do not recommend any insurer and cannot predict your quote.

Because this charge involves a license that was already suspended, insurers may also look closely at your overall record. Ask your insurer directly how a conviction would affect your policy.

For a rough sense of scale, a 20% increase applies to your whole premium, and it can last as long as the conviction stays on your record.

Can a suspended license charge be dismissed or reduced?

It can happen in some cases, but we cannot say whether it applies to you. Outcomes depend on the court, the facts and the law.

Common questions people raise with the court or with their own attorney include:

  • Whether the suspension was valid and properly notified.
  • Whether the license had already been reinstated.
  • Whether the court offers any alternative resolution for this charge.

Because the charge is a criminal offense with possible jail, many drivers choose to speak with a licensed attorney before they respond. TicketCostGuide does not recommend a specific attorney.

The court clerk can explain procedure, but clerks cannot give legal advice.

What happens if I don't pay or don't show up?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts can add costs, and the DMV can act on the record of a conviction or missed obligation.

Fixed processing costs apply to every prepaid ticket, and reckless cases add court-set fines and fees. Delay does not remove those costs.

Since this is a criminal offense with possible jail, missing a required court date is a serious risk. Contact the court as soon as you can to confirm what is required and by when.

The Virginia DMV can tell you your current license status and what is needed to resolve it.
