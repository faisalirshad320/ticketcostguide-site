How much is a driving on a suspended license ticket in Indiana?

Nationally, the cost of this charge is commonly $200–$2,500+. It is a criminal offense with possible jail. That range is a national figure, not an Indiana price (Indiana Bureau of Motor Vehicles).

In Indiana the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not state a single Indiana dollar figure because the court decides it case by case.

Treat the national range as a rough planning guide only. Your citation and the court that handles it control the real number.

The fine is only part of the bill. County court costs are added to the judgment, and those costs can be larger than the fine itself.

How is the fine set?

The exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. That means the amount depends on the court hearing your case, not on a fixed statewide price list.

You can read the Indiana statutes through Indiana statutes via Justia. The Indiana Bureau of Motor Vehicles also publishes license and suspension information at in.gov/bmv.

Because the charge is criminal, the court may also have the option of jail time. What applies to you depends on the citation, the court and your record.

  • The citation shows the charge and the court that will handle it.
  • The court sets the fine within the state's traffic code.
  • County court costs are added to the judgment.

Is driving on a suspended license a criminal offense in Indiana?

Yes, the locked figure we rely on describes it as a criminal offense with possible jail. That sets it apart from an ordinary speeding ticket that many drivers pay by mail.

A criminal charge can bring a court appearance, a record and penalties beyond a fine. We cannot tell you how a particular charge will be classified or sentenced, because that depends on the facts and the court.

If you have received a citation, read it closely for the charge, the court and any date you must appear. For questions about your own case, a licensed attorney is the right person to ask. TicketCostGuide does not recommend specific attorneys.

What court costs and surcharges are added?

County court costs are added to the judgment and often exceed the fine for low speeds. Those costs vary by county and court.

This means two drivers with the same charge could pay different totals depending on where the case is heard. Ask the clerk of the court on your citation for the full amount due before you pay.

  • Fine: set by the court on the citation.
  • County court costs: added to the judgment and vary by county.
  • Other costs: any additional amounts should be itemized by the court.

We do not list exact Indiana surcharge amounts because they are not part of the sourced figures on this page.

How many points does it add in Indiana?

Indiana's points system is described as 2–8 points by mph over. That description is written around speeding, so it does not give a point value for driving on a suspended license.

What the sourced facts do say is that accumulating points triggers BMV probation and suspension. Points matter most if your record already has other violations.

To find out whether this charge adds points to your record, check with the Indiana Bureau of Motor Vehicles. Do not assume a number that is not shown on your record or citation.

Does it raise my car insurance?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Indiana-specific one.

Whether a suspended-license conviction changes your rate depends on your insurer, your record and how the conviction is reported. We do not give advice on insurers or recommend one.

Your own premium change could be higher or lower than the national average. Ask your insurer how a conviction would be treated.

A suspension can also affect whether an insurer will cover you at all. Policy terms differ, so check yours directly.

Can it be dismissed or reduced, and what if I don't pay?

Because the exact amount is set by the court, outcomes can differ. Some courts may offer options, but we cannot say what your court allows, and we do not give legal advice.

If you want to contest the charge or ask about reducing it, a licensed attorney can explain your options. You can also contact the court clerk listed on your citation about procedures and deadlines.

Do not ignore the citation. Because this is a criminal offense with possible jail, missing a court date or leaving the judgment unpaid can make matters worse. The sourced facts also note that accumulating points triggers BMV probation and suspension, so more problems on your record can add to the trouble.

  • Read the citation for the court and any appearance date.
  • Ask the clerk for the total amount due, including county court costs.
  • Check your record with the BMV.
