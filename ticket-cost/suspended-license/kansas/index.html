How much does driving on a suspended license cost in Kansas?

The lead figure is a national one: driving on a suspended license is commonly $200–$2,500+, and it is a criminal offense with possible jail (Kansas Department of Revenue — Division of Vehicles). That range covers many states and does not describe a Kansas price list.

In Kansas, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Kansas dollar figure, because the number you owe depends on the court handling your case and what it adds on top.

Read your citation or court notice first. The amount the court sets, plus any court costs, is the figure that counts for your case.

The fine is only one part of the total cost. Court costs, possible jail exposure, a longer suspension and higher insurance rates can all add to it.

How is the fine set in Kansas?

The exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. There is no one number that applies to every driver in every court.

Court costs are added by the municipal or district court. Which court hears the case usually depends on where the citation was issued and how the charge was filed.

  • Fine: set by the court on the citation.
  • Court costs: added by the municipal or district court.
  • Other consequences: because it is a criminal offense, jail is possible.

Two people cited for the same offense can leave with different totals. Prior history, the court and the circumstances of the stop can all play a role. We cannot say how a particular judge will rule.

For the statutes themselves, Kansas traffic law is published through Kansas statutes via Justia. Statutes can be amended, so check the current text or ask the court clerk.

Is driving on a suspended license a crime in Kansas?

Yes. Driving on a suspended license is treated as a criminal offense with possible jail, not as a routine ticket you can simply mail in without thinking about it. That is why it ranks above most moving violations in seriousness.

"Possible jail" means the court has the option. It does not mean every case ends in jail. We do not state a specific jail term here because we have no locked figure for it, and the right answer depends on the charge written on your citation.

A criminal charge can also appear on a background record. If you are worried about that, a licensed Kansas attorney can explain it. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice or recommend a specific attorney.

How many points does it add in Kansas?

Kansas has no demerit-point system. You will not see a point value attached to this offense the way you would in a point-based state.

That does not mean convictions are harmless. Three moving-violation convictions within 12 months can trigger suspension. If you already have a suspension on record, additional convictions can make your situation worse.

  • No demerit points are assigned.
  • Convictions are still recorded on your driving record.
  • Three moving-violation convictions within 12 months can trigger suspension.

For questions about your own record or reinstatement status, the Kansas Department of Revenue — Division of Vehicles is the agency to ask.

Does it raise insurance rates?

Often, yes. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Kansas-specific one.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your history and how the conviction is recorded. Some drivers see little change and others see more. We do not recommend any insurer and cannot predict your quote.

A ~20% average increase is a rough national benchmark. Ask your insurer how a conviction would affect your own policy.

A suspended license can also cause a lapse in coverage or a need for different coverage. Contact your insurer directly to find out what applies.

Can it be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes cases are resolved in ways other than a straight conviction, but outcomes depend on the facts and the court. We cannot promise any result.

Questions that often matter include whether the suspension was actually in effect at the time of the stop and whether you had proper notice. Documents from the Division of Vehicles may help show your license status on the date of the citation.

  • Get your driving record and suspension paperwork.
  • Read the exact charge on the citation.
  • Contact the court clerk about deadlines and appearance requirements.
  • Consider speaking with a licensed Kansas attorney, since jail is possible.

Because this is a criminal offense, do not treat it like a minor ticket. An attorney can explain what options apply to your case.

What happens if I don't pay or don't show up?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts can add consequences when a fine goes unpaid or a required appearance is missed. The specific steps vary by court, so check your paperwork.

Because this is a criminal offense, missing a court date can carry serious consequences. Contact the court clerk as soon as you can if you cannot make the date or pay in full.

An unresolved case can also complicate efforts to get your license reinstated. The Kansas Department of Revenue — Division of Vehicles can tell you what is needed on the licensing side.

Frequently asked questions

How much is a driving on a suspended license ticket in Kansas?

Is driving on a suspended license a criminal offense in Kansas?

Yes. It is treated as a criminal offense with possible jail, not a simple ticket. Possible jail means the court has that option, not that every case ends that way. A licensed Kansas attorney can explain how the charge applies to you.

Does Kansas use points for a suspended license violation?

No. Kansas has no demerit-point system, so no point value is assigned. Convictions still matter, though. Three moving-violation convictions within 12 months can trigger suspension, so additional convictions can create more problems for your license.

The court sets it. The exact fine is set by the court within the state's traffic code and shown on the citation. Court costs are added by the municipal or district court, so the total can vary depending on which court handles the case.

Can three tickets in a year get my Kansas license suspended?

Yes, it can. Three moving-violation convictions within 12 months can trigger suspension in Kansas, even though the state has no demerit-point system. Check your driving record with the Division of Vehicles for your own status.
