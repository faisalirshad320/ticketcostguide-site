How much does driving on a suspended license cost in Massachusetts?

Nationally, the cost is commonly $200–$2,500+. It is also described as a criminal offense with possible jail. In Massachusetts, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Massachusetts Registry of Motor Vehicles).

That means this page cannot give you one Massachusetts dollar figure. The number on your paperwork is the one that counts.

The $200–$2,500+ range is a national figure. It is not a Massachusetts schedule, and your own amount may fall inside or outside it.

The fine is only part of the cost. Surcharges, possible court-ordered consequences, insurance changes and time away from work can all add to the total.

How is the fine set?

For this violation, the amount is decided by the court and written on the citation, not by a fixed price list you can look up in advance. Because the offense is treated as criminal, with possible jail, it usually goes through a court process rather than a simple pay-by-mail step.

The Massachusetts traffic code gives the court a framework to work within. The text is available through Massachusetts statutes via Justia.

Several things can matter in practice:

  • The exact charge listed on your citation
  • The court that hears the case
  • The reason your license was suspended in the first place
  • Whether the court sees this as a first event or part of a pattern

Read the citation closely. It should show the charge and your court instructions. If anything is unclear, the court clerk can explain procedure, though not give legal advice.

Are there extra surcharges or court costs?

One locked Massachusetts fact is a $50 head-injury surcharge. It applies to every speeding conviction. This page does not claim that the same surcharge is added to a suspended license conviction, because the sourced fact is about speeding.

Court costs and surcharges vary by case. Ask the court what applies to your specific charge, and check the total on any payment notice before you pay.

Do not assume the number on the citation is the final total. Ask whether surcharges or costs are added on top.

How many points does it add?

Massachusetts uses insurance surcharge points (SDIP) rather than license suspension points for ordinary speeding. This page does not give a point value for a suspended license offense, because none is in the sourced facts.

The thresholds that are sourced:

  • 3 surchargeable events in 24 months require a driver retraining course
  • 7 in 3 years means a suspension

Because your license is already suspended, any new event matters. A further suspension can lengthen the time before you can legally drive again. The Massachusetts Registry of Motor Vehicles is the place to check your current status and reinstatement requirements.

Does it raise insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Massachusetts quote.

Your own change could be higher, lower or none at all. It depends on your insurer, your record and how the conviction is classified.

The surcharge point system matters here. Massachusetts uses SDIP points for insurance, so a conviction can affect what you pay for several years.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend insurers. The most reliable way to learn your actual effect is to ask your own insurer or agent how a conviction would be rated.

Can it be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but there is no guaranteed route. The charge, the court and the facts of your case all matter. Possible jail makes this more serious than a typical fine-only ticket.

Common questions to raise with the court or a licensed attorney:

  • Was the suspension notice properly sent to you?
  • Has the underlying suspension issue since been resolved?
  • Are there deadlines to respond or appear?
  • Could the charge be changed?

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice or recommend a specific attorney. If jail is possible, consider speaking with a licensed Massachusetts attorney. Many courts can also explain whether you may ask for court-appointed counsel.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore it?

Ignoring a citation is risky, and more so for a criminal offense with possible jail. A missed court date or unpaid amount can lead to further court action and may complicate getting your license back.

Do not wait until the last day. Check your citation for the date and location you must appear. Call the court clerk if you are unsure what is required.

Fix the underlying suspension through the Massachusetts Registry of Motor Vehicles as well. Resolving the ticket alone does not necessarily restore your driving privileges.
