How much does driving on a suspended license cost in Washington?

Nationally, the cost of driving on a suspended license is commonly $200–$2,500+. It is a criminal offense with possible jail, which sets it apart from an ordinary speeding ticket (Washington State Department of Licensing).

In Washington, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Washington dollar figure here, because the number you owe is the one printed on your paperwork or assigned by the court.

The national range is a guide to how wide the spread can be, not a Washington price list. Treat it as context only.

The $200–$2,500+ range is a national figure. Your Washington amount is the one set by the court on your citation.

The fine is only part of the picture. A criminal charge can bring other consequences, such as possible jail, a record, and effects on your driving privileges and insurance. Those are covered below.

How is the fine set in Washington?

The exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. That means the court, not a website or a fixed statewide number, decides what appears on the citation.

Statutory assessments are built into the IRLJ schedule amounts. IRLJ refers to the Infraction Rules for Courts of Limited Jurisdiction. In practical terms, the figure you see already includes those assessments rather than adding them as separate surprise line items.

Because this is a criminal matter rather than a simple infraction, the amount and any other terms can depend on the court handling the case. Read the citation closely and note the court named on it.

  • Find the court and any listed instructions on the citation.
  • Check whether a hearing or appearance is required.
  • Contact the court clerk with questions about the amount or payment.

You can read the state's traffic code through Washington statutes via Justia. We do not quote a section number or a dollar amount from it here.

How many points does a suspended license ticket add in Washington?

Washington has no point system. There is no number of points added to your record for this offense, so you will not find a point total on the citation.

Instead, suspensions follow repeat moving-violation convictions within set windows. In other words, the state looks at how many moving-violation convictions you have and when they happened, rather than adding up points.

This matters because the way a license gets suspended in Washington is different from point-based states. A driver cannot check a point balance to see how close they are to a suspension.

No points, but repeat convictions inside set windows can still lead to a suspension. The Department of Licensing is the place to confirm your own status.

To see the status of your own driving record, use the Washington State Department of Licensing. We cannot tell you from here whether your license is valid or what steps apply to you.

Does driving on a suspended license raise insurance in Washington?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Washington-specific quote.

Whether a suspended-license conviction affects your own rate depends on your insurer, your policy, and your driving history. We do not recommend any insurer, and we cannot predict what your premium will do.

It is reasonable to expect that a criminal driving offense draws attention at renewal. The 20% average is a reference point for how much a conviction can matter, not a promise.

  • Ask your insurer how it treats driving-record changes.
  • Compare the cost of any possible increase with the cost of the fine itself.
  • Keep copies of any court paperwork in case your insurer asks for it.

Over several years, the premium effect can add up to more than the fine does. That is why people often look at the whole cost, not just the amount on the citation.

Can a suspended license charge be dismissed or reduced in Washington?

It can be possible in some cases, but we cannot say whether it applies to you. Outcomes depend on the facts, the court, and your record. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice.

Since this is a criminal offense with possible jail, the stakes are higher than for a routine infraction. Many people choose to speak with a licensed attorney or a public defense resource before deciding how to respond. We do not recommend any specific attorney.

What you can do on your own is gather facts that may matter to the court:

  • The citation itself, including the court and any dates.
  • Your driving record and license status from the Washington State Department of Licensing.
  • Any notices you received about a suspension.

Do not assume that paying the amount on the citation ends everything. Because of the criminal nature of the offense, ask the court clerk what a payment or plea means before you act.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the citation?

We do not list specific penalty amounts or deadlines, because they are set by the court on your citation. What we can say is that ignoring a criminal citation is a risk, since the offense carries possible jail.

Courts generally expect a response by the date and in the manner shown on the paperwork. Missing that step can make a situation worse, and may lead to added consequences that the court describes in its own notices.

If you are unsure what to do, take these steps:

  1. Read the citation for the court name, date, and instructions.
  2. Contact the court clerk to confirm what is required.
  3. Check your license status with the Washington State Department of Licensing.
  4. Consider getting qualified legal help before your court date.

If you cannot pay the full amount at once, ask the court what options exist. Whether any are available is for the court to decide.
