How much does driving on a suspended license cost in New Jersey?

Nationally, the cost is commonly $200–$2,500+. It is a criminal offense with possible jail, not a simple fine-only ticket. In New Jersey the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (New Jersey Motor Vehicle Commission).

We do not list a single New Jersey fine for this violation. The range above is a national one, and the amount you owe depends on the court that handles your case.

Your citation and the court that issued it are the only reliable source for your exact amount. Treat any single "standard" figure online with caution.

How is the fine set for a suspended license charge?

The fine is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The statutes are available through New Jersey statutes via Justia.

Because this is a criminal offense with possible jail, a judge may have more room to decide than with a basic parking or equipment ticket. The amount can differ from one court to another.

Several things can matter, though we cannot say how any one of them will change your result:

  • The wording of the charge on your citation
  • The court that hears the case
  • Your driving record
  • Any surcharges added by the state

Read the citation closely. It lists the charge, the court and the next steps.

How many points does a suspended license violation add in New Jersey?

The point figures we have are tied to speeding. New Jersey assigns 2 points for 1–14 over, 4 points for 15–29 over, and 5 points for 30+ over. We do not have a point value for driving on a suspended license, so we will not guess one.

Your citation or the court can tell you whether points apply to your charge. You can also check the New Jersey Motor Vehicle Commission.

Points matter because 12 points = suspension. If you are already suspended, more points can make reinstatement harder.

Do not assume this offense carries no points just because it is criminal. Confirm it for your own case.

Are there extra court costs or surcharges?

Yes, surcharges can come on top of the court fine. When a driver reaches 6+ points within 3 years, the MVC adds an insurance surcharge of $150+.

These MVC surcharges at 6+ points are billed annually for 3 years on top of court fines. The first bill is not the whole cost.

Court costs can also be added by the court that handles your case. We do not list those amounts because they are not in our locked sources.

  • Court fine: set by the court on the citation
  • MVC surcharge: $150+ at 6+ points within 3 years
  • Billing: annually for 3 years

Does a suspended license conviction raise insurance?

Often, yes. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a New Jersey quote.

Your own change depends on your insurer, your record and the conviction. TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer and cannot predict your premium.

The MVC surcharge is separate from what your insurer charges. A driver can face both if the points total reaches the 6+ threshold.

Over several years, the insurance effect can cost more than the fine itself. Ask your insurer how a conviction would be treated.

Can it be dismissed or reduced?

It may be possible in some cases, but we cannot say whether it applies to yours. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice.

Because this is a criminal offense with possible jail, many people speak with a licensed attorney or a public defender's office before deciding how to respond. We do not recommend a specific attorney.

Before any court date, it helps to gather:

  1. The citation and any court notices
  2. Your driving record from the New Jersey Motor Vehicle Commission
  3. Any documents about why your license was suspended

Knowing the reason for the suspension is often the first step in understanding the charge.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the ticket?

Ignoring a citation for a criminal offense is risky. We do not have locked figures for late penalties, so we will not name any.

What we can say is that points can lead to suspension: 12 points = suspension. Unpaid costs and ignored court dates can make an existing problem worse, so contact the court listed on your citation promptly.

Jail is possible for this offense, which is why the court notice should be treated as serious. Ask the court clerk or a licensed attorney about your options.
