How much is a driving on a suspended license ticket in Illinois?

Nationally, a driving on a suspended license charge commonly runs $200–$2,500+. It is a criminal offense with possible jail, not a simple fine-only ticket (Illinois Secretary of State).

In Illinois, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not list a single Illinois dollar figure, because the number you owe depends on what the court orders in your case.

The national range is a national range. It is not a promise of what an Illinois court will impose, and your own citation and court paperwork control the real amount.

The fine is also only part of the picture. A conviction can bring points, a longer suspension, higher insurance costs and, in some cases, jail exposure.

How is the fine set for driving on a suspended license in Illinois?

Illinois sets the amount within the state's traffic code, and the court fills in the figure on the citation. You can read the underlying statutes through Illinois statutes via Justia.

Several things can shape the final number:

  • The statutory range the court must work within.
  • Whether the court treats the case as a first or repeat event.
  • Why the license was suspended in the first place.
  • Court costs and surcharges added on top of the fine.

For context on how statutory minimums work in Illinois, work-zone speeding carries a statutory minimum fine of $375 for a first offense (Illinois Secretary of State). That figure applies to work-zone speeding only. It is not the amount for a suspended license charge.

Because this is a criminal offense, you may also be told to appear in court rather than simply mail in a payment. Read the citation closely for appearance instructions.

How many points does driving on a suspended license add in Illinois?

Illinois convictions carry 5–50 point values on the Secretary of State (SOS) scale. Repeat convictions within 12 months trigger suspension (Illinois Secretary of State).

We do not have a sourced point value for this specific offense, so we will not guess one. The SOS can tell you how a particular conviction is scored on your driving record.

Two points matter most here:

  • The point scale is wide, so different violations weigh very differently.
  • A repeat conviction inside the 12-month window can trigger a suspension, which is a problem if your license is already suspended.

Driving while suspended is also different from most tickets because the original suspension usually has its own reinstatement requirements. Paying one fine does not necessarily clear the earlier issue.

Does driving on a suspended license raise insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Illinois-specific quote (Illinois Secretary of State).

Your own change could be smaller or larger. Insurers weigh your record, the type of conviction and their own underwriting rules. A criminal driving charge may be treated more seriously than a basic speeding ticket, though we cannot give an exact figure.

Some practical points:

  • Ask your insurer how a conviction would be treated before you decide how to respond to the citation.
  • Compare the possible premium increase over several years against the fine itself.
  • Keep any proof of coverage you will need when reinstating a license.

Can a suspended license charge be dismissed or reduced in Illinois?

Sometimes outcomes change, but that is decided by the prosecutor and the court, not by a general rule. We cannot say how likely it is in your case.

Things that people commonly look into include:

  • Whether you actually received notice of the suspension.
  • Whether the suspension was in effect on the date of the stop.
  • Whether the record of the suspension is accurate.
  • Whether the underlying suspension has since been resolved.

Because this is a criminal offense with possible jail, it can be worth speaking with a licensed Illinois attorney or a court-based resource. We do not recommend a specific attorney, and nothing here is legal advice.

Do not assume you can simply pay and move on. With a criminal charge, paying or pleading can mean a conviction on your record.

The Illinois Secretary of State is the place to confirm your license status and the steps required to reinstate it (Illinois Secretary of State).

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the citation?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Because driving on a suspended license is a criminal offense with possible jail, missing a court date can lead to additional consequences set by the court.

Common results of ignoring a citation include:

  • Added penalties or costs imposed by the court.
  • A longer or additional suspension.
  • A warrant or other court action for failing to appear.

We cannot give exact amounts for these, because the Illinois figure is set by the court on the citation. Check the court named on your paperwork for deadlines, payment options and whether you must appear in person.

If a repeat conviction lands within 12 months, that alone can trigger suspension under the SOS point rules (Illinois Secretary of State). For a broader view of how states handle traffic laws, see the Governors Highway Safety Association state laws page.
