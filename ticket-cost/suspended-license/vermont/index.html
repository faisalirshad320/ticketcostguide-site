How much is a driving on a suspended license ticket in Vermont?

Nationally, the cost is commonly $200–$2,500+, and it is a criminal offense with possible jail. In Vermont the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Vermont Department of Motor Vehicles).

That national range is a guide to how wide the spread can be. It is not a Vermont price list, and we do not publish an exact Vermont dollar figure for this offense.

The number that matters is the one on your citation or court paperwork. Read it, and check the court's instructions for how and when to respond.

The final cost can also differ from the fine alone. Statutory surcharges are included in the waiver amounts, so the total you are asked to pay may be higher than the base fine.

How is the fine set for a suspended license in Vermont?

The exact fine for this violation in Vermont is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The officer's citation is where the court's amount or instructions will appear.

Because this is treated as a criminal offense with possible jail, it is handled differently from a routine speeding ticket. The court has more room to decide the outcome, and the result can depend on the facts of your case.

Things that may matter to a court, in general terms:

  • Why the license was suspended in the first place
  • Whether you have prior driving-record issues
  • Whether the suspension has since been cleared
  • What the citation itself says about how to respond

We are not a law firm and cannot say how a court will weigh these in your case. For statute text, the Vermont statutes via Justia are a free place to read the traffic code yourself.

How many points does driving on a suspended license add in Vermont?

Our sourced state context describes Vermont's points system this way: 2–8 points by mph over, and 10 points in 2 years = suspension. Those mph-based values are written for speeding violations.

We do not have a sourced point value for driving on a suspended license, so we are not going to guess one. Check your citation and your driving record with the Vermont Department of Motor Vehicles to see what, if anything, was recorded.

The 10-points-in-2-years threshold matters if you already carry points. Points from earlier tickets can leave you closer to another suspension.

Because your license is already suspended, a new conviction can also complicate how and when you become eligible to drive again. The DMV is the right place to ask about reinstatement.

Does a suspended license ticket raise insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Vermont-specific one.

Your own change could be smaller or larger. Insurers rate drivers differently, and a suspended license often sits alongside other items on a record, such as the violation that caused the suspension.

We do not recommend any insurer. If you are worried about a renewal, ask your current insurer how a conviction would be treated, and compare quotes on your own.

  • The ~20% figure is an average, not a quote
  • It applies to moving-violation convictions generally
  • Your actual change depends on your insurer and your full record

Can a suspended license charge be dismissed or reduced?

It can happen in some cases, but outcomes are decided by the court and prosecutor. We cannot predict whether yours would qualify, and nothing here is legal advice.

Because the offense is criminal and can carry possible jail, many people choose to speak with a licensed attorney before responding. We do not recommend a specific attorney. Vermont courts and bar resources can point you to options, including help if cost is a concern.

Before any hearing, it helps to gather:

  • Your citation and any court notices
  • Your driving record from the Vermont Department of Motor Vehicles
  • Any paperwork showing the status of your suspension

Accurate records give you or your attorney a clearer picture of what the court will see.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the citation?

Ignoring a citation for a criminal offense is risky. Because jail is possible for this offense, missing a court date or deadline can make things worse than the original charge.

We cannot list the exact penalties for non-response in Vermont, since they are set by the court and the traffic code. Read the instructions on your citation and contact the court named on it as soon as you can.

If you cannot pay the full amount, ask the court directly what options it offers. Do not simply let the date pass.

Unpaid obligations can also complicate getting your license back later, so check your status with the DMV.

What are my options after getting this citation?

  1. Read the citation closely for the court, the date, and any amount listed.
  2. Confirm your license status and record with the Vermont Department of Motor Vehicles.
  3. Decide whether to consult a licensed attorney, given the possible jail exposure.
  4. Contact the court if you have questions about deadlines or payment.
  5. Do not drive until your license is valid, since another stop could add to the problem.
