How much is a ticket for driving on a suspended license?

The national range is wide. Driving on a suspended license is commonly $200–$2,500+; a criminal offense in every state with possible jail (Governors Highway Safety Association). That spread reflects how differently states write and enforce the offense.

The most important word in that figure is "criminal." Unlike a speeding ticket, which is usually a civil or infraction-level matter, driving while suspended is treated as a crime in every state. That changes what is at stake, because a conviction can mean jail, a criminal record and a longer suspension, not just a payment.

The fine you see on a citation is also rarely the full cost. Courts commonly add surcharges, assessments and administrative fees, and the amounts vary by state, county and court. We do not publish an exact figure for any local court because we cannot source one, and we would rather say "varies" than guess.

Key point: The $200–$2,500+ range describes the fine side only. Jail exposure, added suspension time, reinstatement steps and insurance effects all sit on top of it and vary by state.

Where you land in the range depends on several things: the state, the court, why the license was suspended, and whether this is a first or repeat charge. The sections below cover each of those, starting with one worked example from California.

How much is it in California?

California is our one locked state example. Under the Vehicle Code §14601 family of offenses, California sets fines of $300–$1,000 with possible jail for driving on a suspended license. You can look up the statute text through state codes on Justia.

The phrase "§14601 family" matters. California does not use a single section for every situation. Different subsections cover different reasons for the suspension, and the penalty can depend on which one applies to you. The locked range above is the headline for the family as a whole, not a promise for every subsection.

As with most states, the fine is not the whole bill. Court assessments and fees vary by county and court, and we do not give an exact local total. Check your citation and your court's own notice for the actual amount due.

  • Fine range: $300–$1,000
  • Jail: possible
  • Repeat offenses: penalties escalate sharply
  • DUI-related suspensions: penalties escalate sharply
  • License: extended suspension is a common additional consequence

If you are charged in California, the exact subsection on your citation is the first thing to read. It tells you which version of the offense you face and which penalties are in play.

Why do some suspended license charges cost so much more?

Two factors drive most of the escalation: repeat offenses and the reason for the original suspension. In California, penalties escalate sharply for repeat offenses and for suspensions arising from DUI. Other states follow a similar pattern, although the exact numbers vary by state.

Repeat offenses. A first charge is usually treated as the least serious version. Later charges are commonly handled with higher fines, greater jail exposure and longer suspensions. How many prior charges it takes to move into a higher tier varies by state, so we do not state a threshold.

DUI-related suspensions. If your license was suspended because of a DUI, driving on it is generally treated more seriously than driving on a license suspended for, say, an unpaid fine. The logic is that the state suspended you for a safety reason and views the violation accordingly.

Extended suspension. A longer suspension is a common additional consequence. Even if the fine feels manageable, the extra time without a license can be the larger practical cost, especially if you depend on driving for work.

Why this matters: The same act, driving once while suspended, can lead to very different outcomes depending on your history and the reason for the suspension. That is why the national range runs from the low hundreds to well over $2,500.

Other things that can change the outcome include whether you were driving a commercial vehicle, whether an accident was involved, and whether you knew about the suspension. We do not have sourced figures for those, so the details vary by state and court.

How many points does driving on a suspended license add?

We cannot give you a point number. Point values for driving on a suspended license vary by state, and some states do not use a point system the way others do. We will not guess at a figure.

There is also a logical wrinkle. Points are meant to push a driver toward a suspension, and this offense happens when a license is already suspended. In many places the bigger consequence is therefore not more points but a longer suspension, a criminal record and possible jail. Extended suspension is a common additional consequence.

To find your state's treatment, check the statute for the offense in your state code. Justia's state codes are a free place to read the text. Your state motor vehicle agency's driver record pages can also explain how convictions are recorded.

  • Look up the exact statute named on your citation.
  • Check whether your state assigns points to the offense or handles it only through suspension time.
  • Ask the court clerk what the conviction does to your driving record.

Remember that the criminal conviction itself is often more consequential than any point total. A criminal record can show up in background checks, which is a cost that no fine table captures.

Does driving on a suspended license raise insurance rates?

It can, but we have no sourced percentage or dollar figure, so we will not offer one. The size of any increase varies by insurer, state and your driving history.

What we can say is how the offense tends to be viewed. Because it is a criminal offense in every state, it is a more serious entry on a record than a typical moving violation. A suspension that arose from a DUI makes the record look more serious still, and penalties for those cases escalate sharply in the legal system.

There is a second insurance issue that is separate from rates: whether you can get coverage at all after a suspension. Some drivers find they must go through a different kind of policy or a state filing process to be reinstated. The rules for that vary by state.

What to do: Ask your insurer directly how a suspended license conviction would be treated on your policy. TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer or give insurance advice.

It also helps to understand that an uninsured period during a suspension can create its own problems. If you let a policy lapse while you were not driving, the gap may matter when you apply for coverage again. Details vary by state and by company.

Can a suspended license charge be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but it depends on the facts and the court. We cannot promise any outcome, and this page is not legal advice. Because this is a criminal offense, many people speak with a licensed attorney in their state before deciding how to respond.

These are the general situations in which people commonly raise dismissal or reduction. Whether any of them works varies by state and court.

  • The suspension was not valid. If the record shows the suspension had ended or was entered in error, that can matter.
  • Notice problems. Some offenses require that you knew, or should have known, about the suspension. If the state cannot show notice, that can be raised.
  • Reinstatement before the court date. Some courts look more favorably on a driver who has already cleared the underlying problem and restored the license. This varies widely.
  • Charge reduction. In some places a prosecutor may agree to a lesser charge, particularly for a first offense with a minor cause for the original suspension.

The reason for the suspension matters a great deal. Penalties escalate sharply for suspensions arising from DUI, so dismissal or reduction in those cases is generally harder to obtain than where the suspension came from an administrative issue.

Before you appear: Get a copy of your driving record, read the exact statute on your citation, and confirm your court date. Missing a court date can make a bad situation worse.

Prior offenses also change the picture. Because repeat offenses escalate sharply, a prior charge can limit what a court or prosecutor is willing to offer.

What happens if I don't pay or keep driving?

Ignoring the charge usually makes things worse. The specific steps vary by state and court, but the common pattern is that a missed payment or missed court date leads to added fees, further license consequences or a warrant. We do not give exact timelines or amounts because they vary.

Remember that this is a criminal offense with possible jail. A failure to appear on a criminal matter is treated differently from an unpaid parking ticket. If you cannot make a court date, contact the court clerk before the date, not after.

Continuing to drive is the largest risk. Another stop while suspended can be treated as a repeat offense, and penalties escalate sharply for repeat offenses. An extended suspension is also a common additional consequence, which can push your reinstatement further out.

  • Confirm exactly what your court requires and by when.
  • Ask whether payment plans or other arrangements are available. This varies by court.
  • Find out what you must do to have the license reinstated, which is separate from paying the fine.
  • Do not drive until the license is valid again.

If money is the obstacle, tell the court early. Some courts have options for people who cannot pay at once, but availability varies by county and court, and we cannot name specific programs.

Frequently asked questions

How much is a fine for driving on a suspended license?

The commonly cited range is $200–$2,500+, and it is a criminal offense in every state with possible jail. The exact amount varies by state, county and court. Repeat offenses and DUI-related suspensions escalate penalties, so your history and the reason for the suspension matter.

Is driving on a suspended license a felony or a misdemeanor?

We cannot give a classification for every state. What we can say is that it is a criminal offense in every state, with possible jail. Whether a given charge is a misdemeanor or a felony varies by state and circumstances, so read the statute on your citation.

How much is driving on a suspended license in California?

California's VC §14601 family sets fines of $300–$1,000 with possible jail. Penalties escalate sharply for repeat offenses and for suspensions arising from DUI. Court fees vary by county and court, so check your citation and court notice for the full amount due.

Can I go to jail for driving on a suspended license?

Yes, jail is possible. It is a criminal offense in every state with possible jail, and in California the VC §14601 family allows it alongside a fine. Whether jail is actually imposed varies by state, court and your history, especially repeat offenses.

Does a DUI suspension make the penalty worse?

Generally yes. In California, penalties escalate sharply for suspensions arising from DUI, and other states take a similar approach. The exact fine and jail exposure vary by state, so check the statute and ask the court how your underlying suspension affects the charge.

Can a suspended license ticket be dismissed?

Sometimes, depending on the facts and the court, such as an invalid suspension or notice problems. Outcomes vary by state and court, and nothing is guaranteed. Because it is a criminal offense, many people consult a licensed attorney in their state. This site does not give legal advice.

Will driving on a suspended license extend my suspension?
