How much does driving on a suspended license cost in West Virginia?

Nationally, the cost of driving on a suspended license is commonly $200–$2,500+. It is treated as a criminal offense with possible jail, so it is not handled like a basic parking or equipment ticket (West Virginia Division of Motor Vehicles).

That range is a national figure. In West Virginia, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single state dollar amount because the court decides it case by case.

The number printed on your citation or court notice is the number that matters. This page gives context, not a quote for your case.

The fine is also only part of the bill. Court costs and surcharges are added on top, and those are covered below.

How is the fine set, and what are court costs?

West Virginia courts set the amount within the state's traffic code. You can read the code through the state statutes on Justia. The court that hears your case, not a fixed statewide schedule on this site, determines what you owe.

Municipal and magistrate court costs vary and usually exceed the fine itself. That means the fine on the citation may understate the total you pay.

  • The fine: set by the court on the citation.
  • Court costs and surcharges: vary by court and usually exceed the fine.
  • Possible jail: because this is a criminal offense, a sentence is possible.

To find your real total, ask the clerk of the court named on your citation. Get the figure in writing if you can.

Is driving on a suspended license a criminal offense?

Yes. Nationally it is a criminal offense with possible jail, and West Virginia follows that general approach. That separates it from a civil infraction, where you usually only pay a fine.

A criminal charge can mean a court appearance, a record, and conditions set by a judge. It can also affect how the court views any later traffic case.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot tell you how your own charge will be handled. If jail is possible in your case, consider speaking with a licensed attorney of your own choosing.

How many points does it add to my record?

West Virginia's points system, as described in our locked state context, assigns 2–5 points by mph over the limit. Reaching 12+ points means a suspension.

Those point values are written around speeding. We do not have a locked point value for driving on a suspended license, so we will not guess one.

The more important point is the link between the two problems. If your license was suspended for points, driving anyway adds a separate criminal charge on top of the original problem. To see your current point total and the status of your suspension, contact the West Virginia Division of Motor Vehicles.

Does it raise my car insurance?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a West Virginia rate, and individual results differ by insurer and driving history.

A suspended-license conviction is more serious than a typical moving violation. Some insurers may respond more strongly, or may decline to renew, but we have no locked figure for that and will not estimate one.

  • Ask your insurer how a conviction would be rated before you decide how to resolve the case.
  • Compare quotes after the case closes.
  • Keep proof of continuous coverage once your license is valid again.

We do not recommend specific insurers.

Can it be dismissed or reduced?

It depends on the facts and on the court. Some cases turn on whether you actually received notice of the suspension, or whether the suspension was in effect on the date of the stop. Others turn on records errors.

Because the amount and outcome are set by the court on the citation, the court is the place to ask about options. A clerk can explain the process and deadlines, though clerks cannot give legal advice.

  1. Read the citation for the court name, date and charge.
  2. Check your license status with the DMV.
  3. Gather any paperwork about the suspension and any notices you received.
  4. Decide whether to consult an attorney before your court date.

Do not skip the court date. Missing it usually makes the situation worse.

What happens if I don't pay or don't show up?

Ignoring a criminal citation can lead to additional consequences. Courts commonly add costs or take further action when a case is left open. The details depend on the court that issued the citation.

Unresolved cases can also keep your license from being restored. If you are trying to get a valid license back, an open case can stand in the way.

If you cannot pay the full amount, ask the court clerk what options exist before the due date, not after.

The state's traffic laws are available on Justia. For general state-by-state context on traffic laws, the Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a reference page.
