How much is a driving on a suspended license ticket in Kentucky?

Nationally, the cost of this charge is commonly $200–$2,500+, and it is a criminal offense with possible jail (Kentucky Transportation Cabinet — Driver Licensing). That is a national range, not a Kentucky price.

In Kentucky, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not state a Kentucky dollar figure, because the amount depends on the court and the facts of the case.

Your citation and the court that handles it are the only reliable places to find your actual amount. Treat any single number you see online, including ours, as a reference range.

The fine is also only part of the bill. Court costs come on top and can change the total a great deal, as the next section explains.

How is the fine set, and what are court costs?

In Kentucky, the exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The amount is not fixed by one number on a statewide chart that we can quote here.

Court costs are set by the district court and usually exceed the fine itself. So the total you owe can be noticeably larger than the fine line on the citation.

  • Fine: set by the court on the citation.
  • Court costs: set by the district court, usually more than the fine.
  • Other consequences: because this is a criminal offense with possible jail, a court appearance may matter more than the payment amount.

You can read the Kentucky traffic code through Kentucky statutes via Justia. For licensing questions, the Kentucky Transportation Cabinet — Driver Licensing site is the official starting point.

How many points does it add in Kentucky?

Kentucky uses a points system. The tiers we reference are 3 points (10–15 over) or 6 points (16–25 over); 26+ over triggers a hearing; 12 points in 2 years = suspension.

Those tiers are written around speeding. We do not state a point value for driving on a suspended license, and you should not assume one of those numbers applies to your charge.

If your license is already suspended, the 12 points in 2 years threshold is one common reason it happened. Check the status and reason on your record with the Transportation Cabinet.

Whether your citation adds points, and how many, is something to confirm on the citation itself or with the court clerk. The state's agency pages at Kentucky Transportation Cabinet — Driver Licensing explain how the licensing side works.

Does it raise my insurance?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums ~20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Kentucky quote.

Your own change could be higher, lower or nothing, depending on your insurer, your driving history and how the case ends. We do not recommend any insurer and cannot predict your rate.

  • The ~20% figure applies to moving-violation convictions in general.
  • A criminal charge tied to a suspended license may be treated differently by your insurer than a basic ticket.
  • Ask your insurer how a conviction would be rated before you decide how to resolve the case.

Rate effects usually show up at renewal, once the insurer sees the conviction on your record.

Is driving on a suspended license a criminal offense in Kentucky?

Nationally, this is treated as a criminal offense with possible jail (Kentucky Transportation Cabinet — Driver Licensing). That makes it different from a simple traffic infraction that you pay by mail.

We do not list a Kentucky jail term or a fine ceiling. The court sets the penalty within the state's traffic code, and the exact charge on your citation matters.

Because of the possible jail exposure, many people want to understand the charge before they pay or plead. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot give legal advice. A court clerk can explain procedure, and a licensed attorney can advise on your specific case.

The statutes are available to read through Kentucky statutes via Justia.

Can it be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes charges are changed or dropped, but the locked sources we use do not give Kentucky rules or odds for this violation. We will not guess at them.

Here are general points worth checking, all of which depend on the court:

  • Read the citation. Confirm the charge, the court and the date you must appear.
  • Check your license status. Records at the Kentucky Transportation Cabinet — Driver Licensing site can show whether and why a suspension was in effect.
  • Ask the clerk. The clerk can explain what options the court offers, though not legal advice.
  • Consider counsel. Because it is a criminal offense with possible jail, speaking to a licensed attorney is a reasonable step.

Whatever outcome the court reaches sets the final fine and the court costs, which usually exceed the fine itself.

What happens if I don't pay or don't show up?

Because this is a criminal offense with possible jail, ignoring the citation is a risk. The court, not a state chart, decides what follows, and we do not list specific penalties.

Things that commonly happen when a court date is missed, in general terms, are additional court action and a harder path to resolving the case. Confirm the exact consequences with the court that issued your citation.

Court costs are set by the district court and usually exceed the fine itself, so ask for the full amount owed, not just the fine, before you pay.

If you are unsure of the deadline or the court, contact the clerk listed on the citation. Reinstatement of your license is a separate matter handled through the Kentucky Transportation Cabinet — Driver Licensing.
