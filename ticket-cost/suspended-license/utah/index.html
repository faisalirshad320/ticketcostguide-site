How much does driving on a suspended license cost in Utah?

Nationally, a driving on a suspended license charge commonly runs $200–$2,500+, and it is a criminal offense with possible jail (Utah Driver License Division). That range is a national one. It is not a Utah price list.

In Utah, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Utah dollar figure because the amount depends on the court and on the facts of your case.

Your citation or court notice is the only reliable place to find the amount you owe. If it does not show a figure, contact the court clerk listed on it.

The fine is only part of the cost. Fees built into the schedule, any license reinstatement steps, and insurance changes can all add to the total.

How is the fine set in Utah?

The exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The code is published online, and you can read it through Utah statutes via Justia.

Because the court sets the amount, two drivers with similar citations can end up with different totals. The court's schedule, the judge, and the circumstances of the stop may all matter.

Court costs and surcharges are part of this picture. In Utah, zone enhancements and fees are built into the schedule, so the number on the citation may already include them.

  • Read the citation for the stated amount or a court appearance requirement.
  • Check the court's website or call the clerk to confirm what is owed.
  • Ask whether the amount includes fees and surcharges.

Is driving on a suspended license a criminal offense in Utah?

Yes, it is treated as a criminal offense with possible jail. That sets it apart from a typical speeding or equipment ticket, which is usually handled as a civil or infraction-style matter.

"Possible jail" means jail is available to the court. It does not mean every case ends in jail. What happens depends on the court and the facts of the case.

A criminal charge can also mean a mandatory court appearance rather than a simple payment by mail. Read your citation closely for a date and time.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice. If you face a criminal charge, consider speaking with a licensed Utah attorney.

How many points does it add in Utah?

Our sourced facts do not give a point value for driving on a suspended license, so we do not state one. The Utah point figures we can verify relate to speeding.

For context, speeding carries 35–75 points by mph over on Utah's scale (Utah Driver License Division). Reaching 200 points in 3 years means a suspension for drivers ages 21 and older.

This matters here because a person with a suspended license is often already dealing with a point-based or other suspension. Another conviction on your record can make a bad situation harder to fix.

To see how your own record stands, check it with the Driver License Division rather than relying on an estimate.

Does it raise insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Utah-specific one.

Your own change could be smaller or larger. Insurers use their own rating rules, and a conviction tied to a suspended license may be treated differently from a minor speeding ticket.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer. If your policy renews soon, ask your current insurer how a conviction would affect your rate.

  • Ask whether the charge is rated as a moving violation.
  • Ask how long a conviction stays on your rating record.
  • Compare how a reduced or dismissed charge would be treated.

Can it be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but we cannot promise any outcome. Because the court sets the amount and handles the case as a criminal matter, results depend on the court and the facts.

Common questions worth raising with the court or a licensed attorney include:

  • Was the suspension valid, and were you properly notified of it?
  • Has the underlying reason for the suspension been resolved?
  • Does the court offer any reduction or alternative resolution for this charge?

Fixing the suspension itself is often a separate step handled through the Utah Driver License Division. Do not drive until you have confirmed your status in writing.

Ignoring the charge rarely helps. Contact the court before your date to learn what options exist.

What happens if I don't pay or don't show up?

Because this is a criminal offense, missing a required court date can create a bigger problem than the original charge. The court may take further action, and the matter can grow beyond the first citation.

Unpaid amounts can also hold up your efforts to restore your license. The exact consequences depend on the court and Utah's traffic code.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court clerk before the deadline and ask what arrangements exist. Courts generally respond better to drivers who reach out early.

Keep copies of your citation, any court notices, and proof of anything you pay or file.
