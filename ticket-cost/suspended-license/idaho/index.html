How much is a driving on a suspended license ticket in Idaho?

Nationally, the cost is commonly $200–$2,500+, and the offense is treated as a criminal offense with possible jail (Idaho Transportation Department — DMV). That is a national range, not an Idaho price list.

In Idaho, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Idaho dollar figure because the amount you owe depends on what the court orders in your case.

Bottom line: the number on your citation or on the court's order is the number that counts. The national range only shows how wide the spread can be.

The wide range exists because the penalty is not just a fine. A criminal charge can bring a court-ordered amount, possible jail, and other consequences that sit on top of the money you pay.

Is driving on a suspended license a crime in Idaho?

Yes, it is treated as a criminal offense with possible jail. That makes it different from a typical speeding ticket, which is usually handled as an infraction.

The difference matters in practice. A criminal charge usually means a court appearance, a record that can show up on background checks, and a judge who decides the outcome instead of a preset payment.

Idaho's traffic code is published through Idaho statutes via Justia. If you want the exact wording of the offense and its penalty classification, read the current text there or ask the court clerk.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot tell you how your charge will be classified or resolved. A licensed attorney in Idaho can answer that for your specific facts.

How is the fine set in Idaho?

The exact amount for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. It is not a fixed number we can quote for every driver.

Here is what the locked facts do tell us:

  • The national typical range is $200–$2,500+.
  • The Idaho amount is set by the court on the citation.
  • The fixed penalty already includes court costs for most infractions.

That last point applies to most infractions. Because this offense is criminal, do not assume your total works the same way. Check the citation and the court's written order to see exactly what you owe and whether any costs are listed separately.

If the citation lists a court date instead of a payment amount, the figure will be decided at that stage. Contact the court clerk named on the citation to confirm how and when the amount is set.

How many points does driving on a suspended license add in Idaho?

Idaho's points system assigns 3–4 points per moving violation. If you reach 12–17 points in 12 months, the result is a 30-day suspension (Idaho statutes via Justia).

We do not have a locked point value specific to this offense, so we will not guess one. Whether a suspended-license conviction adds points, and how many, should be confirmed with the Idaho Transportation Department (Idaho Transportation Department — DMV).

Why this matters: you are already suspended. Any new conviction can complicate getting your license back, so ask the DMV how a conviction would affect your record and your reinstatement.

You can request your driving record from the DMV to see your current status. That record shows what the state has on file, which is more reliable than any general guide.

Does it raise car insurance rates?

Often, yes. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Idaho quote.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the conviction appears on your record. Some insurers also review coverage after a license problem, so the effect can go beyond a simple rate bump.

We do not recommend any insurer. If you are worried about your policy, call your own insurer or an independent agent and ask how a suspended-license conviction would be treated.

It also helps to confirm, before driving again, that your license status is cleared and your coverage is in force. Details on state requirements are at Idaho Transportation Department — DMV.

Can it be dismissed or reduced?

It can happen, but outcomes are decided by the court case by case. We cannot predict whether yours will be dismissed or reduced.

Situations that people commonly raise with a court or attorney include:

  • Whether you received proper notice of the suspension.
  • Whether the suspension was still active on the date of the stop.
  • Whether the suspension has since been cleared or reinstated.
  • Whether the citation has errors in dates, names or the code section.

Bring any paperwork showing your license status on the citation date. A DMV record and any reinstatement documents are the most useful items.

Because this is a criminal matter with possible jail, consider speaking with a licensed Idaho attorney or the court's public defender process if you qualify. TicketCostGuide does not recommend a specific attorney.

What happens if I don't pay or don't show up?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts commonly respond to missed dates or unpaid amounts with added consequences, which can include further license problems or a warrant. Ask the court which applies to your case.

Because this offense carries possible jail, missing a required court appearance is a serious risk. Do not assume the matter will go away.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court clerk before the deadline. Many courts can explain payment options or extensions, though we cannot say what Idaho courts will offer in your case.

Practical step: find the court name, case number and due date on your citation today, and call the clerk to confirm them.

Also keep in mind that an unresolved matter can block reinstatement. Check with the DMV on what must be cleared before your license can be restored.
