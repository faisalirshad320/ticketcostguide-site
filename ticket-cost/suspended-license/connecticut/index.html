How much does driving on a suspended license cost in Connecticut?

Nationally, driving on a suspended license commonly costs $200–$2,500+. It is also a criminal offense with possible jail. In Connecticut, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Connecticut Department of Motor Vehicles).

That wording matters. The national range is a reference point, not a promise of what a Connecticut court will impose. The figure that applies to you is the one tied to your citation and your case.

We do not state an exact Connecticut dollar amount on this page. Any number we gave would be a guess, and the court's figure on your paperwork is the one that counts.

The wide national spread, from the low hundreds to well over two thousand, reflects how differently courts treat this offense. Prior history and the reason for the suspension often play a role. Check your own citation for specifics.

How is the fine set for driving on a suspended license in Connecticut?

In Connecticut, the court sets the amount for this violation within the state's traffic code. The amount is shown on the citation or on the court paperwork you receive. You can read the underlying statutes through Connecticut statutes via Justia.

State fees and surcharges are built into the payable amount on the ticket. That means the number you are asked to pay may be higher than the base fine alone.

Because this is a criminal offense with possible jail, the process can look different from paying a simple speeding ticket online. Some cases involve a court appearance rather than a mail-in payment. Read the instructions on your citation closely.

  • The citation is the primary document for what you owe.
  • The court sets the amount within the state's traffic code.
  • Fees and surcharges are included in the payable total.

How many points does driving on a suspended license add in Connecticut?

The sourced facts we use for Connecticut describe the DMV point system in general terms: speeding adds points, and 10 points = 30-day suspension. They do not give a point value for driving on a suspended license, so we do not state one.

The practical point is that points accumulate. A person who is already suspended and gets further points or convictions may face more trouble with their driving record. The DMV is the place to confirm how a specific conviction is scored (Connecticut Department of Motor Vehicles).

Do not assume this offense carries zero points just because it is criminal. Ask the DMV or read your record to see how any conviction was recorded.

For a comparison of how states approach traffic law, the Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a state-by-state law reference.

Does driving on a suspended license raise insurance in Connecticut?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Connecticut quote.

Your own change could be smaller or larger. It depends on your insurer, your history, and how the conviction appears on your record. We do not recommend any insurer, and we cannot predict your rate.

A suspension-related conviction may also lead an insurer to review your policy in other ways. Ask your insurer directly how a conviction would be treated before you decide how to respond to the citation.

  • Treat the ~20% as a rough national average only.
  • Ask your insurer how a conviction would affect your policy.
  • Compare how the cost of the ticket and the premium change fit together over time.

Can a suspended license charge be dismissed or reduced in Connecticut?

Sometimes outcomes change, but we cannot say how often or under what terms in Connecticut. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice.

What we can say is that this is a criminal offense with possible jail, so the stakes are higher than for a fine-only ticket. The amount is set by the court, and the court also decides how your case is resolved.

Many people in this situation consider speaking with a licensed attorney before they respond. We do not recommend a specific one. You may also contact the court clerk with procedural questions, such as deadlines and how to appear.

Whatever you decide, do not ignore the citation. Deadlines on the paperwork are the first thing to confirm.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the citation?

We do not have sourced figures for late fees or added penalties in Connecticut, so we will not state any. What we can say is that this is a criminal offense with possible jail, and ignoring it can make things worse.

Courts generally expect a response by the date on the paperwork. Missing it can lead to further court action. The DMV may also take action on your driving privileges, so it is worth checking your status (Connecticut Department of Motor Vehicles).

If you cannot pay the full payable amount, contact the court before the deadline and ask what options exist. Do this early, not after a missed date.

What are my options after getting this ticket?

Start with the citation. It shows what the court has set, what fees and surcharges are included, and what you are expected to do next.

  1. Read the paperwork. Note the court, the amount and any date to appear.
  2. Check your license status. Confirm with the DMV where things stand and what is needed to restore driving privileges.
  3. Ask about the process. Contact the court clerk about whether you can pay, must appear, or can ask questions in advance.
  4. Consider legal advice. A licensed attorney can explain how this offense could apply to your situation.
  5. Plan for insurance. Ask your insurer how a conviction would be treated.

Until your license is valid again, driving risks a repeat of the same offense. Arrange another way to get around while you sort out the suspension.
