How much does driving on a suspended license cost in Maine?

Nationally, driving on a suspended license commonly runs $200–$2,500+, and it is treated as a criminal offense with possible jail (Maine Bureau of Motor Vehicles). That is a national range, not a Maine price.

In Maine, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Maine dollar figure because the amount depends on the court and the facts of the case.

Your citation and any court notice are the only documents that show what you owe. Read them closely and note any deadline.

The wide national range shows how much the outcome can differ. A low-end result and a high-end result can be thousands of dollars apart, before you count the extra costs below.

How is the fine set for a suspended license charge?

In Maine, the court sets the amount within the state's traffic code, and it appears on the citation. Maine statutes are available through Maine statutes via Justia.

Statutory surcharges are built into the scheduled amounts. That means the total you see on a scheduled amount should already include those add-ons, so you do not add them on top yourself.

Because this is a criminal offense rather than a simple civil infraction, the outcome may involve more than a fine. The charge can carry possible jail, and a court can weigh the circumstances of the case.

  • The number on your citation or court notice controls what you owe.
  • The amount can differ by court and by case.
  • If something on the paperwork is unclear, ask the court clerk before the due date.

How many points does driving on a suspended license add?

Maine's points system adds demerit points per conviction. Twelve points in 12 months leads to a suspension. Those are the only point rules we state here.

We do not list a specific point value for this offense. Maine's records and the court will show what, if anything, is added after a conviction. The Maine Bureau of Motor Vehicles can confirm what is on your driving record (Maine Bureau of Motor Vehicles).

Points follow convictions, not just citations. A charge that is dismissed or never results in a conviction is different from one that ends in a conviction.

If you are already close to the 12-points-in-12-months threshold, a new conviction matters more. Check your record before you decide how to respond.

Does driving on a suspended license raise insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Maine-specific one or a quote for this offense.

What you actually pay depends on your insurer, your driving history and the underlying reason for the suspension. We do not recommend any insurer, and we cannot predict your rate.

  • Ask your insurer how a conviction would be treated before you decide how to respond.
  • Compare the possible premium increase with the cost of the outcome you are weighing.
  • Keep in mind that a conviction stays on your record, and the effect on rates can last past the fine.

Can a suspended license charge be dismissed or reduced?

It can be possible, but we cannot promise any outcome. This is a criminal matter, so the court's process and your case facts decide what happens.

Many drivers talk to a licensed attorney because of the possible jail exposure. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice or recommend any attorney.

Questions you may want answered before you respond:

  1. Was the suspension in effect on the date of the stop?
  2. Did you receive notice of the suspension?
  3. Has the reason for the suspension since been resolved?
  4. What does the citation say about your court date and how to respond?

The clerk of the court named on your citation can explain procedure, though not legal strategy.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the citation?

Because this is a criminal offense with possible jail, ignoring the citation is risky. We do not describe specific Maine penalties for non-response, but a missed court date or unpaid amount can lead to added consequences set by the court.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court before the deadline. Ask what options exist, rather than waiting for a notice.

A suspension is already a restriction on your driving. Adding an unresolved court matter can make it harder to get your license back. Check with the Maine Bureau of Motor Vehicles on what is needed for reinstatement.

The Bureau is the place to confirm your current license status (Maine Bureau of Motor Vehicles).
