How much is a driving on a suspended license ticket in Louisiana?

The lead figure is national: driving on a suspended license commonly runs $200–$2,500+. It is a criminal offense with possible jail, so it is handled differently from a basic speeding ticket (Louisiana Office of Motor Vehicles).

In Louisiana, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Louisiana dollar figure because the court decides it case by case within the state's traffic code.

The number on your citation or court notice is the one that matters. If the notice lists only a court date, the amount may be set at or after your appearance.

The $200–$2,500+ range is a national figure. Your Louisiana total depends on the court and on the local costs added to the fine.

How is the fine set for driving on a suspended license in Louisiana?

The fine is set by the court within the state's traffic code. It is not a fixed amount printed on a statewide schedule that every parish applies the same way.

Several things can shape the outcome in practice:

  • The reason your license was suspended in the first place.
  • Whether you have prior convictions for the same offense.
  • The policies of the parish or city court hearing the case.
  • Whether the charge stays as filed or is changed before disposition.

Because this is a criminal offense with possible jail, a judge generally has more discretion than with a simple infraction. The statutes themselves are available through Louisiana statutes on Justia (Louisiana statutes via Justia).

Read the statute text that applies to your citation rather than relying on summaries, including ours.

Why can court costs be higher than the fine?

Local court costs vary by parish and often exceed the fine itself. That means two drivers with the same charge can owe very different totals depending on where they were cited.

When you plan for the cost, think in two parts: the fine the court sets, and the costs and surcharges the local court adds. Ask the clerk of court for a full payoff figure that includes both.

We do not list a Louisiana dollar amount for these costs. Parish schedules differ, and anything we quoted would be a guess.

Before paying, ask the clerk for the total due in writing, not just the fine line on the citation.

How many points does driving on a suspended license add in Louisiana?

Louisiana has no point system. License action instead follows repeat convictions and specific offenses.

That does not make the charge minor. Without a points tally, the risk comes from the conviction itself, from a record of repeat offenses, and from the criminal nature of the charge. A conviction can also lead to further action on your driving privileges.

To see where your license stands, check your status with the Louisiana Office of Motor Vehicles (Louisiana Office of Motor Vehicles). It can tell you what is needed to clear a suspension, which is separate from resolving the ticket in court.

Does driving on a suspended license raise insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Louisiana-specific rate.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your driving history, and how the conviction appears on your record. A criminal charge connected to a suspension can be treated more seriously than a routine moving violation, though we have no sourced figure for that difference.

  • Ask your insurer how a conviction would affect your policy at renewal.
  • Compare quotes after the matter is resolved.
  • Keep proof of coverage current, since a lapse can create separate problems.

Can a suspended license ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes outcomes change, but we cannot promise one. Because this is a criminal offense, the court process matters more than it would for a minor infraction.

Questions that commonly come up at this stage include:

  • Was the suspension in effect on the date of the stop, or had it already been cleared?
  • Was notice of the suspension properly sent?
  • Can you show proof that your license was reinstated?

If you have documents showing your license status, bring them to court. Because a conviction could carry jail and affect your record, you may want to speak with a licensed Louisiana attorney or ask the court about its process. This is general information, not legal advice, and we do not recommend any particular attorney.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the citation?

Ignoring a citation for a criminal offense with possible jail is risky. Courts can take further action when a required appearance or payment is missed, and your license problems may grow.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the clerk of court before the due date. Many courts have their own processes for questions about payment, though we cannot say what your parish offers.

Keep copies of your citation, any court notices, and proof of payment. If you are unsure whether your license is still suspended, check with the Office of Motor Vehicles (Louisiana Office of Motor Vehicles).

Do not drive until you have confirmed your license status. Another stop can add a new charge to the existing one.
