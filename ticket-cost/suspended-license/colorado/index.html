How much is a driving on a suspended license ticket in Colorado?

Nationally, the penalty is commonly $200–$2,500+. It is also a criminal offense with possible jail. In Colorado, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Colorado Division of Motor Vehicles).

We do not publish a single Colorado dollar figure for this charge. The national range shows how wide the spread can be, but it is not a Colorado fine schedule.

The $200–$2,500+ range is a national figure. Your Colorado amount is whatever the court writes on your citation or sets at your court date.

A statutory surcharge is added to each penalty. That means the total you owe can be higher than the base fine alone.

How is the fine set for driving on a suspended license?

In Colorado, the court sets the amount within the state's traffic code. The figure is not a flat number printed on a state-wide chart that we can quote. It depends on the court handling your case.

Because of this, the same charge can produce different totals in different counties or courts. Look at the citation first, since it is the best source for your own number.

  • The citation: check the charge listed, the court named and any amount or appearance date shown.
  • The court: the clerk's office can tell you what applies to your case.
  • The statute: Colorado statutes are available through Colorado statutes via Justia.

Remember that this is a criminal offense with possible jail, not only a fine. A court may treat it differently from an ordinary speeding ticket.

What surcharges and extra costs get added?

A statutory surcharge is added to each penalty in Colorado. We do not state its dollar amount here, because it is not part of the figures we can verify for this page.

Doubled fines apply in work zones. If your violation happened in a marked work zone, the amount on your citation may reflect that.

  • Base penalty set by the court
  • Statutory surcharge added to each penalty
  • Possible doubling if the violation took place in a work zone

Ask the court clerk for a full total before you pay. That total is the number that matters, not the base fine alone.

How many points does driving on a suspended license carry in Colorado?

The Colorado points system we can cite runs from 1–6 points by mph over the limit. That scale is built around speeding, so it does not give a clear point value for a suspended license charge.

We therefore do not assign a point number to this offense. Check your citation and the Colorado Division of Motor Vehicles for how your conviction is recorded (Colorado Division of Motor Vehicles).

Key threshold: 12 points in 12 months (adult) = suspension. If you are already near that total, any new conviction matters.

This is also why the charge can feel circular. A suspension may already be in place, and further points or convictions can complicate getting the license back.

Does driving on a suspended license raise insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Colorado quote.

We cannot say how much your own insurer will change your rate. Insurers use their own rules, and a criminal conviction may be treated differently from a minor ticket.

  • The ~20% figure is an average across the US.
  • Your result depends on your insurer, your record and the conviction on file.
  • Ask your insurer directly how they treat the conviction at renewal.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer or give insurance advice.

Can a suspended license charge be dismissed or reduced?

It can depend on the facts and on the court. We cannot promise any outcome, and nothing here is legal advice.

Because this is a criminal offense with possible jail, many people choose to talk to a licensed attorney before they appear. We do not recommend any specific attorney.

  • Read the citation carefully for the exact charge and court date.
  • Gather any paperwork about your license status and any notices you received.
  • Ask the court clerk what options exist for your case, such as a hearing or a payment arrangement.

Colorado's traffic code sets the framework, and the court sets the amount (Colorado statutes via Justia).

What happens if I don't pay or don't show up?

Ignoring a citation for a criminal offense is risky. Courts can add consequences when a person fails to appear or to pay, and the total owed may grow.

Since a statutory surcharge is added to each penalty, any late additions come on top of that. We do not list the exact late costs, as they vary by court.

Contact the court named on your citation as soon as you can. Do not rely on the national range to guess what you owe.

Unresolved matters can also affect your license record. Check your status with the state (Colorado Division of Motor Vehicles).
