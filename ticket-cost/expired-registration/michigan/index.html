How much is an expired registration ticket in Michigan?

Nationally, an expired registration ticket costs $25–$250 plus DMV late-renewal penalties (Michigan Secretary of State). That is a national range, not a Michigan price.

In Michigan, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Michigan dollar figure because the court decides it within the state's traffic code.

The fine is only part of the bill. District court costs vary by court and make up much of the total, so two drivers with the same violation can owe different amounts.

Your citation, or the court that issued it, is the only reliable source for what you owe. Treat any other number as an estimate.

How is the fine set for an expired registration ticket?

In Michigan the court sets the fine within the state's traffic code. The amount appears on the citation or in the court's notice after the ticket is processed.

The total you pay can include several parts:

  • The fine set by the court.
  • District court costs, which vary by court.
  • Late-renewal penalties charged by the state when you renew.

The late-renewal penalty is separate from the court's amount. Paying the ticket does not renew your registration, and renewing does not necessarily end the ticket.

The Michigan statutes are available through Michigan statutes via Justia if you want to read the traffic code yourself.

How many points does an expired registration ticket carry in Michigan?

The point schedule we can source is 2–4 points by mph over under §257.320a. It is written around speed, so it does not map neatly onto a registration violation.

Because of that, we do not say how many points, if any, your ticket adds. Check the violation listed on your citation, then confirm with the Michigan Secretary of State, which keeps driver records.

The threshold that matters across all violations is 12 points in 2 years, which leads to driver reexamination. If you already have points on your record, ask whether this ticket could add to them.

A ticket with no points can still cost money. Fines, court costs and late-renewal penalties are separate from the point system.

Does an expired registration ticket raise insurance rates?

The figure we can source is national: moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. It is not a Michigan-specific rate.

Whether your ticket is treated as a moving violation depends on how the citation is written and how your insurer reads your record. We cannot say how any particular insurer will act, and we do not recommend insurers.

If you are worried about a rate increase, ask your insurer how it treats registration violations. Do that before you decide how to resolve the ticket.

Can an expired registration ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Courts decide whether to dismiss or reduce a ticket, and the options differ by court. We cannot promise any outcome.

Common questions to raise with the court clerk:

  • What is the total amount due, including court costs?
  • What are the choices for responding to the citation?
  • Does the court consider proof that the registration has since been renewed?

Keep your renewal receipt and registration paperwork handy. The court may ask for it, and it shows what you did after the stop.

What happens if I don't pay the ticket?

An unpaid ticket can lead to more trouble than the original fine. The exact steps depend on the court, so read the deadlines and instructions on your citation.

If you cannot pay in full, contact the court clerk before the deadline. Some courts discuss options, but we cannot say which ones apply to you.

Meanwhile, your registration is still expired until you renew it. Late-renewal penalties are charged by the state and are separate from the court's amount.

Ignoring a citation rarely makes it go away. Contact the court first, and use the Michigan Secretary of State for renewal questions.

What should I do first after getting the ticket?

  1. Read the citation for the violation, the court and the response deadline.
  2. Renew your registration through the Secretary of State so the vehicle is legal to drive.
  3. Call or visit the court to confirm the amount owed, including court costs.
  4. Keep copies of the citation, your renewal receipt and any payment confirmation.

These steps do not replace legal advice. If you are unsure how the ticket affects your record, ask a licensed attorney.
