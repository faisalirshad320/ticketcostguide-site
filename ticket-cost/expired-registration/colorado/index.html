How much is an expired registration ticket in Colorado?

Nationally, an expired registration ticket costs $25–$250 plus DMV late-renewal penalties (Colorado Division of Motor Vehicles). That is a national range, not a Colorado price.

In Colorado, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Colorado dollar figure because the amount depends on the court and the details of your ticket.

The number printed on your citation, or the amount the court clerk quotes, is the one that counts. Treat any other figure as an estimate.

Two separate costs can apply. One is the court-set fine. The other is whatever the DMV charges to bring the registration current, including late-renewal penalties.

How is the fine set, and what gets added on top?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. That is why two drivers can see different totals for what looks like the same offense.

A statutory surcharge is added to each penalty. This means the amount you pay is usually higher than the base fine alone.

  • Base fine: set by the court on the citation.
  • Statutory surcharge: added to each penalty.
  • Work zones: doubled fines apply in work zones.
  • DMV late-renewal penalties: a separate cost for renewing late.

Work-zone doubling is unlikely to matter for most expired-tag stops. It is worth knowing if your stop happened in a marked work zone.

How many points does an expired registration ticket carry in Colorado?

Colorado's point system runs from 1 to 6 points by mph over the limit. That scale is built around speeding, so it does not tell us how many points, if any, an expired registration ticket adds.

We have no locked point value for this violation, so we will not guess. Check your citation and the DMV (Colorado Division of Motor Vehicles) for how your specific charge is treated.

The suspension threshold is 12 points in 12 months for an adult driver. If you already have points from other violations, ask the court or the DMV how this ticket affects your total before you pay.

Does an expired registration ticket raise insurance?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Colorado-specific one.

We do not have a locked figure for how insurers treat an expired registration ticket. Insurers use their own rating rules, and they differ by company.

  • Check whether the citation is listed as a moving or a non-moving violation.
  • Ask your insurer how it treats this kind of citation, without naming the ticket as a reason to shop around.
  • Keep proof that you paid and that the registration is now current.

Can an expired registration ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Because the court sets the amount on the citation, any change comes from the court, not from the DMV. Whether a court will reduce or dismiss a charge depends on the court and your situation.

Some people renew the registration first and then bring proof to court. We cannot say whether a particular Colorado court will accept that, so read the instructions on your citation.

This page is general information, not legal advice. For advice on your own case, speak with a licensed attorney of your choosing.

Whatever you decide, note the court date and any deadline on the citation. Missing it can make things more expensive and more complicated.

What happens if I don't pay or don't renew?

Ignoring the citation rarely makes the problem smaller. The court can add consequences when a deadline passes, and the DMV late-renewal penalties are a separate cost that can keep growing while the registration stays expired.

We do not have locked figures for additional late fees or for what happens after a missed court date. Check the citation and the court's instructions for the exact consequences in your case.

  1. Read the citation for the court name, date and payment options.
  2. Renew the registration with the DMV as soon as you can.
  3. Keep receipts for both the renewal and any court payment.
  4. Contact the court clerk if you cannot meet a deadline.
