How much is an expired registration ticket in North Dakota?

Nationally, an expired registration ticket costs $25–$250 plus DMV late-renewal penalties. In North Dakota, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (North Dakota Department of Transportation).

We do not list a single North Dakota dollar figure, because the amount on your citation is what counts. The national range is context only. It is not a quote for what a North Dakota court will charge.

Your real cost has two parts: the court's amount on the citation, and any late-renewal penalties charged when you renew the registration.

How is the fine set?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. It is not a fixed number we can publish for every driver. The citation is the document that tells you what the court is asking for.

You can read the underlying law in the state's published code, available through North Dakota statutes via Justia. Statute text can be dense, so read the citation first. It usually lists the charge and the amount or the way to find it.

Two tickets for the same expired registration can come out differently. The court, the facts and whether you fix the problem can all matter.

What are the DMV late-renewal penalties on top of the ticket?

The national range includes late-renewal penalties charged by the motor vehicle agency, separate from the court's amount. These are charged when you renew an expired registration, not when you pay the ticket.

That means paying the court does not renew your registration. You will likely need to deal with both, and the order can matter. Check with the North Dakota Department of Transportation for current renewal rules and what it will charge you.

  • The court's amount is on the citation.
  • Late-renewal penalties come from the registration agency.
  • Renewing does not automatically resolve the citation.

Are there extra court costs and surcharges?

In North Dakota, the statutory fee is usually the whole amount. There is no large stack of added court costs on top of it.

That is different from some states, where surcharges can double a ticket. Still, read the total on your citation or the court's payment notice before you pay. If a line item looks unfamiliar, ask the court clerk what it covers.

Remember that late-renewal penalties are a separate charge. They are not a court surcharge.

How many points does an expired registration ticket carry in North Dakota?

North Dakota's points are assigned by mph over on the ND schedule, and 12+ points = suspension. That schedule is built around speed, so it does not map neatly onto a registration violation.

We do not state a point value for expired registration, because our sources do not give one. Look at your citation to see how the violation is classified. If you are unsure whether points apply, ask the court clerk or check with the North Dakota Department of Transportation.

The suspension threshold is 12+ points. Any points you already have on your record matter more if a new violation adds to them.

Does an expired registration ticket raise insurance rates?

Nationally, moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a North Dakota one, and it is not a prediction for your policy.

Whether an insurer treats an expired registration citation like a moving violation depends on how the violation is recorded and on that insurer's own rules. Many registration problems are paperwork issues rather than driving behavior, but we cannot say how your insurer will treat one.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend insurers. If you are worried about your rate, ask your insurer how it treats this kind of citation. You can also see how states handle traffic laws through the Governors Highway Safety Association state law pages.

Can an expired registration ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Courts sometimes handle registration citations differently from other tickets, but we cannot promise any outcome. Whether yours can be reduced or dismissed is up to the court.

Some practical steps people commonly take, which are not legal advice:

  • Read the citation for the court, the charge and any response deadline.
  • Renew the registration so the vehicle is no longer out of compliance.
  • Keep proof of renewal, such as a receipt or the new registration card.
  • Contact the court clerk and ask what options exist for your citation.

If you want advice about your specific case, a licensed attorney is the right person to ask. We do not recommend a specific one.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts can add consequences when a deadline passes, and an unpaid ticket does not go away on its own.

Because the amount is set by the court on the citation, the deadline and the next steps are also on the citation or the court's notice. Respond by the date shown, even if you plan to contest the ticket.

Driving on an expired registration after receiving a ticket can also bring a new citation. Renewing the registration promptly keeps one problem from turning into two.
