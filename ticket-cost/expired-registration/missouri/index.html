How much is an expired registration ticket in Missouri?

Nationally, expired registration tickets cost $25–$250 plus DMV late-renewal penalties (Missouri Department of Revenue). That range is a national figure. In Missouri, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation.

We do not publish a single Missouri price because one does not exist. The amount depends on the court that hears your case and how the officer wrote the charge.

Your citation is the best source for your own cost. Look for the court name, the charge description and any payable amount printed on it.

There are two separate costs to keep in mind. One is the court fine on the ticket. The other is whatever you owe the state to bring the registration current, including late-renewal penalties.

How is the fine set for expired registration in Missouri?

The fine is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The officer does not choose the dollar amount. The citation names the charge and the court, and the court handles the rest.

Missouri court locations can differ. A municipal court and a county court may both hear this kind of charge, and the amount can vary by county or court.

Court costs are included in the scheduled payable amounts. If your citation lists a payable amount, that figure should already include those costs. If it lists no amount, contact the clerk of the court named on the ticket.

You can read the Missouri statutes on vehicle registration through Justia's Missouri code. We do not cite a specific section here, because the charge and the section on your citation may differ.

How many points does an expired registration ticket carry in Missouri?

Missouri's point treatment depends on how the charge is filed. A municipal conviction carries 2 points. A state charge carries 3 points (Missouri Department of Revenue).

Points matter because they add up. Eight points in 18 months leads to a suspension.

  • Municipal conviction: 2 points
  • State charge: 3 points
  • Suspension threshold: 8 points in 18 months

Points come from convictions, not from receiving the citation. If you already have points from other violations, even a small ticket can bring you closer to the threshold.

To see how many points are on your own record, check with the Department of Revenue's Driver License Bureau. We cannot tell you your total.

Does an expired registration ticket raise insurance rates?

It might, but we cannot say for certain. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Missouri one.

Whether a registration ticket counts as a moving violation for your insurer depends on the insurer and how the conviction is recorded. Insurers use their own rules. We do not recommend or rank any insurer.

If a conviction would add points to your Missouri record, that is the most likely route for it to reach your insurance. Ask your insurer how they treat this type of conviction before you decide how to handle the ticket.

The ~20% number is an average across the country. Your own change could be higher, lower, or nothing at all.

Can an expired registration ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but that decision belongs to the court. The locked figures we use do not include any Missouri rule that guarantees a dismissal or a reduction. We will not promise one.

Here are the general options people have with a registration citation:

  • Pay the ticket. This usually ends the case, but it may count as a conviction and add points.
  • Contest the ticket. You can ask the court for a hearing and present your side.
  • Ask the court clerk about options. The clerk can explain what the court allows, such as whether proof of renewal matters.

Renewing the registration is a separate step. Doing so brings the vehicle into compliance, but it does not by itself cancel the citation.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice. If a conviction could put your license at risk, you may want to speak with a licensed Missouri attorney of your own choosing.

What happens if I don't pay an expired registration ticket?

Ignoring the citation usually makes things worse. The court can treat a missed deadline or a missed appearance as a further problem. Details depend on the court that issued the ticket.

Unpaid tickets can lead to added consequences, which may include extra costs or a licensing action. We do not give exact Missouri amounts for those because the locked figures do not include them.

Meanwhile, the registration itself stays expired until you renew it. DMV late-renewal penalties are part of the national cost range of $25–$250 plus DMV late-renewal penalties, so waiting can add to the total.

Read the citation for the court date or payment deadline. If you are unsure, call the clerk of the court named on the ticket.

What should I do first after getting the ticket?

  1. Read the citation for the court name, the charge and any listed payable amount.
  2. Check whether your registration is current in the Department of Revenue's system, and renew it if not.
  3. Decide whether to pay or contest, keeping in mind the 2 or 3 points that a conviction can carry.
  4. Look at your total points, since 8 points in 18 months means suspension.
  5. Ask your insurer how it treats this conviction, because the national average increase for moving-violation convictions is about 20%.

Keep copies of the citation, your renewal receipt and any payment confirmation.
