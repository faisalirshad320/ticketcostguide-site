How much is a stop sign ticket in Kentucky?

Nationally, stop sign tickets fall in a range of $50–$500 depending on the state. In Kentucky, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Kentucky Transportation Cabinet).

We do not publish a single Kentucky dollar figure, because the number you owe depends on the court handling your case and on the costs added to the fine. The national range is context only. It is not a Kentucky price.

The most reliable number is the one printed on your citation or given by the court clerk. Treat any other figure, including ours, as a general guide.

Read the citation closely. It should show the court, the charge and how to respond.

How is the fine set, and why can the total be higher?

The exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. Kentucky statutes are available through Justia if you want to read the code yourself.

Court costs are set by the district court and usually exceed the fine itself. That means the fine is only part of what you may owe.

  • Fine: set by the court on the citation.
  • Court costs: set by the district court, often larger than the fine.
  • Other effects: points, and possibly higher insurance, which are not part of the payment.

When you compare quotes or anecdotes from other drivers, ask whether the figure was the fine alone or the total with costs.

How many points does a stop sign ticket carry in Kentucky?

The Kentucky points facts we can source are listed by speed: 3 points (10–15 over) or 6 points (16–25 over), and 26+ over triggers a hearing. Those tiers describe speeding. We do not have a sourced point value for a stop sign violation, so we will not state one.

To find out whether your citation carries points, check the charge on the citation and ask the court clerk. You can also look at the Kentucky Transportation Cabinet driver licensing pages.

The suspension rule is the one that matters most for any driver: 12 points in 2 years = suspension. If you already have points on your record, even a small addition could matter.

Does a stop sign ticket raise insurance in Kentucky?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums ~20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Kentucky quote.

Whether your own rate changes depends on your insurer, your driving history and whether the ticket ends in a conviction. We do not recommend insurers, and we cannot predict your premium.

  • Ask your insurer how it treats a moving violation at renewal.
  • Find out from the court whether the outcome would count as a conviction.
  • Weigh the possible premium change against the fine and costs when deciding how to respond.

Over time, an insurance increase can cost more than the ticket itself, so it is worth thinking about before you pay.

Can a stop sign ticket be dismissed or reduced in Kentucky?

Sometimes, but outcomes are decided by the court and the facts of your case. We cannot say how likely a dismissal or reduction is.

Options usually include paying the citation, contesting it in court, or asking the court whether any alternative resolution is available. Availability varies by court, so ask the clerk what your court offers.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice. If the stakes are high, such as a record with many points, consider speaking with a licensed attorney of your own choosing.

Paying a citation generally means accepting the charge. Before you do, confirm what that means for your record.

What happens if I don't pay a stop sign ticket?

Ignoring a citation is rarely a good idea. The court sets the consequences, and they can add to what you already owe. Your driving record and license can also be affected.

We do not have sourced deadlines or penalty amounts, so we are not listing any. The citation and the court clerk are the places to confirm what applies to you.

  • Read the response instructions on the citation.
  • Contact the court named on it if anything is unclear.
  • Respond in the way the court requires, whether that is paying or contesting.

Keep in mind the points rule as well: 12 points in 2 years = suspension.

What should I do after getting a stop sign ticket in Kentucky?

  1. Read the citation. Note the court, the charge and the response instructions.
  2. Ask for the total. Court costs are set by the district court and usually exceed the fine, so ask for the full amount.
  3. Check points. Ask whether the charge carries points and look at your current record.
  4. Think about insurance. A conviction can raise premiums, with ~20% as a national average.
  5. Decide how to respond. Pay, contest or ask about options, following the court's instructions.

Take the same steps whether you plan to pay or fight the ticket. They help you avoid surprises later.
