How much is a stop sign ticket in Alabama?

Nationally, a stop sign ticket runs $50–$500 depending on the state. In Alabama the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Alabama Law Enforcement Agency (ALEA)).

We do not publish a single Alabama dollar figure because the court that handles your case decides it within the state's traffic code. The national range is a general benchmark only.

The number to focus on is the total on your citation or court notice. Municipal and district court costs vary by jurisdiction and often exceed the base fine. A ticket from one city court can therefore total more or less than a similar ticket elsewhere in the state.

Read the amount due on your own citation or court notice. That figure, not a national average, is what you would owe if you pay.

How is the fine for a stop sign violation set in Alabama?

Alabama does not use one flat number printed in a public table for this violation. The court sets the fine within the state's traffic code, and the amount appears on the citation.

Two things make up most of what you pay:

  • The base fine, which the court sets for the violation.
  • Court costs and surcharges, which vary by municipal and district court and often exceed the base fine.

This is why two drivers cited for the same stop sign violation can be told different totals. The court's costs differ, even when the underlying violation is the same.

The Alabama traffic code is available through Alabama statutes via Justia. It is a good place to read the wording of the rules of the road, though it will not tell you what your particular court charges.

How many points does a stop sign ticket carry in Alabama?

The Alabama points system we sourced lists 2 points (25 or less over) or 5 points (more than 25 over). Those tiers are described in terms of speed, so they read as speeding tiers.

Our sourced facts do not state a separate point value for a stop sign violation. Do not assume your ticket carries 2 or 5 points. Confirm how your citation is scored with the court or with Alabama Law Enforcement Agency (ALEA).

What the sourced facts do say is the suspension threshold: 12–14 points in 2 years = 60-day suspension. Points from several tickets add up, so even a lower-point citation matters if your record already has others.

Ask the court clerk how your specific citation is scored, and check your driving record for existing points before deciding what to do.

Does a stop sign ticket raise your insurance in Alabama?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Alabama-specific one (Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws tracks state traffic laws more broadly).

Your own result depends on your insurer, your driving history, and whether the ticket ends in a conviction. We do not know any individual insurer's rules and do not recommend one.

A practical way to think about it: a ticket's real cost is the fine and court costs plus any premium increase over time. A modest fine can become the smaller part of the total if a conviction raises your rates.

If you are worried about rates, ask your insurer how it treats a stop sign conviction versus a ticket that does not result in one.

Can a stop sign ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome depends on the court and the facts. Your citation lists the court and the steps for responding. The choices usually come down to paying, contesting, or asking the court what it allows.

  • Paying generally resolves the ticket but may count as a conviction.
  • Contesting means appearing before the court and presenting your side.
  • Asking about alternatives means checking with the clerk whether the court offers anything other than a standard conviction.

Because municipal and district courts set their own costs and handle cases in their own way, the available options can differ by court. We cannot tell you which will work for you.

What happens if I don't pay a stop sign ticket in Alabama?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts typically add consequences for missed deadlines or missed appearances, and those can raise the total you owe or affect your license.

The exact deadlines and penalties are on your citation and set by the court that issued it. Our sourced facts do not give a specific deadline or late fee, so we do not state one.

If you cannot pay the full amount, or you missed a date, contact the court clerk as soon as possible. Clerks can explain what the court allows for your case.

Keep a copy of your citation and any payment receipt. They are your record if the court's file shows a different status.
