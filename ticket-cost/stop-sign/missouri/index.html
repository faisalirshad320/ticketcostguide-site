How much is a stop sign ticket in Missouri?

Nationally, a stop sign ticket costs $50–$500 depending on the state. In Missouri, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Missouri Department of Revenue — Driver License Bureau).

We do not publish a single Missouri dollar figure, because the amount depends on the court and the charge. The national range is context only. It is not a quote for your ticket.

The most reliable number is the one printed on your citation or given by the court clerk. Court costs are included in the scheduled payable amounts, so that figure should already include them.

If your citation shows no amount, the court clerk can tell you whether the ticket has a scheduled payable amount or requires an appearance.

How is the fine for a stop sign violation set in Missouri?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. Missouri statutes are available through Missouri statutes via Justia.

The amount can differ depending on whether the case is handled in a municipal court or under a state charge. That is the same split that affects points, covered below.

Court costs are included in the scheduled payable amounts. If you pay the scheduled amount, you are not expected to add a separate surcharge on top of it.

  • Look at the citation: it names the court and may show the payable amount.
  • Check the charge: a municipal charge and a state charge are treated differently for points.
  • Ask the clerk: the court that issued the citation is the authority on what you owe.

How many points does a stop sign ticket add in Missouri?

A stop sign conviction adds 2 points if it is a municipal conviction, or 3 points if it is a state charge. Missouri also uses a suspension threshold: 8 points in 18 months = suspension (Missouri Department of Revenue — Driver License Bureau).

Points are tied to a conviction. Paying the ticket is generally treated as a guilty plea, so paying can put points on your record.

Points from every violation count toward the same total. A stop sign ticket by itself is well short of the suspension threshold, but it adds to anything else on your record.

To see your own point total, contact the Missouri Department of Revenue Driver License Bureau. Do not rely on a guess.

Does a stop sign ticket raise insurance rates in Missouri?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Missouri-specific one.

Your own change depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the violation is recorded. Some drivers see no change. Others see a larger one.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer. If you are worried about your rate, your insurer or a licensed insurance agent can explain how they treat a conviction like this.

  • The conviction, not the stop itself, is what usually reaches your record.
  • Points and insurance are separate. A ticket can affect one without the other.
  • A prior record can make the same ticket cost more in premiums.

Can a stop sign ticket be dismissed or reduced in Missouri?

Sometimes, but that is up to the court. Options depend on the court, the charge and your record. We cannot say which ones apply to you.

Common routes people ask about include:

  • Paying the ticket: this usually ends the case quickly, but it is treated as a conviction and can add points.
  • Contesting the ticket: you can plead not guilty and ask for a hearing, where the court decides.
  • Asking about a reduced or amended charge: some courts consider this. Whether yours does is up to the prosecutor and the judge.

The choice between a municipal conviction and a state charge matters, because the point value differs: 2 points versus 3 points.

This page is general information, not legal advice. For advice about your case, speak with a licensed Missouri attorney of your choosing.

What happens if I don't pay a stop sign ticket in Missouri?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. Courts can add consequences when a citation goes unanswered, and the exact steps depend on the court.

Do not assume the ticket will go away. Contact the court named on the citation before the date it sets for payment or appearance.

  • Read the citation for the court name and the appearance or payment instructions.
  • Call the clerk if you are unsure what you owe or what the deadline is.
  • Keep proof of payment or of any court contact.

A missed ticket can also affect your driving record and your license status. The Missouri Department of Revenue Driver License Bureau is the place to confirm your license status.

What should I check on my Missouri citation before deciding?

Before you pay or contest, check a few details. They determine what you owe and what it does to your record.

  1. The court: municipal or state. This affects the 2-point or 3-point treatment.
  2. The charge: confirm it is a stop sign violation and the details are correct.
  3. The amount: the court sets it on the citation, and court costs are included in the scheduled payable amounts.
  4. Your point total: remember that 8 points in 18 months = suspension.
  5. Your insurance: a conviction can raise premiums by about 20% on average nationally.

Weigh the fine against the points and the possible insurance change. The cheapest way to close a ticket is not always the cheapest over time.
