How much is a stop sign ticket in Oklahoma?

Nationally $50–$500 depending on the state; in Oklahoma the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Service Oklahoma). We do not print a single Oklahoma dollar figure, because the court that hears your case decides the amount.

The $50–$500 range is a national range across states. It is not an Oklahoma fine, and a particular ticket could land anywhere in or near it.

What you actually pay is usually the fine plus court costs and statutory fees. Those extras are added by the municipal or district court handling the citation.

Your citation and the court that issued it are the best sources for your exact total. Two drivers cited for the same violation in different courts can owe different amounts.

How is the fine for a stop sign violation set in Oklahoma?

In Oklahoma the amount is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The code is published online, and you can read the statutes through Oklahoma statutes via Justia.

Because the court sets the amount, it can depend on whether your case is in a municipal court or a district court. The citation will usually show which court has it and how to find out the amount due.

We cannot tell you the figure for your ticket from a statewide table. If a website claims one exact Oklahoma price for every stop sign ticket, treat it with caution unless it points to the court's own schedule.

For national context, the GHSA keeps a general overview of state traffic laws (Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws).

What court costs and fees get added to the fine?

Court costs and statutory fees are added by the municipal or district court. They are separate from the base fine and are part of the total you owe.

We do not list dollar amounts for these fees, because they are set by the court and the amounts are not part of our sourced figures for this page.

  • Fine: set by the court on the citation.
  • Court costs: added by the municipal or district court.
  • Statutory fees: added on top, as required by state law.

When you contact the clerk, ask for the total amount due, not just the fine. That avoids surprises if you plan to pay by mail, online or in person.

How many points is a stop sign ticket in Oklahoma?

Oklahoma uses a points system. The sourced figures we have say 2 points for ordinary speeding, or more at higher speeds, and that 10 points in 5 years leads to suspension.

Those numbers describe speeding. We do not have a sourced point value for a stop sign violation, so we are not going to guess one.

To find the point value for your ticket, check the citation, ask the court clerk, or contact Service Oklahoma, which handles driver services (Service Oklahoma).

The suspension threshold is 10 points in 5 years. Points from different violations add up, so one stop sign ticket matters more if you already have other points.

Does a stop sign ticket raise your insurance in Oklahoma?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Oklahoma-specific one.

Whether your own rate changes depends on your insurer, your driving record and whether the ticket ends in a conviction. We do not recommend any insurer and cannot predict your quote.

Because a premium increase can last longer than the fine itself, some drivers weigh the long-term cost when deciding how to handle a ticket. The insurance effect is often larger over time than the one-time payment.

If you are worried about your rate, ask your insurer how it treats a minor moving violation. The answer comes from the company, not from us.

Can a stop sign ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome is up to the court. Oklahoma sets the fine within the state's traffic code, and the court decides how your case is resolved.

Your citation lists how to respond and by when. Responding on time keeps your options open, including paying, contesting the ticket or asking the court about alternatives.

  • Read the citation for the court name, the deadline and how to respond.
  • Contact the clerk and ask which options the court offers for your case.
  • Ask for the full amount due, including court costs and fees.

What happens if I don't pay a stop sign ticket in Oklahoma?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. The court may add consequences for missing the deadline, and the ticket can affect your driving record and license status.

We do not have sourced figures for late penalties, so we are not listing any. The court named on your citation can tell you what applies to your case.

The system also tracks points toward suspension, at 10 points in 5 years. An unresolved ticket can complicate your record, so respond before the deadline.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court clerk before the due date and ask what is available. Courts often handle questions better before a deadline than after it.
