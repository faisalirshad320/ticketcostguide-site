How much is a stop sign ticket in South Carolina?

Nationally, a stop sign ticket runs $50–$500 depending on the state. In South Carolina, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (South Carolina Department of Motor Vehicles).

We do not publish a single South Carolina dollar figure for this violation. The court sets it within the state's traffic code, and we only print numbers we can source. The $50–$500 range is a national spread across states, so treat it as context rather than a South Carolina price.

The amount printed on your citation, or given by the court clerk, is the figure that applies to you. Statewide assessments and surcharges come on top of it.

The sections below explain how the fine is set, why the total is higher than the base fine, and what a conviction can mean for your license and insurance.

How is a stop sign fine set in South Carolina?

The fine is set by the court, within the limits of the state's traffic code. The officer writes the violation on the citation, and the court handling your case determines the amount.

That is why two drivers cited for the same violation can see different paperwork depending on the court. We cannot give you a statewide flat figure, and any site that does without citing the citation or a court schedule is guessing.

You can read the underlying traffic provisions in the South Carolina statutes via Justia. For how your ticket is processed and what it does to your driving record, the South Carolina Department of Motor Vehicles is the primary reference.

  • Start with the citation itself: it lists the violation and the court.
  • Contact the court clerk for the amount owed and accepted payment methods.
  • Ask whether the amount shown includes assessments and surcharges.

Why is the total more than the fine?

Statewide assessments and surcharges are added to every fine and usually exceed it. In practice, the fine is only part of what you owe.

This catches many drivers off guard. If you budget based on a fine figure alone, the amount at the clerk's window can be noticeably higher.

We do not give dollar amounts for the individual assessments, because they are not part of our sourced figures. Ask the court for a total that includes everything before you pay or decide how to respond.

Tip: always ask for the full amount due, not just the base fine, and get it in writing or from the court's official payment page.

How many points is a stop sign ticket in South Carolina?

South Carolina's points system assigns 2–6 points by mph over. The scale is built around speed, so it does not map neatly onto a stop sign violation, which has no mph-over figure.

Because of that, we do not claim a specific point value for a stop sign ticket. Confirm the value for your exact violation with the South Carolina Department of Motor Vehicles.

Two rules matter however many points you receive:

  • 12 points = suspension.
  • Points halve after 12 months.

If you already carry points from other tickets, even a small addition could move you closer to the suspension threshold. Check your own record before deciding how to respond.

Does a stop sign ticket raise insurance in South Carolina?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums ~20% on average. That is a national figure, not a South Carolina rate and not a quote.

Whether your insurer acts on a conviction, and by how much, depends on your insurer, your record and the policy. We do not recommend any insurer, and we cannot predict your premium.

The practical point is that the long-term cost of a conviction can exceed the court bill. Compare the amount owed with a possible premium increase on your own policy, and ask your insurer or agent how a conviction would be treated.

The ~20% average is a national benchmark. Your own change could be higher, lower or none.

Can a stop sign ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome depends on the facts and on the court. We cannot predict it, and this page is not legal advice.

Your usual options are to pay, which generally means accepting the violation, or to contest the citation in court. The citation and the court clerk can tell you the process and how to respond.

  • Read the citation for the court, the violation and any response instructions.
  • Ask the court what paying means for your record and points.
  • Consider speaking with a licensed South Carolina attorney if you want advice on your specific situation.

Before paying, weigh the full amount (fine plus assessments and surcharges), the points risk and the possible insurance effect together.

What happens if I don't pay a stop sign ticket?

Ignoring a citation rarely makes it go away. Courts generally treat an unanswered ticket as a more serious problem than the original violation, and the DMV can act on court reports.

We do not list specific penalty amounts or deadlines, because they are not part of our sourced figures. The citation and the court clerk are the places to confirm what applies to you.

If you cannot afford the full amount, contact the court clerk before the deadline on your citation and ask what options exist. Doing nothing is usually the costliest choice.
