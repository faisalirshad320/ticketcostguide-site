How much is a stop sign ticket in New York?

Nationally, stop sign tickets run $50–$500 depending on the state. In New York, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (New York State Department of Motor Vehicles).

We do not list a single New York fine here, because the court decides it within the state's traffic code. The $50–$500 range is a national spread across all states, not a New York quote.

The number printed on your citation or court notice is the one that counts. If it does not show an amount, the court clerk can tell you what applies to your case.

The fine is only one part of the total. The mandatory surcharge and, in some cases, a DMV assessment are added on top, as the next sections explain.

What gets added on top of the fine?

New York adds a mandatory surcharge of $88–$93 to the court fine. This is separate from the fine itself and is not set by the officer who wrote the ticket.

If a conviction puts 6 or more points on your record, the DMV's Driver Responsibility Assessment of $300+ also applies. It is billed separately from the court.

  • Court fine: set by the court on the citation.
  • Mandatory surcharge: $88–$93, on top of the fine.
  • Driver Responsibility Assessment: $300+, triggered by 6+ points.

Add these together and your out-of-pocket cost can be well above the fine alone. The surcharge applies to the total you owe, so budget for it before you decide how to respond.

How is the fine for a stop sign violation set?

In New York the fine is set by the court within the state's traffic code, not by a flat number we can quote. The code is published online, and you can read it through New York statutes via Justia.

The amount on your citation or court notice reflects how the court is handling your case. Where the amount can vary by court, the citation controls over any general range you find online.

The national $50–$500 range comes from comparing states, and it shows how widely amounts differ. The Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a comparison of state traffic laws at Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws, which is useful context but not a New York price list.

How many points does a stop sign ticket carry in New York?

New York's points schedule, as we have it, is written in tiers by speed over the limit: 3 points (1–10 over), 4 points (11–20), 6 points (21–30), 8 points (31–40), and 11 points (41+).

Those tiers describe speeding. We do not have a locked point value for a stop sign violation, so we are not going to guess one. Check the DMV's published point schedule or your conviction notice for the points tied to your specific charge (New York State Department of Motor Vehicles).

Two thresholds matter whatever the violation: 11 points in 18 months leads to suspension, and 6+ points triggers the separate $300+ Driver Responsibility Assessment.

Points from different tickets add together, so an older conviction can change how a new one affects you. Knowing your current total before you respond helps you judge the stakes.

Does a stop sign ticket raise insurance rates?

It can. Nationally, moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a New York quote.

Your own change could be higher, lower or nothing at all. It depends on your insurer, your driving record and how the ticket is resolved. We do not recommend any insurer, and we cannot predict your rate.

  • A conviction is what usually reaches your record and your insurer.
  • Ask your insurer how a moving violation is treated under your policy.
  • Compare the possible premium change with the cost of the ticket before deciding how to respond.

Because a premium increase can last longer than the fine itself, some drivers weigh the insurance side as heavily as the court amount.

Can a stop sign ticket be dismissed or reduced?

You generally have choices beyond paying right away. Which ones are open depends on the court and on your record. Your citation or court notice will say how to plead and where to respond.

  • Pay: usually means a conviction, the court fine, the $88–$93 surcharge, and any points that go with it.
  • Contest: you can plead not guilty and have the court hear your case.
  • Ask about a reduction: some courts will discuss changing a charge. Whether yours does is up to the court.

We cannot say whether your ticket qualifies for dismissal or a reduction, and this page is not legal advice. For advice on your situation, speak with a licensed attorney of your choosing.

Before you pay, check what the conviction would add: points, the surcharge, and possibly the $300+ DMV assessment if you reach 6+ points.

What happens if I don't pay a stop sign ticket?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes it worse. Courts can treat a missed response as a failure to answer, and the DMV can take action against a license. The specific steps depend on the court and on your record.

The mandatory surcharge of $88–$93 does not go away because the ticket is unpaid. Points, if they apply, can also push you toward the thresholds that matter: 6+ points for the $300+ assessment, and 11 points in 18 months for suspension.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court named on your citation before the deadline. Ask what options exist. The DMV site at dmv.ny.gov explains license status and how to check your record.
