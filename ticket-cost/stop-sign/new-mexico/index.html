How much is a stop sign ticket in New Mexico?

The exact amount is set by the court on your citation. Nationally, stop sign tickets run $50–$500 depending on the state (New Mexico Motor Vehicle Division). That range is a national one, and we do not put a New Mexico dollar figure on it.

The number that matters is the one printed on your citation or given to you by the court. If the citation does not show an amount, the court clerk can tell you what applies to your case.

Bottom line: the national range is $50–$500, and your New Mexico amount is whatever the court sets on the citation, plus any added fees.

How is the fine for running a stop sign set in New Mexico?

In New Mexico, the exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The code itself is published in the state statutes, which you can read through New Mexico statutes via Justia.

That means two drivers cited for the same violation can see different paperwork. The court, the circumstances on the citation and whether the case goes to a hearing can all change the outcome.

Fees are added to the penalty assessment, and court cases can exceed the assessment amounts. Treat any base amount as a starting point, not the final bill.

What fees and court costs get added to the ticket?

Our sourced facts say that fees are added to the penalty assessment. They also say that court cases can exceed the assessment amounts.

We do not list individual New Mexico fee amounts, because we have no sourced figure for them. Ask the court clerk for an itemized total before you pay or schedule a hearing.

  • Ask whether the amount you were quoted includes all added fees.
  • Ask whether the amount changes if you contest the ticket and lose.
  • Keep a receipt or confirmation for any payment.

How many points is a stop sign ticket in New Mexico?

The sourced point scale is 3–8 points by mph over. Because that scale is tied to mph over the limit, it clearly describes speeding-type violations. We cannot confirm from our sourced facts how a stop sign violation is scored.

To find out, check the citation, ask the court clerk, or request your driving record from the New Mexico Motor Vehicle Division.

The thresholds apply to your whole record, whatever the violation:

  • 7–10 points in 12 months risks suspension.
  • 12 points means revocation.

If you already have points from another ticket, a stop sign conviction could matter more than it would on a clean record. The Governors Highway Safety Association tracks state traffic laws if you want a wider comparison.

Does a stop sign ticket raise insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a New Mexico quote.

Your own change depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the ticket is resolved. A stop sign ticket is a moving violation, so a conviction is the kind of record insurers look at.

We do not recommend any insurer. If you are worried about a rate change, ask your insurer how it treats a conviction compared with a ticket that is dismissed or reduced.

Can a stop sign ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Possibly, but that depends on the court and the facts of your case. You generally have three paths, and the right one is your decision.

  1. Pay it. This usually resolves the ticket quickly, but a payment is typically treated as a conviction, which is what can lead to points and an insurance change.
  2. Contest it. You can ask for a hearing and present your side. Court cases can exceed the assessment amounts, so ask about the possible total first.
  3. Ask about alternatives. Ask the court clerk whether any reduction or course option is available for your citation. We do not list specific programs because we have no sourced details on them.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot give legal advice. If the stakes feel high, such as a record close to the point thresholds, you may want to speak with a licensed attorney of your own choosing.

What happens if I don't pay a stop sign ticket?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. Courts generally treat a missed deadline or missed hearing as its own problem, and it can bring added consequences.

We do not have sourced figures for New Mexico late fees or deadlines, so we will not guess at them. Check the date and instructions on your citation, and contact the court before that date if you need more time or have questions.

Unpaid tickets can also affect your driving privileges. If you are close to the point thresholds (7–10 points in 12 months risks suspension, 12 = revocation), an unresolved ticket adds risk.
