How much is a stop sign ticket in Washington?

Nationally, a stop sign ticket runs $50–$500 depending on the state. In Washington, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Washington State Department of Licensing).

We do not list a single Washington dollar amount, because the number that applies to you is the one printed on your ticket or shown by the court that handles it. The national range is shown only for context. It is not a Washington fine.

Your citation is the most reliable source for your amount. If the figure on it differs from anything you read online, go with the citation and the court's instructions.

The amount you owe can also depend on how the violation is written up and whether the court has added anything beyond the base penalty. The next section explains how the schedule handles that.

How is the stop sign fine set in Washington?

In Washington, the fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. Courts that handle traffic infractions work from a schedule under the IRLJ, the Infraction Rules for Courts of Limited Jurisdiction.

Statutory assessments are built into the IRLJ schedule amounts. That means the amount on the schedule is meant to be the full figure for that line, rather than a base fine with several separate surcharges stacked on top.

You can read the underlying traffic code through Washington statutes via Justia. Statutes describe the rules, while the amount you owe comes from the court and your citation.

  • The court sets the amount within the state's traffic code.
  • Statutory assessments are included in the schedule amounts.
  • The citation shows what applies to you.

If anything on the ticket is unclear, the court clerk can explain how to read it. Clerks can explain procedure, but they cannot give legal advice.

How many points does a stop sign ticket add in Washington?

None. Washington has no point system. Many other states assign points to a stop sign violation, but Washington does not.

That does not mean a conviction is irrelevant. Suspensions follow repeat moving-violation convictions within set windows. The state looks at how many moving-violation convictions appear on your record within those windows, rather than adding up a points total.

The Washington State Department of Licensing maintains driving records and handles license actions, so it is the right place to check what is on your record and how it is treated.

We do not state the number of convictions or the length of the windows here, because they are not part of the figures we have verified. Check the Department of Licensing before assuming where you stand.

Does a stop sign ticket raise insurance in Washington?

It can. Nationally, moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Washington-specific one.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your driving history, and how the insurer treats a single conviction. Some insurers look at the conviction itself, others at the overall record. We cannot predict what yours will do, and we do not recommend any insurer.

  • The roughly 20% figure is an average, so your result may be higher or lower.
  • A conviction on your record is what usually matters to insurers.
  • Your insurer or agent can tell you how your own policy is rated.

Because the effect is tied to a conviction, how the ticket is resolved can matter for your premium as well as for your fine.

Can a stop sign ticket be dismissed or reduced in Washington?

Possibly, but that is decided by the court. A Washington traffic citation generally gives you choices: pay it, contest it, or ask the court to consider your circumstances. The citation explains which choices apply to you and how to request them.

Because the fine is set by the court within the state's traffic code, any reduction would also come from the court. We cannot say how likely a reduction or dismissal is, and we do not state any amounts for it.

If you are weighing options, consider these questions:

  1. What does the citation say about response choices and deadlines?
  2. Would a conviction affect your driving record or insurance?
  3. Do you have evidence, such as photos or witness information, if you plan to contest it?

For advice about your specific case, speak with a licensed attorney. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not recommend attorneys.

What happens if I don't pay a stop sign ticket in Washington?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. Courts can treat an unanswered citation as a failure to respond, and that can lead to added consequences on top of the original amount.

Washington links license consequences to convictions, and suspensions follow repeat moving-violation convictions within set windows (Washington State Department of Licensing). An unresolved ticket can complicate your standing with the court and the Department of Licensing.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court listed on your citation before the due date. Courts often have procedures for people who need to arrange payment, and asking early is better than staying silent.

We do not list specific late fees or deadlines, since those come from the court and your citation. Check them there.
