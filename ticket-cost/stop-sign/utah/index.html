How much is a stop sign ticket in Utah?

Nationally, a stop sign ticket runs $50–$500 depending on the state. In Utah, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Utah Driver License Division).

We do not publish a single Utah dollar figure, because the amount you owe depends on the court and the details written on your ticket. A number from a forum post or a friend's ticket may not match yours.

The national range is a reference point only. It spans many states with very different fine structures, so it does not predict what a Utah court will list.

The most reliable number is the one printed on your citation or shown on the court's payment notice. If the two differ, ask the court clerk which one controls.

How is a Utah stop sign fine set?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The statutes are available through Utah statutes via Justia.

Utah's schedule also builds in zone enhancements and fees. That means the amount you see can include more than a base fine, depending on where the stop occurred and how the court applies its schedule.

Because of this, two drivers cited for the same violation in different places could see different totals. We do not give an exact figure for either.

When you read your citation, look for these items:

  • The listed violation and the code section cited
  • Any fine or bail amount printed on the ticket
  • The court name and the date by which you must respond
  • Whether a fee or enhancement is itemized separately

How many points does a stop sign ticket carry in Utah?

Our locked sources do not give a point value for a stop sign violation, so we will not guess one. What they do give is Utah's speeding scale: speeding carries 35–75 points by mph over (Utah Driver License Division).

The same source sets the suspension threshold: 200 points in 3 years = suspension (ages 21+). That is the number to keep in mind when you add up any convictions on your record.

To find the points for your specific violation, check with the Driver License Division or the court handling your case. Points generally attach to a conviction, so how the case ends matters.

The speeding scale is not a stop sign scale. Do not assume the 35–75 range applies to your ticket.

Does a stop sign ticket raise insurance in Utah?

It can. Nationally, moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Utah-specific one.

Your own change could be higher, lower or zero. It depends on your insurer, your driving record and how the case is resolved. TicketCostGuide does not recommend insurers or predict what a particular company will do.

If you are worried about your rate, these steps can help:

  • Ask your insurer how it treats a conviction for a minor moving violation
  • Ask the court how the case would be recorded if you resolve it a certain way
  • Compare the total cost of the fine with a possible premium increase over several years

The national average is a planning figure only. Your policy terms and renewal date matter more.

Can a stop sign ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome depends on the court and the facts. We cannot tell you what will work in your case, and nothing here is legal advice.

In general, a driver cited for a traffic violation can usually pay the ticket, which typically means accepting the conviction, or contest it. Courts may also offer other ways to resolve a case. Ask the clerk what your court allows and what each choice does to your record.

Questions worth asking before you decide:

  • Does paying count as a conviction, and are points assessed?
  • Is there a hearing option, and what is the deadline to request it?
  • Does the court offer any alternative resolution for this violation?

If the case is complicated or you are near the 200-point suspension threshold, you may want to talk with a licensed attorney of your own choosing.

What happens if I don't pay a stop sign ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes the situation worse. Courts set response deadlines, and missing one can lead to added consequences. The details depend on the court and on Utah law.

Because Utah's schedule includes fees, an unpaid or late ticket can end up costing more than the amount first listed. We do not give a figure for that, since it is set by the court.

A suspension is tied to Utah's point system, with 200 points in 3 years = suspension (ages 21+) per the Utah Driver License Division. Other license consequences may arise from unresolved citations, so confirm directly with the court.

If you cannot meet the deadline, contact the court before it passes and ask what options exist.
