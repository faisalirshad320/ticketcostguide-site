How much is a stop sign ticket in Alaska?

The amount is set by the court and printed on your citation. We do not publish an exact Alaska dollar figure here, because the figure that applies to you depends on the court and the details of your stop.

For context, stop sign tickets run nationally $50–$500 depending on the state (Alaska Division of Motor Vehicles). That range covers every state, so treat it as a rough national guide and not a prediction for Alaska.

The number on your citation, or the one the court gives you, is the one that counts. Anything else is an estimate.

How is a stop sign fine set in Alaska?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The code and the court's schedule decide the amount, not the officer who writes the ticket.

Several things can change the final figure:

  • Where the violation happened, since some zones carry doubled fines.
  • Whether the court adds any surcharges or costs.
  • Whether the ticket is reduced, dismissed or paid as written.

The statutes are published through Alaska statutes via Justia. They are written in legal language, so use them as a reference and not as a replacement for the instructions on your citation.

Does a stop sign ticket cost more in a work zone or school zone?

It can. Work-zone and school-zone violations carry doubled fines under the schedule.

If your stop sign was in or near one of these zones, expect the amount to be higher than for the same violation elsewhere. The citation should show whether a zone applies.

We do not give a dollar figure for the doubled amount, because the base fine is set by the court on the citation. Ask the court clerk how the zone affects your total.

How many points does a stop sign ticket carry in Alaska?

Alaska uses a demerit point system. The range is 2–6 demerit points by speed, and 12 points in 12 months means a suspension.

That range is tied to speed, so it may not describe a stop sign violation exactly. Our figures do not say how many points a stop sign conviction adds. Confirm that with the Alaska Division of Motor Vehicles before you assume a number.

Points matter most when they stack. A stop sign ticket on top of other recent violations could bring you closer to the 12-point threshold, so count any other tickets from the past 12 months.

Does a stop sign ticket raise your insurance?

It may. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Alaska-specific one.

Your own change could be higher, lower, or nothing at all. It depends on your insurer, your driving record and how the ticket is resolved. We do not recommend any insurer, and we cannot predict your rate.

  • A conviction is more likely to affect your rate than a ticket that is dismissed.
  • Ask your insurer how it treats a stop sign violation before you decide how to respond.

Can a stop sign ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes. You usually have a few choices: pay the ticket, contest it in court, or ask the court about other resolutions it offers.

Paying is typically treated as admitting the violation, which can mean a conviction, points and an effect on insurance. Contesting means presenting your case to the court. The citation explains which options the court allows and how to use them.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice. If the stakes are high, for example if you are near the 12-point threshold, you may want to speak with a licensed attorney of your choosing.

Read the instructions on your citation first. They tell you how to respond and where.

What happens if I don't pay a stop sign ticket?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. Courts commonly treat an unanswered citation as a further problem, which can bring added costs or action against your driving privileges.

We do not list specific Alaska deadlines or penalty amounts, because they are not in our sourced figures. Your citation and the court clerk are the reliable places to find them.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court before the deadline passes and ask what it offers. That is better than staying silent.
