How much is a stop sign ticket in Louisiana?

Nationally, a stop sign ticket costs $50–$500 depending on the state. In Louisiana the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Louisiana Office of Motor Vehicles). We do not publish a single Louisiana dollar figure, because the amount is not the same for every driver.

The fine is only part of what you pay. Local court costs vary by parish and often exceed the fine itself, so the total on your citation or court notice can be well above the fine line.

The $50–$500 range is a national range across states. Treat it as background, not as a prediction for your Louisiana ticket.

Your citation and the court handling it are the only reliable sources for your exact amount. Anything else is an estimate.

How is a Louisiana stop sign fine set?

The exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The Louisiana statutes are published online through Louisiana statutes via Justia, which is where you can read the traffic provisions yourself.

That means two drivers cited for the same stop sign violation can see different totals depending on the court, the parish and the circumstances written on the citation.

Costs are layered. The fine is one piece, and local court costs are added on top. Because those costs vary by parish and often exceed the fine, comparing your bill with a friend's ticket from another parish is rarely useful.

  • The fine: set by the court on the citation.
  • Court costs: vary by parish and often exceed the fine.
  • Anything else: listed on your citation or court notice.

How many points does a stop sign ticket carry in Louisiana?

None, because Louisiana has no point system. There is no point total to count up or wait out after a stop sign conviction.

That does not mean a conviction has no effect on your license. License action follows repeat convictions and specific offenses. A single stop sign ticket is one entry on your driving record, and a pattern of convictions is what draws attention.

For the state's official record and license information, see the Louisiana Office of Motor Vehicles. For a comparison of how states handle traffic laws, the Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a state laws resource.

No points does not mean no consequences. Repeat convictions are what matter for your license in Louisiana.

Does a stop sign ticket raise insurance in Louisiana?

It can. Nationally, moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Louisiana quote, and your own change could be higher, lower or nothing at all.

Whether a ticket affects your rate depends on your insurer, your policy, your driving history and whether the ticket ends in a conviction. We do not recommend any insurer, and we cannot tell you what yours will do.

If cost matters, the useful question is not only "what is the fine?" but also "what happens to my premium at renewal?" A ticket that looks cheap at the court window can cost more over time through insurance.

  • Ask your insurer how it treats a moving-violation conviction.
  • Check your renewal date, since changes often show up then.
  • Keep the court's final disposition paperwork.

Can a stop sign ticket be dismissed or reduced in Louisiana?

Sometimes outcomes other than paying in full are possible, but that is decided by the court and the facts of your case. We cannot say what is available to you, and this page is not legal advice.

Your options generally include paying the ticket, contesting it, or asking the court what other resolutions it offers. Paying usually resolves the case, but it can also mean a conviction on your record. Contesting means you present your side to the court.

Because fines and costs vary by parish, the clerk's office for the court named on your citation is the best place to ask what steps are open to you. If you want advice on your specific situation, a licensed Louisiana attorney is the person to ask; we do not recommend a particular one.

Read the citation carefully before you decide. The court, the response instructions and the amount owed are all printed there or in the court's notice.

What happens if I don't pay a Louisiana stop sign ticket?

Ignoring a ticket generally makes things worse. Courts can add consequences when a citation goes unanswered, and since license action in Louisiana follows repeat convictions and specific offenses, an unresolved ticket is not something to leave sitting in a drawer.

We do not list specific deadlines or penalty amounts here, because they are not part of our locked figures and they depend on the court. The court named on your citation will tell you how and when to respond.

If you cannot pay in full, contact the clerk of that court before the deadline passes. Courts often can explain what they accept, which is better than staying silent.

What should I check on my citation first?

Before you pay or contest, confirm the basics. Mistakes on a citation, or a misunderstanding of what you owe, are easy to fix early and hard to fix later.

  • The court: which court and parish is named, since costs vary by parish.
  • The amount: the fine is set by the court on the citation, and costs may be listed separately.
  • The response instructions: how and where to pay or contest.
  • The violation: that it matches what happened at the stop.

Keep a copy of the citation and any receipt or court paperwork. If the total looks higher than you expected, remember that local court costs often exceed the fine.
