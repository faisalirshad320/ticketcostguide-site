How much is a stop sign ticket in North Dakota?

Nationally, a stop sign ticket runs $50–$500 depending on the state (North Dakota Department of Transportation). That range covers every state, so it tells you little about one specific ticket.

In North Dakota, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single North Dakota dollar figure, because the amount that applies to you is the one the court puts on your paperwork.

Look at the citation first. The amount, the court, and the instructions for responding are all printed there.

The statutory fee is usually the whole amount. Unlike some states, North Dakota does not typically add a large stack of surcharges on top of it.

How is the fine for a stop sign violation set?

The fine is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The officer writes the violation on the citation, and the court determines the amount that goes with it.

That is why two drivers in different places can see different numbers for what looks like the same offense. We cannot say what any particular court will assess, and we will not guess.

North Dakota traffic law is published in the state's century code. You can read the relevant provisions through North Dakota statutes via Justia. If the wording on your citation does not match what you expected, the statute text is the best place to check.

  • The citation states the violation and the amount.
  • The court sets that amount within the traffic code.
  • The statutory fee is usually the whole bill.

Are there extra court costs or surcharges?

Usually not many. Our sources indicate the statutory fee is usually the whole amount, with no large cost stack added on top.

That makes the number on the citation a fairly reliable guide to what you will pay. Still, read the citation and any court notice closely, because a late payment or a missed deadline can change your situation in ways a fee schedule does not show.

If anything on the paperwork looks unclear, ask the clerk of the court named on the citation. Clerks can explain how to pay and what the amount covers, though they cannot give legal advice.

How many points is a stop sign ticket in North Dakota?

North Dakota assigns points on its schedule, and the locked schedule we rely on describes points by mph over the limit. A stop sign violation is not a speed violation, so we do not state a point value for it here.

To find out how your conviction would be scored, check with the North Dakota Department of Transportation. The Department keeps the driver record, so it is the authority on what lands there.

One threshold is clear: 12+ points = suspension. Points accumulate across violations, so one ticket matters most when it is added to others already on your record.

We are not stating a point value for a stop sign ticket because the schedule we cite does not give one for this offense. Confirm it with the DOT before making decisions based on points.

Does a stop sign ticket raise insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a North Dakota one.

Your own change could be higher, lower, or nothing at all. Insurers set rates using their own rules, and many look at your full driving history, not one ticket.

We do not recommend any insurer. If you are worried about the impact, you can ask your insurer how a conviction would be treated, but remember that the answer depends on your policy and record.

  • The 20% figure is an average across the US.
  • It applies to convictions, not to every citation issued.
  • Whether a ticket becomes a conviction depends on how the case is resolved.

Can a stop sign ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome depends on the court and the facts. We cannot say how likely it is in your case, and TicketCostGuide does not give legal advice.

In general, a person with a citation can pay it, which is usually treated as accepting the violation, or contest it. The citation lists how to respond and where. Contesting means the court decides the case on the evidence.

Because a conviction is what typically drives the insurance effect, the way a case is resolved matters. If the ticket is serious for your record, you may want to speak with a licensed attorney. We do not recommend any specific one.

  • Read the response instructions on the citation.
  • Note the court and any listed deadline.
  • Decide whether to pay or contest before that deadline passes.

What happens if I don't pay a stop sign ticket?

Ignoring a citation is rarely a good idea. Courts generally expect a response, and an unanswered ticket can lead to further action against you.

We do not list specific penalties or timelines here, because our sources do not give locked figures for them. Your citation and the court's notices are the reliable guide to what happens next.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court named on the citation before the deadline. Clerks can tell you what the court allows, and asking early is better than missing a date.

Unpaid or ignored tickets can also affect your driver record. Because suspension is tied to points, keep your total in mind. The threshold is 12+ points.
