How much is a stop sign ticket in Oregon?

Nationally, stop sign tickets run $50–$500 depending on the state. In Oregon, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Oregon DMV (ODOT)).

That national range is a spread across all states. It is not a prediction for Oregon, and we do not put an Oregon dollar figure on this page that our sources do not support.

The number printed on your citation, or on the court's notice, is the amount that applies to you. Check it before you pay or contest.

If you want a number before you have a citation in hand, the court that handles your case is the reliable source.

How is the fine for a stop sign violation set in Oregon?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. It is not a single flat amount that we can quote for every driver.

Some add-ons are written directly into statute. Zone doubling and the 100+ mph enhancements are set in the law itself (Oregon statutes via Justia). Those two rules apply to specific situations, such as certain designated zones or very high speeds.

  • A plain stop sign violation is not a 100+ mph offense, so that enhancement does not apply to it.
  • Zone doubling depends on where the violation happened, so read your citation for location details.

If your citation lists anything beyond the base amount, ask the court clerk what each line is for.

What should I check on my citation?

Start with the amount and the due date. Then look at how the violation is described and where it happened.

  1. The dollar amount the court has listed.
  2. The deadline to pay or respond.
  3. The court name and how it accepts payments or requests.
  4. The location, in case a zone rule is noted.
  5. The offense description, to confirm it matches what happened.

Errors on a citation can matter. If something is wrong or unclear, ask the court how to raise it.

How many points does a stop sign ticket add in Oregon?

Oregon has no point system. Suspensions follow repeat convictions (for example, habitual-offender rules) and specific offenses like 100+ mph (Oregon DMV (ODOT)).

So a stop sign conviction does not add a point value to your record. That does not mean it has no effect. A conviction can still count toward a pattern of repeat convictions.

For a state-by-state comparison of traffic laws, the Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a reference page.

Does a stop sign ticket raise insurance in Oregon?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Oregon-specific rate.

Your own result depends on your insurer, your driving record and how the insurer treats a conviction. We do not recommend any insurer and cannot predict what yours will do.

  • A conviction is more likely to affect rates than a ticket that does not result in one.
  • Insurers often review your record at renewal.
  • Your insurer or agent can tell you how it rates violations.

The ~20% figure is an average across the country. Your premium could move more, less or not at all.

Can a stop sign ticket be dismissed or reduced in Oregon?

Often there are choices besides simply paying. The usual ones are paying, contesting the ticket, or asking the court about other options.

Paying generally means accepting the conviction, which is what can affect insurance. Contesting means asking the court to hear your case. What a court can offer, and how, varies by court.

  • Read the response instructions on your citation.
  • Ask the clerk which options your court offers.
  • Keep copies of anything you submit.

What happens if I don't pay a stop sign ticket in Oregon?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts set deadlines, and missing one can lead to additional consequences, including trouble with your driving privileges.

We do not list specific late fees or penalties, because our sources do not give an Oregon figure. Check your citation and the court's notice for what applies.

If you cannot pay by the deadline, contact the court before it passes and ask what you can do.

Repeat convictions are also what drive suspensions in Oregon, so unresolved tickets are worth dealing with early (Oregon DMV (ODOT)).
