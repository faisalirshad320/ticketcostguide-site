How much is a stop sign ticket in Florida?

There is no single Florida price to quote. Nationally, a stop sign ticket costs $50–$500 depending on the state (FLHSMV). In Florida, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation.

That range is a national figure. It is not a Florida fine, and we do not claim a specific Florida dollar amount on this page.

Your total usually has several parts:

  • The statutory penalty, set within the state's traffic code.
  • County court costs and fees added on top of that penalty.
  • Any enhancement, such as the doubling that applies to school and work-zone amounts.

The number that matters is the one on your citation or the one the clerk of the issuing court gives you. Use that, not a general estimate.

How is the fine set, and why do totals differ by county?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within Florida's traffic code. The statutes are published online, including through Florida statutes via Justia.

The statutory penalty is only part of what you owe. County courts add costs and fees on top of it, and those additions are why two drivers with the same violation can see different totals.

This is what "varies by county and court" means in practice. The violation is the same, but the add-ons and the court handling the case can differ.

Location can also change the amount. School and work-zone amounts are doubled by statute. If your stop sign was in or near one of those zones, the citation may show a higher figure.

Read the citation closely. It should show the amount, the issuing court and the ways to respond. If something looks unclear, the clerk's office can explain the charges, though it cannot give legal advice.

How many points does a stop sign ticket carry in Florida?

Florida's points system, as we have it sourced, is 3 points (15 or less over) or 4 points (16+ over). Those tiers are described in terms of how far over a limit a driver was. They are written for speed violations.

Because of that, we cannot tell you from our sourced facts exactly how many points a stop sign conviction adds. We do not want to guess.

To find out, check the citation and ask the clerk. You can also look at the driver-record information on the FLHSMV site.

The suspension rule is clearer: 12 points in 12 months = 30-day suspension. Points from every violation count toward that total.

A stop sign ticket may look minor. If you already have other recent violations, even a small number of added points can matter.

Does a stop sign ticket raise insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Florida quote.

Your own change could be higher, lower or nothing at all. It depends on your insurer, your driving record and how the violation is recorded.

A few points to keep in mind:

  • The effect usually follows a conviction, so how the ticket is resolved matters.
  • Insurers look at your whole record, not just one ticket.
  • Rate changes often show up at renewal rather than right away.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer. If you want to know how a ticket would affect your policy, ask your insurer or agent directly.

Can a stop sign ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome depends on the facts and on the court. We cannot predict it, and nothing here is legal advice.

In general, a driver who receives a citation has a few paths:

  1. Pay the amount. This is usually treated as accepting the violation, which can lead to points and a possible insurance effect.
  2. Contest the citation. You can ask the court to hear the case.
  3. Ask about other options. Some courts offer alternatives, such as a driver improvement course. Whether one is available to you depends on the court and your record.

The citation lists the ways to respond and where. The clerk of the issuing court can tell you which options apply. If you want advice on whether to fight a ticket, that is a question for a licensed attorney, and we do not recommend one.

What happens if I don't pay a stop sign ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. A traffic ticket carries a response requirement, and missing it can lead to added costs and further action against your license.

The exact steps, timing and extra charges in Florida depend on the court. We do not list specific deadlines or penalty amounts here because they are not part of our sourced figures.

For the correct process, do these things:

  • Read the response instructions printed on the citation.
  • Contact the clerk of the issuing court if you are unsure about the amount or the deadline.
  • Check your status through FLHSMV if you are concerned about your license.

Remember that unresolved points and violations add up. The 12 points in 12 months = 30-day suspension rule applies to your total, not to a single ticket.
