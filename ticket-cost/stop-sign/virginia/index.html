How much is a stop sign ticket in Virginia?

Nationally, a stop sign ticket costs $50–$500 depending on the state. In Virginia, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Virginia Department of Motor Vehicles).

We do not publish a single Virginia dollar figure here. The national range is only a rough frame for comparison, and the number on your own ticket is the one that counts.

Two things shape what you actually pay:

  • The fine itself. This is set by the court within the state's traffic code.
  • Court costs. Fixed processing costs apply to every prepaid ticket.

If the charge is reckless driving rather than a basic stop sign violation, the court can add fines and fees on top. That is a different and more serious situation than a standard stop sign citation.

Read the citation carefully. It shows the charge, the court that will handle it, and how that court wants you to respond.

How is a Virginia stop sign fine set?

The fine is not a flat statewide price you can look up in one place. The amount is set by the court within the state's traffic code, which you can read through Virginia statutes via Justia.

That means the figure printed on your citation, or posted by the court that handles it, is the one to rely on. Different courts can handle tickets differently, so a friend's ticket from another area may not match yours.

Processing costs work differently. Fixed processing costs apply to every prepaid ticket, so paying early does not remove them.

The national range of $50–$500 is a useful reminder that states vary a great deal. It is not a prediction for Virginia.

  1. Find the charge and the court on your citation.
  2. Check the court's own notice or payment page for the amount due.
  3. Confirm whether that amount includes the fixed processing costs.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot tell you what a court will do in your case.

How many points is a stop sign ticket in Virginia?

Virginia's demerit point system assigns 3–6 demerit points by offense, with 6 for reckless driving. Which value applies to you depends on the offense listed on your citation and conviction.

We do not assign a specific point value to a stop sign ticket here. Your driving record, available from the Virginia DMV, is the place to confirm what was recorded.

Points matter because the DMV suspends licenses at point thresholds. The more points on your record, the closer you move to that threshold.

There is also a credit that works in your favor. Drivers earn +1 safe point per clean year.

Points usually come from a conviction, not from the stop itself. How you resolve the ticket can change what lands on your record.

If you already have other violations, ask the DMV or a licensed attorney how one more conviction would affect your total.

Does a stop sign ticket raise insurance in Virginia?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Virginia-specific one.

Your own change could be higher, lower or zero. It depends on your insurer, your history, and whether the ticket ends in a conviction on your record.

A few points to keep in mind:

  • The 20% is an average across the US, so treat it as a ballpark.
  • The insurance cost of a conviction can outlast the fine itself.
  • Insurers decide their own rates. We do not recommend any insurer.

When you weigh whether to pay or contest, think about both the one-time cost and the possible premium change. The cheaper choice on the day is not always the cheaper choice over time.

Your insurer or agent is the only reliable source for how a conviction would affect your policy.

Can a Virginia stop sign ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but we cannot promise an outcome. The court that handles your citation decides what happens, and the fine is set by that court.

In general, you have a few paths:

  • Prepay. This closes the matter quickly. Fixed processing costs apply to every prepaid ticket, and you should check how a prepayment is recorded on your record.
  • Contest it in court. You appear and present your side. The court decides.
  • Ask about other resolutions. Some courts offer alternatives. Ask the court clerk what is available for your charge.

Before choosing, weigh the three costs together: the fine and processing costs, the demerit points of 3–6 by offense, and a possible insurance increase of about 20% on average nationally.

For legal advice on your specific ticket, consult a licensed Virginia attorney. TicketCostGuide does not recommend specific attorneys.

Statutes that govern traffic offenses are available through Virginia statutes via Justia. The wording of a statute can differ from how a court applies it, so use it for background rather than as a prediction.

What happens if I don't pay a stop sign ticket in Virginia?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. Courts expect a response, and an unanswered citation can bring added penalties or action against your license.

We do not state exact deadlines or late fees here, because those are set by the court and printed on your paperwork. Check the citation for the response instructions.

Remember also that the DMV suspends licenses at point thresholds. Letting a ticket go unresolved can add to the risk to your driving privileges.

  1. Read the citation for the court and response instructions.
  2. Contact the court clerk if anything is unclear.
  3. Decide whether to pay or contest, and act before the court's deadline.

If you cannot afford the amount, ask the court what options it offers. Do not assume the answer without asking.

If you have missed a date already, contact the court promptly or talk to a licensed attorney.

Frequently asked questions
