How much is a stop sign ticket in New Jersey?

Nationally, a stop sign ticket runs $50–$500 depending on the state. In New Jersey the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (New Jersey Motor Vehicle Commission).

We do not publish a single New Jersey dollar figure because the court that handles your ticket decides the amount. The number printed on your citation or court notice is the one that applies to you.

The $50–$500 range is a national spread across states. It is not a New Jersey fine schedule.

Beyond the fine itself, a conviction can bring points, an MVC surcharge and higher insurance rates. Those are covered below.

How is the fine for a stop sign violation set?

The exact fine for this violation in New Jersey is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The offense is written into the state's statutes, which you can browse through New Jersey statutes via Justia.

Because the court sets the amount, it can differ depending on which court handles your case. Look at the citation first, then at any notice from the court clerk.

  • The citation lists the violation and the court that has your case.
  • The court notice or payment page shows the amount owed.
  • The MVC handles points and surcharges separately from the court.

If the number on your paperwork looks different from something you read online, trust the paperwork and ask the court clerk.

How many points is a stop sign ticket in New Jersey?

New Jersey's points system lists these tiers: 2 points (1–14 over), 4 points (15–29 over) and 5 points (30+ over). Those tiers are written by how far over a limit a driver was.

That wording is built around speed, so we do not assign a specific point value to a stop sign violation here. Check your citation and the New Jersey Motor Vehicle Commission for how your violation is scored.

What matters most is the running total. Reaching 12 points means a suspension, and 6+ points within 3 years adds an MVC insurance surcharge of $150+.

A single ticket may not reach those thresholds. Points from earlier violations still count toward your total.

Will I owe an MVC surcharge on top of the fine?

Possibly. When you reach 6+ points within 3 years, the MVC adds an insurance surcharge of $150+.

These surcharges are billed annually for 3 years on top of court fines. That means the surcharge is a separate bill from the court's fine and can recur.

  • Court fine: set by the court on your citation.
  • MVC surcharge: $150+ once you hit 6+ points within 3 years.
  • Billing: annually for 3 years.

If a stop sign ticket is your only recent violation, you may be well under the 6-point line. If you already have points, the added points could push you over it.

Does a stop sign ticket raise my insurance?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a New Jersey rate.

Your own change could be smaller, larger or nothing at all. It depends on your insurer, your driving record and how the violation is recorded.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend or compare insurers. If you want to know what a conviction would do to your rate, your insurer or agent can tell you.

Remember the MVC surcharge is separate from any increase your insurance company applies.

Can a stop sign ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes outcomes change after a court appearance, but we cannot predict yours. The fine is set by the court, so the court is where any change to the amount or the charge would be decided.

Here are the general paths, which are not legal advice:

  1. Pay the ticket. This usually resolves the case but may result in a conviction, points and the costs above.
  2. Contest it in court. You can ask the court to hear your case.
  3. Ask the court clerk about options. The clerk can explain what the court allows for your citation.

If you are weighing a serious record problem, such as being close to 12 points, you may want to speak with a licensed attorney. We do not recommend a specific one.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the ticket?

Ignoring a citation tends to make things worse, not better. We do not list specific deadlines or late fees here because those come from the court handling your case.

Read your citation and any court notices for dates and instructions. If you reach 12 points, the result is a license suspension.

  • Unpaid or ignored tickets can lead to additional court action.
  • Points and surcharges are tracked by the MVC.
  • A surcharge at 6+ points is billed annually for 3 years.

If you are unsure what to do, contact the court clerk named on your ticket before any date passes.
