How much is a stop sign ticket in Vermont?

Nationally, a stop sign ticket runs $50–$500 depending on the state. In Vermont the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Vermont Department of Motor Vehicles).

We do not publish a single Vermont dollar figure here, because the number printed on your ticket is the one that counts. The national range is context only. It shows how widely states differ, not what you will owe in Vermont.

Lead figure: Nationally $50–$500 depending on the state; in Vermont the exact amount is set by the court on the citation.

Look at the citation for the amount due and for any waiver amount listed. That total is what you would pay if you resolve the ticket without contesting it.

How is the fine for running a stop sign set in Vermont?

The fine is set by the court within the state's traffic code. It is not a fixed number that every driver pays in every case. The statutes are published online through Vermont statutes via Justia.

Statutory surcharges are included in the waiver amounts. That means the waiver figure on your ticket is not just a base fine with extra fees added later. It already folds the surcharges in.

Things that can shape what you see on a citation include:

  • The violation charged and how the officer wrote it up.
  • The court that handles the ticket.
  • Whether a waiver amount is offered for that violation.

Because these details change from ticket to ticket, treat the citation as the final word on cost.

How many points does a stop sign ticket add in Vermont?

Vermont's point system is summarized in our reference data as 2–8 points by mph over, with 10 points in 2 years leading to suspension.

That scale is described by speed. A stop sign violation is not a speeding offense, so we do not state a specific point value for it here. Guessing would risk giving you the wrong number.

To find out what applies to your ticket:

  1. Read the violation description on the citation.
  2. Check the Vermont Department of Motor Vehicles for how that violation is treated on your record.
  3. Ask the court clerk if the citation is unclear.

Why points matter: reaching 10 points in 2 years means suspension. Even a small number of points can move you closer to that line if you already have other violations.

Does a stop sign ticket raise your insurance?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Vermont rate.

Your own change could be higher, lower or nothing at all. It depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the ticket is resolved.

Some general points to keep in mind:

  • A conviction is more likely to affect your rates than a ticket that is dismissed.
  • Insurers usually look at your record when your policy renews.
  • TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer, so ask your own carrier how a violation would be treated.

When you weigh the fine against a possible premium increase, remember the increase can last longer than the one-time fine.

Can a stop sign ticket be dismissed or reduced in Vermont?

Sometimes, but we cannot promise an outcome. The court sets the amount on the citation, and the court is also where you can contest the ticket.

Your options generally look like this:

  • Pay the waiver amount. This resolves the ticket quickly. It usually means a conviction, which can mean points and a possible insurance effect.
  • Contest the ticket. You ask the court to hear the case. The outcome depends on the evidence and the court.
  • Ask the clerk about alternatives. Courts differ in what they offer, so ask what is available for your citation.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice. If the stakes are high, such as a record already close to the 10-point threshold, you may want to speak with a licensed attorney of your choosing.

What happens if I don't pay a stop sign ticket in Vermont?

Ignoring a ticket tends to make things worse. Courts can add consequences when a citation is left unanswered, and a license problem can follow.

We do not list specific deadlines or penalty amounts here because they are not part of our verified figures. Your citation states how and when to respond.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court clerk before the deadline on your ticket. Asking early is better than missing the date.

An unresolved ticket can also interact with points. If you are already near 10 points in 2 years, a suspension would be a bigger problem than the fine itself.
