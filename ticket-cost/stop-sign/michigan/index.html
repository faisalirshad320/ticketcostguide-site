How much is a stop sign ticket in Michigan?

Nationally, a stop sign ticket runs $50–$500 depending on the state. In Michigan the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Michigan Secretary of State).

We do not publish a single Michigan dollar figure because the sources we rely on do not give one. The national range is a comparison point across states. It is not a quote for what a Michigan court will charge you.

The number printed on your citation, or the amount the court clerk gives you, is the real cost. Anything else is an estimate.

The fine is only one piece of the total. Court costs are added on top, and they are covered in the next section.

How is the fine and total cost set?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The traffic code is available through Michigan statutes via Justia.

District court costs vary by court and make up much of the total. Two drivers with the same violation can therefore owe different totals depending on which court handles the case.

When you compare what you owe, look at these items separately:

  • The fine itself, as set by the court on the citation.
  • Court costs, which vary by court.
  • Any other amounts the court lists on the notice.

Ask the court clerk for the total if the citation does not show it. Use the court that is named on your ticket, not one in a neighboring county.

How many points does a stop sign ticket carry in Michigan?

Michigan's point scale under §257.320a runs 2–4 points by mph over. That scale is tied to speed, so it does not map directly onto a stop sign violation, which has no mph-over figure.

We therefore do not state a point value for a stop sign conviction. The citation and the Michigan Secretary of State are the places to confirm how your specific conviction is scored.

The threshold that matters most is this: 12 points in 2 years = driver reexamination. Points from separate violations can add up toward it. A single stop sign ticket matters more if you already have other recent convictions.

Check your own driver record through the Secretary of State before deciding how to respond to a ticket, especially if you already have other points.

Does a stop sign ticket raise insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums ~20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Michigan-specific one.

Whether a stop sign ticket affects your premium depends on your insurer, your record and whether the ticket ends in a conviction. We do not recommend or compare insurers, and we cannot predict your quote.

Over time, a premium increase can cost more than the fine itself. Think about the full cost of a conviction, not only the amount due to the court.

Your insurer or agent can tell you how they treat a violation like this one. Ask before you decide how to resolve the ticket.

Can a stop sign ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome is up to the court. We do not give legal advice and cannot say whether your case qualifies.

In general, a driver who receives a ticket has a few paths:

  • Pay the amount on the citation, which typically ends the matter but may result in a conviction on your record.
  • Contest the ticket and present your side to the court.
  • Contact the court clerk to ask what options and deadlines apply to your case.

Reasons people contest stop sign tickets include unclear or obstructed signage, or disagreement about what happened at the intersection. Whether any of those reasons applies to you is for the court to decide.

If you want advice on your specific case, a licensed attorney is the right person to ask. We do not recommend any particular attorney.

Read the citation carefully. It states how and where to respond, and missing the response requirements can make things worse.

What happens if I don't pay a stop sign ticket?

Ignoring a ticket rarely makes it go away. Courts generally treat unpaid or unanswered tickets as a separate problem, and the total owed can grow.

The exact consequences and timelines in Michigan depend on the court and on the instructions on your citation. We do not state specific deadlines or penalty amounts, because our sources do not give them.

Unresolved tickets can also lead to trouble with your driving record. The Michigan Secretary of State manages driver records and licensing, so that is the place to check your status.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court clerk early and ask what the court allows. Courts often have more room to work with someone who reaches out than with someone who stays silent.

How does Michigan compare with other states?

Stop sign fines differ widely across the country. The national range is $50–$500 depending on the state, and Michigan's exact amount is set by the court on the citation.

Other states handle this differently. Some use fixed schedules, others leave more to local courts, and point values vary as well. The Governors Highway Safety Association tracks state traffic safety laws if you want a broader view.

Do not assume a figure from another state applies in Michigan. Court costs in particular differ by court, so the same violation can total differently in different places.
