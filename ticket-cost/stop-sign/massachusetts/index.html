How much is a stop sign ticket in Massachusetts?

Nationally $50–$500 depending on the state; in Massachusetts the exact amount is set by the court on the citation — Stop Sign Ticket cost in Massachusetts. Source: state statutes and schedules, last reviewed Oct 2026 (Massachusetts Registry of Motor Vehicles).

That means this page cannot give you a single Massachusetts dollar figure. The number to rely on is the one printed on your citation or confirmed by the court.

The $50–$500 range is a national spread across states, not a Massachusetts fine schedule. Use it as context for how widely stop sign fines differ, not as a prediction of your bill.

The fine is only one part of the cost. Surcharges, points and insurance changes can matter more over time, and each is covered below.

How is a stop sign fine set in Massachusetts?

In Massachusetts the exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The citation is the document that shows what you are being asked to pay for your specific violation. You can read the code itself through Massachusetts statutes via Justia.

Because the court sets the amount, we do not publish a fixed Massachusetts number. A figure quoted without reference to your citation is a guess.

A few things can shape what you actually owe:

  • The exact violation written on the citation, including how the officer described it.
  • Whether the court adds any surcharge or cost on top of the base amount.
  • How the case is resolved: paid, contested at a hearing, or handled another way the court allows.

If your citation and an online figure disagree, the citation and the court control.

How many points does a stop sign ticket carry in Massachusetts?

Massachusetts uses insurance surcharge points (SDIP) rather than license suspension points for ordinary speeding. In practice the system is built around surchargeable events, which affect what insurers charge and can eventually affect your license.

Two thresholds apply:

  • 3 surchargeable events in 24 months require a driver retraining course.
  • 7 in 3 years means suspension.

We do not list a specific point value for a stop sign violation here, because it is not part of the figures we have verified. Ask the Registry of Motor Vehicles or check your citation and driving record for how your violation is treated (Massachusetts Registry of Motor Vehicles).

Why this matters: one ticket is rarely the problem. The thresholds above count events over time, so an earlier violation can change what a new one costs you.

Does a stop sign ticket raise insurance in Massachusetts?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Massachusetts-specific one.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the violation is recorded. Some drivers see no change, and others see a larger one.

Massachusetts surcharge points (SDIP) are the mechanism that connects a violation to your premium. Because a 20% increase applies year after year while the violation counts, the insurance cost can exceed the fine itself.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend insurers. To see your own effect, ask your insurer how a conviction would be rated before you decide how to respond to the citation.

Are there extra court costs or surcharges?

Massachusetts applies a $50 head-injury surcharge to every speeding conviction. That locked fact is about speeding, and a stop sign violation is a different offense.

So do not assume the $50 surcharge is part of your stop sign ticket. Look at the citation and any court notice for a list of what is owed, and ask the court clerk if a line item is unclear.

Costs that can sit on top of a fine include:

  • Surcharges that the court or statute attaches to certain convictions.
  • Insurance consequences after the conviction is reported.
  • Any extra charges for late payment, if you miss a deadline on the citation.

We only state dollar figures we have verified, which is why none of these are given an invented amount.

Can a stop sign ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome is up to the court. Your citation explains the options available to you, which generally include paying it or asking for a hearing to contest it.

Paying is usually treated as accepting responsibility, which can bring the points and insurance effects described above. Contesting gives you the chance to present your side, with no guarantee of the result.

Before deciding, it helps to:

  1. Read the citation closely for the violation, location and any response instructions.
  2. Check your driving record to see how close you are to the 3-in-24-months and 7-in-3-years thresholds.
  3. Ask your insurer how a conviction would be rated.
  4. Gather anything relevant, such as photos of the intersection or sign.

What happens if I don't pay a stop sign ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Unpaid or unanswered tickets can bring additional penalties and may lead to action involving your license or registration.

The exact consequences and deadlines are on your citation and in the court's notices. We do not list specific deadlines or late fees here because they are not among the figures we have verified.

If you cannot pay on time or cannot meet a deadline, contact the court named on the citation before the date passes. Court staff can explain what is available. For rules that apply to licensing, the Massachusetts Registry of Motor Vehicles is the primary source.
