How much is a stop sign ticket in Montana?

Nationally, a stop sign ticket costs $50–$500 depending on the state. In Montana, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Montana Department of Justice — Motor Vehicle Division).

This guide does not give a single Montana dollar figure. The amount depends on the court handling your case, and it is printed on the citation or in the court's notice to you.

Treat the national range as a comparison point only. It shows how widely states differ, and it does not tell you what Montana will charge you.

The number that matters is the one on your citation or the court's notice. If it is unclear, ask the court clerk for the total owed, including any surcharges.

How is a Montana stop sign fine set?

Montana's traffic code sets the framework for the penalty. The court sets the exact fine within that framework, and the amount appears on the citation.

That is why two drivers cited for the same violation can see different totals. The court, the circumstances and your record can all affect the outcome.

The statutes are published online. You can read Montana's traffic provisions through Montana statutes via Justia, though a published statute is not a substitute for the notice you received.

When you read your citation, look for:

  • The named court and how to contact it.
  • The listed violation and the date of the stop.
  • Whether the citation requires an appearance or allows payment without one.
  • The fine amount and any listed costs.

What court costs and surcharges are added?

Court surcharges are added to the statutory fine in Montana. The figure you pay can therefore be higher than the base fine alone.

The locked sources do not give a surcharge amount, so we do not state one. Ask the court for an itemized total before you pay.

Comparing totals is the most reliable approach. If a payment page shows a figure that differs from the citation, the difference is often surcharges. Confirm with the court before you assume an error.

Budget for the total, not the base fine. Surcharges are part of what the court expects you to pay.

How many points does a stop sign ticket carry in Montana?

In Montana, convictions carry conviction points toward the state's habitual-offender threshold: 30 points in 3 years results in revocation (Montana Department of Justice — Motor Vehicle Division).

We do not state a point value for a stop sign violation, because the locked sources do not give one. The Motor Vehicle Division can tell you what a specific conviction adds to your record.

Points attach to convictions. A ticket that has not resulted in a conviction is a different situation from one you have paid or lost at a hearing, so confirm how the court treats your payment.

Points count within a 3-year window. One stop sign conviction is far from the 30-point threshold on its own. Several violations over a short period can add up.

Does a stop sign ticket raise insurance in Montana?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Montana-specific rate.

A stop sign violation is generally a moving violation, so a conviction could affect your rate. Your insurer decides how, and TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer.

What your insurer does depends on its own rules, your driving history and the timing of your renewal. To find out, you can ask your insurer how it treats a single moving-violation conviction.

  • The 20% figure is an average, so your change could be higher, lower or none.
  • Many insurers check your record at renewal.
  • The effect depends on a conviction, so how the case ends matters.

Can a Montana stop sign ticket be dismissed or reduced?

You generally have two broad paths: pay the citation or contest it. Paying usually means accepting the violation. Contesting means asking the court to hear your case.

Whether a reduction, deferral or other arrangement is available depends on the court and your record. The locked sources do not describe a specific program, so we do not promise one.

To explore options, contact the court named on the citation. Ask what is available, what it would cost and how it would affect points on your record.

If you want legal advice about your situation, speak with a licensed Montana attorney. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not recommend specific attorneys.

Decide before you pay. Payment can be treated as a conviction, and a conviction carries points.

What happens if I don't pay a Montana stop sign ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes the situation worse. Courts can add consequences when a citation goes unanswered, and the details come from the court and Montana's traffic code.

The locked sources do not list specific penalties or deadlines, so we do not state any. Your citation or the court's notice should show the response requirements.

If you cannot pay the full amount, call the court before the deadline. Clerks can explain whether the court allows other arrangements.

Unpaid matters can also affect your driving record. For questions about your record and license status, the Montana Department of Justice — Motor Vehicle Division is the official source.
