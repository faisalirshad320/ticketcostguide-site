How much is a stop sign ticket in Colorado?

Nationally, a stop sign ticket runs $50–$500 depending on the state. In Colorado, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Colorado Division of Motor Vehicles).

We do not publish a single Colorado dollar amount because the number printed on your ticket, or assigned by the court, is the one that counts. Any exact figure from a third-party site may not match your case.

The national range is a national range. It is not a Colorado fine schedule. Your citation and the court that issued it are the final word on what you owe.

The total you pay can also include a statutory surcharge, covered below.

How is the fine set, and what gets added to it?

In Colorado, the exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The code is available through Colorado statutes via Justia. Because the court sets the amount, two drivers cited for the same violation can see different paperwork depending on the circumstances and the court.

Two state-level rules affect the total:

  • Statutory surcharge: a surcharge is added to each penalty, so the amount you pay can be higher than the base fine alone.
  • Work zones: doubled fines apply in work zones. If a stop sign was in or near a posted work zone, ask the court whether the doubled amount applies.

Read your citation closely. It should show the violation, the court, and instructions for paying or contesting. If something is unclear, the court clerk's office can explain the process, though clerks cannot give legal advice.

How many points does a stop sign ticket carry in Colorado?

The Colorado points data we have sourced describes a system of 1–6 points by mph over, with 12 points in 12 months (adult) = suspension. That scale is tied to speed over the limit, so it does not map neatly onto a stop sign violation, which has no mph-over figure.

We have not sourced a specific point value for a stop sign violation, and we will not guess one. Whether points are assessed, and how many, is something to confirm with the Colorado Division of Motor Vehicles or in the traffic code.

Why points matter: the 12-points-in-12-months threshold applies to adults. Points from several violations add up, so a single ticket may matter more if you already have others on your record.

Your driving record is held by the DMV. Asking the DMV for a copy shows what is currently recorded against you.

Does a stop sign ticket raise insurance rates?

It can. Nationally, moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Colorado-specific quote.

Your own change could be higher, lower, or nothing at all. It depends on your insurer, your driving history, and whether the ticket ends in a conviction.

  • Insurers generally look at convictions on your record, not just the citation.
  • Rules on how long a violation affects pricing differ by company.
  • A clean record beforehand may soften the effect, though we cannot promise that.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend insurers. To learn how a ticket might affect your policy, ask your own insurer or agent before deciding how to resolve the citation.

Can a stop sign ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Often there are options, but the choices depend on the court and your circumstances. Common paths include paying the ticket, contesting it in court, or asking the court whether any alternative resolution is available.

  1. Pay it. This usually resolves the case but may be treated as an admission, with the fine and surcharge due and possible record consequences.
  2. Contest it. You can ask for a hearing and present your side. Bring any evidence, such as photos of the intersection or signage.
  3. Ask about alternatives. Some courts offer other resolutions. Whether yours does is a question for the court clerk.

We cannot tell you which option fits your situation, and this page is not legal advice. If the outcome matters for your license or job, consider speaking with a licensed attorney of your own choosing.

What happens if I don't pay or respond?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts set deadlines, and missing them can lead to added penalties or action on your driving privileges. The specifics depend on the court and the state's traffic code.

Do not rely on a ticket going away by itself. If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court clerk before the deadline and ask what is available.

If you are close to the 12-points-in-12-months suspension threshold for adults, an unresolved or mishandled ticket matters more. Check your record with the Colorado Division of Motor Vehicles if you are unsure where you stand.
