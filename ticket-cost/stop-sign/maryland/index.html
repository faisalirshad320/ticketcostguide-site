How much is a stop sign ticket in Maryland?

Nationally, stop sign tickets run $50–$500 depending on the state. In Maryland the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Maryland Motor Vehicle Administration).

That means the single most reliable number is the one printed on your own ticket. We do not publish a flat Maryland dollar amount because the amount is tied to the citation and the court handling it.

If you decide to request a court date, the prepay amount no longer applies. A judge sets the fine instead, and it can be up to $500.

The $50–$500 range is a national figure. It is not a Maryland price list, and your Maryland citation may differ from it.

How is the fine for a stop sign violation set in Maryland?

The fine is set by the court within the limits of the state's traffic code. The code itself is published online, and you can read Maryland statutes through Justia.

There are two practical paths:

  • Prepay the citation. You pay the amount shown on the ticket and the matter is handled without a hearing.
  • Request a court date. The prepay amount is replaced by a judge-set fine of up to $500.

Because the court sets the amount, it can vary by court. We do not list county-by-county figures, since none are in our sourced data.

Read the citation closely. It should show the charge, the court and the instructions for paying or asking for a hearing.

How many points does a stop sign ticket add in Maryland?

Maryland uses a points system administered by the Motor Vehicle Administration. The tiers we have sourced are: 1 point (9 or less over), 2 points (10–29 over) and 5 points (30+ over).

Those tiers are written in speed terms, so they describe over-the-limit violations. We do not have a sourced point value that we can state for a stop sign violation specifically.

To find the points tied to your charge, check your citation and the Maryland Motor Vehicle Administration.

The thresholds matter once points add up. At 8 points the state's system lists a suspension, and at 12 points a revocation.

Points are counted across all your violations, so a stop sign ticket matters more if you already have others on your record.

Does a stop sign ticket raise my insurance in Maryland?

It can. Nationally, moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Maryland-specific one.

A stop sign violation is generally treated as a moving violation, so the same pattern may apply. How much your own premium changes depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the ticket is resolved.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend insurers or predict your rate. If you are worried about the effect, ask your insurer how it treats a conviction before you decide how to respond to the citation.

Paying the ticket is generally treated as accepting the charge. Think about that before choosing the quickest option.

Can a stop sign ticket be dismissed or reduced in Maryland?

Requesting a court date is the route that puts the charge in front of a judge. At that point a judge sets the fine, up to $500, rather than you paying the prepay amount.

Outcomes depend on the facts, the court and the evidence. We cannot say how likely a dismissal or reduction is, and we do not give legal advice.

Things worth doing before you decide:

  1. Read the citation for the charge, the court and the response instructions.
  2. Compare the prepay amount with the possible judge-set fine of up to $500.
  3. Check your current point total with the Maryland Motor Vehicle Administration.
  4. If you want legal advice, consult a licensed attorney of your own choosing.

The trade-off is that going to court replaces a known prepay amount with a judge's decision.

What happens if I don't pay or respond to the ticket?

Ignoring a citation rarely makes it go away. Our sourced data does not list exact late penalties for Maryland, so we will not guess at them.

What we can say is that points feed into the state's thresholds: 8 points = suspension and 12 points = revocation. Unresolved or added violations can make those totals harder to manage.

The citation and the Maryland Motor Vehicle Administration are the places to confirm what happens next for your case. If the deadline on your ticket is close, deal with it first.

Choose to pay or to request a court date before the deadline on the citation. Missing it removes both choices.
