How much is a stop sign ticket in Mississippi?

Nationally, a stop sign ticket costs $50–$500 depending on the state. In Mississippi the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Mississippi Department of Public Safety).

This page does not give a single Mississippi dollar figure, because we cannot source one that applies to every court. The $50–$500 range is a national spread. It is not a promise about what a Mississippi court will charge.

The number that matters is the one on your citation or in the court's records. Read it before you pay or respond.

Remember that the fine is only part of the bill. Mandatory state assessments are added to every fine and often exceed it, so the amount you owe can be noticeably larger than the fine alone.

How is the fine for a stop sign violation set in Mississippi?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The officer writes the citation, but the court decides the amount. You can browse the state's statutes through Justia's Mississippi code.

Because the court sets the amount, two drivers cited for the same stop sign violation in different places could see different totals. That is why we do not publish a flat Mississippi number.

  • Start with the citation. It normally shows the charge and how to respond.
  • Contact the court clerk. The clerk can tell you the amount due and the accepted ways to pay.
  • Check the traffic code. The statutes explain the offense and the range the court works within.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm. We cannot tell you how a particular judge will rule or what your case is worth.

What court costs and surcharges are added to the fine?

Mandatory state assessments are added to every fine in Mississippi, and they often exceed the fine itself. This is the most common reason the total on a ticket surprises people.

We do not list individual assessment amounts, because we cannot source them from the figures we hold. Treat any total you see as the fine plus these assessments.

  • Ask whether the amount quoted is the fine alone or the full amount due.
  • Ask which payment methods the court accepts and whether they add fees.
  • Keep a receipt or confirmation once you pay.

If you are comparing a Mississippi ticket with one in another state, compare the full total, not just the fine line. The national $50–$500 range describes fines and does not account for the assessments added in each state.

How many points is a stop sign ticket in Mississippi?

Mississippi has no point system. A stop sign ticket does not add points to your license the way it would in a point-based state.

That does not mean a conviction has no licensing effect. Suspensions follow repeat convictions and specific offenses, so a record of several moving violations can matter even without points.

No points does not mean no record. A conviction can still appear on your driving history and count toward repeat-conviction rules.

For current licensing rules and your own driving record, contact the Mississippi Department of Public Safety. For a comparison of how states handle traffic laws, the Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a state-laws resource.

Does a stop sign ticket raise insurance in Mississippi?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Mississippi-specific one.

Whether your own rate changes depends on your insurer, your driving history, and whether the ticket ends in a conviction. We do not recommend or compare insurers, and we cannot predict your quote.

  • Ask your insurer how it treats a stop sign conviction before you decide how to respond.
  • Ask the court clerk what outcome your case would be recorded as.
  • Remember that an increase, if any, usually lasts longer than the one-time cost of the fine.

Over time, the premium effect can add up to more than the ticket. That is worth weighing when you decide how to handle the citation.

Can a stop sign ticket be dismissed or reduced in Mississippi?

Sometimes a ticket can be contested, and some courts offer other outcomes. We cannot say what your court offers or whether you qualify, because that is set locally and depends on the facts.

The usual choices are:

  1. Pay the ticket. This normally resolves the matter quickly, but it is typically treated as accepting the violation.
  2. Contest it. You can ask the court how to request a hearing and what to bring.
  3. Ask about alternatives. Ask the clerk whether the court offers any option that changes how the ticket is recorded.

Paying and contesting lead to different outcomes for your record and possibly your insurance. Read the citation carefully, because it should explain how to respond.

What happens if I don't pay a stop sign ticket in Mississippi?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes the problem worse. The court can treat a missed response or unpaid fine as a separate issue, and the consequences can include further action against your license or additional costs.

Since Mississippi suspensions follow repeat convictions and specific offenses, an unresolved ticket is not something to leave sitting. Check the response instructions on the citation and contact the court before any deadline listed there.

  • Do not rely on a ticket being lost or forgotten.
  • If you cannot pay the full amount, ask the clerk what options exist.
  • Confirm in writing or by receipt that the matter is closed.

The Mississippi Department of Public Safety is the place to check the status of your license.
