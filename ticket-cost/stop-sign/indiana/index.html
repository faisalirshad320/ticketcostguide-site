How much is a stop sign ticket in Indiana?

Nationally the typical range is $50–$500 depending on the state. In Indiana the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Indiana Bureau of Motor Vehicles).

We do not publish a single Indiana dollar figure because none exists that fits every driver. The number on your ticket or court notice is the one that counts.

The national range is a comparison point only. It blends many states with very different fine schedules, so your Indiana total could land anywhere the court's rules allow.

Read the citation closely. The amount, the court name and any appearance instructions are printed on it or on the notice that follows.

How is the fine for running a stop sign set in Indiana?

The fine is not a flat statewide price tag. The court sets the amount on the citation, within the state's traffic code. You can read the code through Indiana statutes via Justia.

Several things can shape what the court puts on your citation:

  • The court that handles your case, which depends on where the stop took place.
  • The exact violation written on the ticket.
  • Whether the ticket lists a court appearance or allows payment without one.

Different officers and courts can describe the same stop differently. That is one reason two drivers can see different totals for what sounds like the same offense.

Why can court costs matter as much as the fine?

County court costs are added to the judgment and often exceed the fine for low speeds. That statement comes from our state context data on Indiana traffic cases, and it shows how much the add-ons can matter on a minor ticket.

Because costs are set at the county level, the same fine can lead to different totals in different counties. We do not list a dollar amount for these costs, since it varies by county and court.

When you compare your options, compare the full amount due, not just the fine line. Ask the clerk for the total owed if the notice does not show it.

  • Fine: set by the court on the citation.
  • County court costs: added to the judgment.
  • Total: the sum the clerk tells you is due.

How many points does a stop sign ticket carry in Indiana?

Our locked Indiana data describes the points system as 2–8 points by mph over. That scale is written around speed, so it does not map neatly onto a stop sign violation, where no speed is measured.

We therefore do not state a specific point value for a stop sign ticket. Check your BMV driving record or ask the court clerk what, if anything, will be assessed after a conviction.

What we can say is the consequence of accumulation: points trigger BMV probation and suspension. A ticket that looks minor can matter more if your record already has other entries.

The Indiana Bureau of Motor Vehicles is the place to confirm your current record and how it treats this violation.

Does a stop sign ticket raise insurance in Indiana?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums ~20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Indiana-specific one.

Your own change could be smaller, larger or nothing at all. It depends on your insurer, your history and whether the ticket ends in a conviction.

Insurers usually check driving records at renewal, so the effect often shows up later rather than right away. We do not recommend any insurer, and we cannot predict what yours will do.

  • Ask your insurer how it treats a conviction like this one.
  • Find out how the case will be recorded before you decide how to resolve it.

Can a stop sign ticket be dismissed or reduced in Indiana?

Sometimes, but the outcome depends on the court and the facts. We do not have locked data on specific programs or dismissal rates, so we will not promise any.

The usual routes are:

  • Pay the ticket. This generally resolves the case, but a conviction can affect your record and insurance.
  • Contest it. You can ask for a hearing and present your side. The court decides.
  • Ask the clerk about alternatives. Some courts may offer other ways to resolve a minor ticket. Ask what your court allows.

Because paying is usually treated as accepting the violation, think about the points and insurance effects first. If the stakes feel high, a licensed Indiana attorney can give you legal advice. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not recommend any attorney.

What happens if I don't pay a stop sign ticket in Indiana?

Ignoring a ticket rarely makes it go away. The court can add to the amount owed and take further action, and your driving privileges can be affected. We have no locked figures on specific penalties, so check your notice for the court's terms.

Points matter here too. Accumulating points triggers BMV probation and suspension, so a ticket left unresolved can compound an existing record.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court clerk before any deadline passes. Courts often have more flexibility when you reach out early.

Do not rely on a single notice. Confirm deadlines and the amount due directly with the court named on your citation.
