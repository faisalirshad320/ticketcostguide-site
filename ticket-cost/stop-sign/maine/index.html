How much is a stop sign ticket in Maine?

Nationally, a stop sign ticket runs $50–$500 depending on the state. In Maine, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Maine Bureau of Motor Vehicles).

This page does not give a single Maine dollar figure. The national range is a national range, and it should not be read as Maine's fine.

The amount you owe is the one printed on your citation or stated by the court. If the paperwork lists a number, that number controls, not an estimate from a website.

Bottom line: the $50–$500 range is national. For Maine, read the amount on your citation.

How is a stop sign fine set in Maine?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. Maine's statutes are published online (Maine statutes via Justia).

That means the figure comes from the law and the court's schedule, not from the officer's discretion. The officer writes the citation. The court determines what the violation costs.

Statutory surcharges are built into the scheduled amounts. In practice, the total on your citation should already include those add-ons, so you should not expect to add them yourself.

If the total looks different from what you expected, compare it with the citation itself. Ask the court clerk to explain how the amount was reached.

How many points does a stop sign ticket add in Maine?

Demerit points accrue per conviction. A stop sign ticket that ends in a conviction can therefore add to your record, while a ticket that is dismissed generally does not.

The locked state facts for this page set one threshold: 12 points in 12 months = suspension. They do not give a point value for a stop sign violation, so this page does not state one.

To see how many points apply to you and what your record currently shows, check with the Maine Bureau of Motor Vehicles (Maine Bureau of Motor Vehicles).

  • Points are tied to convictions, not to the act of being stopped.
  • The 12-in-12 threshold counts points within a 12-month window.
  • Points from several violations add together.

Does a stop sign ticket raise insurance in Maine?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums ~20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Maine-specific one.

Your own change could be higher, lower or zero. It depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the violation is recorded.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend insurers or predict what a particular company will do. If you are worried about a rate change, ask your insurer how it treats a conviction like this one.

Rule of thumb: the fine is a one-time cost, while a rate increase can last for as long as the conviction counts against you with your insurer.

That is one reason the outcome of the ticket can matter more than the fine itself.

Can a stop sign ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome depends on the facts and on the court. This page cannot predict how a Maine court will rule on your citation.

Generally, a driver has a few paths:

  • Pay the amount. This usually resolves the ticket, and it generally results in a conviction, which is when points accrue.
  • Contest it. You can ask the court for a hearing and present your side. The citation should explain how to do this and by when.
  • Ask the court about alternatives. Some courts offer options such as payment arrangements or other resolutions. Whether Maine's court offers one for your ticket is something to confirm with the clerk.

Deadlines matter. The dates on the citation are the ones to follow.

If you want advice on your specific situation, you may wish to speak with a licensed attorney. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice.

What happens if I don't pay a stop sign ticket in Maine?

Ignoring a citation tends to make things worse. Courts commonly add consequences when a ticket is neither paid nor contested, and those can affect your license.

The exact steps Maine takes are set by the state's traffic code and the court handling your case (Maine statutes via Justia). This page does not list specific penalties because they are not part of the locked facts.

Two practical points:

  • Read the response deadline on the citation as soon as you get it.
  • If you cannot meet it, contact the court before it passes rather than after.

A license suspension can also come from point totals. The locked threshold is 12 points in 12 months = suspension, so an unpaid or unresolved ticket on top of other convictions can compound the problem.

What should I do after getting a stop sign ticket?

Start with the citation. Find the amount, the court, the response deadline and the instructions for paying or contesting.

  1. Note the date you must respond by.
  2. Compare the amount with what the court lists for the violation.
  3. Decide whether to pay or contest, knowing that a conviction is what adds points.
  4. Check your driving record with the Maine Bureau of Motor Vehicles if you are unsure how many points you have (Maine Bureau of Motor Vehicles).
  5. Keep copies of everything you send or receive.

Weigh the fine against the possible insurance effect. The national figure of ~20% on average for moving-violation convictions is a reminder that the total cost can exceed the fine.
