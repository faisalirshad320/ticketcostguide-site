How much is a stop sign ticket in Wyoming?

Nationally, a stop sign ticket costs $50–$500 depending on the state. In Wyoming the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Wyoming Department of Transportation).

That national range is a spread across all states. It is not a Wyoming price, and we do not publish a single Wyoming dollar figure because the amount is decided by the court handling your case.

Court fees are added per the local schedule. The total you pay can therefore be higher than the fine alone.

The most reliable number is the one on your citation or the one the court clerk quotes. Use it instead of any estimate you find online, including ours.

How is the fine for a stop sign violation set in Wyoming?

The exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. Wyoming statutes are published online through Wyoming statutes via Justia, which is a useful place to read the traffic code itself.

In practice, that means the officer's citation, the court that receives it and the local schedule all matter. Two drivers cited for the same violation in different places could be quoted different totals.

We cannot tell you the figure for your case. We can tell you where it comes from: the court, working within the traffic code, and not a number set by TicketCostGuide or by a private company.

  • Read the citation for the stated amount or instructions.
  • Check whether the court lists a payment schedule.
  • Ask the clerk whether the quoted figure includes court fees.

Are court fees added on top of the fine?

Yes. Court fees are added per the local schedule. This is the part of the bill that most often surprises people, because the fine and the fees are not always shown as one number.

Because the schedule is local, we do not list a fee amount. Any figure we gave would be a guess, and a wrong guess could lead you to underpay or overpay.

When you contact the court, ask for the total due, not just the fine. That is the amount that closes the case if you pay it.

Fine and fees are two separate questions. Ask the clerk for the full amount before you send payment.

How many points does a stop sign ticket carry in Wyoming?

Wyoming has no demerit-point system. A stop sign ticket therefore does not add points to your record in the way it would in many other states.

That does not mean the ticket is harmless. Suspensions follow repeat convictions and court action, so a conviction can still count toward a pattern.

If you want to see how the state handles licensing and records, the Wyoming Department of Transportation is the agency to check. For a comparison with other states, Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws collects state traffic-safety laws in one place.

  • No points are assigned because there is no point system.
  • Repeat convictions are what can lead to a suspension.
  • Court action can also lead to a suspension.

Does a stop sign ticket raise insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. This is a national figure, not a Wyoming one.

Your own change could be higher, lower or none at all. It depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the conviction appears on your record.

We do not recommend insurers or tell you which one to use. If you are worried about the effect, the plain step is to ask your own insurer how it treats a conviction like this one.

The ~20% is a national average for moving-violation convictions. Do not treat it as a prediction for your policy.

Can a stop sign ticket be dismissed or reduced?

That decision belongs to the court. Because the amount is set by the court on the citation, the court is also where any change to the outcome would be handled.

Your general options are to pay, to contest the citation, or to ask the court what it allows. We cannot say which fits your case, and this page is not legal advice.

If you want advice about your specific situation, you can speak with a licensed attorney of your own choosing. TicketCostGuide does not recommend a particular attorney.

  1. Read the citation and note any court instructions.
  2. Contact the court listed on it.
  3. Ask what options and total amount apply to you.
  4. Decide whether to pay, contest or seek advice.

What happens if I don't pay a Wyoming stop sign ticket?

Ignoring a citation is usually the worst choice. In Wyoming, suspensions follow repeat convictions and court action, so an unresolved ticket can turn into a larger problem.

We do not state deadlines or penalty amounts because they are not part of the figures we can verify. The citation and the court are the right places to find them.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court before anything is due. Courts can tell you what they accept, and asking early is better than staying silent.
