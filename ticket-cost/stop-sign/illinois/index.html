How much is a stop sign ticket in Illinois?

Nationally, stop sign tickets fall in a range of $50–$500 depending on the state. In Illinois, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Illinois Secretary of State).

We do not publish a single Illinois dollar figure for this violation, because the amount is not a flat number you can look up in advance. The number printed on your ticket, or confirmed by the court, is the one that counts.

The $50–$500 range is a national spread across states. It is not an Illinois price list, and your Illinois ticket could be priced differently from anything in that range.

If the citation does not show an amount, the clerk of the court named on the ticket is the right place to ask.

How is the fine for a stop sign violation set in Illinois?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The traffic code sets the framework, and the court handling your case decides the amount that appears on your citation.

That is why two drivers with the same violation can see different numbers depending on the court. Always rely on your own citation or the court clerk, not a general estimate.

Statutes can be read in full through Illinois statutes via Justia. Some violations carry a fixed statutory minimum. For example, work-zone speeding carries a statutory minimum fine of $375 for a first offense. That is a different violation, and we do not apply it to stop signs.

  • National range: $50–$500 depending on the state.
  • Illinois: set by the court on the citation.
  • Court-specific costs or surcharges: confirm with the clerk, since we do not list unverified amounts.

How many points does a stop sign ticket carry in Illinois?

Illinois records convictions on the Secretary of State's scale, where convictions carry 5–50 point values (Illinois Secretary of State). That range covers many types of violations, from minor to serious.

We do not state a specific point value for a stop sign conviction, because the figures we verified give only the overall 5–50 range. Ask the Secretary of State or the court how your particular conviction will be recorded.

Repeat convictions within 12 months trigger suspension. Even if one ticket seems minor, a second conviction in that window can put your license at risk.

Points depend on a conviction. A ticket that is not a conviction, such as one that is dismissed, is treated differently from one that is. Ask the court which outcomes would count.

Does a stop sign ticket raise insurance rates in Illinois?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Illinois-specific one.

Whether your own rate changes depends on your insurer, your driving record and whether the ticket becomes a conviction. We do not recommend any insurer, and we cannot predict what yours will do.

  • The national average increase is about 20%.
  • Your actual change may be higher, lower or zero.
  • Your insurer can tell you how it treats a conviction, and when it reviews your record.

Because the possible effect on premiums is tied to a conviction, the outcome of your case matters as much as the fine itself. A modest fine can still cost more over time if your premium goes up.

Can a stop sign ticket be dismissed or reduced in Illinois?

That decision belongs to the court. The citation lists the court and the steps you have, which usually include paying, or contesting the ticket at a hearing.

Paying is generally treated as accepting responsibility, and that may lead to a conviction on your record. Contesting means appearing in court or following the court's instructions to do so.

Before you choose, consider these questions:

  1. What exact amount does the citation or court list?
  2. Would payment result in a conviction, and how would it be recorded?
  3. Does the court offer any alternative outcome for your situation?
  4. Have you had another conviction within the past 12 months?

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot give legal advice or recommend an attorney. If the outcome matters to your license or insurance, you may want to consult a licensed attorney of your own choosing.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the ticket?

Ignoring a citation rarely makes it go away. The court can add consequences when a deadline is missed, and those can affect your driving privileges.

We have not verified specific penalty amounts for late payment in Illinois, so we do not list any. The citation and the court clerk are the reliable sources for deadlines and what follows a missed date.

If you cannot pay or cannot attend on the listed date, contact the court before the deadline. Asking early is usually better than waiting.

Remember also that repeat convictions within 12 months trigger suspension. A missed ticket that turns into a conviction counts alongside any other recent one.

How does the Illinois ticket compare with the national range?

Nationally, a stop sign ticket costs $50–$500 depending on the state. That wide gap reflects how differently states and courts set fines.

For Illinois, the court sets the amount on the citation. So the national range is useful context, but it is not a prediction of your bill.

It is also worth separating the fine from other costs. The fine is only one part of the total. Points on your record and a possible rise in insurance premiums, about 20% on average nationally for a moving-violation conviction, can add to the real cost.
