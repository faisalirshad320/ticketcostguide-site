How much is a stop sign ticket in Texas?

Nationally the range is $50–$500 depending on the state. In Texas, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Texas Department of Public Safety).

We do not publish a single statewide Texas dollar figure for this violation, because the amount you owe depends on the court handling your ticket. The national range is a national figure, not a Texas price.

The most reliable number is the one printed on your citation or shown by the court that issued it. If the paperwork shows no amount, contact the court clerk listed on the ticket.

Expect the total to include more than the base fine. State court costs are uniform, while municipal add-ons vary by court.

How is a Texas stop sign fine set?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The statutes are collected in the Texas statutes via Justia if you want to read the text yourself.

Two parts make up what you pay:

  • The fine: chosen by the court that handles your case, within what the traffic code allows.
  • Court costs and add-ons: state court costs are uniform across Texas, but municipal add-ons vary by court.

This is why two drivers cited for the same stop sign violation in different cities can see different totals. Check the court named on your citation rather than relying on a general average.

The same violation can also be written up differently depending on the circumstances of the stop. The citation lists the charge, so confirm it matches what the officer described.

How many points does a stop sign ticket carry in Texas?

Texas abolished its driver-point and surcharge program in 2019. License action now follows specific offenses and repeat-conviction rules.

In practice, that means a stop sign conviction is not tracked through a point total the way it is in many other states. We do not state a point value for this violation in Texas.

No points does not mean no consequences. A conviction can still appear on your driving record, and repeated convictions can matter under the state's repeat-conviction rules.

For the current rules on license status and record requests, see the Texas Department of Public Safety.

Does a stop sign ticket raise insurance in Texas?

It can. Nationally, moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Texas-specific one.

Whether your own rate changes depends on your insurer, your record and how the ticket is resolved. A stop sign violation is a moving violation, so a conviction is the kind of event insurers look at.

  • Ask your insurer how it treats a single minor moving violation.
  • Ask whether the outcome of the case, such as a conviction or a different disposition, changes how it is rated.
  • Compare the possible premium increase with the cost of the ticket before deciding how to respond.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any specific insurer and cannot predict your quote.

Can a Texas stop sign ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the options depend on the court and on your record. Your citation and the court clerk are the best places to see which responses that court accepts.

Common routes people ask about include:

  • Paying the ticket: this usually resolves the case quickly, but a payment is typically treated as a conviction.
  • Contesting the ticket: you can ask for a hearing and present your side to the court.
  • Asking about alternatives: some courts offer programs or other dispositions to eligible drivers. Eligibility and fees vary by court, so ask the clerk.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice. If the outcome matters for your license or job, consider speaking with a licensed Texas attorney of your own choosing.

What happens if I don't pay a stop sign ticket in Texas?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts set deadlines, and missing them can lead to additional penalties or further action on your license or case.

We do not give specific deadlines or added-fee amounts here, because they are set by the court and the traffic code. The date and instructions on your citation control.

  • Read the response date on the citation as soon as you get it.
  • Contact the court before the date if you need more time or have questions.
  • Keep a record of any payment or filing.

License action in Texas now follows specific offenses and repeat-conviction rules, so an unresolved ticket is worth settling early. Check Texas Department of Public Safety for current license information.
