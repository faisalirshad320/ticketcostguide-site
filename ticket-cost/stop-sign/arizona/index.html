How much is a stop sign ticket in Arizona?

Nationally, a stop sign ticket costs $50–$500 depending on the state (Arizona Department of Transportation — Motor Vehicle Division). In Arizona, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation.

We do not print a single Arizona dollar amount on this page. The figure depends on the court that handles your ticket, and the number written on your citation is the one that counts.

The $50–$500 range is a national range across states. It is not an Arizona price list and not a prediction for your ticket.

The total you owe can include more than the base fine. Court surcharges and fees are added by the justice or municipal court, and those amounts vary by court.

How is the fine for running a stop sign set in Arizona?

The exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. Arizona statutes are available through Arizona statutes via Justia if you want to read the code itself.

Two people cited for the same violation in different places can see different totals. That is because the court, not a statewide flat rate, sets the amount, and each court adds its own surcharges and fees.

What to look for on your citation:

  • The fine amount the court has listed.
  • The name of the justice or municipal court that will handle the case.
  • The response or appearance deadline.
  • Any instructions on paying, contesting or asking for a hearing.

If anything is unclear, the court clerk named on the citation is the best place to confirm the total. Clerks can explain how to pay or respond, though they cannot give legal advice.

What fees and surcharges get added to the fine?

Court surcharges and fees are added by the justice or municipal court. The amounts vary by court, so we do not list a fixed number.

This is why the total on a ticket can look higher than the fine you expected. The base fine and the added charges are separate pieces of the same bill.

  • Base fine: set by the court within the state's traffic code.
  • Surcharges and fees: added by the court that handles the ticket.
  • Other costs: any later cost, such as a class you choose or are ordered to take, is separate and set by whoever runs it.

Before you pay, ask the court for the full amount due, not just the base fine.

How many points does a stop sign ticket carry in Arizona?

Our locked Arizona facts give one example of the point scale: 3 points for speeding. They do not give a point value for a stop sign violation, so we do not state one here.

To find out how your conviction would be scored, check with the Arizona Department of Transportation — Motor Vehicle Division or the court handling your case.

Threshold to know: 8+ points in 12 months = suspension or Traffic Survival School.

Points matter most if you already have other violations on your record. Several smaller tickets within a year can add up toward that 8-point threshold.

Ask the court or MVD how a conviction, or a completed course if one is offered, would be recorded on your driving record.

Does a stop sign ticket raise your insurance?

It can. As a national average, moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20%. That is a national figure, not an Arizona-specific one.

Your own change could be higher, lower or zero. It depends on your insurer, your driving history and whether the ticket ends in a conviction.

  • Ask your insurer how it treats a moving violation at your next renewal.
  • Keep in mind that the outcome of the case, not just the ticket, is what usually matters.
  • Compare the cost of the fine with a possible premium increase over several years.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer and cannot predict your rate.

Can a stop sign ticket be dismissed or reduced in Arizona?

Generally you have a few paths: pay the ticket, contest it, or ask the court about other resolutions it may offer. Which of these are available depends on the court and the details of your citation.

Paying usually means accepting responsibility for the violation. That can bring the fine and any points, and it may be reported to your insurer.

Contesting means asking for a hearing. Read the citation for the deadline and the steps, and ask the court clerk how to request one.

Some courts may offer a course or similar option. Whether that is available to you, and what it does to your record, is something to ask the court directly.

What happens if I don't pay or respond to the ticket?

Ignoring a ticket rarely makes it go away. Courts set deadlines, and missing one can lead to added consequences that depend on the court and the case.

Those consequences can include additional fees and trouble with your driving record. Arizona also has a suspension threshold of 8+ points in 12 months, which is a separate matter from unpaid tickets but shows how the state treats repeat problems.

  • Find the deadline on your citation as soon as you get it.
  • If you cannot pay in full, contact the court clerk before the deadline and ask what is possible.
  • Keep a record of anything you pay or file.

Contacting the court early is almost always better than waiting.
