How much is a stop sign ticket in Kansas?

Nationally, a stop sign ticket runs $50–$500 depending on the state. In Kansas, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Kansas Department of Revenue — Division of Vehicles).

That national range is not a Kansas price. We do not state a Kansas dollar figure because the amount you owe is the one printed on your ticket or assigned by the court handling it.

The fine is also not necessarily the whole bill. Court costs are added by the municipal or district court, so the total can be higher than the fine line alone.

Quick check: look at your citation for the fine, any court costs, the name of the court and the response instructions. Those four items tell you what you actually owe.

How is the fine for running a stop sign set in Kansas?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. There is no single number we can quote for every driver in the state.

Two things shape what you pay. The first is the court that hears the ticket. The second is the costs that court adds on top of the fine.

Court costs are added by the municipal or district court. Which court that is depends on where the stop happened and who issued the citation, and the ticket names it.

If two drivers get the same violation in different places, their totals may differ. For that reason, treat any single "average Kansas stop sign fine" you see elsewhere with caution unless it cites a court schedule.

You can read the traffic code itself through Kansas statutes via Justia. It sets the framework, but the citation shows the amount.

How many points does a stop sign ticket carry in Kansas?

Kansas has no demerit-point system. A stop sign ticket therefore does not add a set number of points to your license.

That does not mean the ticket is harmless. Three moving-violation convictions within 12 months can trigger suspension (Kansas Department of Revenue — Division of Vehicles).

Running a stop sign is generally treated as a moving violation, but confirm how your specific citation is classified with the court or the Division of Vehicles.

  • No point total to track, but convictions are what count.
  • The 12-month window matters if you already have other recent tickets.
  • A dismissed ticket is not a conviction, which is one reason drivers look at their options before paying.

Does a stop sign ticket raise insurance in Kansas?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Kansas-specific one.

Your own change could be higher, lower or nothing at all. It depends on your insurer, your history and how long ago the conviction was.

We do not recommend any insurer or tell you whether to shop around. If the cost matters, ask your insurer how it treats a conviction for a stop sign violation, and when it reviews your record at renewal.

A 20% increase on a premium can add up over several renewals. That can outweigh the fine itself, so consider the full cost of a conviction, not just the amount on the ticket.

Can a stop sign ticket be dismissed or reduced in Kansas?

Sometimes courts reduce or dismiss citations, but we cannot predict whether yours qualifies. We are not a law firm and do not give legal advice.

In general, the usual paths on a traffic citation are:

  • Pay it. This typically resolves the ticket, but paying is generally treated as a conviction.
  • Contest it. You ask the court to hear the case instead of paying.
  • Ask the clerk. The court clerk can tell you what the court allows, what the deadlines are and how to respond.

Since a conviction can count toward the three-in-12-months suspension trigger, check your recent record before deciding.

If you want advice on a specific case, a licensed Kansas attorney is the right person to ask. We do not recommend a particular one.

What happens if I don't pay a stop sign ticket in Kansas?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. The court that issued it controls the next steps, and the consequences can differ by court.

Typical risks of not responding include additional court action and possible effects on your driving privileges. We do not give exact deadlines or penalty amounts because those come from the citation and the court.

  1. Read the response instructions on the ticket.
  2. Contact the court named on the citation before the deadline passes.
  3. Ask whether you can pay, contest or request more time.
  4. Keep proof of whatever you do.

For license status questions, the Kansas Department of Revenue — Division of Vehicles is the state agency to contact.
