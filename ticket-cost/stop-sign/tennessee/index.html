How much is a stop sign ticket in Tennessee?

There is no single posted price. Nationally, stop sign tickets run $50–$500 depending on the state, and in Tennessee the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Tennessee Department of Safety and Homeland Security).

We do not publish an exact Tennessee dollar figure, because the total depends on the court handling your case. The national range is a benchmark only. It is not a Tennessee fine.

The most reliable number is the one on your own citation or the one the court clerk gives you for your case.

Two drivers who run the same stop sign in different counties can end up with different totals. That is because of how the total is built, which the next section explains.

How is the fine set, and why does the total differ by county?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The traffic code is published in the Tennessee statutes, which you can browse through Justia.

The fine is only part of what you pay. County court costs and state litigation taxes make up most of the total. Those add-ons are why a ticket total can look larger than the fine alone.

Because court costs are county-level, the same offense can cost different amounts depending on where you were cited. We give no exact county figures, since those are not part of the figures we can verify.

  • Fine: set by the court on the citation.
  • County court costs: vary by county and court.
  • State litigation taxes: added on top, and part of the bulk of the total.

When you look at your paperwork, check whether the amount shown is the fine alone or the full amount due.

How many points does a stop sign ticket add in Tennessee?

Tennessee's points system runs from 1–8 points, and the published scale is tied to mph over the limit. Examples are 3 points at 6–15 over and 4 points at 16–25 over.

A stop sign violation does not involve a speed measurement, so that scale does not map neatly onto it. We do not state a point value for a stop sign violation, because we have no verified figure for it.

To find out how your violation is scored, check the Tennessee Department of Safety and Homeland Security (Tennessee Department of Safety and Homeland Security) or ask the court clerk.

Suspension threshold: 12 points in 12 months = suspension notice. Any points from a stop sign ticket would count alongside points from other violations in that window.

If you already have other violations on your record, ask how a new conviction would change your total before you decide how to respond.

Does a stop sign ticket raise my insurance?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Tennessee-specific one.

Whether your rate changes depends on your insurer, your driving history, and whether the ticket ends in a conviction. We do not recommend or compare insurers, and we cannot predict your rate.

A practical way to think about the cost is to add the possible premium increase to the ticket total. A smaller fine can still be expensive if it leads to a higher premium.

  • Ask your insurer how it treats a conviction versus a case that did not end in a conviction.
  • Ask the court what the outcome would be recorded as before you agree to anything.

Can a stop sign ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but we cannot promise an outcome. The options depend on the court, the facts of your stop, and your record.

In general, a driver has these choices:

  • Pay the ticket. This usually resolves the case quickly, but it can mean a conviction on your record.
  • Contest the ticket. You can ask for a hearing and present your side. Evidence such as photos of a hidden or damaged sign may matter.
  • Ask the court about alternatives. Some courts offer other ways to resolve a ticket. Availability varies by county and court, so ask the clerk.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice or recommend attorneys. If the stakes are high, such as many points already on your record, consider getting advice from a licensed professional.

Before paying, read the citation for the court date, the court's contact information and the payment instructions.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts can add costs, and unresolved tickets can lead to licensing problems.

We do not state specific deadlines or penalty amounts, because we have not verified those figures. Your citation lists the court and instructions for responding.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court clerk before the due date. Many courts have their own processes for questions like this. They vary by county.

If points are added to your record, remember that 12 points in 12 months triggers a suspension notice. Leaving a ticket unresolved can complicate that picture.

What should I check on my citation before deciding?

A short checklist can save money and confusion:

  1. Find the amount due and whether it includes county court costs and state litigation taxes.
  2. Confirm which court is handling the case.
  3. Check the response instructions on the citation.
  4. Ask how a conviction would be recorded and whether points apply.
  5. Think about the insurance effect. The national average increase after a moving-violation conviction is about 20%.

The state's traffic code is available through Justia. A national overview of state traffic laws is available from the Governors Highway Safety Association.

Frequently asked questions

How much is a stop sign ticket in Tennessee?

The exact amount is set by the court on the citation. Nationally, stop sign tickets run $50–$500 depending on the state, but that is a national range, not a Tennessee price. County court costs and state litigation taxes make up most of the total, so check your citation or ask the clerk.

Why does the total change by county in Tennessee?

County court costs and state litigation taxes make up most of the total, and the court costs vary by county and court. The fine itself is set by the court within the state's traffic code. Two drivers cited for the same offense in different counties may see different totals.

How many points is a stop sign ticket in Tennessee?

We do not state a point value for a stop sign violation. The published scale is 1–8 points by mph over, such as 3 points at 6–15 over and 4 at 16–25, which is speed-based. Confirm what applies to your violation with the Department of Safety or the court clerk.

How many points lead to a suspension in Tennessee?

The threshold is 12 points in 12 months, which results in a suspension notice. Points from all violations count toward that total, not just one ticket. If you already have points on your record, ask how a new conviction could affect your total before you respond.

Will a stop sign ticket raise my car insurance?

It might. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average, and that is a national figure. Your actual change depends on your insurer and your driving history. TicketCostGuide does not recommend insurers, so ask your own insurer how it treats a conviction.

Can I fight a stop sign ticket in Tennessee?

You can generally contest a ticket and ask for a hearing, but we cannot promise an outcome or give legal advice. Options and alternatives vary by county and court. Read the response instructions on your citation and ask the clerk what choices are available in your case.
