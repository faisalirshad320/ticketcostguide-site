How much is a stop sign ticket in Minnesota?

The lead figure for this violation is: nationally $50–$500 depending on the state; in Minnesota the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Minnesota Department of Public Safety — Driver and Vehicle Services). The $50–$500 range is a national spread across states. It is not a Minnesota price list.

We do not publish a single Minnesota dollar amount for a stop sign ticket, because the figure on your citation is what counts. The court sets it within the state's traffic code.

Your citation is the best guide to what you owe. Read it for the amount, the court name and the instructions for responding.

A statutory state surcharge is also added to every payable fine. That means the total you pay can be higher than the base fine printed for the violation.

How is a Minnesota stop sign fine set?

In Minnesota, the exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The officer writes the violation on the citation. The court then determines the amount that applies to your case.

This is why two drivers with the same violation can see different paperwork. The court handling the citation and the details of the case both matter.

The state's traffic laws are published in the Minnesota statutes, which you can browse through Minnesota statutes via Justia. The statute lists the rule you are accused of breaking. The amount owed comes from the court and the citation.

Here is what to check on your ticket:

  • The violation description and the date and location of the stop.
  • The amount shown as payable, if any.
  • The court name and how to contact its clerk.
  • Any instructions about paying or contesting the citation.

Are there surcharges or court costs on top of the fine?

Yes. A statutory state surcharge is added to every payable fine in Minnesota. It is part of what you owe when you pay the citation.

We do not give a dollar amount for the surcharge or for any other court cost. If the amount is not clear on your citation, ask the court clerk for the total payable amount before you send money.

When comparing what you owe with the national $50–$500 range, remember that range is national. It should not be read as the Minnesota fine plus surcharge.

How many points does a stop sign ticket add in Minnesota?

Minnesota has no point system. License action follows the record of convictions and specific high-speed offenses.

So there is no number of points to look up for a stop sign ticket. What matters for your license is your record of convictions and whether any high-speed offenses are on it.

If you want to see what is on your driving record, Minnesota Department of Public Safety — Driver and Vehicle Services is the state agency that handles driver licensing. Other states do count points, and the Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws page gives a broader view of how state traffic laws differ.

No points does not mean no consequences. A conviction can still sit on your record and affect your insurance.

Will a stop sign ticket raise my insurance?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Minnesota quote.

Your own change could be higher, lower or zero. It depends on your insurer, your driving history and whether the ticket ends in a conviction on your record.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer. To see how a ticket could affect you, ask your insurer or agent how they treat moving violations at renewal.

Points do not drive this in Minnesota, because the state has no point system. Insurers can still look at your record of convictions.

  • Check whether your policy is up for renewal soon.
  • Ask how a conviction is treated compared with other outcomes.
  • Keep a copy of your citation and the court's final paperwork.

Can a stop sign ticket be dismissed or reduced?

That is a decision for the court and the facts of your case. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot tell you whether your ticket can be dismissed or reduced.

In general, a person who gets a citation can usually do one of two things. They can pay it, or they can respond to the court in the way the citation describes. Paying generally means accepting the violation. Contesting means asking the court to hear the matter.

Because the fine and the surcharge are set by the court and the state, any change in the amount would also come from the court. Ask the clerk what options exist for your citation and what each one means for your record.

Before paying, think about the effect on your record of convictions and your insurance. A fine you pay quickly can still carry a longer-term cost.

If you want advice on your specific situation, speak with a licensed attorney of your own choosing. We do not recommend one.

What happens if I don't pay a stop sign ticket?

Ignoring a citation is rarely a good idea. The citation lists the court and the steps to respond, and missing them can lead to further court action. Minnesota ties license action to the record of convictions and specific high-speed offenses, but an unresolved ticket can still create problems.

We do not list deadlines or late fees here, because those details come from the court and your citation. Contact the court clerk named on the ticket as soon as you can.

  1. Find the court and any response instructions on the citation.
  2. Ask the clerk for the total payable amount, including the state surcharge.
  3. Decide whether to pay or contest, and confirm how to do so.
  4. Keep proof of payment or your court paperwork.
