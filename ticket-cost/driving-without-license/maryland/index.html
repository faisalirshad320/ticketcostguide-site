How much is a driving without a license ticket in Maryland?

Nationally, a first offense for driving without a license commonly runs $50–$500, and it is often chargeable as a misdemeanor. In Maryland the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Maryland Motor Vehicle Administration).

We do not publish a single Maryland dollar figure for this violation. The amount depends on the court and on what the citation says, so the national range is a guide and not a quote.

Read the citation itself first. It shows what you were charged with, whether a prepay amount is listed, and whether a court appearance is required.

How is the fine set in Maryland?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code, not by a fixed price list we can quote. That is why two people charged with the same offense can see different amounts.

There are two paths that matter for cost:

  • Prepaying: the citation may list an amount you can pay without appearing in court. Paying generally resolves the ticket, so confirm what that means for your record before you pay.
  • Requesting a court date: this replaces the prepay amount with a judge-set fine of up to $500.

Because the judge-set fine can be higher or lower than a prepay amount, requesting a court date is a real decision. It is worth weighing before you choose.

The state's traffic code is available through Maryland statutes via Justia. Because the charge itself may be a misdemeanor, the wording of your specific citation matters more than any general number.

How many points does driving without a license add in Maryland?

The Maryland points schedule in our data reads: 1 point (9 or less over), 2 points (10–29 over), 5 points (30+ over). Eight points leads to suspension and 12 points to revocation.

Those tiers are written by how far over a limit a driver was, so they read like a speeding schedule. Our data does not say how many points, if any, a driving without a license charge carries.

Do not assume a number. Check your citation and your driving record with the Maryland Motor Vehicle Administration to see what was actually assessed.

Points still matter because they add up. If you already carry points from other violations, even a small addition can move you closer to the 8-point suspension threshold.

Does driving without a license raise insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Maryland quote.

Whether this charge counts the same way as a speeding conviction depends on the insurer and how the conviction is recorded. We do not recommend any insurer, and we cannot predict what your own company will do.

A few practical points:

  • Premium changes usually show up at renewal, not right away.
  • A misdemeanor conviction may be viewed differently from a minor infraction, so the exact charge matters.
  • If you were uninsured as well as unlicensed, that is a separate issue and may affect your options for coverage.

The 20% figure is useful for budgeting. If your yearly premium is high, the extra cost over time can exceed the fine itself.

Can it be dismissed or reduced?

Outcomes are decided by the court, and we cannot give legal advice or say what will happen in your case. What we can describe is what the process generally allows.

  • Read the charge carefully. Some citations are for never having held a license, others for not having one in your possession. The wording can change how a court treats it.
  • Request a court date. This puts the fine in a judge's hands, up to $500, and gives you a chance to explain your situation.
  • Bring documents. If you hold a valid license, proof of it may matter. Ask the court clerk what is accepted and when.
  • Consider legal advice. Because the charge is often chargeable as a misdemeanor, you may want to speak with a licensed attorney. We do not recommend any specific one.

Do not assume a reduction is likely. Courts have discretion, and results differ by court.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the citation?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. A missed deadline or missed court date can lead to added penalties, a hold on your license or registration, or a warrant, depending on the court.

Since this violation is often chargeable as a misdemeanor, a missed appearance can carry more weight than it would for a minor infraction.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court listed on the citation before the due date. Ask what payment options exist, and get any arrangement in writing.

If you have no license, remember that driving to court can create a new problem. Plan another way to get there.
