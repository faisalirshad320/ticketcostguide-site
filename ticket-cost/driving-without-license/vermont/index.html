How much is a driving without a license ticket in Vermont?

Nationally, a first offense for driving without a license commonly runs $50–$500, and it is often chargeable as a misdemeanor. In Vermont, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Vermont Department of Motor Vehicles).

We do not publish a single Vermont dollar figure here, because the amount depends on the court and on what is written on your citation. The national range is a general reference, not a Vermont price list.

The $50–$500 range is a national figure. The amount you owe in Vermont is whatever the court sets on your citation.

The total can also differ from the base fine. Statutory surcharges are included in the waiver amounts, so the number you pay may be higher than the fine alone.

How is the fine set?

In Vermont, the exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The citation you receive is the document that shows the amount or tells you how to find it.

Several things can shape the outcome:

  • Whether the offense is charged as a civil traffic matter or as a misdemeanor.
  • Whether it is a first offense or a repeat.
  • Why the license was missing: never issued, expired, suspended, or simply not carried.
  • The court handling the case.

Charging and penalty language comes from Vermont's statutes, which are available through Vermont statutes via Justia. Read the statute cited on your ticket rather than relying on a general summary.

Is driving without a license a misdemeanor?

Nationally, driving without a license is often chargeable as a misdemeanor. That does not mean every case is charged that way, and we cannot tell you how your case will be charged.

The difference matters. A misdemeanor can involve a court appearance and a criminal record, while a lesser charge may be handled more like a standard traffic ticket.

Your citation should name the offense and the statute. If it is unclear whether you face a criminal charge, the court clerk can explain what the paperwork means. For advice on your specific situation, you would need to speak with a licensed attorney. TicketCostGuide does not recommend specific attorneys.

How many points is driving without a license in Vermont?

Vermont's points system is described in our locked state data as 2–8 points by mph over. That scale is built around speed, so it does not map neatly onto a license offense.

We therefore do not state a specific point value for driving without a license. Whether points attach to your case depends on the offense named on your citation and the statute behind it.

One threshold does apply to the system as a whole: 10 points in 2 years = suspension. If you already have points on your record, ask the Vermont Department of Motor Vehicles how a new conviction would be recorded.

Points and fines are separate. You can owe a fine with or without points attached.

Does driving without a license raise insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Vermont-specific one.

Whether a license offense counts the same way as a moving violation depends on the insurer and how the conviction is reported. We do not know how any particular insurer will treat it, and we do not recommend insurers.

Some practical points:

  • An unlicensed driver may have trouble getting a standard policy at all.
  • Your own insurer can tell you how it treats this type of conviction.
  • The effect, if any, usually shows up at renewal.

Treat the 20% average as a rough national benchmark and not a prediction of your premium.

Can it be dismissed or reduced?

Options depend on the court and the facts. Because the Vermont amount is set by the court on the citation, the court is the right place to ask what is available.

Questions worth asking the clerk:

  1. Is this charged as a civil matter or a misdemeanor?
  2. Is a waiver amount listed, and does it include the statutory surcharges?
  3. Can I appear to contest the charge or ask for a hearing?
  4. What deadline applies to my citation?

Do not assume that getting a valid license after the ticket will resolve the charge. Whether that affects the outcome is a question for the court. For legal advice, consult a licensed attorney of your own choosing.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts commonly add consequences for missed deadlines or missed appearances, and a misdemeanor charge can carry more serious steps than a standard ticket.

We do not list Vermont-specific late penalties here, because they were not part of our locked figures. The citation and the court clerk are the reliable sources for deadlines and consequences.

If you cannot pay the full amount, ask the court what options exist. Do this before the deadline passes, not after.

Read the citation the day you get it and note every date on it.
