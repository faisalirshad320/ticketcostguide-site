How much is a driving without a license ticket in Montana?

Nationally, a first offense for driving without a license commonly costs $50–$500, and it is often chargeable as a misdemeanor. In Montana, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Montana Department of Justice).

We do not list a single Montana dollar figure, because none applies to every case. The $50–$500 range is a national picture, not a Montana schedule.

Your citation, or the court that issued it, is the only reliable source for your exact amount. Court surcharges are added to the statutory fine, so the total you pay can be higher than the fine alone.

Because the charge is often a misdemeanor, the consequences can go beyond a payment. Read the citation closely to see whether it lists a fine, requires an appearance, or both.

How is the fine for driving without a license set in Montana?

In Montana, the fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The statutes are available through Montana statutes via Justia, which is a useful place to read the actual language.

Several things can shape the amount on your citation:

  • The specific charge written on the citation.
  • Whether it is a first offense or a repeat.
  • The court that handles the case.
  • Surcharges added on top of the statutory fine.

Charges that sound alike can be different offenses. Never having been licensed, driving on an expired license, and not carrying a license you hold are often treated differently. The citation will name the exact charge, and that charge determines which rules apply.

A court setting the amount means that two people with similar citations may still see different totals. Do not rely on a figure from a friend or a forum, since it may come from another court or another charge.

How many points does driving without a license carry in Montana?

Convictions carry conviction points toward Montana's habitual-offender threshold. Under that threshold, 30 points in 3 years leads to revocation of driving privileges.

We are not stating a specific point value for this offense, because our sourced facts do not give one. Whether a particular charge adds points depends on the exact charge and the conviction.

To see what is on your record, contact the Montana Department of Justice Motor Vehicle Division through its driving information page. The Governors Highway Safety Association also keeps a general reference on state traffic laws.

Points are tied to convictions, not just to being cited. The outcome of your case matters more than the ticket itself.

Does driving without a license raise your insurance?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Montana-specific one, and it is not a prediction for your policy.

Whether a no-license conviction affects your rate depends on your insurer, your history, and how the violation is recorded. Some drivers who were never licensed may not have a policy at all, which is a separate problem to sort out.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer. If you are worried about a rate change, ask your own insurer how it treats this kind of conviction, and ask before you decide how to handle the citation.

A rough way to think about it: a 20% increase applies to what you already pay each period, and it can last for as long as the insurer looks back at your record. The length of that look-back varies by company.

Can a driving without a license ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but it depends on the facts and the court. We cannot say how likely it is in your case, and this page is not legal advice.

Common questions to raise with the court clerk or a licensed attorney of your choosing include:

  • Was the charge the right one for what happened, for example a license you held but did not have with you?
  • Is there documentation showing the license was valid when you were cited?
  • Does the court offer any process to resolve the citation without a conviction?

Whether any of these apply is up to the court. Because the Montana amount is set by the court on the citation, the court is also where you ask what options exist for your case.

If the offense is charged as a misdemeanor, the stakes are higher than for a basic traffic infraction. Many people choose to get individual legal advice before pleading.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the citation?

Ignoring a citation rarely makes it go away. Courts generally treat a missed appearance or unpaid fine as a new problem on top of the original charge.

Where the charge is a misdemeanor, a missed court date can lead to further action by the court. The specific steps depend on the court and the charge, so check the instructions printed on the citation.

Court surcharges are added to the statutory fine, so delay does not lower what you owe. If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court clerk before the deadline on the citation and ask what is available.

Also keep in mind the habitual-offender rule: 30 points in 3 years leads to revocation. A conviction that adds points moves you closer to that line.

What should I do after getting this citation?

  1. Read the citation and note the exact charge, the court, and any appearance or payment deadline.
  2. Gather proof that may matter, such as a license record or registration documents.
  3. Contact the court clerk to confirm the amount, including surcharges.
  4. Check your driving record through the Montana Department of Justice.
  5. Decide whether to seek individual legal advice, especially if a misdemeanor is charged.

Do these before the deadline, not after. The choice you make on the citation, such as paying or contesting, can affect points and insurance later.
