How much is a driving without a license ticket in Indiana?

Nationally, a first offense for driving without a license is commonly $50–$500, and it is often chargeable as a misdemeanor. In Indiana, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Indiana Bureau of Motor Vehicles).

The $50–$500 range is a national figure. It is not an Indiana price list, and we do not state an exact Indiana dollar amount here because the court sets it.

Your citation, summons or court notice is the best source for your amount. If it does not show one, contact the court clerk named on the paperwork.

The fine is only part of the total. County court costs are added to the judgment, as covered below.

How is the fine set for driving without a license?

The fine is set by the court, within the state's traffic code. It is not a fixed number you can look up and pay in advance in every case.

Several things can matter in practice:

  • Whether the charge is treated as an infraction or a misdemeanor.
  • The circumstances behind the charge. Never having held a license is different from an expired or suspended one.
  • Whether you have prior offenses on your record.
  • The court that hears the case and how it handles the charge.

We cannot tell you how a particular Indiana court will weigh these factors. The Indiana Code, which you can read through Indiana statutes via Justia, contains the traffic provisions the court applies.

Because the charge is often chargeable as a misdemeanor, the court process can be more formal than a routine speeding ticket. Read the citation closely for a court date or a required appearance.

Do court costs and surcharges add to the fine?

Yes. County court costs are added to the judgment. According to the state context we rely on, these costs often exceed the fine for low speeds.

That detail comes from the speeding context, but it shows how Indiana totals work: the amount you owe is the fine plus county court costs, not the fine alone. We do not publish a dollar figure for those costs because they vary by county and court.

When you compare a quoted amount with what you are asked to pay, check whether it includes court costs. Ask the clerk for a total if the notice lists only the fine.

Budget for a total that is higher than the fine on the citation. The clerk can confirm the full amount due.

How many points does driving without a license add in Indiana?

Indiana uses a points system. The state context we rely on describes it as 2–8 points by mph over, and says that accumulating points triggers BMV probation and suspension.

That scale is built around speeding. It does not give a specific point value for driving without a license, so we do not state one. If you want to know whether this offense adds points to your record, ask the Indiana Bureau of Motor Vehicles.

The more useful point is the consequence. When points accumulate, the BMV can place a driver on probation and later suspend the license. If you hold a license and were cited for not carrying or showing it, check your record after the case closes.

If you were driving with no valid license at all, a conviction can still affect your later licensing, so ask the BMV what applies to you.

Does driving without a license raise your insurance?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Indiana quote.

Insurers set their own rules. Some treat a licensing offense differently from a speeding conviction, and some may ask about it when you apply or renew. We do not recommend any insurer, and we cannot predict what yours will do.

  • Premiums are usually reviewed at renewal.
  • The effect depends on your insurer and your driving record.
  • A pending case is not the same as a conviction, so outcomes can differ.

If the premium effect worries you, ask your insurer how it treats licensing offenses before you decide how to respond to the citation.

Can a driving without a license charge be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome is up to the court and the prosecutor. We cannot say what is likely in your case, and we do not give legal advice.

Options people commonly look at include:

  1. Paying the amount on the citation, if the court allows it. This usually resolves the case but may count as a conviction.
  2. Appearing in court to ask about the charge or the amount.
  3. Contesting the charge at a hearing.
  4. Talking with a licensed attorney, especially if the charge is a misdemeanor.

Because the charge is often chargeable as a misdemeanor, a conviction can carry more weight than a simple fine. Read the paperwork before you pay anything, since payment can be treated as an admission.

We are not a law firm and do not recommend attorneys. For case-specific advice, consult a licensed Indiana attorney.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the citation?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. The court can add costs, and the matter can escalate.

Points matter here too. The state context says that accumulating points triggers BMV probation and suspension. Because the exact fine and added costs are set by the court, ask the clerk what happens to an unpaid balance in your county.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court clerk before the deadline on your notice. Courts often have their own procedures for requests, but we do not state specific terms because they vary by court.

Do not assume a missed date will be forgiven. Check the citation for the court date and the instructions for responding.
