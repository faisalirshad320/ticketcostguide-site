How much is a driving without a license ticket in South Dakota?

Nationally, the cost is commonly $50–$500 for a first offense. It is often chargeable as a misdemeanor. In South Dakota, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (South Dakota Department of Public Safety).

That range is a national one. We do not publish a single South Dakota dollar amount, because the court decides the figure within the state's traffic code.

Your citation is the best source for your own number. If it lists an amount or a court date, that is what applies to your case.

The amount you pay can also depend on the county or court handling the case. Two drivers with the same charge may see different totals.

How is the fine set for driving without a license in South Dakota?

The fine is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The statutes are published online through South Dakota statutes via Justia, which is a useful place to read the legal text.

Several things can matter when a court or prosecutor looks at this charge:

  • Whether you never held a license, or one expired or was suspended.
  • Whether it is a first offense or a repeat.
  • Whether you were cited for other violations at the same stop.
  • How the charge is classified, since this offense is often chargeable as a misdemeanor.

Court costs are included in the standard scheduled amounts. That means a scheduled figure should already cover them, rather than adding them on top. Check the citation or ask the court clerk to confirm what the total includes.

Is driving without a license a misdemeanor in South Dakota?

Nationally, it is often chargeable as a misdemeanor. That does not mean every citation is treated that way. The charge on your paperwork and the statute listed there tell you how your case is classified.

A misdemeanor can mean a court appearance instead of a simple payment. It can also bring consequences beyond the fine. Read the citation closely for a mandatory court date.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot tell you how your case will be charged. For a legal reading of the statute, see the code text via South Dakota statutes via Justia or speak with a licensed attorney of your own choosing.

How many points does driving without a license add in South Dakota?

South Dakota assigns points per conviction toward suspension thresholds. The locked threshold we can cite is 15 points in 12 months.

We do not state a specific point value for this violation, because our sources do not give one. Do not assume a number from another state. Ask the Department of Public Safety how this conviction is recorded (South Dakota Department of Public Safety).

One practical point: if you were driving without a license, you may have no active license to carry points. The conviction can still become part of your driving record and could matter when you apply for a license.

The Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a general reference to state traffic laws at Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws.

Does driving without a license raise insurance in South Dakota?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a South Dakota rate.

Whether this specific offense counts as a moving violation for your insurer depends on the insurer's own rules. Some insurers look at the underlying driving record, license status and any gaps in coverage.

  • Ask your insurer how it treats a license-related conviction.
  • Do not assume the 20% figure will match your quote.
  • Compare any change at your renewal date.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer. The ~20% average is only a rough guide to the scale of the possible change.

Can a driving without a license ticket be dismissed or reduced?

That decision belongs to the prosecutor and the court. The facts of your case matter, including your license status at the time of the stop and whether this is a first offense.

Common steps people take, none of them guaranteed:

  1. Read the citation for the charge, court date and any deadline.
  2. Gather records about your license status on the date of the stop.
  3. Contact the court clerk to ask about your options and how to respond.
  4. Consider speaking with a licensed attorney if the charge is a misdemeanor.

This page is information, not legal advice. We do not recommend any specific attorney.

If your license was suspended or revoked, the licensing agency may require steps before you can drive again. Start with the South Dakota Department of Public Safety.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the citation?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts can add penalties, issue a warrant for a missed appearance, or take action on driving privileges. The exact consequences depend on the court and the charge.

If the citation is a misdemeanor, a missed court date is a bigger risk than a late payment. Contact the court before the deadline if you cannot pay or cannot attend.

Ask the clerk whether payment plans or extensions are available. We cannot confirm the options in each county or court.
