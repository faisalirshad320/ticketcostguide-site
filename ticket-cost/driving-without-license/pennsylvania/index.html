How Much Does a Driving Without a License Ticket Cost in Pennsylvania?

By Editorial Team, TicketCostGuide · Last reviewed 2026-10-16

Nationally, a first offense for driving without a license is commonly $50–$500 and often chargeable as a misdemeanor. In Pennsylvania the exact amount is set by the court on the citation, and court costs and surcharges are added on top (PennDOT Driver and Vehicle Services). It can vary by county and court.

How much is a driving without a license ticket in Pennsylvania?

Nationally, a first offense is commonly $50–$500, and it is often chargeable as a misdemeanor. In Pennsylvania the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (PennDOT Driver and Vehicle Services).

We do not publish an exact Pennsylvania dollar figure, because we have no sourced number to give. The $50–$500 range is a national range, and your amount can vary by county and court.

Lead figure: Nationally commonly $50–$500 for a first offense; often chargeable as a misdemeanor; in Pennsylvania the exact amount is set by the court on the citation.

The base fine is only part of what you owe. See the section on court costs below.

How is the fine set for driving without a license in Pennsylvania?

The court sets the amount within the limits of the state's traffic code. The code is published online through Pennsylvania statutes via Justia.

The citation or court notice is the document that matters. It names the charge, and it may show an amount or tell you how the court will set one.

Several things can affect what a court does with this charge:

  • Whether you never held a license, or held one that was expired or suspended. Courts often treat these differently.
  • Whether the charge is graded as a summary offense or a misdemeanor.
  • Your prior record.
  • The practices of the county or court that hears the case.

Read the exact wording on your citation. The label "driving without a license" is used loosely, and the charge written on your paperwork controls what applies to you.

Why is the total more than the fine?

Court costs, EMS surcharge and other statutory fees typically exceed the base fine. A ticket with a modest fine can therefore cost noticeably more once these are added.

We cannot give a dollar amount for these add-ons, because they vary by court and we have no sourced figure. Ask the clerk's office for the total due, not just the fine.

Tip: ask for an itemized total. It should list the fine, court costs, the EMS surcharge and any other fees separately.

If a payment plan is available, the clerk can tell you how it works for the total balance.

How many points does driving without a license carry in Pennsylvania?

We cannot confirm a point value for this specific charge from our sourced facts. The point figures we hold come from the state's speeding schedule.

  • 2–5 points for 11+ mph over.
  • 6 points triggers a departmental exam/hearing.
  • High-speed convictions add a 15-day suspension at 31+ over.

These figures show how the point system works, not what your license charge adds. Check your citation and your driving record with PennDOT Driver and Vehicle Services to see whether any points apply.

If you already hold points, a new conviction could matter for the 6-point threshold. That is the level that triggers a departmental exam/hearing.

Does driving without a license raise insurance?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Pennsylvania one.

Whether this charge is reported to your insurer, and how your insurer rates it, depends on the conviction and the company. We do not recommend or compare insurers.

If you have no license, you may also have trouble getting standard coverage until one is issued. Your insurer or agent can explain how they treat unlicensed drivers.

A conviction is what usually drives a rate change, so the outcome of your case matters more than the citation itself.

Can it be dismissed or reduced?

It sometimes can, depending on the facts and the court. We cannot predict an outcome, and this page is not legal advice.

Common questions to raise with the court clerk or a licensed attorney of your choosing:

  • Did you hold a valid license at the time, and does the court accept proof of it?
  • Is the charge a summary offense or a misdemeanor, and what follows from each?
  • Are there ways to resolve the case without a conviction on your record?
  • What are the options if you cannot pay the full balance at once?

Because the charge is often chargeable as a misdemeanor, a conviction may carry more than a fine. If the stakes feel high, consider speaking with a licensed attorney before you respond.

Whatever you decide, do not ignore the citation. It states how and where to respond.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the citation?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts can add costs, and unresolved cases can lead to further action against you or your driving privilege.

We do not have sourced deadlines or penalty amounts for Pennsylvania, so check your citation for the response instructions. The clerk's office can confirm what is due and by when.

If you cannot pay, contact the court before the due date and ask what options exist. A call or visit early is generally better than waiting for a notice.

Keep copies of anything you file or pay, and note the date you did so.
