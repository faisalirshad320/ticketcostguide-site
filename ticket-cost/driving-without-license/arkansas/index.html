How much is a driving without a license ticket in Arkansas?

Nationally, a first offense for driving without a license commonly runs $50–$500, and it is often chargeable as a misdemeanor (Arkansas DFA Driver Services). That is a national range. It is not an Arkansas price list.

In Arkansas the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Arkansas dollar figure because the amount you owe depends on the court handling your case.

Look at your citation first. It will show the charge, the court and the date you must respond. The court's clerk can tell you the amount due or the steps to find out.

Court costs are a separate piece. They are added by the district court and vary by county, so the total on your paperwork may be higher than the fine alone.

How is the fine set, and why can it be a misdemeanor?

Arkansas courts set the fine for this violation within the state's traffic code. The citation and the court's own schedule are where your actual number appears.

Nationally, this offense is often chargeable as a misdemeanor rather than a simple infraction. That matters because a misdemeanor can carry more than a fine, and it can show up on a record in a way a basic traffic infraction may not. Whether your citation is charged that way is something to read directly on the paperwork.

Several things can change what a court does with a first offense:

  • The exact charge written on the citation.
  • The county and district court that hears it.
  • Your driving history.
  • Whether you were never licensed, or your license was expired, suspended or left at home.

These are general factors, not Arkansas-specific rules we can quote. For statute text, the Arkansas code is available through Justia.

What are court costs and surcharges on top of the fine?

Court costs are added by the district court and vary by county. They are not the same as the fine, and they are not always shown on the citation itself.

This is why asking only "what is the fine?" can leave you short. Ask the clerk for the total amount due, including costs, before you pay or appear.

We do not list a dollar amount for Arkansas court costs. No sourced figure supports one, and quoting a guess would mislead you.

Questions to ask the clerk: What is the total due? Are costs included? Can I pay online, by mail or in person? Is there a payment plan?

How many points does driving without a license add?

The sourced Arkansas points system is 3–8 points by speed, and 14+ points can trigger suspension. Those figures describe points scored by how fast a driver was going.

That range is tied to speeding, so we cannot say it applies to a no-license charge. Whether your citation adds any points is something to confirm with the court or with Arkansas DFA Driver Services.

If you do have points from other tickets, the 14-point threshold is worth knowing. Reaching it can trigger a suspension, so a new conviction on top of existing points deserves a closer look.

The Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a general reference on state traffic laws at GHSA State Laws.

Does driving without a license raise my insurance?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Arkansas one.

Whether a no-license conviction is treated like a moving violation depends on your insurer. Some insurers look closely at unlicensed driving, others may weigh it differently. We cannot predict your rate.

What usually matters is whether there is a conviction on your record. A charge that is dismissed or never results in a conviction is a different situation from one you plead to and pay.

If you are uninsured or newly licensed, ask insurers directly how they treat this charge. TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer.

Can a driving without a license ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes courts treat the charge differently depending on the facts, but we have no sourced Arkansas rule that guarantees a dismissal or a reduction. The outcome is up to the court.

Steps people commonly take:

  1. Read the citation and note the court date and the exact charge.
  2. Contact the court clerk to ask what the court accepts and what is due.
  3. If you hold a valid license, ask the clerk whether and how you can show it to the court.
  4. If a misdemeanor is charged, consider speaking with a licensed Arkansas attorney. We do not recommend a specific one.

Do not assume the charge goes away on its own. Respond by the date on the citation.

What happens if I don't pay or don't show up?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Because this offense is often chargeable as a misdemeanor, a missed court date can lead to additional consequences set by the court.

We do not have a sourced list of Arkansas penalties for non-payment, so we will not name specific ones. Ask the clerk what happens in your case if you miss a deadline.

If you cannot pay the full amount, call the court before the due date. Many courts will explain payment options, but we cannot promise yours will.

Also remember that 14+ points can trigger suspension, so unresolved tickets stacked on an existing record can add up.
