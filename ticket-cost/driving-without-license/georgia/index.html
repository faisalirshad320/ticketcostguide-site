How much is a driving without a license ticket in Georgia?

Nationally, a first offense for driving without a license is commonly $50–$500, and it is often chargeable as a misdemeanor (Georgia Department of Driver Services). That is a national range, not a Georgia price list.

In Georgia, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Georgia dollar figure because it depends on the court and the charge written on your ticket.

Your citation is the best source for your number. Look for the court name, the charge and any listed amount, or ask the court clerk what applies to your case.

Court costs and state fees can change the total you actually pay, so ask the clerk for the full amount due and not only the base fine.

How is the fine set in Georgia?

Georgia courts set the amount within the state's traffic code. The code is published online through Georgia statutes via Justia, and the Department of Driver Services explains licensing rules on its own site.

Several things can affect what a court does with a license charge:

  • Whether you ever held a Georgia license.
  • Whether the license was expired, suspended or revoked.
  • Whether it is a first offense or a repeat.
  • Which court hears the case, since amounts vary by court.

These are general factors, not a formula. Because the amount is set by the court on the citation, two drivers can end up with different totals.

How many points does driving without a license add in Georgia?

Georgia's points system on record for us runs from 2 to 6 points, based on miles per hour over the limit: 2 points for 15–18 over, 3 points for 19–23 over, 4 points for 24–33 over and 6 points for 34 or more over.

That schedule is built around speeding. We do not state a point value for a license charge, because the locked figures do not give one. Ask the court or check with Georgia Department of Driver Services to see what, if anything, will be recorded.

The key threshold: 15 points in 24 months means a suspension. Any points already on your record matter when a new conviction is added.

Are there extra state fees on top of the fine?

One state fee is on record in Georgia: a $200 Super Speeder fee that is added on top of the court fine. If it is not paid within 120 days, the license is suspended.

By its name, this fee is tied to speeding offenses. It would not normally be expected on a license charge alone, but it could matter if your citation also includes a speeding charge. Check every line of the citation.

Other court costs may apply. We do not list exact local amounts because they differ by court. Ask the clerk for an itemized total before you pay.

Does driving without a license raise insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Georgia quote.

Whether a license charge counts the same way depends on your insurer and how the conviction is recorded. Insurers set their own rules, and we do not recommend any company.

If you are worried about a premium change, ask your insurer how it treats this type of conviction. You can also ask the court how the case will be reported.

Can a driving without a license charge be dismissed or reduced?

It sometimes can, but that is up to the court. Outcomes depend on the facts, the judge and the local court's practices.

Common steps people consider:

  • Reading the citation closely for errors in the charge or your details.
  • Gathering proof if you held a valid license at the time of the stop.
  • Contacting the court clerk to learn your options and deadlines.
  • Talking with a licensed attorney if a misdemeanor charge or a suspended license is involved.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot give legal advice. A misdemeanor charge is more serious than a simple fine, so it is worth understanding it before you respond.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts can add consequences when a deadline passes, and a missed court date can bring its own problems.

For the one state fee on record, the rule is clear: failure to pay the $200 Super Speeder fee within 120 days suspends the license. Separately, reaching 15 points in 24 months also leads to a suspension.

If you cannot pay in full, contact the court before the due date and ask what options exist. The court on your citation decides, and policies vary by court.
