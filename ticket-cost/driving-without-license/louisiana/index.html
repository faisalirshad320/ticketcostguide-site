How much does driving without a license cost in Louisiana?

Nationally, a first offense for driving without a license commonly runs $50–$500, and it is often chargeable as a misdemeanor. That is a national range, not a Louisiana price tag.

In Louisiana, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Louisiana Office of Motor Vehicles). We do not publish an exact Louisiana dollar figure, because the court decides the amount in your case.

Your total bill is usually the fine plus local court costs. Those costs vary by parish and often exceed the fine itself.

Read your citation closely. The amount, the charge listed, and the court date or payment instructions printed on it are the numbers that apply to you.

How is the fine for driving without a license set?

The fine is set by the court within the state's traffic code. There is no single price that every Louisiana driver pays for this charge.

The Louisiana statutes are published online (Louisiana statutes via Justia). They define the offense and set the framework a judge or court works within.

Several things can shape what ends up on your citation or in the court's final order:

  • The specific charge written by the officer. Never having held a license, having an expired license and not carrying a valid license can be treated differently.
  • Your driving and conviction history, since repeat convictions matter in Louisiana.
  • The court that hears the case and its local practices.
  • Whether the offense is charged as a misdemeanor, which is common nationally.

Because the court sets the figure, two drivers cited for similar conduct in different parishes can end up with different totals.

Why can court costs be higher than the fine?

Local court costs vary by parish and often exceed the fine. A driver who sees a modest fine on a citation can still owe a noticeably larger total once costs are added.

This is why the fine alone is a poor guide to what you will actually pay. Ask the clerk of the court that handles your citation for the full amount due, including every cost and surcharge.

We cannot give you a parish-by-parish dollar amount, because the costs vary and we do not have a verified figure for each court. Treat any number you find online with caution unless it comes from the court itself.

Before paying, confirm whether the amount quoted is the fine only or the fine plus court costs. Get the total in writing or on the court's official payment page.

How many points does driving without a license add in Louisiana?

Louisiana has no point system. License action follows repeat convictions and specific offenses rather than a running point total.

In practice, that means a single conviction is not scored in points the way it would be in many other states. The concern is what happens if convictions repeat, or if the offense is one that triggers license action on its own.

Your driving record is kept by the state's motor vehicle agency (Louisiana Office of Motor Vehicles). If you want to know how a conviction would appear on your record, check with that office.

State laws differ on how they treat unlicensed driving. The Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a general overview of state traffic laws (Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws).

Does driving without a license raise insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Louisiana-specific one.

How much your own premium changes depends on your insurer, your record and the policy you hold. Some insurers weigh a conviction heavily, and others weigh it less.

A conviction for driving without a license may also matter to insurers in other ways, for example if it raises questions about your licensing status. We do not recommend any insurer. If you want to know how a conviction might affect your policy, ask your insurer or agent directly.

  • The ~20% figure is an average, so your change could be higher or lower.
  • The effect is usually tied to a conviction, not just to being cited.
  • Rates are typically reviewed at renewal.

Can a driving without a license ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but it depends on the court and the facts. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice, so this is general information only.

Common paths people look into include:

  1. Paying the citation. This usually resolves the matter, but it can mean a conviction on your record.
  2. Contesting the charge. You can ask for a court date and present your side.
  3. Asking the clerk about options. Courts differ in what they offer, and the clerk can explain the process for your citation.
  4. Speaking with a licensed attorney. An attorney can tell you how the law applies to your situation.

If you held a valid license at the time and simply did not have it with you, or it had lapsed, the details of your situation may matter. Ask the court what documents it accepts, and do not assume a particular outcome.

Because this offense is often chargeable as a misdemeanor, it is worth understanding the charge before you decide to pay or contest.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the citation?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. The court that issued it still expects a response, and the matter does not go away on its own.

Depending on the court, an unpaid or unanswered citation can lead to added costs, further court action and possible license consequences. Because Louisiana license action follows repeat convictions and specific offenses, an unresolved matter on top of other convictions could carry more weight.

If you cannot pay the full amount at once, contact the clerk of the court before the due date. Ask what payment options exist for your citation. We cannot say which options a given court offers.

If you are unsure what the citation requires, a licensed attorney can explain how the process works in your parish.
