How much is a driving without a license ticket in North Dakota?

Nationally, a first offense for driving without a license commonly runs $50–$500, and it is often chargeable as a misdemeanor (North Dakota Department of Transportation). That is a national range, not a North Dakota price list.

In North Dakota the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single state figure, because the number you owe depends on what you were charged with and what the court decides.

Read the citation itself. The charge, the court named on it and any listed amount are the numbers that apply to you.

The statutory fee is usually the whole amount. North Dakota does not typically add a large stack of surcharges on top, so the fine on the citation is usually close to what you pay.

How is the fine set?

The fine is set by the court within the state's traffic code. It is not a fixed number that applies the same way to every driver. You can find the code through North Dakota statutes via Justia.

Several things can matter in practice:

  • Whether the charge is a plain no-license violation or something more serious.
  • Whether it is classed as a misdemeanor, which is common nationally.
  • Whether you have prior offenses on your record.
  • What the court decides within the limits the traffic code allows.

Because the amount comes from the court, two people with similar tickets can see different results. That is why we use the national range only as context.

Is driving without a license a misdemeanor in North Dakota?

Nationally, the offense is often chargeable as a misdemeanor. A misdemeanor is a criminal charge, not just a payable traffic infraction, and it can show up on a background check.

Whether your North Dakota citation is treated that way depends on the charge written on it. Look for wording that tells you whether you can simply pay or must appear in court.

Not having a license on you differs from never having been licensed, and both differ from driving on a suspended license. Courts can treat these separately. Your citation will name the specific violation.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot tell you how your charge will be classified. The court clerk or a licensed attorney can answer that for your case.

How many points does driving without a license add in North Dakota?

North Dakota assigns points on its schedule, and for speeding they are based on how many mph over the limit you were going. 12+ points = suspension (North Dakota Department of Transportation).

We do not have a sourced point value for a no-license charge, so we are not going to guess one. Many people assume a point total applies, but it depends on how the offense is coded on your record.

To find out, you can request your driving record from the North Dakota Department of Transportation after the case is resolved. It shows what, if anything, was recorded.

If you already carry points from other violations, the 12-point suspension threshold is worth keeping in mind.

Does driving without a license raise insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a North Dakota rate.

Whether a no-license conviction is rated the same way depends on the insurer and on how the offense appears on your record. We do not recommend or compare insurers.

If you do not currently have a license, you may also have trouble getting a policy at all. Insurers generally look at your record and license status when they price coverage.

  • Ask your insurer how a conviction would be treated before you decide how to respond.
  • Check whether the charge could be resolved in a way that does not create a conviction.

Can it be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes courts reduce or dismiss charges, but outcomes depend on the facts and on the court. We cannot promise any result.

Common options people consider include:

  • Paying the citation. This is usually quick, but it may count as a conviction or an admission.
  • Contesting it. You can ask for a hearing and present your side.
  • Showing proof of a valid license. If you were licensed when you were stopped, ask the court how to submit proof.
  • Asking about a reduced charge. The court or prosecutor decides whether that is possible.

For advice on which path fits your situation, speak with a licensed North Dakota attorney. TicketCostGuide does not recommend specific attorneys.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts commonly respond to missed deadlines or missed appearances with additional penalties, and a misdemeanor charge can lead to further court action.

Unpaid traffic matters can also affect your driving privileges. If you are already near the 12-point suspension threshold, a new problem on your record is a bigger risk.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court named on the citation before the deadline. Clerks can explain what the court allows.

Deadlines and appearance dates are printed on your citation. Missing them is the most avoidable way to turn a small matter into a bigger one.
