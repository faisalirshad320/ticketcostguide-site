How much is a driving without a license ticket in Virginia?

Nationally, a first offense for driving without a license commonly runs $50–$500, and it is often chargeable as a misdemeanor. That is a national range, not a Virginia price list (Virginia Department of Motor Vehicles).

In Virginia, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Virginia dollar figure because the court decides it within the state's traffic code.

Your citation, summons or the court's own paperwork is the only reliable source for what you owe. If the paperwork lists a court date instead of a prepayable amount, the court will set the outcome.

Fixed processing costs apply to every prepaid ticket, so the total you pay can be more than the fine alone. Reckless cases add court-set fines and fees on top.

How is the fine set for driving without a license?

The fine is not a flat statewide number. The Virginia traffic code sets the framework, and the court handling your case sets the amount on the citation.

Several things can influence what a court decides:

  • Whether the charge is treated as a misdemeanor or something lesser.
  • Whether it is a first offense or a repeat.
  • Whether other charges, such as reckless driving, were filed at the same stop.
  • Whether the court requires an appearance rather than allowing prepayment.

We cannot tell you how a particular court weighs these. Statutes are available through Virginia statutes via Justia, though reading a code section is not the same as getting legal advice.

Also keep in mind that "without a license" can mean different things in practice, such as never having been licensed, having an expired license, or not carrying it. The charge on your citation tells you which one applies, and the label can affect how the court treats it.

How many points does driving without a license add in Virginia?

Virginia's demerit point system assigns 3–6 demerit points by offense, with 6 for reckless driving. The locked data we rely on does not say which point value, if any, attaches to an unlicensed-driving charge.

Look at your citation and the conviction record the court sends to the DMV. The Virginia DMV is the authority on how points are applied to your record.

Two points mechanics are worth knowing:

  • The DMV suspends at point thresholds, so several violations in a short time matter more than any single one.
  • Drivers earn +1 safe point per clean year, which offsets demerit points over time.

If you have no valid license, points may be a smaller worry than getting licensed. Points accumulate on a driving record, and a record may be affected differently if you are not currently licensed. Ask the DMV how your situation is handled.

Does driving without a license raise insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Virginia quote.

Whether an unlicensed-driving conviction counts the same way as a speeding or reckless conviction depends on the insurer's own rules and on how the conviction appears on your record. We do not give insurer recommendations or predict your premium.

Practical points:

  • An insurer may look at the conviction, any points and your license status when it reprices or renews.
  • Being unlicensed can also create problems getting a policy at all, separate from the ticket.
  • A reckless-related charge, which carries 6 points, is more likely to draw attention than a lesser one.

If you are worried about your premium, ask your insurer how it treats this type of conviction before you decide how to resolve the ticket.

Can a driving without a license ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes outcomes change, but we cannot promise any result. Whether a charge is reduced or dismissed is up to the prosecutor and the court, and it varies by court.

Common paths people consider:

  1. Pay the ticket. This is usually treated as accepting the conviction, with fixed processing costs added to prepaid tickets.
  2. Contest it in court. You appear on the court date and present your case. Bring the citation and any documents about your license status.
  3. Talk to a licensed attorney. This is worth considering if the charge is a misdemeanor or comes with other charges. We do not recommend any specific attorney.

Paying a ticket can have record and insurance consequences. Read the citation carefully before you choose to prepay.

If your license was valid but you could not show it at the stop, ask the court clerk what documentation it accepts. Practices differ between courts, and the clerk can tell you what applies to your case.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts can add fines and fees, and the DMV can act on the record.

Because driving without a license is often chargeable as a misdemeanor, missing a required court date is a bigger risk than missing a payment on a minor ticket. A court can treat a missed appearance as a separate problem.

  • Read the citation for the court name, date and payment instructions.
  • Contact the court clerk before the date if you cannot attend.
  • Do not assume a prepaid amount is available. Some citations require an appearance.

The court listed on your citation is the right place to confirm deadlines and amounts. The Virginia DMV can tell you about any effect on your driving record or license status.
