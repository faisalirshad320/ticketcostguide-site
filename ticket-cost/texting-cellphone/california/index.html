How much is a texting or cell phone ticket in California?

Nationally, a first-offense texting or cell phone ticket runs $20–$500, depending on the state (California Department of Motor Vehicles). That is a national range. It is not a California price.

In California, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. It can vary by county and court, so the number on your ticket or notice is the one that applies to you.

California's base fine is $20 for a first offense and $50 for a subsequent offense. Totals reach ≈$160+ once assessments and fees are added.

Read the amount printed on your citation or court notice. Do not rely on a general estimate, including ours, for what you owe.

How is the fine set, and why is the total so much higher than the base fine?

The base fine is only the starting point. The court sets the exact fine for this violation within the state's traffic code, and the amount appears on your citation or in the court's notice.

Penalty assessments, county fees and surcharges multiply the base fine roughly 4–5×. That is why a $20 base fine can turn into a total of ≈$160+.

  • Base fine: $20 first offense, $50 subsequent offense.
  • Add-ons: penalty assessments, county fees and surcharges.
  • Result: totals reach ≈$160+ with assessments.

Because county fees and surcharges can vary, two drivers cited for the same thing in different counties may see different totals. The court's figure is the one that controls.

Is a second texting ticket more expensive?

Yes. California's base fine is $20 for a first offense and $50 for a subsequent offense. Because assessments and surcharges are multiplied against the base fine, a higher base raises the total as well.

We do not publish an exact California total for a repeat offense. The court sets the exact amount on the citation, and county fees can vary.

If you have a prior citation, expect the court to treat the new one as a subsequent offense. The notice you receive will show the amount due.

How many points does a texting ticket add in California?

The locked state context for California's point system is that ordinary speeding carries 1 point (2 points at 100+ mph). Accumulating 4 points in 12 months means a suspension under the Negligent Operator Treatment System.

Those are the figures we can verify here. We do not state a point value for a texting or cell phone violation, because it is not one of our locked facts.

To find out how your specific violation will be recorded, check your citation, the court, or the California Department of Motor Vehicles. Statutes are also available through California statutes via Justia.

Points from several moving violations can stack. If you already have other violations on your record, find out where you stand before you decide what to do about this ticket.

Does a texting ticket raise my insurance?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums ~20% on average. That is a national figure, not a California quote.

Whether your insurer changes your rate depends on the company, your driving history and how the ticket is resolved. We do not recommend any insurer and cannot predict your rate.

The fine is usually a one-time cost. A rate increase can last longer, so many drivers weigh both when deciding how to respond. Your insurer or policy documents are the place to check how a conviction is treated.

Can a texting ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but outcomes depend on the court and the facts of your case. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot tell you whether your ticket qualifies.

In general, drivers who receive a citation can usually do one of the following:

  • Pay the amount on the citation, which typically resolves the ticket.
  • Contest the citation through the court listed on it.
  • Ask the court clerk what other options the court offers for your situation.

Because the court sets the amount and county fees can vary, ask the clerk to confirm the total before you pay or decide anything. Compare the cost against the possible effect on points and insurance.

For personal advice about your case, you would need to speak with a licensed professional. We do not recommend a specific attorney.

What happens if I don't pay a texting ticket in California?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts commonly add fees for late or missed payments, and unresolved tickets can lead to further action on your license or record.

We do not state exact penalties or deadlines here, because they are not part of our locked facts. The deadline and instructions are printed on your citation or court notice.

If you cannot pay the full amount by the due date, contact the court before the deadline. Ask what is available. The California Department of Motor Vehicles can explain how unresolved citations affect a driver record.
