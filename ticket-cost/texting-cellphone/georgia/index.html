How much is a texting or cell phone ticket in Georgia?

Nationally, a first-offense texting or cell phone ticket falls in a range of $20–$500 depending on the state (Georgia Department of Driver Services). That is a national range, not a Georgia price.

In Georgia, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Georgia dollar figure, because the amount you owe is the one written on your ticket or set by the court that hears it.

Check the citation first. It shows the charge, the court, and usually how and where to pay or contest.

The fine is only one part of the cost. Points, possible state fees, and insurance changes can matter more over time.

How is the fine for a Georgia cell phone ticket set?

The fine is set by the court within the limits of the state's traffic code. The code is the starting point, but the court that handles your citation decides the amount.

Two people with the same charge can therefore see different totals, because the amount depends on the court and the facts of the case. Prior offenses can also matter.

The text of the law is available through Georgia statutes via Justia. For a plain-language summary of how states treat phone use behind the wheel, see the Governors Highway Safety Association state laws page.

  • Read the charge listed on the citation.
  • Note the court name and any date shown.
  • Ask the court clerk what the total due is for that charge.

How many points is a texting ticket in Georgia?

Georgia uses a points system for moving violations. The schedule we have sourced is tied to speed: 2–6 points by mph over, with 2 points for 15–18 over, 3 points for 19–23 over, 4 points for 24–33 over, and 6 points for 34+ over.

Those tiers describe speeding, so they do not tell you the points for a phone charge. We do not state a points value for a texting or cell phone conviction. Confirm it with the Georgia Department of Driver Services.

The key threshold: 15 points in 24 months = suspension.

Points from different violations can add up inside that window. A phone ticket on top of earlier points brings you closer to that line.

Are there extra state fees on top of the fine?

Georgia adds a $200 Super Speeder state fee on top of the court fine when it applies. If it is not paid within 120 days, the license is suspended.

That fee is tied to Super Speeder cases. We have not confirmed that it applies to a texting or cell phone citation, so do not assume it does. Read the paperwork from the court and the DDS carefully.

  • Court fine: set by the court on the citation.
  • State fee, where it applies: $200, added on top.
  • Deadline on that fee: 120 days before a suspension.

Ask the clerk for the total due, including any fees, before you pay or plead.

Does a texting ticket raise my insurance?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Georgia-specific rate.

Your own change depends on your insurer, your driving record, and how the conviction is recorded. We do not recommend or compare insurers.

The effect usually lasts for several renewal periods, so the long-term cost of a conviction can exceed the fine itself. That is one reason people look at their options before simply paying.

Paying the citation is generally treated as a conviction. Before you pay, ask the court what the outcome will be on your record.

Can a texting ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome depends on the court and the facts. We cannot give legal advice or say what will happen in your case.

Common routes people consider:

  • Pay the ticket. This is usually the quickest route, but it generally results in a conviction.
  • Contest it. You can ask for a hearing and present your side. The citation lists how to do this.
  • Ask the court about alternatives. Some courts may allow other resolutions. The clerk can tell you what your court offers.
  • Talk to a licensed attorney. A lawyer can explain how the law applies to you.

Read the statute through Georgia statutes via Justia to see exactly what the law covers. Pay attention to what the charge on your citation actually says.

What happens if I don't pay a Georgia cell phone ticket?

Ignoring a citation can make things worse. The court can treat a missed payment or missed court date as a separate problem, and the license can be affected.

For the state fee we have sourced, the rule is specific: the $200 Super Speeder fee must be paid within 120 days or the license is suspended. That fee is added on top of the court fine.

Points also matter. A suspension follows 15 points in 24 months, so an unresolved ticket plus earlier points can add up.

If you cannot pay by the deadline, contact the court before it passes. Clerks can explain what options exist.
