How much is a texting or cell phone ticket in Massachusetts?

Nationally, a first-offense texting or cell phone ticket costs $20–$500 depending on the state (Massachusetts Registry of Motor Vehicles). That range is national. It is not a Massachusetts price list.

In Massachusetts, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single state dollar figure, because the number you owe is the one printed on your ticket or set by the court handling it.

Your citation is the controlling document. If the amount you see differs from anything you read online, go with the citation and the court or Registry that issued it.

How is the fine set?

The Massachusetts fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. It is not a flat number we can quote for every driver.

Several things can affect what ends up on a citation or in a court record:

  • The specific violation charged (texting, handheld phone use, or another distracted driving charge).
  • Whether it is a first offense or a repeat.
  • Any surcharges or costs the court adds to the base amount.

The statutes are published for reference through Massachusetts statutes via Justia. Reading the code can show how the offense is defined, but your citation shows what you owe.

Are there extra fees or surcharges?

Massachusetts has a $50 head-injury surcharge that applies to every speeding conviction (Massachusetts Registry of Motor Vehicles). That locked fact is about speeding convictions specifically.

We do not have a locked figure saying the same surcharge applies to a texting or cell phone conviction. Do not assume it does or does not. Check the total on your citation or ask the court clerk.

Total cost is often more than the base fine, so compare the full amount due, not only the headline number.

How many points does a texting ticket carry in Massachusetts?

Massachusetts uses insurance surcharge points (SDIP) rather than license suspension points for ordinary speeding (Massachusetts Registry of Motor Vehicles). The state does not run a points-to-suspension ladder like some others.

We do not have a locked point value for a texting or cell phone violation, so we will not state one. Ask the Registry or check your record for how this violation is treated.

The thresholds that do apply to surchargeable events are:

  • 3 surchargeable events in 24 months require a driver retraining course.
  • 7 surchargeable events in 3 years mean suspension.

Whether a given texting conviction counts as a surchargeable event is something to confirm with the Registry.

Does a texting ticket raise my insurance?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Massachusetts or texting-specific one.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your driving record, and how the violation is recorded. Massachusetts insurance surcharge points (SDIP) are the mechanism tied to premiums in the state's system.

We do not recommend insurers or give insurance advice. If you want to know how a conviction would affect your policy, ask your insurer directly.

Can a texting ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Whether a ticket can be reduced or dismissed depends on the facts, the court, and the process the citation describes. We cannot say what would work in your case, and this page is not legal advice.

General steps that people take:

  1. Read the citation for the amount, the violation charged, and the response options and deadlines.
  2. Contact the court listed on the citation to ask how to pay or contest.
  3. Consider whether you want to speak with a licensed attorney, who can advise on your situation.

Because a conviction can affect surcharge points and insurance, it is worth weighing the full cost before simply paying.

What happens if I don't pay?

We do not have locked figures for late fees or penalties, so we will not quote any. Unpaid or ignored tickets generally create bigger problems than the original fine, which is why the citation lists deadlines.

Do not confuse unpaid tickets with the surchargeable-event thresholds. Those thresholds, 3 events in 24 months for retraining and 7 in 3 years for suspension, count events on your record. They are not a late-payment rule.

If you cannot pay by the deadline, contact the court on the citation before it passes and ask what options exist.
