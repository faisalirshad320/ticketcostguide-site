How much is a texting or cell phone ticket in Maine?

Nationally, a first-offense texting or cell phone ticket costs $20–$500, depending on the state. In Maine, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Maine Bureau of Motor Vehicles).

This page does not give a single Maine dollar figure, because the amount depends on what the court puts on your citation. The national range is context only. It is not a prediction of what you will owe.

The number that matters is the one printed on your citation or on the court's notice. Use the national range only to judge whether that number looks ordinary.

Statutory surcharges are built into the scheduled amounts. That means the amount you are told to pay should already include them, rather than having them stacked on top. Read the notice closely, and ask the court clerk if anything is unclear.

How is the fine for a texting ticket set in Maine?

The fine for this violation in Maine is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The officer's citation and the court's schedule together determine what appears on your paperwork.

Several things can affect the amount you see:

  • Whether this is a first offense or a repeat.
  • How the violation is written on the citation.
  • The court handling the case.
  • Surcharges, which are built into the scheduled amounts.

Nationally, first-offense amounts span a wide range of $20–$500, which shows how much states differ in how they treat the same behavior. Maine's number falls somewhere in how its own court and traffic code set it, so confirm it against your citation rather than a general estimate.

For the statutes themselves, Maine's laws are published online (Maine statutes via Justia). Statute text can be dense, and a licensed attorney can explain how it applies to your case. We do not recommend any specific attorney.

How many points does a texting ticket add in Maine?

Maine's points system adds demerit points per conviction. A suspension is triggered at 12 points in 12 months.

We do not state a specific point value for a texting conviction here, because it is not part of the figures we have verified. Your citation, the court, or the Bureau of Motor Vehicles can tell you how a conviction is recorded (Maine Bureau of Motor Vehicles).

Two details are worth knowing:

  • Points accrue on conviction, not when the officer writes the ticket.
  • The suspension threshold counts all points inside the 12-month window, not just those from texting.

If you already have other recent convictions, a texting conviction could matter more than it would on a clean record. Check your own driving record before deciding how to respond.

Does a texting ticket raise car insurance in Maine?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Maine-specific rate.

What you actually pay depends on your insurer, your driving history, and how the company treats this kind of violation. We cannot predict your quote, and we do not recommend any insurer.

A few practical points:

  • The increase is tied to a conviction, so the outcome of your case matters.
  • Increases usually show up at renewal, not immediately.
  • Comparing quotes after a conviction is a normal step for many drivers.

If premium impact is your main worry, ask your insurer how it treats a moving-violation conviction before you decide whether to pay or contest. Treat the 20% as a rough national benchmark and nothing more.

Can a texting ticket be dismissed or reduced in Maine?

Possibly, but the outcome depends on the facts and on the court. We cannot promise any result, and nothing here is legal advice.

In general, you have these choices:

  1. Pay the ticket. This usually resolves the case, but it can count as a conviction, which is what triggers points and insurance consequences.
  2. Contest it. You can ask the court for a hearing and present your side. Evidence about what the officer saw and what you were doing matters here.
  3. Ask the court about alternatives. Some courts offer other ways to resolve a case. Ask the clerk what is available for your citation.

The citation and court notice state your deadlines and how to respond. Read them first, because missing a deadline can limit your options.

If you are weighing a contest, consider speaking with a licensed attorney about your situation.

What happens if I don't pay a texting ticket in Maine?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. Courts generally treat an unpaid or unanswered citation as a separate problem, and it can bring added penalties or action involving your license.

The specifics for Maine depend on your court and your citation. We do not list exact late fees or deadlines here, because those figures are not among our verified facts. Your court notice will state them, and the Bureau of Motor Vehicles can explain how license status is affected (Maine Bureau of Motor Vehicles).

If you cannot pay the full amount or cannot meet a deadline, contact the court clerk before the deadline passes. Asking early is usually better than staying silent.
