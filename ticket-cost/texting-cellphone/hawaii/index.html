How much is a texting / cell phone ticket in Hawaii?

Nationally, a first texting or cell phone ticket costs $20–$500 depending on the state (Hawaii Department of Transportation). In Hawaii, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation.

That is why this page does not print a single Hawaii dollar figure. Any number we quoted would be a guess, and the citation you were handed is the only reliable record of what you owe.

The $20–$500 range is a national comparison across states. It is not a Hawaii fine schedule, and it should not be read as the amount you will be charged.

Read the citation first. It shows the amount the court set, and fees and assessments added by the district court can change the total you actually pay.

How is the fine for a cell phone ticket set in Hawaii?

In Hawaii, the fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. It is not a number we can calculate for you from a formula.

The amount you see on the citation is the starting point. Court fees and assessments are added by the district court, so the final total can be higher than the fine alone.

If you want to confirm the details, you can read the state's traffic code through Hawaii statutes via Justia. For a state-by-state comparison of handheld and texting laws, the Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws page is a useful reference.

  • The fine: set by the court on the citation.
  • Fees and assessments: added by the district court.
  • Total owed: confirm with the court handling your case.

How many points does a texting ticket add in Hawaii?

None, because Hawaii has no demerit-point system. There is no running point total to build up or to work off.

License action works differently. In Hawaii, repeat convictions and excessive-speeding convictions drive license action directly.

So the useful question is not how many points you have, but how many convictions are on your record. A repeat conviction for the same kind of violation is the situation to take seriously.

No points does not mean no consequences. The conviction still goes on your driving record, and that record is what insurers and the licensing system look at.

Does a texting or cell phone ticket raise insurance in Hawaii?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Hawaii-specific one.

Your own change could be smaller, larger, or none at all. It depends on your insurer, your driving history, and whether the ticket ends in a conviction.

We do not recommend insurers or predict what a given company will do. If you are worried about your rate, the most reliable step is to ask your insurer how it treats this kind of violation.

  • The ~20% figure is an average across the US.
  • The effect generally follows a conviction on your record.
  • Rates may be reviewed at renewal, not the day you pay.

Can a texting ticket be dismissed or reduced in Hawaii?

Sometimes a court will dismiss or reduce a citation, but we cannot tell you whether that applies to your case. That decision belongs to the court, and the options depend on the facts and your record.

The citation itself should explain how to respond and where. Follow those instructions exactly, because the way you respond affects what the court can do.

Generally, a driver facing a citation can choose among a few paths:

  • Pay the amount on the citation, which usually resolves the case but may result in a conviction.
  • Contest the citation and present your side to the court.
  • Ask the court about any alternatives it offers.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice. If the stakes feel high, such as a repeat conviction, consider speaking with a licensed attorney of your own choosing.

What happens if I don't pay a texting ticket in Hawaii?

Ignoring a citation tends to make things worse. Courts generally treat an unanswered ticket as a separate problem, which can add costs and create license trouble.

Because Hawaii has no demerit-point system, the risk is not points piling up. The risk is that an unresolved case can lead to additional court action, on top of the fees and assessments the district court already adds.

Check the response instructions on your citation and contact the court listed on it if anything is unclear. Do that before the date shown on the citation, not after.

If you cannot pay the full amount, ask the court what it allows. Do not simply wait.
