How much is a texting / cell phone ticket in Minnesota?

Nationally, a first-offense texting or cell phone ticket falls in a range of $20–$500 depending on the state (Minnesota Department of Public Safety). That is a national range, not a Minnesota price.

In Minnesota, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not print a single Minnesota dollar figure, because the amount you owe is the one shown on your ticket or by the court that handles it.

A statutory state surcharge is added to every payable fine. The total you pay can therefore be higher than the base fine alone.

Start with the citation itself. It lists the violation, the court, and the amount or the way to find out what you owe.

How is the fine set for a cell phone ticket in Minnesota?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. Minnesota statutes are published online, and you can read the traffic provisions through Minnesota statutes via Justia.

Because the court sets the amount, two people cited for the same behavior can see different totals on their citations. The amount on your paperwork is the one that counts.

The pieces that usually make up a total are:

  • The fine for the violation, as set by the court.
  • The statutory state surcharge, which is added to every payable fine.
  • Any other court costs the court lists on your citation or payment notice.

If your paperwork lists several amounts, ask the court clerk which one is the total due. Do not rely on a figure from a forum post or an old article.

How many points does a texting ticket add in Minnesota?

None, because Minnesota has no point system (Minnesota Department of Public Safety). You will not see a points total attached to this ticket the way you would in many other states.

License action in Minnesota follows the record of convictions and specific high-speed offenses. In practice, what matters is whether the ticket becomes a conviction on your driving record.

That is why the way you resolve the ticket matters. A conviction is the event that goes on the record, and the record is what the state looks at.

No points does not mean no consequences. A conviction still appears on your record, and insurers may see it.

Does a texting ticket raise insurance rates in Minnesota?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average (Minnesota Department of Public Safety). That is a national average, not a Minnesota quote.

Your own change could be smaller, larger, or zero. It depends on your insurer, your prior record, and whether the ticket ends up as a conviction.

We do not recommend insurers or predict what a given company will charge. If you want to know the effect on your policy, ask your insurer or agent how it treats a moving-violation conviction.

A fine is paid once. A premium increase can last as long as the conviction stays relevant to your insurer, so it is worth weighing both costs together.

Can a texting ticket be dismissed or reduced in Minnesota?

Sometimes the court offers choices, but we do not have locked figures or rules on dismissal or reduction, so we will not guess. What is available depends on the court, the facts, and your record.

Your general options after a citation are usually:

  1. Pay the amount on the citation, which typically resolves the ticket.
  2. Contest the ticket and ask the court for a hearing.
  3. Ask the court clerk what resolution options it offers for this violation.

Paying is usually the quickest route, but it usually means accepting the violation. Since a conviction is what the state tracks, think about that before you pay.

This page is not legal advice. If the stakes feel high, a licensed attorney can explain your options for your specific situation. We do not recommend any particular attorney.

What happens if I don't pay a texting ticket in Minnesota?

The citation tells you where and how to respond. Ignoring it does not make it go away, and the court can add consequences under the state's traffic code.

We do not have locked figures for late fees or deadlines, so we are not listing any. Check your citation for the response date and the court's contact details.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court clerk before the date on your ticket. Courts can tell you what payment arrangements, if any, they accept.

For license questions, the state's driver licensing agency is the place to check your status. The Minnesota Department of Public Safety Driver and Vehicle Services page is the starting point.

How does Minnesota compare with other states?

Texting and handheld phone laws differ widely by state. What counts as a violation, and what the penalty is, is set state by state. The Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a summary at Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws.

The national first-offense range of $20–$500 reflects that spread. Where Minnesota falls on your ticket is decided by the court, not by a national average.

Use the national range as context only. Your citation is the number to plan around.
