How much is a texting / cell phone ticket in Alabama?

Nationally, a first-offense texting or cell phone ticket costs $20–$500 depending on the state. That is a national range, and it is not an Alabama price.

In Alabama, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Alabama Law Enforcement Agency (ALEA)). We do not list a single statewide dollar figure because the amount you owe is the one printed on your ticket or given by the court.

Your total is usually more than the base fine. Municipal and district court costs vary by jurisdiction and often exceed the base fine.

When you compare what you owe with any number you read online, check whether it is a base fine or the full amount with court costs.

How is the fine for a cell phone ticket set in Alabama?

The fine is set by the court within the state's traffic code. It is not a fixed number we can quote for every county.

Two things shape the final amount:

  • The base fine the court applies under the traffic code.
  • Court costs and surcharges, which vary by municipal and district court.

This is why two drivers with the same violation can be asked to pay different totals in different places. The citation, the court's notice or the court clerk will show the amount for your case.

You can read the statutes themselves through Alabama statutes via Justia. For a comparison of state phone laws, see the Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws page.

Why can court costs be higher than the fine?

Municipal and district court costs vary by jurisdiction and often exceed the base fine. Costs are added on top of the fine, and each court sets up its own.

That means a ticket with a modest base fine can still produce a larger bill. It also means the same ticket can cost a different total depending on which court hears it.

  • Look for a line-by-line breakdown if the court provides one.
  • Ask the clerk what is fine and what is cost.
  • Confirm the total before you pay, especially for online payments.

We do not publish an Alabama court-cost figure, because it differs by court and the locked data gives none.

How many points does a texting ticket add in Alabama?

The Alabama points system, as we have it, reads: 2 points (25 or less over) or 5 points (more than 25 over). The threshold for suspension is 12–14 points in 2 years = 60-day suspension (Alabama Law Enforcement Agency (ALEA)).

Those two tiers are written in terms of how far over a limit a driver was, so they are speed-based. They do not tell us how a cell phone violation is scored.

Check your citation or ask the court how your specific violation is scored. Do not assume it matches either tier.

What the figures do show is how the system works. Points add up over a 2-year window, and reaching 12–14 points in that period leads to a 60-day suspension.

Does a texting ticket raise insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Alabama-specific one.

Your own change could be higher, lower or none at all. It depends on your insurer, your driving record and whether the ticket ends in a conviction on your record.

  • The ~20% figure is an average, not a promise.
  • The outcome of the case matters more than the ticket itself.
  • Your insurer can tell you how it treats a violation like this.

We do not recommend any insurer. For questions about your own policy, contact your insurance company directly.

Can a texting ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the options depend on the court and the facts of your case. We cannot tell you whether yours qualifies, and this page is not legal advice.

In general, you have these choices after getting a citation:

  1. Pay the amount on the citation, which usually ends the case.
  2. Contest the ticket in court.
  3. Ask the court clerk what options it offers before your date.

Paying can have consequences for your record and points, so read the citation before you decide. Because the amount and costs vary by court, ask what the full total is before you choose.

If you want advice about your specific situation, a licensed attorney is the right person to ask.

What happens if I don't pay a texting ticket in Alabama?

Ignoring a ticket rarely makes it go away. Courts usually have a process for missed dates and unpaid amounts, and what that involves depends on the court.

Reading the citation is the first step. It lists the court, the due date and how to respond.

  • Note the deadline and the court's contact details.
  • Contact the clerk early if you cannot pay by the date.
  • Keep proof of any payment or response.

Because points matter, an unresolved ticket can also complicate your driving record. Reaching 12–14 points in 2 years leads to a 60-day suspension, so it is worth knowing where your record stands.
