How much is a texting / cell phone ticket in Missouri?

Nationally, a texting / cell phone ticket costs $20–$500 for a first offense, depending on the state (Missouri Department of Revenue). That is a national range, not a Missouri price.

In Missouri, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Missouri dollar figure, because the amount on your ticket depends on the court handling it and how the charge was written.

The most reliable way to find your cost is to read the citation, then check the court's payment instructions or ask the court clerk.

Court costs are included in the scheduled payable amounts. If your violation has a scheduled amount, that number already contains the costs rather than adding them on top.

How is the fine for a Missouri texting ticket set?

Missouri's traffic code defines the violation. The court then sets the amount that appears on your citation. You can browse the statutes themselves on Missouri statutes via Justia.

Two tickets for the same behavior can carry different amounts. The court that hears the case matters, and so does whether the charge was filed as a municipal ordinance violation or under state law.

Some courts publish a schedule of payable amounts so that drivers can pay without appearing. In those cases, court costs are included in the scheduled figure. Whether your citation qualifies for a scheduled payment is printed on the ticket or explained by the clerk.

Because we cannot see your citation, we do not estimate a Missouri total. Any specific dollar figure you see elsewhere should be checked against your own paperwork.

How many points does a texting ticket add in Missouri?

Missouri treats the points differently depending on where the charge is filed. A municipal conviction carries 2 points. A state charge carries 3 points (Missouri Department of Revenue).

Points are tied to a conviction. A ticket that has not been resolved, or one that is dismissed, is not the same as a conviction.

The suspension threshold is 8 points in 18 months. A single texting conviction at 2 or 3 points sits below that line. It still counts toward your total alongside any other moving violations in the same 18 months.

  • Municipal conviction: 2 points
  • State charge: 3 points
  • Suspension: 8 points in 18 months

The difference between 2 and 3 points comes down to how the charge was filed. Check your citation to see which court and which type of charge you have.

Does a texting ticket raise my insurance?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Missouri-specific one.

Your own change could be higher, lower or nothing at all. Insurers use their own rating rules, and they weigh your driving record, your policy and your history with the company.

The conviction is what usually matters to a rating review. That is one reason people look at how a ticket is resolved before paying it.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend insurers or predict your rate. If you want to know how your insurer treats a conviction, ask the insurer or your agent directly.

Can a texting ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes the outcome can change, but it depends on the court and the facts of the stop. We cannot tell you whether a particular ticket qualifies.

In general, a driver has these paths:

  • Pay the ticket. This is usually treated as a conviction, so points and insurance reporting may follow. Confirm this with the court.
  • Contest the ticket. You can ask the court for a hearing and present your side.
  • Ask about other outcomes. Some courts and prosecutors may consider amending a charge. Ask the clerk what your court allows.

Because the points differ for a municipal conviction (2 points) and a state charge (3 points), the type of charge can matter as much as the fine itself.

This is general information, not legal advice. If a license suspension or your insurance is a real concern, consider speaking with a licensed Missouri attorney.

What happens if I don't pay a Missouri texting ticket?

Ignoring a ticket rarely makes it go away. Courts generally have ways to respond to unpaid or unanswered citations, and those can add cost and complications.

Possible consequences can include additional penalties or action affecting your driving privileges. The details depend on the court and on what the citation says, so check your paperwork and contact the clerk.

If you cannot pay the amount on the ticket, or you need more time, ask the court what options exist before the deadline on your citation passes. We do not state a deadline here because it is printed on your ticket and varies by court.

If a conviction eventually lands on your record, the 2 or 3 points count toward the 8-points-in-18-months suspension threshold.

What should I check on my citation before deciding?

A few details on the ticket drive your cost and your record:

  1. The court name. This tells you who sets the amount and where to pay or appear.
  2. The charge type. A municipal charge and a state charge carry different points: 2 versus 3.
  3. The payable amount. If one is listed, court costs are included in it.
  4. Your options. Look for instructions on paying, contesting or contacting the clerk.
