How much is a texting / cell phone ticket in Vermont?

Nationally, a first texting or cell phone offense costs $20–$500, depending on the state (Vermont Department of Motor Vehicles). That range is a national spread. It is not a Vermont price.

In Vermont, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Vermont dollar figure here, because the number printed on your ticket is the one that counts.

Look at the citation itself. The amount, the violation description and the instructions for responding are all printed on it.

Two things can change what you actually pay compared with a friend's ticket. One is the court's own setting of the amount. The other is the statutory surcharges, which are included in the waiver amounts.

If your citation lists a waiver amount, that figure is meant to cover the fine and those surcharges together. Check whether any other costs appear on the paperwork before you pay.

How is the fine for a texting ticket set in Vermont?

The exact fine for this violation in Vermont is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The officer does not choose the amount on the roadside, and neither does a website or an insurer.

The state's statutes are available through Vermont statutes via Justia. If you want to see the wording of the law behind your citation, start there and match it to the violation described on your ticket.

Statutory surcharges are included in the waiver amounts. That means the number you see to pay without appearing is not just a base fine. It already folds those surcharges in.

For a broader view of how states treat handheld phone use, the Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a state-by-state law summary. It is useful for context, but your citation controls what you owe.

  • National range: $20–$500 for a first offense, by state.
  • Vermont: set by the court on the citation.
  • Surcharges: included in the waiver amounts.

How many points does a texting ticket carry in Vermont?

Vermont's points system runs 2–8 points by mph over. That description is written around speed, so it does not by itself tell you how many points a texting violation adds.

We do not state a point value for a texting conviction, because our sourced facts do not give one. The Vermont Department of Motor Vehicles is the place to confirm whether your specific violation adds points and how many.

What the state does set out is the suspension threshold: 10 points in 2 years = suspension. If you already have points on your record, any new conviction that adds points moves you closer to that line.

Before you decide how to respond, ask the DMV or check your own driving record for your current point total. Then compare it with the 10-point threshold.

Points matter beyond the fine. A ticket that costs a modest amount today can matter more if it pushes a record near suspension.

Does a texting ticket raise car insurance?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Vermont-specific one.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the violation is recorded. We cannot predict your quote, and we do not recommend any insurer.

Some practical points to keep in mind:

  • The 20% figure is an average across the country. Your result could be higher, lower or none at all.
  • The effect generally follows a conviction, so how a ticket is resolved can matter.
  • Insurers set their own rules, so the way to know is to ask yours how it treats a violation like this.

When you weigh whether to just pay, remember that the cost of a ticket is not only the amount on the citation. A premium increase, if one applies, can continue after the fine is paid.

Can a texting ticket be dismissed or reduced in Vermont?

Our sourced facts do not list specific dismissal or reduction programs for this violation in Vermont, so we will not describe any. What we can say is where to look.

Your citation sets out how to respond. It is the first document to read, since it ties your case to a particular court and explains your choices.

In general, people with a traffic citation can:

  1. Pay the amount on the citation, which resolves the ticket.
  2. Contest the ticket and present their case to the court.
  3. Contact the court named on the citation with questions about the process.

Each choice has a different effect on cost, points and insurance. Paying is usually treated as accepting the violation, while contesting keeps the outcome open.

What happens if I don't pay a texting ticket in Vermont?

Ignoring a citation rarely makes it go away. We do not list specific deadlines or penalty amounts here, because our sourced facts do not include them. Your citation should state the response requirements.

What is clear is the risk to your license. Vermont uses a points system, with a suspension threshold of 10 points in 2 years. An unresolved ticket can also lead to other consequences set by the court and the DMV.

If you think you may miss a deadline, contact the court on the citation before it passes. The Vermont Department of Motor Vehicles can answer questions about how a violation affects your driving record.

  • Read the response instructions on the citation.
  • Note the court and any listed deadline.
  • Ask the court or DMV directly if anything is unclear.
