How much is a texting or cell phone ticket in Michigan?

Nationally, a first-offense texting or cell phone ticket runs $20–$500 by state. In Michigan, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Michigan Secretary of State).

We do not list a single Michigan dollar figure because the sources do not give one. Any number you see elsewhere that claims to be the fixed Michigan price should be checked against your own citation.

The national $20–$500 range is a spread across states. It is not a Michigan fine schedule.

The amount you owe is also not just the fine. Court costs are added, and they are covered below.

How is the fine set, and what are court costs?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. That is why two drivers cited for the same thing can see different totals.

District court costs vary by court and make up much of the total. A court with higher costs can produce a bigger bill even when the base fine is similar.

  • The citation or the court's notice is the document that shows what you owe.
  • The court clerk can tell you the total amount and the accepted ways to pay.
  • Ask whether the total shown includes costs, not only the fine.

The Michigan traffic code is available through Michigan statutes via Justia, and Michigan's driver records are handled by the Michigan Secretary of State.

How many points does a texting ticket add in Michigan?

The locked point schedule for Michigan is 2–4 points by mph over under §257.320a. That schedule is written around speed, so it does not map neatly onto a texting or cell phone citation.

We therefore do not state a specific point value for a texting ticket. Check how your citation is scored with the court or the Secretary of State before assuming any number.

The threshold that matters is 12 points in 2 years, which leads to driver reexamination.

Points from different violations can add up over time. Even a citation with a low point value can matter if your record already has others.

Does a texting ticket raise my insurance?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Michigan-specific one.

How much your own rate changes depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the violation is recorded. We cannot predict your quote, and we do not recommend any insurer.

  • A conviction is more likely to affect rates than a ticket that is dismissed.
  • Ask your insurer how it treats a cell phone violation on your record.
  • Compare the possible premium increase with the cost of the ticket itself.

Over several years, the insurance effect can exceed the fine and court costs combined.

Can a texting ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome depends on the facts and on the court. We cannot tell you whether yours qualifies.

In general, a driver cited for a traffic violation can pay it, which usually means a conviction, or contest it. Some courts also have processes to discuss the charge. The court clerk can explain the options that apply to your citation.

  • Read the citation for the stated violation and the court's instructions.
  • Note any response deadline on the notice.
  • Contact the court listed on the citation with questions about procedure.

For advice on your specific case, talk with a licensed attorney. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice.

What happens if I don't pay a texting ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts can add costs, and unresolved tickets can lead to action against a driver's license.

The exact steps depend on the court and on Michigan procedure. Because district court costs vary by court, an overdue balance may grow in ways that differ from one court to the next.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court before the deadline and ask what it allows.

Keep your citation number and any receipts. They help if there is ever a dispute about what was paid.
