How much is a texting ticket in Indiana?

Nationally, a first-offense texting or cell phone ticket falls in a range of $20–$500 by state. In Indiana, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Indiana Bureau of Motor Vehicles).

That means no single number applies to every driver. The figure printed on your ticket, or the amount the court assigns, is the one that counts.

The $20–$500 range is a national figure. It is not a quote for Indiana, and we do not state an exact Indiana dollar amount.

Your total cost can also include county court costs on top of the fine. The next section explains how that works.

How is the fine set, and what are court costs?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. You can read the Indiana code itself through Indiana statutes via Justia.

County court costs are added to the judgment. They often exceed the fine for low speeds, and the same pattern can make a small fine look much larger once costs are included.

Because costs are county-based, two drivers with the same violation can owe different totals. Court costs vary by county and court.

  • Fine: set by the court on the citation.
  • County court costs: added to the judgment.
  • Total owed: the judgment amount, not just the fine line.

When you check what you owe, look for the full judgment amount rather than the fine alone. If the paperwork is unclear, the court clerk named on the citation is the place to confirm it.

How many points does a texting ticket add in Indiana?

The Indiana points system we have on file runs 2–8 points by mph over. Accumulating points triggers BMV probation and suspension.

That scale is described in terms of speed, so it does not map neatly onto a texting or cell phone violation. We do not state a specific point value for this offense.

To find out whether a conviction adds points to your record, check with the Indiana Bureau of Motor Vehicles or the court handling your citation.

The key point is that points add up. A single ticket may matter less than the total on your record, because it is the accumulation that triggers probation and suspension.

If you already have other moving violations, ask the BMV how your record currently stands before deciding how to handle this ticket.

Does a texting ticket raise insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Indiana-specific one.

Your own change could be higher, lower or nothing at all. Insurers set rates using their own rules, your driving history and the type of policy you hold.

  • The ~20% figure is an average across the country.
  • It applies to convictions, which is why how a case ends matters.
  • We do not recommend any insurer, and we cannot predict your quote.

If you are worried about your premium, ask your insurer how it treats a conviction of this kind. Do that before you decide whether to pay or contest the ticket.

Can a texting ticket be dismissed or reduced?

That depends on the court and the facts of your case. The citation will say how and where to respond, and the court clerk can explain the choices in your county.

In general, drivers with a traffic citation have a few paths:

  1. Pay the amount. This usually resolves the ticket, but it can mean a conviction on your record.
  2. Contest it. You can ask the court to hear your case.
  3. Ask the court what other resolutions exist. Availability varies by court.

Because a conviction is what typically affects insurance, the way a case ends can matter as much as the fine.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice or recommend attorneys. For advice on your specific case, consult a licensed attorney.

For a general view of how states treat distracted-driving laws, see Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws.

What happens if I don't pay a texting ticket?

Ignoring a citation rarely makes it go away. The court may take further action, and the amount owed can grow once court costs are added to the judgment.

Points also matter here. Accumulating points triggers BMV probation and suspension, so a ticket that ends in a conviction can add to a record that is already under pressure.

We do not list specific penalties, deadlines or late fees for Indiana, because those are set by the court and the citation. Read the ticket for the response instructions and contact the court clerk if anything is unclear.

  • Check the citation for how and where to respond.
  • Confirm the full amount owed, including county court costs.
  • Ask the court about your options before any deadline passes.
