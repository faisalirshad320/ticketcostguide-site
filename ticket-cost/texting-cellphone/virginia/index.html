How much is a texting or cell phone ticket in Virginia?

Nationally, a first-offense texting or cell phone ticket falls in a range of $20–$500 depending on the state (Virginia Department of Motor Vehicles). That range is a national picture, not a Virginia price.

In Virginia, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Virginia dollar figure here, because the amount you owe is the one printed on your ticket or set by the judge.

Read the citation first. The amount, the offense listed and the court named on it are what matter, not a national average.

On top of the fine, fixed processing costs apply to every prepaid ticket. If the case is treated as reckless, the court can add fines and fees.

How is the fine for a texting ticket set in Virginia?

The fine is set by the court within the state's traffic code. Lawmakers define the violation and the penalty framework, and the court applies it to your case.

That is why two drivers cited for similar conduct can see different totals. The offense charged, any added court-set amounts and the court handling the case can all change the final number.

The amount can differ by court, so a figure you hear from a friend or see in a forum may not match your citation. Treat outside numbers as rough context only.

  • Offense charged: the specific violation written on the citation.
  • Court costs: fixed processing costs apply to every prepaid ticket.
  • Reckless cases: court-set fines and fees are added.

For the state's statutes, see the Virginia code via Virginia statutes via Justia. For a state-by-state look at phone laws, see the Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws.

How many points does a texting ticket add in Virginia?

Virginia's system assigns 3–6 demerit points by offense, with 6 for reckless. Which number applies to you depends on the offense listed on your citation and any conviction that results.

The DMV suspends a license at point thresholds. Drivers also earn +1 safe point per clean year, which offsets the effect of demerit points over time (Virginia Department of Motor Vehicles).

Points generally follow a conviction, not just a citation. Ask the court what the outcome of your case would mean for your record.

To see your own point balance, check your driving record with the DMV. Do not assume a texting ticket carries the lowest or highest value in the range until you confirm the offense charged.

Does a texting ticket raise car insurance rates?

It can. Nationally, moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a prediction for your policy.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the case ends. A conviction is more likely to affect your rate than a ticket that is dismissed.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer. If you are concerned about a rate change, ask your own insurer how it treats moving violations, and compare that with the total cost of the ticket.

  • The ~20% figure is an average across the US.
  • Some drivers may see no change; others may see more.
  • The outcome of the case matters as much as the ticket itself.

Can a texting ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but that is decided by the court and the facts of your case. We cannot tell you whether yours qualifies, and this page is not legal advice.

Your usual choices are to prepay, to contest the ticket in court, or to ask the court what other resolutions it offers. Each has a different cost and record outcome.

  1. Prepay: fixed processing costs apply to every prepaid ticket. Paying usually resolves the case as a conviction, so points may follow.
  2. Contest: you appear before the court, which sets any fine within the state's traffic code.
  3. Ask about alternatives: the court clerk can tell you what the court allows for your citation.

If the ticket involves reckless driving, the stakes are higher. Reckless cases add court-set fines and fees and carry the top end of the point range.

If you want legal advice, consult a licensed attorney of your own choosing.

What happens if I don't pay a texting ticket in Virginia?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. Courts can add costs, and the DMV can act on unresolved matters. The exact steps depend on the court and the offense.

Do not rely on a national average for planning. The amount owed is the one on your citation, plus any fixed processing costs or court-set amounts.

Check the due date and court instructions on the citation. If anything is unclear, contact the court clerk directly.

Unpaid or unresolved tickets can also lead to license problems separate from point thresholds. Contact the court or the Virginia Department of Motor Vehicles to confirm the status of your record.
