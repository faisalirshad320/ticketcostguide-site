Nationally, a texting or cell phone ticket runs $20–$500 for a first offense depending on the state. In South Dakota, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (South Dakota Department of Public Safety). Check your citation or contact the court that issued it for your amount.

How much is a texting or cell phone ticket in South Dakota?

The exact amount is set by the court on your citation. We do not publish a single South Dakota dollar figure, because the amount you owe is the one printed on your ticket or given by the court.

For context, the national range for a first offense is $20–$500, depending on the state (South Dakota Department of Public Safety). That range is a national one. It is not a South Dakota price list.

Your citation is the best source for your amount. If the paper is unclear, contact the court named on it and ask what is owed on your specific charge.

Two drivers cited for the same thing can end up with different totals. The charge, the court, and your record can all affect what is owed.

How is the fine for a texting ticket set in South Dakota?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The officer writes the charge, and the court determines the amount tied to it.

That is why a national average is a weak guide for any one driver. The range of $20–$500 shows how widely states differ for a first offense. Within South Dakota, your own citation controls.

The statutes themselves are published online. You can read the state code through South Dakota statutes via Justia and look up the section listed on your citation.

  • Find the statute or code section printed on your ticket.
  • Read the exact wording of what you were charged with.
  • Compare it with what the court lists as owed.

If the section number on the ticket does not match the behavior described, ask the court clerk how to raise that.

Are court costs and surcharges added on top?

Court costs are included in the standard scheduled amounts. That means the scheduled figure on a ticket generally already accounts for them, rather than adding a separate line.

That is not a guarantee about every case. If you are told to appear in court instead of paying a scheduled amount, the total can be handled differently. Ask the clerk to confirm the full amount before you pay.

  • Look for a total on the citation, not just a base fine.
  • Ask whether the amount you are quoted is the full amount due.
  • Keep your receipt or confirmation after paying.

We do not list individual surcharge amounts here because we have no sourced figure for them. Anything you read elsewhere should be checked against your citation.

How many points does a texting ticket add in South Dakota?

South Dakota assigns points per conviction toward suspension thresholds. The threshold is 15 points in 12 months.

We do not state a point value for this specific violation. We have no sourced figure for it, and we would rather say so than guess. Your citation, the court, or the South Dakota Department of Public Safety are the places to confirm how a conviction is counted on your record.

Points attach to convictions. A ticket you have not yet resolved is not the same as a conviction, so how you respond to it matters.

If you already have other moving violations, ask how they stack against the 15-point threshold before you decide how to respond.

Does a texting ticket raise my insurance?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a South Dakota-specific one.

Your own change depends on your insurer, your driving history, and how the violation is recorded. Some drivers see no change at renewal. Others see a larger increase.

We do not recommend any insurer, and we cannot tell you what your policy will do. Your insurer or agent is the right source for that answer.

  • The increase usually shows up at renewal, not right away.
  • A conviction is what typically triggers a rate review.
  • Compare the possible premium change against the fine itself, since it can cost more over time.

Can a texting ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but we cannot promise an outcome. What is available depends on the court, the charge, and your record.

Your usual options are to pay, to contest the ticket, or to ask the court what other resolutions it offers. Each has different consequences for points and insurance, because points attach to convictions.

  1. Read the citation for the response deadline and instructions.
  2. Confirm the exact charge and statute section.
  3. Ask the court clerk which options apply to your case.
  4. Consider speaking with a licensed attorney if you want legal advice.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice or recommend specific attorneys. Check the statutes at South Dakota statutes via Justia and compare with Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws for an overview of how states treat distracted driving.

What happens if I don't pay a texting ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. The instructions and deadlines on the ticket are the ones that count.

Unresolved tickets can lead to additional court action. We do not have sourced figures for late fees or specific penalties, so we will not state any. The court named on your citation can tell you what applies.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court before the deadline and ask what is possible. Do this early rather than after a missed date.

Unpaid tickets can also complicate your driving record, which matters if you are already near the 15-points-in-12-months suspension threshold.
