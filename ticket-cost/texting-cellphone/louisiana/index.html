How much is a texting or cell phone ticket in Louisiana?

Nationally, a first-offense texting or cell phone ticket runs $20–$500 by state (Louisiana Office of Motor Vehicles). That is a national range, not a Louisiana price.

In Louisiana the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Louisiana dollar figure because the number you owe depends on the court handling your case.

The fine is only part of the bill. Local court costs vary by parish and often exceed the fine, so read the full amount due on your citation or court notice.

If you want the real number for your ticket, look at the citation itself or contact the clerk of the court named on it.

How is the fine set for a texting ticket in Louisiana?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The code defines the offense and the court decides the amount that applies to your case.

You can read the Louisiana statutes through Justia. Search the traffic and motor vehicle titles for the provisions on wireless devices and texting while driving.

For a state-by-state view of how texting and handheld laws differ, the Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a state laws overview.

Rules can change between legislative sessions. Confirm the current wording of the law before relying on any summary, including this one.

Why can court costs be higher than the fine?

Local court costs vary by parish and often exceed the fine. The fine is what the court assesses for the violation. Costs are separate charges added by the court handling the case.

Two people with the same type of ticket in different parishes can end up owing different totals. The difference is usually in the costs, not the fine.

  • Look for a line-by-line breakdown on the citation or court notice.
  • Ask the clerk of court what the total due is and which payment methods are accepted.
  • Keep a receipt or confirmation once you pay.

Do not assume a figure you read online matches your parish. Treat the court's number as the one that counts.

How many points does a texting ticket add in Louisiana?

Louisiana has no point system. License action follows repeat convictions and specific offenses rather than a running point total.

That does not mean a conviction has no effect on your license. Repeat convictions can matter, and the state tracks your driving record through the Louisiana Office of Motor Vehicles.

No points does not mean no consequences. A conviction can still appear on your driving record and may be seen by insurers.

If you are worried about your license status, check your record with the OMV or speak with a licensed attorney. We do not give legal advice.

Does a texting ticket raise car insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Louisiana-specific one.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the violation is recorded. Some drivers see little change and others see more.

  • The increase is tied to a conviction on your record, not just receiving a citation.
  • Insurers set their own rules, so the same ticket can cost different drivers different amounts.
  • Your insurer can tell you how it treats a moving violation at renewal.

We do not recommend any insurer. If the premium impact matters to you, compare quotes yourself before and after your case is resolved.

Can a texting ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes outcomes other than paying in full are possible, but that depends on the court, the parish and the facts of your case. We cannot promise any outcome.

Common steps people consider include:

  1. Reading the citation carefully for the court, the date and the instructions on how to respond.
  2. Contacting the clerk of court to ask what options the court offers for your type of citation.
  3. Deciding whether to pay or to contest the ticket in court.
  4. Consulting a licensed attorney if you want advice on your situation.

Paying a ticket is generally treated as resolving the citation, which may mean a conviction on your record. Ask the court how payment is handled before you pay if that concerns you.

What happens if I don't pay a texting ticket in Louisiana?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts can add costs, and unresolved traffic matters can lead to action against your driving privileges. The details depend on the court.

Because local court costs vary by parish and often exceed the fine, the amount owed can grow once a deadline is missed. We do not state specific penalties or deadlines here, since they depend on your citation.

If you cannot pay by the due date, contact the clerk of court before the deadline and ask what options exist.

Check your status with the Louisiana Office of Motor Vehicles if you are unsure whether anything has been reported against your license.
