How much is a texting / cell phone ticket in Delaware?

Nationally, a first-offense texting or cell phone ticket costs $20–$500 depending on the state (Delaware Division of Motor Vehicles). That is a national range, not a Delaware price.

In Delaware, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Delaware dollar figure because the number you owe is the one printed on your ticket or assigned by the court handling your case.

Bottom line: Use the $20–$500 range only to understand how states differ. Your real cost is whatever the Delaware court puts on your citation, plus court costs and state fees.

If your citation is unclear, the court named on it is the place to confirm the amount before you pay or respond.

How is a Delaware texting fine set?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The statute establishes the framework, and the court applies it to your citation.

That is why two drivers cited for the same thing can see different totals on paper. The court, the details of the citation and any added costs all play a part.

  • The statute: Delaware's traffic code defines the violation. You can read the codes through Delaware statutes via Justia.
  • The court: It sets the amount that appears on the citation.
  • The add-ons: Court costs and state fees are added to the statutory fine.

For a national comparison of how states treat distracted driving, the Governors Highway Safety Association state laws page is a useful reference.

Are court costs and fees added to the fine?

Yes. Court costs and state fees are added to the statutory fine. The amount on your citation or payment notice may therefore be higher than the base fine alone.

When you compare your ticket to the national $20–$500 range, remember that the range describes fines by state. It does not tell you how a particular court adds its costs.

Before paying, check whether the figure you see is the fine only or the total due. If the notice does not itemize, the court clerk named on the citation can explain what is included.

How many points does a texting ticket carry in Delaware?

Delaware's points system is described as 2–5 points by mph over. That description is built around speed, so it does not tell us the exact point value for a texting or cell phone conviction.

We therefore do not state a point value for this violation. Check your citation, the court, or the Delaware Division of Motor Vehicles to find out whether points apply to your conviction and how many.

The thresholds below are the part that matters most if points do apply:

  • 12 points in 2 years: a behavior modification course.
  • 14 or more points: suspension.

Points from a texting conviction, if any, would add to everything else on your record. A driver already carrying points should look closely at how close they are to these thresholds.

Does a texting ticket raise car insurance in Delaware?

It can. Nationally, moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Delaware-specific one.

Whether your insurer raises your rate depends on the insurer, your driving history and whether the ticket ends in a conviction. We cannot predict your premium or recommend a particular insurer.

  • Ask your insurer how it treats a conviction for this type of violation.
  • Compare the possible premium change against the cost of the ticket, not just the fine.
  • Keep any court paperwork that shows how the case ended.

Because insurance effects usually last longer than the fine itself, the outcome of the case can matter more than the amount paid.

Can a texting ticket be dismissed or reduced in Delaware?

Possibly, but the outcome is up to the court. The fine is set by the court on the citation, and the court decides how your case is resolved.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot say whether your ticket qualifies for dismissal or a reduction. Here is what you can do to prepare:

  1. Read the citation carefully for the court name, the violation listed and any response instructions.
  2. Contact the court to ask what options exist for your case.
  3. Read the relevant statute through Delaware statutes via Justia so you understand the charge.
  4. If you want legal advice, consider speaking with a licensed attorney of your own choosing.

A reduction could affect more than the fine. It could also change whether points or an insurance increase follow.

What happens if I don't pay a Delaware texting ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts commonly add consequences for missed payments or missed responses, and these can affect your driving record or license.

The specific consequences in Delaware depend on the court and the citation. We do not list penalty amounts here because they are not part of our locked figures.

  • Check the response instructions on your citation first.
  • Contact the court if you cannot pay the full amount or need more information.
  • Confirm your status with the Delaware Division of Motor Vehicles if you are worried about your license.

Because court costs and state fees are added to the fine, waiting rarely lowers what you owe and may leave you with more to resolve.
