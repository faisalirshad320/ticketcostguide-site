How much is a texting or cell phone ticket in Colorado?

Nationally, a first-offense texting or cell phone ticket costs $20–$500, depending on the state (Colorado Division of Motor Vehicles). That range is a national figure, not a Colorado price.

In Colorado, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not state a specific Colorado dollar amount, because the number you owe is the one printed on your ticket or set by the court handling your case.

Read your citation first. The amount, the court name and the violation listed there decide what you owe, not the national range.

The base fine is also not the whole bill. A statutory surcharge is added to each penalty, so the total can be higher than the fine alone.

How is the fine for a texting ticket set in Colorado?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. Colorado statutes are published online through Colorado statutes via Justia, and they are the primary text for what the offense is and how it is penalized.

What that means in practice:

  • The court, not a national table, determines the amount.
  • The amount appears on the citation or in the court's notice.
  • Surcharges and any work zone doubling are applied on top of the base penalty.

If the citation lists a fine and your court's payment notice shows a higher total, the difference is usually the surcharge or other court costs. Ask the court clerk for an itemized total.

Do surcharges and work zones make the ticket cost more?

Yes. A statutory surcharge is added to each penalty in Colorado, and doubled fines apply in work zones. Both raise what you pay compared with the base fine.

We have no locked figure for the surcharge amount, so we do not give one. The court's payment notice will show the total.

If your ticket was issued in a work zone, expect the penalty to be higher than for the same violation elsewhere.

When you compare costs, use the total due, not just the fine line on the citation.

How many points does a texting ticket add in Colorado?

Colorado's points system assigns 1–6 points by mph over. That scale is built around speed, so it does not map cleanly onto a phone violation.

We do not state a specific point value for a texting or cell phone ticket. Check your citation, the court, or the Colorado Division of Motor Vehicles to see how your conviction is scored.

The suspension threshold is fixed: 12 points in 12 months for an adult driver leads to suspension. Points from other tickets in the same period count toward that total.

  • Add up any other moving violations from the past 12 months.
  • Ask the DMV how a phone-related conviction is recorded on your record.

Does a texting ticket raise my insurance?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Colorado quote.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your driving record and whether the ticket ends in a conviction. We do not recommend any insurer and cannot predict your rate.

A ticket that is dismissed or does not result in a conviction may not be treated the same way as a conviction. Your insurer or agent can tell you how they handle it.

Can a texting ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Outcomes are decided by the court. Some courts allow a plea to a different charge, or a dismissal for reasons specific to the case. We cannot say which options your court offers.

Steps that are generally worth taking:

  1. Read the citation for the court, the listed violation and the response instructions.
  2. Contact the court clerk to ask what options exist for your case.
  3. Request an itemized total so you can see the fine and surcharge separately.
  4. Consider speaking with a licensed attorney if a conviction would put your license at risk.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice or recommend attorneys.

What happens if I don't pay a texting ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts can add penalties, and unresolved tickets can lead to license problems or further action.

The specific consequences and deadlines are set by the court and the Colorado traffic code, so use the dates on your citation. If you cannot pay the full amount, ask the clerk whether the court offers other arrangements.

Do not rely on a national range to decide whether to respond. Respond to the court listed on your citation.
