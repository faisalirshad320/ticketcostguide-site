How much is a texting / cell phone ticket in North Dakota?

Nationally, a first-offense texting or cell phone ticket runs $20–$500 depending on the state (North Dakota Department of Transportation). That range is a national spread, not a North Dakota price.

In North Dakota, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish an exact North Dakota dollar figure because the amount is set within the state's traffic code and appears on your ticket.

The number printed on your citation, or provided by the court that handles it, is the number that matters. Use the national range only as context.

How is the fine set?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within North Dakota's traffic code. The citation or the court is where you find the amount for your case.

The state statutes are published through Justia (North Dakota statutes via Justia). They are the place to read the legal text behind the charge.

If the citation lists a specific amount, that is usually what you will be asked to pay. If it does not, contact the court clerk named on the ticket and ask. Keep a written record of what you are told.

Are there extra court costs and surcharges?

Usually not many. The statutory fee is usually the whole amount, so there is no large cost stack on top of it.

That is different from states where a small base fine grows once assessments and fees are added. In North Dakota, the amount on the citation is generally the figure to plan around.

  • Check the citation for any listed fee beyond the fine.
  • Ask the court clerk if the total is unclear.
  • Get the payment amount and deadline in writing.

How many points does a texting ticket add in North Dakota?

North Dakota's points schedule assigns points by mph over the limit, and 12+ points means suspension (North Dakota Department of Transportation).

That schedule is built around speed. A texting ticket has no speed component, so we cannot state a point value for it from our sourced facts.

Ask the Department of Transportation or the court whether this violation carries points, and how many. Do not assume zero or any other number until you have confirmed it.

The 12+ point threshold is the only point figure we can confirm here. It applies to suspension, not to this specific violation.

Does a texting ticket raise insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a North Dakota or insurer-specific one.

Your own change could be higher, lower, or zero. It depends on your insurer, your driving record, and whether the ticket ends in a conviction.

We do not recommend insurers. If you are worried about a rate change, ask your insurer how it treats a citation like this before you decide how to respond.

Can a texting ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome depends on the facts and on the court. The citation will explain how to pay, how to contest, and the deadline for each.

Many people look at the ticket for errors first: wrong date, wrong location, or a description that does not match what happened. Whether an error matters is a legal question.

  • Read the citation for response deadlines.
  • Contact the court clerk about your options.
  • Consider asking a licensed attorney if a conviction could affect your license or job.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot give legal advice or recommend an attorney.

What happens if I don't pay?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. The court can treat a missed deadline as a failure to respond, and that can bring added consequences.

We do not list specific late penalties because they are not in our sourced facts. The citation and the court clerk are the reliable sources for your deadline and what follows a missed one.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court before the deadline and ask what is available. Getting an answer in writing protects you later.
