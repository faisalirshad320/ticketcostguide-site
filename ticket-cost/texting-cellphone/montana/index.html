How much is a texting / cell phone ticket in Montana?

Nationally, a first-offense texting or cell phone ticket runs $20–$500, depending on the state (Montana Department of Justice — Motor Vehicle Division). That range is a national one, not a Montana price.

In Montana, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Montana dollar figure because the amount you owe is the one the court assigns to your case.

The number printed on your citation, or the one the court clerk gives you, is the one that counts. A national range cannot tell you what your court will charge.

Court surcharges are added to the statutory fine. Your total can therefore be higher than the base fine alone, and the surcharge amount is not something we state here.

How is the fine for a texting ticket set in Montana?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. That means the court, not a national chart or a website, decides the amount that applies to your citation.

Several things can sit behind the final total:

  • The statutory fine for the violation as charged.
  • Court surcharges added on top of that fine.
  • Any other costs the court lists on your paperwork.

Because the amount depends on the court and the charge, two drivers cited for similar conduct can see different totals. Read the citation closely for the charge and the statute or ordinance listed.

The Montana traffic code is available through Montana statutes via Justia. It is a reference for reading the law, not a substitute for the figure on your citation.

How many points does a texting ticket add in Montana?

Convictions carry conviction points toward Montana's habitual-offender threshold. That threshold is 30 points in 3 years, which results in revocation (Montana Department of Justice — Motor Vehicle Division).

We do not list a point value for a texting or cell phone conviction, because it is not among the figures we have confirmed. Your citation, the court, or the Motor Vehicle Division can tell you whether and how many points apply to your specific charge.

Two points about how this works:

  • Points attach to convictions, not to the act of receiving a citation.
  • The 3-year window means older convictions can matter alongside a new one.

If you already have other moving-violation convictions, ask what your current total is before deciding how to respond to a new ticket.

Does a texting ticket raise car insurance?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Montana one, and not a promise for your policy.

What you actually pay depends on your insurer, your driving record, and how the insurer rates this kind of conviction. Some insurers weigh recent convictions more heavily than others.

The ~20% figure is an average across the US. Your own change could be smaller, larger, or none at all.

A conviction is what generally gets reported and rated. That is one reason some drivers look closely at their options before simply paying. TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer, and we cannot predict your quote.

Can a texting ticket be dismissed or reduced in Montana?

It may be possible, but outcomes depend on the court and the facts of the case. We cannot tell you whether yours qualifies, and nothing here is legal advice.

In general, a driver cited for a texting or cell phone violation has a few paths:

  1. Pay the citation. This usually resolves the case, but it typically means a conviction, with the points and insurance effects described above.
  2. Contest the citation. You can ask the court for a hearing and present your side.
  3. Ask the court what else is available. Some courts may offer other resolutions. Availability varies, so the clerk is the right person to ask.

Before choosing, check the deadline and instructions on the citation itself. If the stakes feel high, for example because of your point total, you may want to speak with a licensed attorney of your own choosing.

What happens if I don't pay a texting ticket in Montana?

Ignoring a citation rarely makes it go away. Depending on the court, unpaid or unanswered tickets can lead to added costs, further court action, or problems with your driving privileges.

We do not list specific deadlines or penalty amounts, because those come from the court handling your case. Your citation should state how and when to respond.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court clerk before the deadline passes. Courts differ on what they allow, and asking early is usually better than missing the date.

Remember that surcharges are added to the fine, so the total you owe may be more than the base fine you expect.
