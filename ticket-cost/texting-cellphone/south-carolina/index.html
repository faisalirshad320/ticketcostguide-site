How much is a texting / cell phone ticket in South Carolina?

Nationally, a first texting or cell phone offense carries a fine of $20–$500 depending on the state (South Carolina DMV). That is a national range, not a South Carolina price.

In South Carolina, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single statewide dollar figure, because the number you owe is the one printed on your ticket or given by the court that handles it.

Read the citation first. It shows the charge, the court, and the amount or the way to find out the amount owed.

Keep in mind that the fine is only part of the bill. Statewide assessments and surcharges are added on top, as the next section explains.

How is the fine set, and what gets added to it?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The traffic code sets the framework, and the court that hears your case applies it to your citation. You can read the code through South Carolina statutes via Justia.

Statewide assessments and surcharges are added to every fine and usually exceed it. A ticket with a modest base fine can therefore cost much more once everything is added up.

  • Base fine: set by the court on the citation.
  • Assessments and surcharges: added statewide to every fine.
  • Total due: the combined amount, which is what you actually pay.

When you compare a ticket to a national range like $20–$500, remember that the range describes fines, and your total may include more than that.

How many points does a texting ticket add in South Carolina?

South Carolina uses a points system. The locked figures we rely on describe it as 2–6 points by mph over, with 12 points leading to suspension, and points halve after 12 months (South Carolina DMV).

That scale is written around speed, so it does not tell you the point value for a texting or cell phone offense. We do not guess at a number. Check the violation listed on your citation and the DMV's point information to see how your specific charge is scored.

Points matter because 12 points = suspension. Any points from a texting conviction add to what is already on your record.

Because points halve after 12 months, the same ticket carries less weight on your record as time passes. A driver who already has points should be more careful about what a conviction adds.

Does a texting ticket raise my insurance?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a South Carolina one.

Your own change depends on your insurer, your driving record, and whether the ticket ends in a conviction. We do not recommend any insurer and cannot predict your rate. If you want to know what a conviction would do to your premium, ask your insurer or agent directly.

  • The 20% figure is an average, so your change may be higher or lower.
  • The effect generally follows a conviction on your record, not just the citation itself.
  • Points and premiums are separate things. A ticket can affect one more than the other.

Can a texting ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Whether a ticket can be dismissed or reduced is decided by the court that has your case. The citation explains how to respond, and the court clerk can tell you what choices are available for your charge.

In general, drivers with a traffic citation can pay it, contest it, or ask the court about other resolutions. Paying is usually treated as accepting the charge, which can mean a conviction and any points that go with it. Contesting means appearing before the court.

  1. Read the citation for the charge, the court, and the response instructions.
  2. Contact the court named on the ticket to confirm the amount and your choices.
  3. Check how the charge would be scored under the state's points system.
  4. If you want legal advice, speak with a licensed attorney. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not recommend attorneys.

Because surcharges usually exceed the fine, ask the court for the full amount due, not just the base fine.

What happens if I don't pay a texting ticket?

Ignoring a ticket rarely makes it go away. Courts set their own consequences for unpaid or unanswered citations, and the details depend on the court handling your case.

The citation and the court clerk are the reliable places to learn what applies to you. If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court before the due date and ask what it allows. We do not state specific deadlines or penalties here because they are not part of our locked figures.

Unresolved points and convictions also feed into the 12-point suspension threshold, so letting a ticket slide can create bigger problems than the original fine.
