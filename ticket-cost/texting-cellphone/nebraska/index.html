How much is a texting or cell phone ticket in Nebraska?

Nationally, a first-offense texting or cell phone ticket costs $20–$500 depending on the state (Nebraska Department of Motor Vehicles, last reviewed Oct 2026). That is a national range, not a Nebraska price.

In Nebraska, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Nebraska dollar figure because the amount you owe depends on what the court assigns and what is added on top.

The number printed on your citation, or provided by the court clerk, is the number that matters. Treat any other figure you see online, including the national range here, as background only.

Court costs are added statewide, so the total you pay is usually more than the base fine alone.

How is the fine set?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. You can read Nebraska's statutes through Nebraska statutes via Justia. Statute text is the authority on what the offense is and what penalty range applies.

Three things can shape what you owe:

  • The court's decision. The court sets the exact amount and writes it on the citation.
  • Court costs. These are added statewide on top of the fine.
  • Zone doubling. Fines are doubled in some zones, so the location of the stop can change the amount.

Because of these layers, two drivers cited for the same behavior can owe different totals. Read the citation closely and ask the clerk for an itemized total if it is unclear.

How many points does a texting ticket add in Nebraska?

Nebraska's points system assigns 1–3 points by mph over for speed-related violations. A texting or cell phone citation is not a speed violation, so that tiering does not map cleanly onto it.

We therefore do not state a point value for texting. Ask the court clerk or check with the Nebraska Department of Motor Vehicles how your specific citation is treated on your driving record.

The key threshold is this: 12 points in 2 years = revocation. Points from every violation count toward that total, so a texting conviction on top of earlier tickets can matter more than it looks.

Points and fines are separate. Paying the fine on time does not erase points that come with a conviction.

Does a texting ticket raise insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Nebraska-specific one.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your record and how the violation is reported. We do not recommend any insurer and cannot predict your rate.

Two practical points:

  • A conviction is what usually triggers a rate review. How your case ends matters.
  • Your policy and renewal notice are the best guide to how your insurer treats violations.

If you want to know how the ticket would be reported, ask the court how the case will be recorded before you decide how to respond.

Can a texting ticket be dismissed or reduced in Nebraska?

Often there are options, but the court controls them. Because the court sets the amount on the citation, the court is also where you ask about your choices.

Common paths people consider:

  • Pay the fine. This usually resolves the case but can mean a conviction and any points that go with it.
  • Contest the citation. You can ask for a hearing and present your side.
  • Ask the clerk about alternatives. Some courts offer other ways to resolve a citation. Availability varies by court, so ask.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot say which option fits your situation. For legal advice, speak with a licensed attorney of your choosing.

Check the response deadline on the citation. Missing it can limit your options.

What happens if I don't pay?

Ignoring a citation generally makes things worse. Unpaid or unanswered tickets can lead to added penalties, and in many places to license consequences.

We do not list specific late fees or deadlines because they are not part of our sourced figures. The citation and the court's notices spell out what applies to your case.

Remember the 12 points in 2 years = revocation threshold. A conviction that adds points can bring you closer to it, so a missed payment is not the only risk to your license.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court clerk before the deadline and ask what the court allows.

What should I check on my citation?

Before paying or responding, look at these items:

  1. The amount. Confirm whether it is the base fine or a total that includes court costs.
  2. The location. Fines are doubled in some zones, so see whether a zone applies.
  3. The offense description. Make sure it matches what happened.
  4. The deadline and court. Note where and when you must respond.
  5. Point treatment. Ask how the violation is recorded on your driving record.

These checks cost nothing and give you the facts you need to decide whether to pay, contest or ask about alternatives.
