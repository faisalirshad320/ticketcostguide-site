How much is a texting or cell phone ticket in Utah?

Nationally, a first-offense texting or cell phone ticket falls in a range of $20–$500, depending on the state (Utah Driver License Division). That range is a national figure. It is not the Utah fine.

In Utah, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Utah dollar figure because the amount you owe is the one printed on your ticket or assigned by the court handling it.

Bottom line: Read the citation. The amount and the due date on that document control what you owe, not a general estimate.

The total can also include other items. Zone enhancements and fees are built into the schedule, so two drivers cited for the same act can see different totals.

How is the fine for a cell phone ticket set in Utah?

The exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. Utah's statutes are available through Utah statutes via Justia, and the Driver License Division publishes licensing information at dld.utah.gov.

Three things shape the number on your citation:

  • The traffic code. It sets the framework the court works within.
  • The court's schedule. Zone enhancements and fees are built into the schedule.
  • The facts of the stop. The citation records what you were cited for and where.

Because the court sets the amount, we do not guess at it. If the amount on your citation looks different from something you saw online, trust the citation and confirm with the court clerk.

How many points does a texting ticket add in Utah?

Our locked data for Utah covers the points system as a whole. Speeding carries 35–75 points by mph over on Utah's scale. A suspension applies at 200 points in 3 years for drivers ages 21 and older.

Those numbers describe speeding. We do not have a locked point value for a texting or cell phone violation, so we will not state one. Confirm the point value for your specific offense with the Utah Driver License Division or on your driving record.

The 200-point threshold matters because points from different violations add up over the same 3-year window. A texting conviction plus other tickets can bring you closer to it.

Points and fines are separate. Paying the fine does not remove points that come with a conviction.

Does a texting ticket raise car insurance?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Utah-specific one.

Whether a cell phone ticket affects your rate depends on several things:

  • Whether the ticket results in a conviction on your record.
  • Your insurer's own rating rules.
  • Your prior driving history.
  • When your policy next renews.

We do not recommend insurers or tell you what to do with your policy. If you are concerned, ask your insurer how it treats this type of violation. The most useful question is whether a conviction, as opposed to just a citation, changes your rate.

Can a texting ticket be dismissed or reduced in Utah?

Your options are set by the court handling the citation. The citation lists the court and the steps for responding. Contact the court clerk to learn what choices are available for your case.

Common ways people respond to a ticket include:

  • Paying it. This usually resolves the matter, but it can mean a conviction and any points that go with it.
  • Contesting it. You ask the court to hear the case.
  • Asking about alternatives. Some courts offer other resolutions. Ask the clerk what your court allows.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot give legal advice. If you are weighing a contest or are worried about your license, you may want to speak with a licensed Utah attorney of your own choosing.

Do not assume paying is risk-free. A conviction can carry points and may affect insurance.

What happens if I don't pay a texting ticket in Utah?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. The citation sets a deadline or court date, and missing it can lead to additional consequences set by the court.

Because fees are built into the court's schedule, delays can leave you owing more than the original amount. Unresolved matters can also create problems with your license. The Utah Driver License Division is the place to check your license status.

If you cannot pay by the due date, contact the court before the deadline and ask what options exist. Courts handle these questions every day, and asking early is better than waiting.
