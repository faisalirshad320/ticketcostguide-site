How much is a texting / cell phone ticket in Ohio?

Nationally, a first-offense texting or cell phone ticket runs $20–$500, depending on the state (Ohio Bureau of Motor Vehicles). That is a national range, not an Ohio price.

In Ohio, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Ohio dollar figure because the amount you owe is the one printed on your ticket or stated by the court that handles your case.

Bottom line: use the national range only as a rough guide. Your citation, or the clerk of the court named on it, is the authority for what you owe.

The fine is also only part of the cost. Court costs, points and insurance can add to the total, as the sections below explain.

How is the fine set for a cell phone ticket in Ohio?

The court sets the fine within the state's traffic code. Ohio's statutes are available through Ohio statutes via Justia, and the Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a state-by-state overview of distracted driving laws at GHSA State Laws.

Several things can affect what is written on your citation:

  • The specific charge the officer wrote, such as texting versus holding a phone.
  • Whether it is a first offense or a repeat.
  • The court that hears the case.

We cannot tell you which charge or amount applies to your situation. Read the citation closely and look for the court name, the charge, and the amount or appearance instructions.

Why can the total be higher than the fine?

Municipal court costs vary by court and often exceed the fine. So two drivers cited for the same thing in different Ohio courts can pay different totals.

When you look at what you owe, separate these items if the court lists them:

  • The fine itself.
  • Court costs and any surcharges.
  • Any extra charges for payment plans or late payment, if the court applies them.

The total on the citation or on the court's payment notice is the number that matters. If the breakdown is unclear, ask the clerk of the court.

Do not budget from the fine alone. Because court costs often exceed the fine, the total can be much higher than the fine suggests.

How many points does a texting / cell phone ticket carry in Ohio?

Our locked Ohio points facts are about speeding. Ohio assigns 2 points for most speeding convictions, with 0 points for small overages in some circumstances and 4 points at 30+ over. A driver who reaches 12 points in 2 years faces a suspension (Ohio Bureau of Motor Vehicles).

We do not state a point value for texting or cell phone violations, because we have no verified figure for it. Check the Ohio BMV or ask the court how your specific charge is treated.

Points matter because they add up. Points from a texting conviction, if any apply, count alongside points from other violations toward the 12-point threshold.

Does a texting / cell phone ticket raise insurance in Ohio?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Ohio-specific one.

Whether your own rate changes depends on your insurer, your driving record and whether the ticket ends in a conviction. We do not recommend insurers or predict your premium.

If you are weighing options on the ticket, the possible insurance effect is worth counting alongside the fine and court costs. Over time it can matter more than the fine itself.

Can a texting ticket be dismissed or reduced in Ohio?

Sometimes outcomes other than paying in full are possible, but that is decided by the court and the facts of your case. We cannot say whether you qualify.

Your usual choices are:

  • Pay the ticket. This generally resolves the case, but it may result in a conviction on your record.
  • Contest the ticket. You ask the court for a hearing and present your side.
  • Ask the court about alternatives. Some courts have their own procedures. The clerk can tell you what is available.

Paying can be treated as a conviction, and a conviction is what drives points and insurance effects. Read the citation for deadlines and instructions.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice. For advice on your case, consider speaking with a licensed Ohio attorney of your choosing.

What happens if I don't pay or respond?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. The court can add consequences beyond the original fine, and the details depend on the court and your record.

Steps worth taking:

  1. Find the court name and response instructions on your citation.
  2. Contact the clerk of the court to confirm your total and your options.
  3. Respond within the time the citation or court notice gives.

If you cannot pay in full, ask the clerk whether the court offers a payment arrangement. Do this before the deadline passes.
