How much is a texting or cell phone ticket in Rhode Island?

Nationally, a first-offense texting or cell phone ticket falls between $20 and $500, depending on the state (Rhode Island Division of Motor Vehicles). That is a national range, not a Rhode Island price.

In Rhode Island, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Rhode Island dollar figure, because the amount that applies to you is the one printed on your ticket or stated by the court.

Treat the national range as a rough frame only. Your own citation is the only reliable source for what you owe.

Look at the citation first. The amount, the violation described and any court instructions on it matter more than any average.

How is the fine for a cell phone ticket set in Rhode Island?

The fine is set by the court within the state's traffic code. It is not a number the officer picks at the roadside.

Rhode Island handles traffic violations through the Traffic Tribunal. The court's scheduled amount for the violation appears on your citation, and the court can tell you how it applies to your case.

The national range exists because states set their own penalties. Some are at the low end of $20–$500 and some at the high end. Rhode Island's amount should be read from the citation, not estimated from the national range.

The state's statutes are available through Rhode Island statutes via Justia. The Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a state-by-state summary of distracted driving laws at GHSA State Laws. Neither replaces the figure on your citation.

Are there extra court fees on top of the fine?

Yes. Court technology and assessment fees are added to the scheduled fine. That means the total you pay can be higher than the base fine alone.

We do not state a dollar amount for these fees, because the locked sources we rely on do not give one. The citation or the court's notice should show the full amount due.

  • Ask whether the amount shown is the base fine or the total.
  • Check for added fees before you budget for the ticket.
  • Keep the paperwork showing what you paid.

If the numbers do not add up, contact the court clerk and ask for an itemized breakdown.

How many points does a texting ticket carry in Rhode Island?

Rhode Island has no running point system for ordinary speeding. The Traffic Tribunal instead escalates sanctions, including suspension, for repeat offenses.

This is the state context we can source. We do not state a point value for a texting or cell phone violation, because we have no sourced figure for one.

In practice, a first ticket and a repeat ticket may be treated differently. Repeat violations can bring stronger sanctions, so a prior record matters. For questions about your own driving record, the Rhode Island Division of Motor Vehicles is the place to ask.

Does a texting ticket raise my insurance?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Rhode Island one.

Whether your own rate changes depends on your insurer, your record and how the ticket is resolved. We cannot predict your premium or recommend an insurer.

  • A conviction is more likely to affect rates than a ticket that is dismissed.
  • Insurers set their own rules for how far back they look.
  • Your insurer or agent can tell you how they treat moving violations.

Because the premium effect can be larger than the fine itself, it is worth weighing when you decide how to respond.

Can a texting ticket be dismissed or reduced?

That depends on the facts and on the court. We cannot say whether your ticket qualifies, and this page is not legal advice.

In general, a person who receives a citation can pay it or ask the court to hear the case. Paying is usually treated as accepting the violation. Contesting it means presenting your side to the Traffic Tribunal.

  • Read the citation for the response instructions and any deadline the court gives.
  • Check that the violation described matches what happened.
  • Ask the court clerk what options exist for your situation.
  • Consider speaking with a licensed attorney if the stakes are high, such as a repeat offense.

Since the Traffic Tribunal escalates sanctions for repeat offenses, your history may affect how risky it is to simply pay.

What happens if I don't pay a texting ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts commonly add penalties for missed responses, and unresolved traffic matters can lead to license consequences.

We do not state specific late fees or deadlines here, because we have no sourced figures for them. The citation and the court's notices set out what applies to you.

Rhode Island's Traffic Tribunal escalates sanctions, including suspension, for repeat offenses. An unresolved ticket can complicate your record, so respond to it before the court's stated deadline.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court clerk and ask what arrangements are available.
