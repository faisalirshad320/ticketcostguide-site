How much is a texting or cell phone ticket in Mississippi?

Nationally, a first-offense texting or cell phone ticket falls in a range of $20–$500, depending on the state (Mississippi Department of Public Safety). That is a national range, not a Mississippi price tag.

In Mississippi, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Mississippi dollar figure here because the number you owe is the one printed on your ticket or confirmed by the court that handles it.

Bottom line: the national range is $20–$500 for a first offense by state. Your Mississippi amount is whatever the court sets on your citation, plus mandatory state assessments.

Read the citation closely. It usually shows the charge, the court, and instructions for responding.

How is the fine set, and why is the total higher than the fine?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The judge or court sets the amount that appears on your citation, so two drivers cited under the same law can see different paperwork depending on the court.

On top of the fine, mandatory state assessments are added to every fine and often exceed it. That means the base fine is only part of what you pay.

  • Base fine: set by the court on the citation.
  • State assessments: mandatory and added to every fine.
  • Total due: the combined amount the court tells you to pay.

When you compare what you owe with a national figure like $20–$500, remember that the national range is a first-offense fine by state. It does not include Mississippi's added assessments.

Ask the court clerk for the full amount due, not just the fine line. That is the number that matters when you decide how to respond.

How many points does a texting ticket add in Mississippi?

Mississippi has no point system. A texting or cell phone conviction therefore does not add points to your license in the way it would in a state that tracks them.

That does not make the ticket harmless. Suspensions follow repeat convictions and specific offenses, so a conviction still becomes part of your driving history.

  • No points are assigned under a point system, because the state has none.
  • Repeat convictions can lead to suspension.
  • Certain specific offenses can also lead to suspension.

For license questions, the Mississippi Department of Public Safety is the official source (Mississippi Department of Public Safety). We do not state suspension thresholds here because they are not part of our locked facts.

Does a texting ticket raise car insurance in Mississippi?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Mississippi-specific one.

Whether a texting ticket counts as a moving violation for your insurer, and how much your rate changes, depends on your insurer and your record. We do not recommend any insurer and cannot predict your quote.

  • The ~20% figure is an average across the US.
  • Your own change could be higher, lower, or none.
  • Insurers usually look at convictions, not just citations.

If the cost matters to you, ask your insurer how it treats a conviction like this before you decide how to respond to the citation.

Can a texting ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Possibly, but the outcome depends on the court and the facts of your case. Because the court sets the amount on the citation, the court is also where you ask about your options.

Common steps people take are:

  1. Read the citation for the charge, the court, and the response instructions.
  2. Contact the court clerk to confirm the total due, including state assessments.
  3. Decide whether to pay or contest the ticket.
  4. If you want legal advice, consider speaking with a licensed Mississippi attorney. We do not recommend a specific one.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice. The statutes are available through Justia (Mississippi statutes via Justia) if you want to read the traffic code yourself.

Paying a ticket generally means accepting the conviction. Since a conviction can affect insurance, weigh that before you pay.

What happens if I don't pay a texting ticket in Mississippi?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. The court that set the amount controls what happens next, and the consequences for missing a deadline are set by that court and state law.

Because mandatory state assessments are added to every fine and often exceed it, the amount owed is already larger than the base fine. Unresolved tickets can add further problems on top of that.

  • Look for the response date and instructions on your citation.
  • Contact the court before the deadline if you need clarification.
  • Keep records of any payment or contact.

Suspensions in Mississippi follow repeat convictions and specific offenses. For license status questions, check with the Mississippi Department of Public Safety (Mississippi Department of Public Safety).
