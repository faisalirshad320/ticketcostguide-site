How much is a texting / cell phone ticket in North Carolina?

Nationally, a first-offense texting or cell phone ticket costs $20–$500 depending on the state. In North Carolina, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (North Carolina Division of Motor Vehicles).

This page does not list an exact North Carolina dollar figure, because the amount depends on the court and the citation you receive. The national range is context only. It is not a North Carolina price list.

The number to trust is the one printed on your citation or shown by the court handling your case. Anything else is an estimate.

The fine is also only part of the bill. The next section explains why.

Why is the total more than the fine?

Uniform statewide court costs dwarf the fine on most tickets. That means the amount you pay at the clerk's office can be well above the fine line on the citation.

If you are comparing a texting ticket against another violation, compare the total due, not just the fine. Two tickets with similar fines can cost about the same in the end because the same statewide costs apply to both.

  • Look for the fine amount on the citation.
  • Ask the clerk or check the court's payment notice for the total including costs.
  • Keep a receipt or confirmation once you pay.

Do not assume an online estimate from a third party includes everything. Confirm with the court.

How is the fine for a texting ticket set?

The exact fine for this violation in North Carolina is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The offense and its penalties come from North Carolina statutes, which you can read through North Carolina statutes via Justia.

The citation names the offense you are charged with. The court then applies the amount that goes with that charge, and the costs are added.

Other states handle this differently. The Governors Highway Safety Association tracks how state laws on phone use differ, including what is banned and who is covered. That is why the national range is so wide.

If you want to know what conduct is covered, read the statute text itself rather than a summary. Details such as hands-free exceptions are in the law, not on this page.

How many points does a texting ticket add in North Carolina?

The locked figure we have for license points is for typical speeding: 2–3 license points. We do not have a verified point value for a texting or cell phone conviction, so we are not stating one.

To find out, check your citation and the North Carolina Division of Motor Vehicles for how it treats the specific offense you were charged with.

There is also a second kind of points. Separate insurance (SDIP) points can raise premiums 30%+ per point tier. These are not the same as license points, and they matter because your insurer, not the DMV, decides how to price them in.

License points and insurance points are tracked separately. A ticket can affect one, the other, or both.

Does a texting ticket raise my insurance?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a North Carolina one, and your own change could be higher or lower.

The SDIP point system described above can push premiums up by 30%+ per point tier. Whether a texting conviction carries insurance points is something to confirm with the DMV or your insurer.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer or product. If cost is a concern, ask your own insurer how a conviction would affect your policy before you decide how to handle the ticket.

  • A conviction is what usually matters, not the stop itself.
  • Premium changes often show up at renewal.
  • The long-term cost can exceed the fine and court costs combined.

Can a texting ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes. Options generally include paying the ticket, contesting it in court, or asking the court whether any reduction or alternative resolution is available in your case.

Which of these exist for you depends on the court, the charge, and your record. We do not give legal advice and cannot say whether you qualify.

Before you pick an option, weigh these points:

  1. Paying usually ends the case, but it may mean a conviction on your record.
  2. A conviction can lead to license points and higher insurance costs.
  3. Contesting means attending court, and the costs may still apply depending on the outcome.

If you want advice on a specific case, speak with a licensed attorney of your own choosing. Read the instructions on your citation first, since they state how to respond.

What happens if I don't pay a texting ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts can add penalties, and unresolved tickets can lead to problems with your license or further court action, depending on the court and state rules.

We are not stating specific deadlines or added fees here, because the court sets them and they are shown on your paperwork. Read the citation for the response date and the court's contact information.

If you cannot pay the full amount or cannot attend on the date given, contact the court clerk before the deadline passes. Courts can often explain what options exist.

Also check the North Carolina Division of Motor Vehicles for how unresolved tickets may affect your driving record or license status.
