How much is a texting or cell phone ticket in New Mexico?

Nationally, a first-offense texting or cell phone ticket runs $20–$500 by state (New Mexico Motor Vehicle Division). That is a national range, not a New Mexico price.

In New Mexico, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single New Mexico dollar figure because the number you owe is the one printed on your ticket or assigned by the court handling your case.

Bottom line: read the citation first. The amount, the court and the response instructions on that document control what you owe, not a range on a website.

The fine is only one piece of the total. Fees are added to the penalty assessment, so the amount you pay can be higher than the base figure.

How is the fine for a texting ticket set?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The code gives the framework, and the court applies it to your citation.

Two details matter in practice:

  • Penalty assessment vs. court case. Fees are added to the penalty assessment. If your matter goes through a court case instead, the total can exceed the assessment amounts.
  • The citation is the starting point. It shows the charge, the court and how to respond.

Because the amount is court-set, two drivers with similar tickets may see different totals. We cannot predict yours from outside the court record.

For the statutory text, New Mexico's laws are available through New Mexico statutes via Justia. For a state-by-state view of how texting and handheld laws differ, see the Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws page.

What court costs and surcharges can be added?

The locked fact we rely on is simple: fees are added to the penalty assessment, and court cases can exceed the assessment amounts.

That means the headline fine is not always the final bill. When you compare options, ask what the total due is, not just the fine.

  • Check whether the amount on the citation already includes fees.
  • If a court sets the amount, ask the clerk for the total due in writing.
  • Keep your receipt and case or citation number.

We do not list individual fee names or dollar amounts, because we have no sourced figures for them.

How many points does a texting ticket carry in New Mexico?

New Mexico's points system is described as 3–8 points by mph over. Those tiers are tied to speed, so they do not map neatly onto a texting citation.

We do not state a point value for texting. Check your citation or ask the New Mexico Motor Vehicle Division whether and how your violation is recorded on your driving record.

The thresholds that apply across violations are:

  • 7–10 points in 12 months risks suspension.
  • 12 points means revocation.

If you already have points from other violations, even a modest addition could matter. Review your own record before deciding how to respond.

Does a texting ticket raise car insurance?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a New Mexico or insurer-specific one.

Your own change could be higher, lower or none, depending on your insurer, your history and whether the violation ends up as a conviction on your record. We do not recommend any insurer or predict your rate.

Practical steps:

  • Ask your insurer how it treats this type of violation at renewal.
  • Check whether the outcome of your case counts as a conviction.
  • Compare the possible premium effect with the fine when deciding how to respond.

Over several years, a premium increase can cost more than the fine itself, which is why the outcome matters beyond the ticket price.

Can a texting ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome depends on the court and the facts. We cannot say what a particular court will do, and this page is not legal advice.

Your general options are:

  1. Pay the citation. This is usually the fastest route, but it may mean accepting the violation on your record.
  2. Contest it. You can ask the court for a hearing and present your side. A court case can end up costing more than the assessment amounts.
  3. Ask the court what resolutions it offers. Some courts have processes that affect the final outcome. Ask the clerk what is available for your citation.

Before choosing, compare the total cost, the possible points and the likely insurance effect. If the stakes are high, such as existing points near the suspension threshold, you may want to speak with a licensed attorney. We do not recommend a specific one.

What happens if I don't pay a texting ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. The court's instructions on your ticket set the deadlines and the consequences for missing them.

We do not state specific late fees or timelines, because we have no sourced figures for them. Contact the court listed on your citation to confirm the status and the total due.

  • Do not assume the ticket goes away if you do nothing.
  • Ask the clerk about the amount, deadline and options.
  • Keep records of every payment or filing.

Unresolved tickets can also interact with the points thresholds: 7–10 points in 12 months risks suspension, and 12 means revocation.
