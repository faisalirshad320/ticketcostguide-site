How much is a texting / cell phone ticket in Illinois?

Nationally, a first-offense texting or cell phone ticket falls in a range of $20–$500, depending on the state (Illinois Secretary of State). That range is a national spread, not an Illinois price.

In Illinois, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not state a single Illinois dollar figure because the amount you owe is the one printed on your ticket or assigned by the court handling your case.

Your citation is the most reliable source for your fine. If the amount is unclear, contact the clerk of the court listed on the ticket.

The amount can also vary by county or court. Two drivers cited for the same behavior may see different totals depending on where the case is heard.

How is the fine set for an Illinois texting ticket?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The officer writes the violation on the citation, and the court determines the amount that applies to your case.

Several things can sit on top of a base fine:

  • Court costs charged by the county or court.
  • Statutory surcharges, which vary by violation.
  • Any additional charges listed on the citation or the court's schedule.

For context on statutory minimums, work-zone speeding carries a statutory minimum fine of $375 for a first offense. That is a different violation from texting, and it shows that some Illinois violations have fixed floors while others are set by the court.

If your citation lists a fine and a separate total, ask the clerk what the difference covers.

How many points does a texting ticket add in Illinois?

Illinois uses a point scale administered by the Secretary of State. Convictions carry 5–50 point values on the SOS scale (Illinois Secretary of State).

That range covers many types of violations, so we do not assign a specific number to a texting conviction. Check your driving record or ask the Secretary of State for the value that applies to your conviction.

Points generally attach to convictions, not to the citation itself. A ticket that is dismissed or does not result in a conviction is treated differently from one that does.

Can a texting ticket lead to a license suspension?

One conviction alone is unlikely to be the issue. The risk grows with repeat violations: repeat convictions within 12 months trigger suspension.

That means a texting conviction can matter more if you already have other moving violations on your record. Count recent convictions before deciding how to handle a new ticket.

Confirm your own record with the Secretary of State before you decide to pay or contest. Your record, not just this ticket, determines your exposure.

Suspension rules are set by the state, so check the Illinois Secretary of State for current details (Illinois Secretary of State).

Does a texting ticket raise insurance rates in Illinois?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. This is a national figure, not an Illinois-specific one.

What you actually pay depends on your insurer, your driving history, and whether the ticket becomes a conviction on your record. We do not recommend or compare insurers.

  • A conviction is more likely to affect rates than a ticket that is dismissed.
  • Prior violations can make an insurer weigh a new one more heavily.
  • Ask your insurer how it treats a conviction like this at renewal.

When weighing whether to contest, include the possible insurance cost, not only the fine.

Can a texting ticket be dismissed or reduced in Illinois?

Sometimes. The court sets the outcome, and options depend on the court, the facts, and your record. We cannot say what will work in your case.

Common steps people consider:

  1. Read the citation carefully for errors and the listed court date.
  2. Check whether the court offers any alternative resolution for this violation. Availability varies by court.
  3. Decide whether to appear in person, respond by mail, or use another method the court allows.
  4. Consider speaking with a licensed attorney if a conviction would put your license at risk.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice or recommend a specific attorney. Treat this as general information only.

What happens if I don't pay a texting ticket in Illinois?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts can treat a missed payment or missed court date as a separate problem, and the original amount may no longer be the full cost.

Because the amount is set by the court on the citation, check the deadline printed on it. If you cannot pay in time, contact the clerk of the court before the date passes to ask what is possible.

An unresolved ticket can also affect your license status. Together with other convictions, it could contribute to a suspension (Illinois Secretary of State).
