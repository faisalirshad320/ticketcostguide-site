How much is a texting or cell phone ticket in Arizona?

Nationally, a first-offense texting or cell phone ticket costs $20–$500 depending on the state (Arizona Department of Transportation — Motor Vehicle Division). That is a national range. It is not an Arizona price.

In Arizona, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Arizona dollar figure, because the court decides it and we will not guess.

The total you pay can also be higher than the base fine. Court surcharges and fees are added by the justice or municipal court, and the amounts vary by court. Two drivers cited for the same thing in different courts can owe different totals.

The number on your citation or court notice is what counts. Treat any online average, including the national range on this page, as background only.

How is the fine for a cell phone ticket set in Arizona?

The violation sits within Arizona's traffic code, and the court handling your case sets the amount on the citation. Arizona traffic law is published by the state, and you can read the current text through Arizona statutes via Justia.

Several things can change what you end up paying:

  • The court. Justice courts and municipal courts handle traffic citations, and each adds its own surcharges and fees.
  • The citation itself. The violation written on the ticket decides which rule applies.
  • Your record. The national range we cite is for a first offense. Repeat offenses are often treated differently, so check your own situation.

Rules on phone use while driving differ a lot between states. The Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws page is a useful place to compare how states approach the issue. Always confirm the current Arizona rule against the statute itself.

How many points does a texting ticket add in Arizona?

Our sourced data gives Arizona's points system in general terms: 3 points for speeding, and 8+ points in 12 months leads to a license suspension or Traffic Survival School.

We do not have a sourced point value for a texting or cell phone violation, so we will not state one. Your citation, the court, or the Motor Vehicle Division (Arizona Department of Transportation — Motor Vehicle Division) can tell you whether and how many points apply to your specific violation.

Points from different violations add up. A phone ticket on top of other recent tickets could matter more than the single ticket suggests, so look at your whole record, not just this citation.

If you are close to the 8-point threshold, it is worth asking the court how a conviction would be recorded before you decide how to respond.

Does a texting or cell phone ticket raise insurance in Arizona?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Arizona-specific one.

What your insurer actually does depends on its own rules, your driving history and whether the ticket ends in a conviction. We do not recommend any insurer and cannot predict your rate.

Some practical points:

  • The 20% figure is an average. Your change could be higher, lower or nothing.
  • Insurers usually look at convictions on your record, so how the case ends can matter.
  • A premium increase can cost more over time than the fine itself, so factor it in when deciding how to respond.

If the premium impact worries you, ask your insurer how it treats citations of this kind. Do not rely on a general average alone.

Can a texting ticket be dismissed or reduced in Arizona?

Sometimes, but outcomes depend on the court, the facts and the evidence. We cannot say how likely it is in your case, and this page is not legal advice.

The usual routes for any traffic citation are:

  1. Pay the citation. This is typically treated as admitting the violation, which may mean a conviction on your record and possible points.
  2. Contest it. You ask for a hearing and the court decides whether the violation was proven.
  3. Ask about alternatives. Some courts offer other ways to resolve a citation. Ask the court clerk what is available for your ticket, since this varies by court.

Read the deadlines and instructions on your citation carefully. Missing them can close off options. If you want advice on your specific case, a licensed attorney can give it. We do not recommend a particular attorney.

What happens if I don't pay a cell phone ticket in Arizona?

Ignoring a citation is usually the most expensive choice. Courts can add fees when a deadline is missed, and because court surcharges and fees vary by court, the added cost is hard to predict.

An unresolved citation can also lead to problems with your driver license. Check your court notice and the Motor Vehicle Division (Arizona Department of Transportation — Motor Vehicle Division) for how an unpaid or unanswered citation would affect your record.

If you cannot pay the full amount by the deadline, contact the court before the deadline passes and ask what options it has. Courts generally respond better to drivers who get in touch early.

We do not list specific penalties or deadlines here, because they are set by the court and your citation.
