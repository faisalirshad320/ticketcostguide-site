How much is a texting or cell phone ticket in New York?

Nationally, a first-offense texting or cell phone ticket costs $20–$500 depending on the state (New York State Department of Motor Vehicles). That is a national range, not a New York price.

In New York, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. The state-specific fact we have on file is that New York adds 5 points per handheld conviction, with fines of $50–$200 for a first offense.

The fine is only part of the bill. A mandatory surcharge and, in some cases, a DMV assessment are added to it. Those are covered below.

Your citation and the court that handles it are the final word on what you owe. Use the numbers on this page for planning, not as a quote.

How is the fine for a texting ticket set?

The exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. That means two drivers cited for the same handheld offense can see different amounts, depending on the court and the facts on the citation.

New York's traffic statutes are published online, including through New York statutes via Justia. We point to the code rather than quote a section number we could not verify for this page.

For a first offense, the range we have on file is $50–$200. A repeat offense may be treated differently, so check your citation and the court's instructions.

  • Where to look: the fine or appearance information printed on the citation.
  • Who sets it: the court handling your case.
  • What is added: the mandatory surcharge, and possibly a DMV assessment.

What fees get added on top of the fine?

New York's $88–$93 mandatory surcharge comes on top of the court fine. The DMV's Driver Responsibility Assessment also comes on top of the fine.

The assessment is separate from the court. It is a DMV charge of $300+ that is triggered at 6+ points. A single handheld conviction carries 5 points, so by itself it falls just under that mark.

Points from other violations on your record can change that. If you have other convictions, check your total with the DMV before assuming the assessment will not apply.

Add the pieces together when you budget: the court fine, the surcharge, and any assessment. The fine alone understates the real cost.

How many points does a texting ticket carry in New York?

New York adds 5 points per handheld conviction. That figure applies to the conviction, not just to being cited.

Points matter because of the state's thresholds. Reaching 11 points in 18 months leads to suspension. Reaching 6+ points also triggers the separate Driver Responsibility Assessment of $300+.

New York's general points scale, 3 points (1–10 over), 4 points (11–20), 6 points (21–30), 8 points (31–40) and 11 points (41+), is organized by miles per hour over the limit. It describes speeding tiers and is not the texting penalty. We include it so you can see how a 5-point handheld conviction compares with other violations.

  • A handheld conviction: 5 points.
  • 6+ points: the $300+ assessment is also triggered.
  • 11 points in 18 months: suspension.

Two or more convictions can add up quickly, so check your own record through the New York State Department of Motor Vehicles.

Does a texting ticket raise your insurance?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a New York quote.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the ticket is resolved. We do not recommend or compare insurers, and we cannot predict your rate.

Points on your state record and an insurer's own rating are separate systems. A conviction that adds points is the kind of entry insurers commonly review.

To estimate your own impact, ask your insurer how it treats a moving-violation conviction. Do this before you decide how to handle the ticket.

Can a texting ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but there is no guarantee. Because the court sets the amount and handles the case, your options depend on that court and on the facts of your citation.

In general, drivers have a few paths. Which one fits is your decision, and none carries a promised result.

  • Pay the ticket: this usually resolves the case quickly, but a conviction carries the 5 points, the surcharge and possible insurance effects.
  • Contest it: you can ask the court to hear your case. Follow the instructions and deadlines on the citation.
  • Ask about other outcomes: some courts may offer different resolutions. Ask the court clerk what is available, because we cannot confirm this for every court.

If you want legal advice about your situation, a licensed attorney can give it. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not recommend any attorney.

What happens if I don't pay a texting ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. The citation sets out what you must do and by when, and missing that step can bring added consequences from the court or the DMV.

The specific penalties vary by court, so we do not state exact amounts or timelines. Read your citation and contact the court clerk if anything is unclear.

Unpaid or unresolved tickets can also interact with your record and your license. With a 5-point conviction, an 11-point total in 18 months leads to suspension. Keep that threshold in mind if you already have points.

If you cannot pay in full, ask the court whether it has a payment arrangement before the deadline passes.
