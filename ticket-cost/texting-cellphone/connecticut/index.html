How much is a texting or cell phone ticket in Connecticut?

The figure we can anchor to is national: $20–$500 for a first offense, depending on the state. In Connecticut, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Connecticut Department of Motor Vehicles).

We do not publish an exact Connecticut dollar amount, because the number that matters is the one printed on your ticket. State fees and surcharges are built into the payable amount, so the total is not a fine plus a separate add-on.

The $20–$500 range is a national spread across states. It is a way to gauge the scale of a first-offense ticket, not a promise about what a Connecticut court will charge.

How is the fine for a cell phone ticket set?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The law defines what conduct is prohibited, and the citation and court process determine the payable amount.

Connecticut's statutes are published online through Connecticut statutes via Justia. If you want to read the text of the rule you were cited under, start there and match it to the statute listed on your ticket.

Several things can differ from one ticket to the next:

  • The statute or violation code written on the citation.
  • Whether the ticket is treated as a first offense or a repeat.
  • The state fees and surcharges added into the total.
  • The court that handles your case.

Because the amount can vary by court, compare the figure on your own citation with any number you find online, including ours.

What should I check on my citation?

The citation is the best source for your actual cost. A few minutes reading it can prevent mistakes.

  1. The payable amount. This should already include state fees and surcharges.
  2. The violation and statute. Confirm it describes phone use and not a different offense.
  3. The court and instructions. These say where and how the ticket is handled.
  4. Your personal details. Check your name, license number and vehicle for errors.

If anything is unclear, the court named on the citation is the right place to ask what the payable amount covers.

How many points does a texting or cell phone ticket add?

The point context we can state comes from the DMV point system: speeding adds points under the DMV point system, and 10 points = 30-day suspension. That is the threshold in our sources.

Our locked facts do not give a point value for a texting or cell phone violation, so we do not state one. Check with the Connecticut Department of Motor Vehicles to see how the violation on your citation is treated on your record.

Points and suspension rules are set by the DMV, not by us. Ask the DMV how a conviction would be recorded before you decide what to do with the ticket.

Does a cell phone ticket raise my insurance?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Connecticut-specific one.

Your own change could be higher, lower or nothing at all. It depends on your insurer, your driving record and how the violation is recorded. We do not recommend any insurer, and we cannot predict what yours will do.

If you want a real answer, ask your insurer how it treats a conviction for this type of violation at renewal. A conviction is what typically drives the increase, so how the ticket is resolved matters.

Can a texting ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Whether a ticket can be reduced or dismissed is decided by the court, based on the facts of your case and Connecticut's rules. We cannot tell you whether yours qualifies.

In general, the options on a traffic citation are:

  • Pay it. This usually resolves the ticket, but it may count as a conviction, which is what typically affects insurance and your record.
  • Contest it. You can ask the court to hear your case. The citation lists the court and its instructions.
  • Ask the court what other options exist. Procedures differ by court, so the clerk's office is the best place to ask.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice or recommend attorneys. If the stakes feel high, such as a license already close to the 10-point threshold, consider speaking with a licensed Connecticut attorney of your choosing.

What happens if I don't pay a cell phone ticket?

Ignoring a traffic ticket usually makes the situation worse. Courts generally treat an unaddressed citation as a failure to respond, and that can lead to added consequences.

Our locked sources do not list specific late fees, deadlines or suspension triggers for unpaid Connecticut tickets, so we do not state any. Look at the instructions on the citation, and contact the court named on it if you are unsure about your deadline.

The Connecticut Department of Motor Vehicles is the place to ask how an unresolved ticket could affect your license.
