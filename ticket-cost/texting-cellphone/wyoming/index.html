How much is a texting / cell phone ticket in Wyoming?

Nationally, the fine for a first-offense texting or cell phone ticket ranges from $20 to $500, depending on the state. In Wyoming, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Wyoming Department of Transportation).

We do not list a single Wyoming dollar figure, because the amount depends on the court and the citation. The $20–$500 range is a national spread, not a Wyoming schedule.

The number printed on your citation, or given to you by the court clerk, is the number that matters. Court fees are added per the local schedule, so the total can be higher than the fine alone.

If the citation shows only a court date and no amount, contact the clerk of the court named on it and ask for the total due, including fees.

How is the fine set for a texting ticket in Wyoming?

The exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. That is why two drivers cited for the same thing can see different totals depending on where the ticket was issued.

Two parts make up what you owe:

  • The fine itself: set by the court on the citation.
  • Court fees: added per the local schedule.

The statutes are published online, and you can read the traffic code yourself through Wyoming statutes via Justia. Statutes can be amended, so check the current text or ask the court which version applies to your citation.

For a state-by-state view of how texting and handheld phone laws differ, the Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a state law reference.

How many points does a texting ticket add in Wyoming?

Wyoming has no demerit-point system. There is no point value to add up for this ticket.

That does not mean the ticket has no effect on your license. Suspensions follow repeat convictions and court action, so a conviction can still count if more follow.

  • No points are assigned in a demerit system.
  • A conviction may still be part of your driving record.
  • Repeat convictions and court action are what lead to suspensions.

For questions about your own record, the Wyoming Department of Transportation is the place to start. We cannot tell you how a particular conviction will be recorded in your case.

Does a texting ticket raise my insurance?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Wyoming-specific rate.

Whether your insurer treats a texting or cell phone conviction as a surcharge event depends on the company, your policy and your driving history. We do not recommend any insurer and cannot predict what yours will do.

The fine is a one-time cost. An insurance increase can apply for as long as your insurer counts the conviction, so it can end up costing more than the ticket itself.

If you are weighing whether to contest the ticket, the possible premium effect is worth including in your thinking. Your insurer or agent can tell you how they handle convictions on a record.

Can a texting ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but outcomes depend on the court, the facts and the evidence. We cannot promise any result.

Here are the general options people have after a citation:

  1. Pay it. This usually resolves the matter, but paying generally means accepting the conviction.
  2. Contest it. You can ask for a hearing and present your side to the court.
  3. Ask the court what is available. The clerk can tell you which procedures that court offers, and whether a different resolution is possible.
  4. Speak to a licensed attorney of your choosing. We are not a law firm and do not give legal advice or recommend attorneys.

Read the citation carefully for deadlines. Missing a court date or response deadline can make things worse than the original ticket.

What happens if I don't pay a texting ticket in Wyoming?

Ignoring a citation tends to cause more trouble than the ticket itself. Court fees are added per the local schedule, and the court can take further action if you do not respond.

Under the locked state context, suspensions follow repeat convictions and court action. An unpaid or ignored ticket can lead to court action, so it is risky to leave it alone.

  • Check the due date and court date on the citation.
  • Contact the clerk of the court if you are unsure of the amount or the deadline.
  • Ask the court about any options before the deadline passes, not after.

We cannot say exactly what a given court will do in your case. The court named on your citation is the authority on that.

What should I do right after getting a texting ticket?

Keep a few simple steps in mind:

  1. Read the citation for the charge, the court, the amount if shown, and the deadline.
  2. Confirm the total with the court clerk, since court fees are added per the local schedule.
  3. Decide whether to pay or contest, keeping in mind the possible insurance effect of about 20% nationally.
  4. Keep copies of the citation and any receipt or court paperwork.

If you are unsure about the law that applies, read the traffic code through Wyoming statutes via Justia or consult a licensed attorney you choose yourself.
