How much is a texting or cell phone ticket in Maryland?

Nationally, a first-offense texting or cell phone ticket costs $20–$500 depending on the state. In Maryland, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Maryland Motor Vehicle Administration).

We do not print a single Maryland dollar figure here, because the amount on your ticket is what counts. The national range is context only. It is not a promise about what a Maryland officer or judge will assign.

Look at your citation first. It shows the amount for your charge and tells you whether you can prepay or must go to court.

If you ask for a court date, the picture changes. The prepay amount is replaced by a judge-set fine of up to $500.

How is the fine for a Maryland cell phone ticket set?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The officer writes the charge on the citation, and the court amount follows from it. Maryland statutes are available through Maryland statutes via Justia.

There are two broad paths:

  • Pay the citation as written. You use the amount shown and the case generally ends there.
  • Request a court date. A judge sets the fine, up to $500, in place of the prepay amount.

Because the court sets the number, two drivers with similar tickets can see different results. Specific local differences are not listed in the sources we use, so we do not guess at them.

Texting and handheld phone rules also differ across states. The Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a state-by-state overview of distracted driving laws if you want to compare.

How many points does a texting ticket add in Maryland?

Maryland uses a points system tracked by the MVA. The published tiers are 1 point (9 or less over), 2 points (10–29 over) and 5 points (30+ over).

Those tiers are written in terms of how far over a limit a driver was. We do not claim that a specific tier applies to a texting or cell phone charge. The points for your violation are tied to the charge on your citation, so confirm them there or with the MVA.

The thresholds that matter most are cumulative:

  • 8 points = suspension
  • 12 points = revocation

A single ticket may not come close to those levels. Points from several violations add up, which is why a driver with an existing record should pay more attention to a new conviction.

Details of your own record are available from the Maryland Motor Vehicle Administration.

Does a texting ticket raise insurance in Maryland?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Maryland quote, and we do not have a Maryland-specific percentage.

What your insurer does depends on your company, your record and how the violation is recorded. Some insurers look only at convictions. Others may look at points or at the number of recent violations.

A rough way to think about it: if your yearly premium is a certain amount, a 20% increase adds a fifth of it. The increase is usually applied at renewal, and can last for as long as the insurer counts the violation.

We do not recommend any insurer. Ask your own company how it treats a conviction like this, and check your policy documents.

Should I pay the ticket or request a court date?

That depends on your situation, and TicketCostGuide cannot give legal advice. Here are the facts that bear on the choice.

  • Paying the ticket as written is quick, but it generally means accepting the charge.
  • Requesting a court date replaces the prepay amount with a judge-set fine of up to $500.
  • A conviction may add points and may affect insurance, which nationally rises about 20% on average.

A court date means the final amount is not fixed in advance. It also gives you a chance to be heard, which paying does not.

If the stakes feel high, such as a record that already has points close to the 8-point suspension threshold, consider getting advice from a licensed professional of your own choosing. We do not recommend a particular attorney.

Can a texting ticket be dismissed or reduced in Maryland?

Going to court is the route by which a charge can be contested, dismissed or changed. Whether that happens in your case is up to the court. Our sources do not publish dismissal rates or standard reductions, so we do not offer any.

Things worth gathering if you plan to contest:

  • A copy of the citation, with the charge and statute listed.
  • Any notes you made about where and when the stop took place.
  • Your driving record from the MVA.

Remember the trade-off. When you request a court date, the prepay amount is replaced by a judge-set fine of up to $500. That ceiling is locked in our sources, but the actual amount is the judge's decision.

The Maryland traffic code is posted at Maryland statutes via Justia if you want to read the language yourself.

What happens if I don't pay a Maryland cell phone ticket?

Ignoring a citation is rarely a good idea. A ticket usually comes with a way to respond, either by paying or by requesting a court date. Skipping both can lead to further action from the court or the MVA.

Our sources do not list specific late fees or deadlines for this violation, so we do not state any. Your citation is the best place to find the response instructions that apply to you.

If you cannot respond in time, contact the court listed on the citation as soon as you can. For questions about your license status or points, use the Maryland Motor Vehicle Administration.

Unpaid or ignored tickets can also interact with the points system, where 8 points means suspension and 12 points means revocation.
