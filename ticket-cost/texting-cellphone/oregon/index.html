How much is a texting / cell phone ticket in Oregon?

Nationally, a first texting / cell phone ticket runs $20–$500 depending on the state (Oregon DMV (ODOT)). That range is a national figure, not an Oregon price.

In Oregon, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Oregon dollar figure because the number you owe is the one printed on your ticket or set by the court handling your case.

Your citation is the most reliable source for what you owe. If the amount is unclear, ask the court clerk named on the ticket.

Because the amount is court-set, two drivers cited for the same thing can see different totals. Check the citation, and look for any added costs listed with it.

How is the fine for a cell phone ticket set in Oregon?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The officer writes the violation on the citation, and the court determines the amount tied to it.

Some enhancements are written directly into statute rather than left to the court. Zone doubling and the 100+ mph enhancements are examples. These are set in the statutes, which you can read through Oregon statutes via Justia.

Zone doubling and 100+ mph enhancements are tied to speeding-type conduct. They are not a texting-specific add-on, so do not assume they apply to a phone citation. Read the violation and any listed enhancements on your ticket.

  • Look at the violation description on the citation.
  • Look at the amount the court lists, and whether any additional costs appear.
  • Compare it with the statute text if you want to confirm what was charged.

How many points does a cell phone ticket add in Oregon?

Oregon has no point system. A texting / cell phone conviction does not add points to your record the way it would in a state that scores violations.

That does not mean the ticket has no consequences for your license. Suspensions in Oregon follow repeat convictions (for example, habitual-offender rules) and specific offenses like 100+ mph.

In practice, a single phone ticket is a different situation from a pattern of convictions. The risk to your license grows as convictions accumulate, so it is worth knowing your own record. You can learn more about driver records from Oregon DMV (ODOT).

Does a cell phone ticket raise insurance in Oregon?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Oregon-specific one.

Whether your rate changes depends on your insurer, your history and how the violation is recorded. We do not recommend any insurer, and we cannot tell you what your own premium will do.

  • Ask your insurer how it treats a conviction like this one.
  • Check when your policy renews, since rate changes often show up then.
  • Keep a copy of the court's final disposition of the ticket.

The ~20% average is a rough benchmark. Your outcome could be higher, lower or unchanged.

Can a texting / cell phone ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Possibly, but the outcome depends on the facts and on the court. Because the Oregon court sets the amount on the citation, any change would also come through the court.

Common routes people look into include contesting the citation, asking the court about its options, or speaking with a licensed attorney. We do not give legal advice or recommend a specific attorney.

Read the citation closely for the court name, the violation listed and the instructions for responding. Those instructions control your options.

If you plan to contest, gather what you have: the citation, any photos or notes from the stop, and details about what the officer observed. The statutes on Justia show what the law actually requires.

What happens if I don't pay a cell phone ticket in Oregon?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts can add costs, and unresolved traffic matters can lead to license consequences. The details depend on the court handling your case.

Do not rely on the ticket going away. Respond using the method on the citation, whether that is paying, contesting or asking the court about other options.

Oregon has no point system, but suspensions can follow repeat convictions. An unpaid or mishandled ticket can complicate your record, so deal with it early. For license and record questions, see Oregon DMV (ODOT).

How does Oregon compare with other states on phone laws?

State rules on phones while driving vary, and so do penalties. The national first-offense range of $20–$500 reflects that spread.

The Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a state-by-state overview of distracted-driving laws at GHSA — State Laws. It is useful if you drive across state lines or want to see how other states treat the same conduct.

Do not apply another state's fine to an Oregon ticket. In Oregon, the court sets the amount on the citation.
