How much is a texting / cell phone ticket in Kansas?

Nationally, the first-offense range is $20–$500 by state. In Kansas, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Kansas Department of Revenue — Division of Vehicles).

We do not print a single Kansas dollar figure here. The national range is a national figure, and the Kansas amount depends on the court that handles your ticket.

The number on your citation, or the amount the court clerk quotes you, is the one that counts. Treat any other figure online as an estimate.

Your total can differ from the base fine because of:

  • the fine the court sets within the state's traffic code,
  • court costs added by the municipal or district court,
  • whether you pay the ticket or contest it.

Call the court listed on your citation or check its website to confirm the full amount before you pay.

How is the fine for a Kansas texting ticket set?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. There is no single statewide number that applies the same way everywhere.

Which court hears your case depends on where the ticket was issued. A municipal court handles many tickets written inside city limits, and a district court handles others. Each adds its own court costs on top of the fine.

That is why two drivers cited for the same behavior can see different totals. The court, not the officer, sets the amount.

If you want to read the law yourself, Kansas statutes are published online (Kansas statutes via Justia). For a comparison of how states treat distracted driving, the Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws page is a useful starting point.

Statutes change, and we are not a law firm. Check current text or ask the court if you need to know exactly how a rule applies to you.

How many points does a texting ticket add in Kansas?

Kansas has no demerit-point system. You will not see a number of points added to your record the way you would in many other states.

That does not make a conviction harmless. Three moving-violation convictions within 12 months can trigger suspension.

The threshold is about convictions, not points. Three moving-violation convictions inside a 12-month window is the locked figure to remember.

Whether a particular texting conviction counts toward that threshold is something to confirm with the court or the Kansas Department of Revenue — Division of Vehicles. We do not guess at how an individual case will be classified.

If you already have other recent tickets, count them before deciding how to handle this one. A conviction, not just a citation, is what moves you toward the threshold.

Does a texting ticket raise car insurance in Kansas?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Kansas-specific one.

Your own change could be higher, lower or nothing at all. It depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the conviction is recorded.

  • A first conviction often matters less than a pattern of several.
  • Insurers use their own rating rules, so the same ticket can affect two drivers differently.
  • A ticket that does not end in a conviction may be treated differently from one that does.

We do not recommend insurers. If you are worried about your rate, ask your own insurer or agent how a conviction would be handled before you decide whether to pay or contest.

Keep in mind that the 20% is an average across moving violations generally, not a prediction for texting tickets in Kansas.

Can a Kansas texting ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but there is no guarantee. Outcomes depend on the court, the facts of the stop and the options that court offers.

Some courts allow a defendant to ask for an amended charge, a different resolution or a hearing. Whether yours does is a question for the court clerk. We cannot promise any result.

Some practical points:

  • Read the citation closely for the court, the charge and any instructions.
  • Ask the clerk what choices you have, such as paying, requesting a hearing or asking about other resolutions.
  • Keep copies of everything you submit.

Paying the ticket generally resolves the case, and it may result in a conviction. Because convictions can count toward the suspension threshold, it is worth understanding that before you pay.

What happens if I don't pay a Kansas texting ticket?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. Courts can add costs or take further action when a citation goes unanswered, and the details are set by the court.

We do not list specific deadlines or penalties here because those depend on the court and the instructions on your citation. Look for a response date on the ticket itself.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court before the date on the citation. Clerks can explain what options exist.

License consequences are handled through the Kansas Department of Revenue — Division of Vehicles. As noted above, three moving-violation convictions within 12 months can trigger suspension.

The safest approach is to respond to the court, either by paying or by contesting, rather than letting the citation sit.

What should I do after getting a texting ticket in Kansas?

Start with the paperwork. The citation tells you which court has your case and how to respond.

  1. Read the citation and note the court, the charge and any response date.
  2. Contact the court clerk to confirm the total, including court costs.
  3. Count any other moving-violation convictions from the past 12 months.
  4. Decide whether to pay or contest, knowing that a conviction can affect insurance and your record.
  5. Keep receipts, confirmation numbers and copies of anything you send.

Do not rely on a national range to budget for the ticket. The $20–$500 figure describes first offenses across states, and Kansas sets the exact amount through the court.

No. Kansas has no demerit-point system, so a ticket does not add a set number of points. However, three moving-violation convictions within 12 months can trigger suspension, so the number of convictions on your record still matters.

Who sets the fine for a texting ticket in Kansas?

Can my license be suspended for texting tickets in Kansas?

Is the $20–$500 range the Kansas fine?
