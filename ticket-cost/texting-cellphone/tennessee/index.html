How much is a texting or cell phone ticket in Tennessee?

Nationally, a first-offense texting or cell phone ticket runs from $20 to $500, depending on the state. In Tennessee, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Tennessee Department of Safety and Homeland Security).

We do not publish a single Tennessee dollar figure, because the amount you owe depends on the court that hears your case. The number printed on your citation or on the court's payment notice is the number that counts.

The $20–$500 range is a national range across states. It is not a Tennessee price. Treat it as context, then check your own citation.

Most of the total is usually not the base fine. County court costs and state litigation taxes make up most of what people actually pay.

How is the fine set, and why do totals differ by county?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. Tennessee statutes are published online (Tennessee statutes via Justia), and the Department of Safety and Homeland Security is the state's main source for driver-record rules (Tennessee Department of Safety and Homeland Security).

Two drivers cited for the same thing can see different totals. That is because county court costs and state litigation taxes are added to the base amount, and those costs vary by county.

Here is what usually goes into a total:

  • Base fine: set by the court within the state traffic code.
  • County court costs: vary by county and make up much of the bill.
  • State litigation taxes: added by the state on top of the fine.

Your citation or the court clerk can tell you the full total. Ask whether the figure shown includes costs and taxes or only the base fine.

How many points does a texting ticket add in Tennessee?

Tennessee's points system runs from 1 to 8 points, and the scale is tied to how many miles per hour over the limit a driver was going. Examples are 3 points at 6–15 over and 4 points at 16–25 over.

That scale describes speeding. Our locked sources do not list a point value for a texting or cell phone violation, so we do not state one. To find out how your violation is scored, check the Tennessee Department of Safety and Homeland Security (official site) or ask the court handling your citation.

The point that matters most is the cumulative limit. Reaching 12 points in 12 months results in a suspension notice. Points from any violation on your record count toward that total, so a phone ticket on top of other tickets can matter more than it would alone.

Does a texting ticket raise insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Tennessee quote.

What you would actually pay depends on your insurer, your driving record, your age, and whether the ticket becomes a conviction. We do not recommend any insurer and cannot predict your rate.

Some things to keep in mind:

  • An increase usually shows up at renewal, not right away.
  • A conviction is what typically drives the change, so how the case ends matters.
  • The effect can add up with other violations on the same record.

If you are worried about your premium, your insurer or agent is the right place to ask how a ticket would be treated.

Can a texting ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome depends on the court and the facts of your case. We cannot say what a given court will do, and this page is not legal advice.

Common paths people look into include:

  • Paying the ticket: usually closes the case, but it typically counts as a conviction.
  • Contesting it: you ask the court for a hearing and present your side.
  • Asking about court programs: some courts have options that change how a case is recorded. Ask the clerk whether any apply to your citation.

Before you choose, read the citation for deadlines and instructions. Because insurance and points can follow a conviction, it is worth knowing how each option would be recorded. Some people talk to a licensed attorney in their area before deciding.

What happens if I don't pay a texting ticket in Tennessee?

Ignoring a ticket rarely makes it go away. Courts generally add consequences when a deadline passes, and the Tennessee Department of Safety and Homeland Security handles driver-license actions (official site).

What happens in your case depends on the court. Typical risks include extra costs, a hold on your license, or a missed chance to contest the ticket.

Find the deadline on your citation first. If you cannot pay the full amount by then, contact the court clerk before the date passes and ask what is available.

Points matter here too. A cumulative 12 points in 12 months triggers a suspension notice, so unresolved tickets on a record that already has points deserve extra attention.

What should I check on my citation first?

Before paying or contesting, look at five things on the paper in your hand:

  1. The violation description and the statute or code listed.
  2. The court name and the county, since county costs vary.
  3. The amount due and whether it includes court costs and taxes.
  4. The deadline to pay or appear.
  5. Any instructions for contesting or asking about options.

If anything is unclear, the court clerk can explain the total. Tennessee statutes are available online if you want to read the code itself (Tennessee statutes via Justia). For a comparison of how states treat phone use behind the wheel, the Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a state-by-state summary (GHSA State Laws).
