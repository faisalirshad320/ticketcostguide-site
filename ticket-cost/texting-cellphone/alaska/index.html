How much is a texting or cell phone ticket in Alaska?

Nationally, a first-offense texting or cell phone ticket costs $20–$500 by state. In Alaska, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Alaska Division of Motor Vehicles).

We do not list a single Alaska dollar figure. The $20–$500 range is a national spread across states, and it is not the Alaska fine. Your own citation is the only reliable place to find what you owe.

The amount can vary by court. Treat the national range as background and the number on your citation as the real cost.

Other charges can change the total. Work-zone and school-zone violations carry doubled fines under the schedule. If your stop happened in or near one of those zones, check whether the amount on the citation reflects that.

How is the fine set for a texting ticket?

In Alaska, the fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The amount is written on the citation or tied to it.

That means two drivers cited for similar conduct could see different amounts, depending on the court and the circumstances. We do not publish a statewide Alaska figure because we cannot source one.

Things that can affect what appears on a citation:

  • The specific violation charged, such as texting versus another phone-related charge.
  • Whether the stop was in a work zone or school zone, where fines are doubled under the schedule.
  • The court handling the citation and its own procedures.

For the legal text, the Alaska traffic code is available through Alaska statutes via Justia. For a plain-language comparison of state phone laws, see the Governors Highway Safety Association state laws page.

How many points does a texting ticket add in Alaska?

The state context we rely on describes the points system as 2–6 demerit points by speed, with 12 points in 12 months = suspension. The "by speed" wording shows the scale is built around speeding violations.

We do not have a locked, texting-specific point value for Alaska, so we will not guess one. Whether and how a texting conviction is scored is something to confirm with the Alaska Division of Motor Vehicles.

The suspension threshold is 12 points in 12 months. Points from several violations in that window can add up, so one more ticket matters more if you already have others.

If you already carry points, check your driving record before deciding how to respond to a new citation.

Does a texting ticket raise insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure and not an Alaska-specific one.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your record and how the violation is reported. We do not recommend any insurer and cannot predict your rate.

A few practical points:

  • The increase usually follows a conviction, so how the ticket is resolved can matter.
  • A rate change often shows up at renewal rather than right away.
  • Your insurer or agent is the right source for how they treat a phone-related violation.

Over a few years, the added premium can exceed the fine itself, so weigh both when deciding how to handle the citation.

Can a texting ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome is decided by the court, not by us. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice or recommend attorneys.

Because the Alaska fine is set by the court on the citation, the court is also where any request to contest or adjust the ticket would be handled. Your citation should explain the options and how to respond.

Before you decide, it helps to:

  1. Read the citation closely for the charge, the court and the response instructions.
  2. Check whether the stop was in a work zone or school zone, since fines there are doubled under the schedule.
  3. Look up your current point total with the DMV.
  4. Consider the possible insurance effect of a conviction.

If the stakes are high, such as many existing points or a commercial license, consider asking a licensed attorney. We cannot say which outcome is likely in your case.

What happens if I don't pay a texting ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. We do not have locked Alaska figures for late fees or deadlines, so we will not state any.

What we can say is that the court sets the fine and controls the process. If you do not respond as the citation directs, the court may add consequences. The citation and the court are the right places to learn the specific deadlines and penalties.

The suspension rule is 12 points in 12 months. An unresolved ticket that later results in a conviction could add to your point total, so confirm your standing with the DMV.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court named on the citation and ask what options exist. Do not wait until after the response date.
