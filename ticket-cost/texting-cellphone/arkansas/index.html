How much is a texting / cell phone ticket in Arkansas?

Nationally, a first-offense texting / cell phone ticket runs $20–$500 by state (Arkansas DFA Driver Services). That is a national range, not an Arkansas price tag.

In Arkansas, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Arkansas dollar figure because the number you owe depends on the court handling your case and what it adds to the fine.

The most reliable number for your situation is the one printed on your citation or given by the court clerk. Treat any other figure as an estimate.

The rest of this page explains what drives the total, what to look for on the paperwork, and the options people commonly ask about.

How is the fine for a texting ticket set in Arkansas?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The officer's citation shows the charge, and the court determines the amount tied to it.

That is why two drivers cited for the same thing can see different totals. The court, the county and the specifics of the case all play a part.

Statutes are published online, and Arkansas law is available through Arkansas statutes via Justia. For a plain-language comparison of how states treat distracted driving, the Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a state law directory.

  • Read the charge listed on your citation.
  • Note the court name and any listed appearance or payment instructions.
  • Ask the clerk for the total due, including costs, before you pay.

Will court costs be added to my Arkansas ticket?

Yes. Court costs are added by the district court and vary by county. They are separate from the fine itself.

This is the main reason a published fine amount rarely matches what people actually pay. The fine is one part of the total, and the court's added costs are another.

Because costs vary by county, the same citation can produce a different total in a different place. We do not give a county-by-county figure because the locked sources do not set one.

Before paying, ask the court for an itemized total so you can see the fine and the added costs separately.

How many points does a texting ticket add in Arkansas?

Arkansas uses a points system. The locked state context describes it as 3–8 points by speed, and 14+ points can trigger suspension.

That description is tied to speed-based violations. It does not tell us how many points, if any, a texting / cell phone conviction adds, and we will not guess.

To find out how your specific violation is treated, check your citation and the Arkansas Department of Finance and Administration's Driver Services page. Driver Services is the agency that handles driving records.

  • Points from different violations can add up over time.
  • Reaching 14+ points can trigger a suspension.
  • Ask the court or Driver Services how your conviction will be recorded.

Does a texting ticket raise my insurance in Arkansas?

It can. Nationally, moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Arkansas-specific rate.

Whether your insurer reacts to a texting / cell phone conviction depends on the company, your policy and your driving record. We do not recommend or compare insurers.

If a conviction is what triggers a rate change, the outcome of your case matters as much as the fine. Your insurer or agent can tell you how it treats violations like this.

The ~20% figure is a national average. Your own change could be higher, lower or none at all.

Can a texting ticket be dismissed or reduced in Arkansas?

Sometimes, but the outcome is up to the court. We cannot predict whether yours would be dismissed, reduced or upheld.

Common options people look into include paying the ticket, contesting it in court, or asking the court about alternatives it may offer. Each carries trade-offs. Paying usually ends the matter quickly but may mean a conviction on your record, which is the thing that can affect points and insurance.

The court clerk can explain which procedures apply to your citation and what deadlines are printed on it. If you want advice on your specific case, a licensed attorney is the right person to ask. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice.

  • Find the court date or response deadline on your citation.
  • Ask the clerk what your choices are and what each would cost.
  • Keep copies of everything you file or pay.

What happens if I don't pay an Arkansas texting ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts can add consequences when a ticket goes unanswered, and a missed deadline may limit the choices you had.

Because Arkansas uses a points system where 14+ points can trigger suspension, a driving record already carrying points deserves extra care. Unresolved tickets can also complicate matters with Driver Services.

The exact penalties depend on the court. Check the instructions on your citation and contact the clerk if you cannot meet a deadline or cannot pay the full amount at once.
