How much is a texting or cell phone ticket in West Virginia?

Nationally, a first texting or cell phone offense carries a fine of $20–$500, depending on the state. In West Virginia, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (West Virginia Division of Motor Vehicles).

We do not list a single West Virginia dollar figure, because the amount is not fixed in one place we can verify. The number printed on your citation, or on the notice from the court, is the one that counts.

The $20–$500 range is a national comparison across states. It is not a West Virginia fine schedule.

Expect the total you pay to be higher than the fine alone. The next sections explain why.

How is the fine for a texting ticket set?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. That means the amount is tied to the court handling your case, not to a flat figure that applies the same way everywhere.

The West Virginia traffic code is published by the state, and you can read the statutes through West Virginia statutes via Justia. If you want the exact wording of the offense you were charged with, find the code section listed on your citation and read it there.

For a state-by-state comparison of how texting and handheld laws differ, the Governors Highway Safety Association state laws page is a useful reference.

  • Check the offense or code section written on the citation.
  • Check the amount or court instructions on the citation or notice.
  • Ask the court clerk if the paperwork is unclear.

Why is the total often more than the fine?

Municipal and magistrate court costs vary and usually exceed the fine. So the amount you owe at the clerk's window can be noticeably higher than the fine line on the citation.

Because those costs depend on the court, two drivers cited for the same offense in different places can owe different totals. We do not give a court-cost figure, since it varies and we would be guessing.

Before paying, ask the clerk for the total due, including all costs and surcharges. Ask what payment methods are accepted and what the deadline is.

Tip: compare the fine amount with the total due. The gap is the court costs and surcharges.

How many points does a texting ticket add in West Virginia?

The state's points system is described as 2–5 points by mph over, with 12+ points leading to a suspension. That scale is written around speed, so it does not map neatly onto a texting or cell phone violation.

We do not state a specific point value for texting, because that figure is not among the facts we can verify. Do not assume a number from the speeding scale.

To find out whether your conviction adds points, check with the West Virginia Division of Motor Vehicles and read the code section listed on your citation.

  • Points matter because 12+ points = suspension under the state's points system.
  • Points from several violations can add up over time.
  • Ask the DMV how your record would look after a conviction.

Does a texting ticket raise my insurance?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a West Virginia-specific one.

Whether a texting or cell phone conviction affects your rate depends on your insurer, your driving record and how the violation is recorded. We do not recommend or compare insurers.

If you are weighing options, remember that the cost of a higher premium can last longer than the fine itself. A premium increase usually continues for as long as your insurer counts the conviction.

The ~20% figure is a national average. Your own change could be higher, lower or none.

Your insurer or agent is the only source for what would happen to your own policy.

Can a texting ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes courts offer ways to resolve a citation other than paying it as written. Whether that is possible for you depends on the court, the charge and your record.

The usual starting points are on the citation: it will tell you whether you can pay, how to ask for a hearing and what the deadline is. The clerk of the court named on the citation can explain the process, though clerks cannot give legal advice.

  • Read the citation for the court name, the due date and the options listed.
  • Ask the clerk what the total due is and what choices exist.
  • Consider the effect on points and insurance before paying.
  • If you want legal advice, you may consult a licensed attorney of your own choosing.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm. We do not give legal advice and do not recommend any attorney.

What happens if I don't pay a texting ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts commonly respond to missed deadlines with added consequences, and a license problem can follow.

The exact steps in West Virginia depend on the court and the offense. Check your citation and the notice from the court, and ask the clerk or the West Virginia Division of Motor Vehicles what applies to your case.

Since court costs vary and usually exceed the fine, a late or unpaid ticket can also become more expensive to resolve than it first looked.

  • Note the due date as soon as you get the citation.
  • Keep a copy of the citation and any payment receipt.
  • Contact the court early if you cannot meet the deadline.
