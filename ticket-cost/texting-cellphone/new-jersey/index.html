How much is a texting or cell phone ticket in New Jersey?

Nationally, a first-offense texting or cell phone ticket costs $20–$500 depending on the state (New Jersey Motor Vehicle Commission). That is a national range, not a New Jersey price tag.

In New Jersey, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single statewide dollar figure here, because the number you owe is the one printed on your ticket or set by the court that handles it.

Bottom line: the fine is whatever your citation or the court says it is. The national $20–$500 range is context only.

The fine is only part of the cost. Points, a possible MVC surcharge and your insurance rate can add to the total over time. The sections below cover each one.

How is the fine for a texting ticket set in New Jersey?

The exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The statutes are published online, including through New Jersey statutes via Justia.

Because the court sets the amount, two drivers cited for similar conduct can see different totals. The court that hears your case, the details on the citation and any prior record can all matter.

To find your amount, check these places:

  • The citation itself, which may list a fine or a court date.
  • The court named on the ticket, which can tell you the amount owed.
  • The New Jersey Motor Vehicle Commission for points and license questions.

We do not guess at the figure. If a website quotes an exact New Jersey amount without pointing to a court or schedule, treat it with caution.

How many points is a texting ticket in New Jersey?

New Jersey's points system uses these tiers: 2 points (1–14 over), 4 points (15–29 over) and 5 points (30+ over). Reaching 12 points means a suspension.

These tiers are described in terms of how far "over" a limit someone is. We do not state a specific point value for a texting conviction, because the locked data does not give one. Check your citation and the New Jersey Motor Vehicle Commission to see whether points apply to your violation.

Points matter because they accumulate. A single ticket may not threaten your license, but several in a short period can.

Key threshold: 12 points = suspension.

What is the MVC surcharge, and when does it apply?

If you reach 6+ points within 3 years, the MVC adds an insurance surcharge of $150+. These surcharges are billed annually for 3 years on top of court fines.

This surcharge is separate from the court fine. You may pay the court first, then receive MVC bills in later years.

The surcharge only applies if your points reach the threshold. A driver with a clean record who gets one ticket may not reach 6 points, depending on what the citation carries.

  • Court fine: set by the court on the citation.
  • MVC surcharge: $150+ at 6+ points within 3 years.
  • Billing: annually for 3 years.

Look at the full picture before deciding how to handle a ticket. A modest fine can be followed by years of surcharge bills if points push you over the line.

Does a texting ticket raise car insurance in New Jersey?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a New Jersey rate.

Whether your insurer raises your rate depends on the company, your history and how the ticket is resolved. We do not recommend any insurer and cannot predict your premium.

Some practical points:

  • A conviction on your record is what usually drives rate changes.
  • Insurers set their own rules, so ask yours how it treats tickets.
  • The MVC surcharge is separate from anything your insurer charges.

Over several years, an insurance increase can cost more than the fine itself. That is worth weighing when you decide what to do.

Can a texting ticket be dismissed or reduced?

The court handling your citation decides what happens to it. Because the fine is set by the court, the outcome depends on the court and the facts of your case.

Drivers generally have a few paths: pay the ticket, contest it in court, or ask the court what options exist. Each has tradeoffs for points, surcharges and insurance.

  • Paying usually ends the matter quickly but is typically treated as a conviction.
  • Contesting means appearing before the court and presenting your side.
  • Asking the court about options can clarify what is available for your citation.

This page is not legal advice. For questions about your specific case, a licensed attorney can advise you. We do not recommend specific attorneys.

What happens if I don't pay a texting ticket?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. The citation sets out what you must do and by when, and the court can add consequences if you miss it.

We do not list specific late fees or deadlines, because those come from the court and your citation. Contact the court named on the ticket to confirm what is owed and what your options are.

Don't let a ticket sit. Read the citation, find the court and respond before the stated date.

Unresolved tickets can also affect your license standing with the New Jersey Motor Vehicle Commission. Confirm details with the MVC and the court rather than relying on third-party summaries.
