How much is a texting / cell phone ticket in Oklahoma?

Nationally, a first-offense texting or cell phone ticket falls between $20 and $500 depending on the state. In Oklahoma, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Service Oklahoma).

We do not publish a single Oklahoma dollar figure because the amount is not fixed in one place. The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code, and the number printed on your ticket or court notice is the one that counts.

The $20–$500 range is a national range across states. It is not a statement of what any Oklahoma court will charge.

The fine is only part of the bill. Court costs and statutory fees are added on top, which is covered below.

How is the fine set?

Oklahoma's traffic laws are in the state statutes, which you can read through Oklahoma statutes via Justia. The code sets the framework, and the court that handles your citation sets the exact amount within it.

That means two drivers cited for the same behavior can see different totals if their cases are handled by different courts. A municipal court and a district court may not list the same amount.

Where to look for your number:

  • The citation itself, which may show an amount or a court appearance requirement.
  • The court's notice or payment page for your case.
  • The clerk of the municipal or district court named on the ticket.

If the citation does not list an amount, ask the clerk rather than relying on a general estimate from a website, including this one.

What court costs and fees get added?

Court costs and statutory fees are added by the municipal or district court. These are separate from the fine and can change the total you owe.

We do not list a dollar amount for these fees because the amount depends on the court. Your payment notice should show the fine and the added costs, or a single total.

When comparing what you owe with a figure you saw elsewhere, check whether that figure included court costs. A fine-only number and an all-in total can be quite different.

How many points does a texting ticket carry in Oklahoma?

The sourced points information we use describes the state system this way: 2 points for ordinary speeding, or more at higher speeds, and 10 points in 5 years leads to suspension.

Those figures are the speeding example in the state system. We do not state a specific point value for a texting or cell phone violation, because that value was not part of the sourced facts for this page.

To find out whether your citation adds points, check the violation listed on the ticket and the statute it references. Service Oklahoma handles driver services, and its records are the place to confirm what is on your driving record.

Points matter because of the threshold: 10 points in 5 years = suspension. Several violations close together can add up.

Does a texting ticket raise my insurance?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Oklahoma-specific one.

Whether your own rate changes depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the violation is recorded. We do not recommend or compare insurers.

A few practical points:

  • The effect usually follows a conviction, so how the case ends matters.
  • The increase may last for as long as the violation stays on the record your insurer reviews.
  • Your insurer or agent can tell you how they treat this kind of violation.

Can a texting ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome depends on the court and the facts of your case. We cannot say whether any option is available to you, and nothing here is legal advice.

Common paths people look into include:

  • Paying the ticket, which generally resolves it but may count as a conviction.
  • Contesting the ticket and appearing in court.
  • Asking the court clerk what resolution options exist for your citation.

Because the court sets the amount and adds its own costs, the total can differ depending on how the case is resolved. Read your citation for deadlines and instructions, and confirm options with the court directly. For questions about your specific situation, a licensed Oklahoma attorney is the right person to ask.

What happens if I don't pay?

Ignoring a ticket tends to make things worse. Unpaid or unanswered citations can lead to added court action, and a license problem can follow, though the specifics depend on the court and your citation.

Your citation lists what you must do and by when. If you cannot meet the date or cannot pay the full amount, contact the court clerk before the deadline passes and ask what is possible.

Driver license status and records are handled through Service Oklahoma. If you are worried about the points threshold of 10 points in 5 years, check your record there.
