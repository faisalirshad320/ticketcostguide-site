How much is a texting or cell phone ticket in Wisconsin?

Nationally, a first-offense texting or cell phone ticket costs $20–$500, depending on the state (Wisconsin Department of Transportation — DMV). In Wisconsin, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation.

We do not publish a single Wisconsin dollar figure, because the amount that matters is the one printed on your citation or set by the court that handles it. Any website that quotes one fixed number for every driver is simplifying.

Two things shape what you actually owe:

  • The base amount the court sets for the violation.
  • The statutory surcharge stack, which is added to every forfeiture.

That means the total on your paperwork will be higher than the base amount alone. Read the whole citation or court notice, not just the first line.

Bottom line: the national range is $20–$500 for a first offense. Your Wisconsin amount is set by the court on the citation.

How is the fine set, and what are the surcharges?

Wisconsin sets the exact amount for this violation within the state's traffic code, and the court applies it to your case. You can read the statutes themselves through Wisconsin statutes via Justia.

Wisconsin traffic fines are generally called forfeitures. On top of the base forfeiture, a statutory surcharge stack is added to every forfeiture. We do not list individual surcharge amounts here, because we only publish figures we can source.

What this means in practice:

  • The base amount comes from the court's schedule for the violation.
  • Surcharges are added by statute and are not optional.
  • The total is what you pay if you resolve the ticket without contesting it.

If the total on your citation looks much higher than a number you saw online, the surcharge stack is the usual reason.

For a national comparison of how states handle phone-use laws, the Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws page is a useful reference.

How many points does a texting ticket add in Wisconsin?

Wisconsin's points system is 3 points (10 or less over), 4 points (11–19 over), and 6 points (20+ over). Accumulating 12 points in 12 months means suspension.

Those tiers are written by how far over a limit a driver was, so they read most naturally for speeding. A texting or cell phone citation does not have a speed to measure.

We therefore do not claim a specific point value for a texting conviction. To find out whether your conviction carries points, and how many, check:

  • The citation and any court paperwork.
  • Your driver record through the Wisconsin Department of Transportation — DMV.

If points do apply, they count together with points from other violations. That is why the 12-point threshold matters most to drivers who already have other tickets on their record.

Check your own record. A single ticket rarely threatens a license by itself, but several in a short period can.

Does a texting ticket raise insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Wisconsin-specific one.

Your own change could be smaller, larger, or nothing at all. It depends on your insurer, your driving history, and whether the ticket ends in a conviction on your record.

Points to keep in mind:

  • A 20% average increase can cost more over several years than the ticket itself.
  • Insurers generally look at convictions, not just citations.
  • Rules and rating practices differ by company, so we cannot predict your quote.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any specific insurer. If you want to know how a conviction might affect your premium, ask your own insurer or agent before you decide how to handle the ticket.

Can a texting ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome depends on the facts and on the court. We cannot tell you whether your ticket qualifies, and nothing here is legal advice.

Common ways drivers approach a citation include:

  • Paying it, which generally resolves the case but may mean a conviction on your record.
  • Contesting it, which means the court hears the case and decides.
  • Asking the court about reduced or alternative outcomes it may offer.

Because the Wisconsin amount is set by the court on the citation, the court that handles your case is the place to ask what options exist and what each would cost. Ask about the surcharge stack too, since it is added to every forfeiture.

Before you pay, weigh three things:

  1. The total on the citation, including surcharges.
  2. Whether a conviction could add points to your record.
  3. The possible insurance impact, about 20% on average nationally for moving-violation convictions.

If you want advice on your specific situation, speak to a licensed Wisconsin attorney of your own choosing. We do not recommend one.

What happens if I don't pay a texting ticket in Wisconsin?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. The specific penalties and deadlines are printed on your citation or court notice, so follow those rather than a general summary.

In general, unpaid or ignored tickets can lead to:

  • Additional court action on the case.
  • Added costs beyond the original amount.
  • Possible problems with your driving privileges.

We do not state exact deadlines or late fees because we do not have a sourced figure for them. The court listed on your citation can tell you the current due date and what happens after it.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court before the due date and ask what options it offers. That is generally a better path than waiting.

Also remember that points from this and other violations count toward the 12-points-in-12-months suspension threshold.
