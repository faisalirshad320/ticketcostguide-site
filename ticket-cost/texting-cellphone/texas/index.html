How much is a texting or cell phone ticket in Texas?

Nationally, a first-offense texting or cell phone ticket runs $20–$500 depending on the state (Texas Department of Public Safety). That is a national range, not a Texas price. In Texas, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation.

We do not publish a single Texas dollar figure because the number printed on your ticket or court paperwork is the one that counts. Anything else would be a guess.

Look at the citation itself. The amount due, the court name and the response deadline are all printed there or on the court's notice.

The total you pay can include more than the fine. State court costs are uniform, but municipal add-ons vary by court. Two drivers cited for the same act in different cities can end up with different totals.

How is the fine for texting while driving set in Texas?

The exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The statute defines the offense and the limits. The court handling your case settles the amount that appears on your citation.

That is why a statewide chart can only go so far. The pieces that make up what you owe are:

  • The fine: set by the court on the citation.
  • State court costs: uniform across Texas.
  • Municipal add-ons: these vary by court.

To read the underlying law, the Texas statutes are available through Justia. For a comparison of how states approach distracted driving, the Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a state-by-state overview.

If the amount on your paperwork looks unclear, the court clerk can explain what the total includes. Clerks can describe the paperwork, but they cannot give legal advice.

How many points does a texting ticket add in Texas?

Texas abolished its driver-point and surcharge program in 2019. Because of that, you should not expect a points tally to be added to your record for a texting ticket in the way older guides describe.

License action now follows specific offenses and repeat-conviction rules. In other words, the question is less about a running point total and more about what you were convicted of and whether it is a repeat.

Old articles that mention Texas points or annual surcharges may be out of date. Check the current rules with the Texas Department of Public Safety.

A repeat conviction is where the risk to your license is more likely to come from. If you already have other convictions on your record, ask the court or check your driver record before deciding how to respond to the ticket.

Does a texting ticket raise my insurance?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Texas-specific one, and your own change could be higher, lower or nothing at all.

Several things influence what an insurer does:

  • Whether the ticket ends in a conviction on your driving record.
  • Your insurer's own rating rules.
  • Your other tickets and claims history.
  • When your policy renews.

Because the fine is only part of the cost, a ticket with a modest fine can still cost more over time if it raises your premium. We do not recommend any insurer. Your insurer or agent can tell you how your policy treats a conviction.

Remember that the 20% is an average across moving violations generally. It does not describe a typical texting ticket specifically.

Can a texting ticket be dismissed or reduced in Texas?

Sometimes outcomes other than paying in full are available, but this depends on the court and the facts of your case. We have no locked figures on how often that happens, so we will not suggest odds.

The usual routes are:

  1. Pay the ticket. This generally resolves the matter but may mean a conviction on your record.
  2. Contest it. You can ask for a hearing and present your side to the court.
  3. Ask about court programs. Some courts offer alternatives. Ask the clerk what your court allows and what it costs.

Paying and contesting have different consequences for your record, and for insurance, so decide before the deadline on the citation. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot advise which choice is right for you. If you want legal advice, talk to a licensed attorney of your choosing.

What happens if I don't pay a texting ticket in Texas?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts set deadlines, and missing one can lead to added costs or further action on your case or license. We cannot give exact penalties because the locked sources do not list them.

Because municipal add-ons vary by court, late costs also depend on where the ticket was issued. The notice you receive or the court clerk will tell you what applies to your case.

If you cannot pay by the deadline, contact the court before it passes. Do not wait for the deadline to expire.

Also keep in mind that license action in Texas now follows specific offenses and repeat-conviction rules rather than a points system. An unresolved ticket can add to your record in ways that matter later, so deal with it early.
