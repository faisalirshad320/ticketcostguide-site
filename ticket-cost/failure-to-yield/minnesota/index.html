How much is a failure to yield ticket in Minnesota?

Nationally, failure to yield tickets cost $50–$500 by state. In Minnesota, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Minnesota Department of Public Safety — Driver and Vehicle Services).

We do not publish a single Minnesota dollar figure because the amount is not fixed in one place for every case. The number printed on your citation, or on the court notice that follows it, is the one that applies to you.

The $50–$500 range is a national figure across states. It is not a Minnesota schedule, and your Minnesota ticket may not fall at any particular point inside it.

On top of the fine, a statutory state surcharge is added to every payable fine. Your total can therefore be higher than the fine line alone.

How is the fine for failure to yield set in Minnesota?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. That means the court, not the officer, settles the amount that appears on the citation or in the court's notice.

Failure to yield covers a range of situations, such as not giving way at an intersection, to a pedestrian, or to an emergency vehicle. The circumstances in your case can matter to how the court treats it.

  • Read the citation first. It shows the charge and usually points to where the amount and next steps are listed.
  • Check the court notice. The court handling your case is the source for the exact figure.
  • Ask the court clerk. Clerks can confirm amounts and payment options, though they cannot give legal advice.

Because the Minnesota amount is set by the court on the citation, any website quoting one flat Minnesota price for every driver is guessing.

What extra costs are added to the fine?

A statutory state surcharge is added to every payable fine in Minnesota. This applies on top of the base fine for failure to yield.

We do not list a dollar amount for the surcharge or for any other court costs here, because we only publish figures we can tie to a source. Your court notice should show the total due once any surcharge is included.

When you compare what you owe against the national $50–$500 range, remember the national range describes typical fines by state. Your Minnesota total may include the surcharge as a separate addition.

  • Fine: set by the court on the citation.
  • Surcharge: statutory, added to every payable fine.
  • Possible insurance cost: a separate, ongoing expense (see below).

How many points does failure to yield add in Minnesota?

None. Minnesota has no point system. License action follows the record of convictions and specific high-speed offenses (Minnesota Department of Public Safety — Driver and Vehicle Services).

That does not mean the ticket is harmless. A conviction can go on your record, and the record is what drives license action in Minnesota.

Many other states count points for the same violation. If you have read that failure to yield adds points, that information probably refers to a different state.

No points does not mean no consequences. The conviction itself can matter for your driving record and for how insurers view you.

For the rules that govern license action, see Minnesota Driver and Vehicle Services and the state traffic code through the Minnesota statutes on Justia.

Does a failure to yield ticket raise insurance in Minnesota?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Minnesota-specific one.

Your own change could be higher, lower, or nothing at all. It depends on your insurer, your driving history, and how the violation is recorded.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer and cannot predict what your company will do. If you want to know how a ticket might affect your rate, your insurer or agent is the right place to ask.

  • The ~20% figure is an average across the US.
  • It applies to moving-violation convictions in general, not only failure to yield.
  • The fine and surcharge are one-time costs, while a premium increase can continue after the ticket is paid.

Can a failure to yield ticket be dismissed or reduced in Minnesota?

Possibly, but we cannot tell you how your case will turn out. The court sets the amount on the citation, and the court decides how a contested case is handled.

The citation and the court notice explain your choices, such as paying or contesting the charge. Read them carefully before you act, because paying a ticket usually ends the matter and may mean a conviction goes on your record.

Since the record of convictions drives license action in Minnesota, it is worth understanding the consequences before you pay. Many drivers weigh the fine against possible insurance effects.

  • Ask the court clerk what options your citation allows.
  • Keep your citation and any court paperwork.
  • If you want advice on your specific case, consider speaking with a licensed attorney. We do not recommend any particular attorney.

What happens if I don't pay a failure to yield ticket?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. The specific penalties and deadlines are set by the court and Minnesota law, and we do not state them here because we cannot confirm them from our sources.

What we can say is that the citation and court notice are the authoritative source for your due date and your options. Missing the deadline can add consequences beyond the original fine.

  1. Find the due date or court date on your citation.
  2. Contact the court listed on the citation if anything is unclear.
  3. Decide whether to pay or contest before that date.
  4. Keep a record of how and when you responded.

Because a statutory surcharge is added to every payable fine, the amount due on paper may not match the base fine you expected.
