How much is a failure to yield ticket in Connecticut?

Nationally the typical range is $50–$500 by state. In Connecticut, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Connecticut Department of Motor Vehicles).

We do not publish a single Connecticut dollar figure for this violation. The number that applies to you is the one printed on your ticket or given by the court.

The $50–$500 range is a national spread across states. It is not a Connecticut fine schedule, and your ticket may fall inside or outside it.

If you only have a rough idea of the cost, look at the citation first. It is the most reliable source for what you would owe if you pay without contesting.

How is the fine set?

The fine for failure to yield in Connecticut is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The amount on the citation reflects that court-set figure, not a number from a third-party website.

The statutes are published online, including through Connecticut statutes via Justia. They describe the offense and the legal framework. The payable amount on your ticket is what you will actually be asked to pay.

Because the amount comes from the citation, two drivers cited for similar conduct can see different totals. Details of the stop and the specific charge written on the ticket can matter.

Do not rely on estimates from other drivers. Use the citation and the court's instructions.

What fees and surcharges are added to the fine?

State fees and surcharges are built into the payable amount on the ticket. That means the number you are told to pay already includes them.

In practice, this matters in two ways:

  • You should not expect a separate surcharge bill after paying the stated amount.
  • The payable total can be higher than the base fine alone, so compare the full payable amount, not just the fine line.

We do not list individual surcharge amounts because they are not part of our locked figures for this page. If the ticket shows a breakdown, use it. If it does not, ask the court clerk what the total covers.

How many points does a failure to yield ticket carry in Connecticut?

Connecticut uses a DMV point system. Under it, speeding adds points, and 10 points = 30-day suspension (Connecticut Department of Motor Vehicles).

Those are the only point figures we have verified for this page. We do not state a specific point value for failure to yield, because we have not confirmed one from the sources listed.

Before you pay, check the Connecticut DMV for how your specific violation is treated. Paying a ticket is generally treated as a conviction, which can affect your record.

The 10-point threshold is the one to watch. Points from several violations can add up, so a single ticket may matter more if your record is not clean.

Does a failure to yield ticket raise insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums ~20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Connecticut-specific rate.

Your own change, if any, depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the violation is recorded. We do not recommend or compare insurers.

A few things affect whether the ticket reaches your premium:

  • Whether the ticket results in a conviction on your record.
  • Whether you have other recent violations.
  • When your policy next renews and when your insurer reviews your record.

If premiums are a concern, ask your insurer how it treats a moving violation before deciding how to respond to the ticket.

Can a failure to yield ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but we cannot tell you whether it will happen in your case. We are not a law firm and do not give legal advice.

In general, a driver cited for a moving violation can choose between:

  • Paying the amount on the citation, which usually ends the case but may result in a conviction.
  • Contesting the ticket and asking the court to review it.
  • Asking the court clerk what other options exist for your specific citation.

The citation and the court will tell you which choices are open to you and how to respond. Read it carefully, because the instructions and deadlines are specific to your ticket.

For questions about your rights or the strength of your case, a licensed Connecticut attorney is the right person to ask. We do not recommend a specific attorney.

What happens if I don't pay?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. The amount owed is set by the court, and the court controls what follows if it goes unpaid.

Common consequences of ignoring a traffic citation in many states include added penalties, a hold or action on your license, and a harder time resolving the case later. We have not confirmed Connecticut's specific consequences from our listed sources, so check with the court and the DMV.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court before the deadline on your citation and ask what is available.

Responding on time, whether you pay or contest, keeps your options open.
