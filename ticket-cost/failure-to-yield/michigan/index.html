How much is a failure to yield ticket in Michigan?

The figure most often quoted is national: $50–$500 by state. In Michigan, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Michigan Secretary of State).

We do not publish a single Michigan dollar amount, because the sources we rely on do not set one. The number you owe is the number the court assigns to your citation.

The $50–$500 range is a national spread across states. It is not a Michigan schedule, and your Michigan ticket may fall anywhere the court decides.

The total also includes more than the fine itself. District court costs vary by court and make up much of the total, so two drivers cited for the same thing can see different bottom lines.

How is the fine for failure to yield set?

In Michigan, the court sets the exact fine for this violation within the state's traffic code. The amount is not fixed by the officer who writes the ticket.

Several things can sit behind the final number:

  • The specific violation written on your citation.
  • The court that handles your case, since district court costs vary by court.
  • Whether the matter ends in a conviction, a reduced charge or a dismissal.

Because court costs make up much of the total, the cost line deserves as much attention as the fine line. Read the citation and any court notice for both.

The Michigan Vehicle Code is available through Michigan statutes via Justia. It is a reference for reading the law, not a way to predict your exact amount.

What does a failure to yield citation mean?

A failure to yield citation generally says a driver did not give the right of way when required. That can come up at intersections, when turning, at stop or yield signs, or around pedestrians.

The wording on your citation matters. It names the offense the court is dealing with, and that offense drives what happens next with cost and your driving record.

If a crash was involved, other issues may apply, such as insurance claims or additional charges. TicketCostGuide does not give legal advice, so a citation tied to a crash is a good reason to read official court information closely.

How many points does a failure to yield ticket carry in Michigan?

The points framework we can cite is this: 2–4 points by mph over under §257.320a, and 12 points in 2 years = driver reexamination (Michigan statutes via Justia).

That schedule is described by mph over, which is a speed-based measure. Failure to yield is not a speed offense, so we do not claim a specific point value for it.

To learn what, if anything, a conviction adds to your record, check your citation and your driving record with the Michigan Secretary of State.

The reexamination threshold still matters. Points from different violations add up, and 12 points in 2 years means driver reexamination.

Does a failure to yield ticket raise insurance in Michigan?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Michigan-specific one.

Your own change could be higher, lower or none. It depends on your insurer, your history and whether the ticket becomes a conviction on your record.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer. If you are worried about your rate, ask your insurer how it treats a violation like this before you decide how to respond to the citation.

A ticket that costs a modest fine can still cost more over time if it leads to higher premiums. That is why the outcome in court matters beyond the amount you pay on the day.

Can a failure to yield ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but we cannot say how often or under what terms in your court. Outcomes depend on the facts, the court and the charge.

Common paths drivers consider include:

  • Paying the citation, which usually resolves the ticket but may mean a conviction on your record.
  • Contesting the ticket and presenting your side to the court.
  • Asking the court what options it offers for your citation.

Each path affects cost, points and insurance differently. Because court costs vary by court, ask the clerk what the total would be before you decide.

For personal advice about your case, a licensed attorney is the right person to ask. We do not recommend a specific one.

What happens if I don't pay a failure to yield ticket?

Ignoring a citation tends to make things worse. Courts generally treat an unaddressed ticket as a problem that can grow beyond the original amount.

We do not state exact penalties or deadlines here, since our sources do not set them for your case. Your citation and court notice list the dates and steps that apply to you.

If you are unsure of a deadline, contact the court named on your citation. The clerk can tell you the amount due and how to respond.

Unresolved violations can also affect your driving record. The Michigan Secretary of State is the place to check your record status.
