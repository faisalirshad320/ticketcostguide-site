How much is a failure to yield ticket in North Dakota?

Nationally, failure to yield tickets run $50–$500 by state. In North Dakota the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (North Dakota Department of Transportation, last reviewed Oct 2026).

We do not publish a single North Dakota dollar figure, because we can only report amounts we can source. The national range is a general guide to how much states differ, not a prediction of your fine.

The number that matters is the one printed on your citation or given by the court. Use the national range only for rough context.

Failure to yield usually means you did not give way to another driver, a pedestrian or a cyclist when the law required it. The details of the violation can affect how the court handles the case.

How is the fine for failure to yield set?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. It is not a figure that changes with your car or your insurer. The state statutes are available through North Dakota statutes via Justia.

Several things can lead to different amounts on different tickets:

  • The specific right-of-way provision cited on the ticket.
  • Whether the court uses a set amount or has discretion in your case.
  • Whether anyone was hurt or property was damaged, which can mean different or additional charges.

If the citation lists an amount, that is your starting point. If it only gives a court date, the court will tell you the amount.

Are there extra court costs or surcharges?

For this violation in North Dakota, the statutory fee is usually the whole amount. There is no large stack of added court costs and surcharges, which is common in some other states.

That does not mean extras are impossible. If you miss a deadline or the case goes beyond a simple payment, the total can change. Ask the court clerk to confirm the full amount before you pay.

When you compare North Dakota with other states, remember that a low fine and a low total are not always the same thing. In North Dakota the two are usually close.

How many points does failure to yield add in North Dakota?

North Dakota uses a points system. The locked state information says points are assigned by mph over on the ND schedule, and that 12+ points = suspension.

That schedule is built around speed, so it does not map neatly onto a right-of-way violation. We therefore do not state a point value for failure to yield. Confirm how your conviction is scored with the North Dakota Department of Transportation (NDOT).

The suspension threshold of 12+ points applies to points added together. A single ticket is unlikely to reach it alone, but it adds to your record.

Points are one reason to look carefully at how a ticket is resolved, not only at the fine.

Does a failure to yield ticket raise insurance?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a North Dakota one.

Your own change could be higher, lower or nothing at all. It depends on your insurer, your driving record and whether the violation was part of a crash.

Failure to yield is often tied to collisions, so an at-fault accident can affect rates more than the ticket does. TicketCostGuide does not recommend insurers. Ask your own insurer how a conviction would affect your policy.

Over several years, the premium effect can add up to more than the fine itself.

Can a failure to yield ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes. The citation will say whether you can pay, appear in court, or contest the charge. The options available depend on the court and the facts of your case.

Common routes include:

  • Paying the ticket. This usually resolves it quickly, but it can mean a conviction on your record and points.
  • Contesting the charge. You can ask the court to review the facts, such as who had the right of way.
  • Asking the court about alternatives. Some courts offer other outcomes. Ask the clerk what is available for your citation.

This is general information, not legal advice. For advice on your case, consider speaking with a licensed attorney. We do not recommend any specific one.

What happens if I don't pay the ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts can add penalties, issue a warrant, or report the unpaid matter to licensing authorities. The details depend on the court.

Because the North Dakota amount is set on the citation, you should know what you owe and when it is due. If you cannot pay in full or need more time, contact the court clerk before the deadline passes.

Failure to respond can also put your license at risk. That is separate from the 12+ point suspension threshold, which relates to accumulated points.
