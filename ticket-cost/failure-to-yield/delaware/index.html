How much is a failure to yield ticket in Delaware?

The only figure we can anchor to is the national one: $50–$500 by state. In Delaware, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Delaware Division of Motor Vehicles).

We do not publish an exact Delaware dollar amount for this violation, because the court decides it within the state's traffic code. Any single number you see elsewhere may not match what is printed on your ticket.

The national range is not a Delaware fine. Use it only as a rough sense of scale, then read your own citation.

How is the fine set?

Delaware's traffic code sets the framework, and the court assigns the amount for your case. That amount appears on the citation or in the court's notice to you.

The statutory fine is not the whole bill. Court costs and state fees are added to it, so the total can be higher than the base fine.

  • Read the citation for the fine and any listed costs.
  • Ask the court clerk for the full amount due before you pay.
  • Compare the total, not just the fine line.

The Delaware code is available through Delaware statutes via Justia if you want to read the text yourself.

What court costs and surcharges are added?

Our sources state that court costs and state fees are added to the statutory fine. They do not give us a Delaware dollar amount for these add-ons, so we do not list one.

What this means in practice is that two people with the same base fine may not owe the same total. The final figure depends on what the court assesses in your case.

The court's notice is the best place to find the number that applies to you. If the paperwork is unclear, the court clerk can explain how the total was reached.

How many points does failure to yield add in Delaware?

The locked state context describes the points system this way: 2–5 points by mph over. That wording is tied to speed, so it does not map neatly onto a right-of-way violation.

Because of that, we cannot tell you the exact point value for your failure to yield charge. Confirm it with the Delaware Division of Motor Vehicles or check the code section named on your citation.

The thresholds that matter are clear:

  • 12 points in 2 years: behavior modification course.
  • 14 or more: suspension.

Points from other tickets in the same period count toward those totals, so one more violation can matter more than it seems.

Does a failure to yield ticket raise insurance in Delaware?

It can. Nationally, moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Delaware-specific one.

Your own change could be higher, lower or none at all. It depends on your insurer, your driving record and whether the ticket ends in a conviction.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend insurers or give insurance advice. If you are worried about your rate, your insurer or policy documents are the place to check how violations are treated.

The 20% figure is an average across the US. Treat it as a rough benchmark, not a prediction for your policy.

Can a failure to yield ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but our sources do not give us Delaware-specific rules on dismissal or reduction, so we cannot promise any outcome. Outcomes depend on the facts of the stop, the evidence and the court handling your case.

In general, a driver who receives a citation has a few options:

  • Pay the amount and accept the conviction.
  • Contest the ticket and present your side to the court.
  • Ask the court what options exist for your situation, such as a course or a different resolution.

Each option can affect your record, points and insurance differently. Because convictions drive the insurance effect, it is worth understanding the consequences before paying. If you want legal advice, speak with a licensed attorney; we do not recommend specific ones.

What happens if I don't pay?

Ignoring a citation is rarely a good idea. Unpaid tickets can lead to added penalties and can cause trouble with your driving privileges. Our sources do not give Delaware dollar amounts for late penalties, so we do not list any.

If you cannot pay the full amount by the deadline, contact the court before the due date and ask what is possible. The court's notice should list the deadline and how to respond.

Also keep the points thresholds in mind. Reaching 12 points in 2 years leads to a behavior modification course, and 14 or more leads to suspension.
