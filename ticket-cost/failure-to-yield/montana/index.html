How much is a failure to yield ticket in Montana?

Nationally, failure to yield tickets fall in a range of $50–$500 by state. In Montana the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Montana Department of Justice — Motor Vehicle Division).

We do not publish a single Montana dollar figure, because the amount depends on the court and the citation. The national range is context only. It is not a quote for your ticket.

The number printed on your citation, or the figure the court gives you, is the one that counts. Court surcharges are added to the statutory fine, so the total you owe can be higher than the fine alone.

If the citation does not show a clear total, contact the court that issued it and ask for the full amount due, including surcharges.

How is a failure to yield fine set in Montana?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. It is not a fixed amount that applies the same way everywhere.

That is why two drivers with the same violation can see different totals. The court, the details of the citation and added surcharges can all change the number.

  • Statutory fine: the amount the court sets within the traffic code.
  • Court surcharges: added on top of the statutory fine.
  • Total due: the fine plus surcharges, as shown by the court.

The Montana traffic statutes are available through Montana statutes via Justia. Read the exact section named on your citation rather than relying on a general summary.

How many points does a failure to yield ticket carry in Montana?

Convictions carry conviction points toward Montana's habitual-offender threshold. That threshold is 30 points in 3 years, which results in revocation (Montana Department of Justice — Motor Vehicle Division).

We do not state a per-violation point value for failure to yield, because it is not among the figures we verified. To find the points for your specific conviction, check with the Motor Vehicle Division or review your driving record.

Points attach to convictions, not to the act of receiving a citation. The outcome of your case matters for what ends up on your record.

Because the threshold counts points over a 3-year window, a single failure to yield conviction is only part of the picture. Other violations in the same period add to the total.

Does a failure to yield ticket raise insurance in Montana?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Montana-specific one.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your driving history and whether the ticket results in a conviction. Some drivers see no change. Others see more than the average.

  • The 20% figure is an average across the US.
  • It applies to moving-violation convictions in general.
  • Your insurer decides how it rates your policy, usually at renewal.

We do not recommend any insurer. If you want to know how a ticket would affect your rate, ask your own insurer or agent.

Can a failure to yield ticket be dismissed or reduced in Montana?

Some tickets are dismissed or resolved in a way that changes the cost. That is decided by the court, not by this guide. We cannot say how likely it is in your case.

The citation usually explains your options, such as paying, contesting or contacting the court. Read it closely before you decide.

The choice matters because a conviction is what triggers points and insurance effects. Paying the fine is generally treated as accepting the violation, so consider the points and premium impact before you pay.

This page is not legal advice. If you are weighing whether to contest the ticket, you may want to speak with a licensed Montana attorney of your own choosing.

What happens if I don't pay a failure to yield ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. The details depend on the court and on Montana's rules, so we do not list specific penalties or deadlines here.

Because court surcharges are added to the statutory fine, the amount due can already be higher than the fine alone. Late handling may add further consequences.

  • Find the court and any response date printed on the citation.
  • Contact the court directly if you are unsure of the total or the deadline.
  • Ask the court what options exist if you cannot pay the full amount at once.

Unresolved tickets can also affect your driving record and license status. The Motor Vehicle Division at Montana Department of Justice — Motor Vehicle Division is the place to confirm your record.

What should I do after getting a failure to yield ticket in Montana?

Start with the citation itself. It shows the violation, the court and how to respond.

  1. Read the citation and note the court and the response instructions.
  2. Ask the court for the total due, including surcharges.
  3. Decide whether to pay or contest, keeping points and insurance in mind.
  4. Check your driving record for conviction points after the case ends.
