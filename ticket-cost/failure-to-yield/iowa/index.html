How much is a failure to yield ticket in Iowa?

Nationally, a failure to yield ticket runs $50–$500 by state. In Iowa, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Iowa Department of Transportation — Motor Vehicle Division).

We do not publish a single Iowa dollar figure, because the amount on your citation is what counts. The national range is context, not a promise of what you will pay.

Your citation is the best source for your amount. Read it for the fine, the court name and any instructions for responding.

The total you owe is usually more than the base fine, because a statutory surcharge and court costs are added. The next sections explain how those pieces fit together.

How is the fine for failure to yield set in Iowa?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. It is not a flat number that every driver pays in every case.

Failure to yield covers many situations, such as turning left across traffic, entering a road from a stop, or not giving way to a pedestrian or emergency vehicle. The facts of the stop, and any crash that followed, can matter to how the court treats the citation.

Iowa statutes are published online (Iowa statutes via Justia). You can read the traffic code there, but your citation tells you which provision you were charged under. We do not cite a specific section number here because we have not verified one for your situation.

  • The amount is set by the court, not by a national table.
  • The citation is the controlling document for your case.
  • A different charge on the same stop can carry a different amount.

Do surcharges and court costs add to the fine?

Yes. A statutory surcharge and court costs are added to every scheduled fine in Iowa.

This means the number you see for the fine alone is not the number you hand over. The payment total on your citation or on the court's payment page is the figure to plan around.

We do not state dollar amounts for the surcharge or court costs, because they are not part of our verified figures. Ask the clerk of court for the full amount due if the citation is unclear.

Before paying, confirm whether the total shown already includes the surcharge and court costs.

How many points is a failure to yield in Iowa?

Iowa tracks convictions for its habitual-offender and suspension rules rather than a public running point total for ordinary speeding. That is the point treatment we can verify.

We do not list a point value for failure to yield, and we will not guess one. What matters in practice is that a conviction goes on your driving record and can count toward Iowa's suspension and habitual-offender rules.

If you want to know where you stand, the Iowa DOT Motor Vehicle Division is the place to ask about your driving record and any license status questions (Iowa Department of Transportation — Motor Vehicle Division).

  • No public running point total is the verified treatment for ordinary speeding.
  • Convictions are what the state tracks.
  • Repeat convictions are the concern under the habitual-offender and suspension rules.

Does a failure to yield ticket raise insurance in Iowa?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums ~20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Iowa-specific one.

Whether your own rate changes depends on your insurer, your history and whether the ticket ends in a conviction. We do not recommend or compare insurers.

A failure to yield is often tied to a collision, and a crash can affect your premium separately from the ticket. If you are worried about the effect, ask your insurer how it treats a conviction before you decide how to respond to the citation.

The ~20% figure is a national average. Your change could be higher, lower or none at all.

Can a failure to yield ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but we cannot tell you whether it will happen in your case. We are not a law firm and do not give legal advice.

Because the court sets the amount within the state's traffic code, the court is where any outcome is decided. Your citation lists how to respond, whether by paying or by contesting it.

Common questions to consider before choosing:

  • Is paying treated as a conviction on your record?
  • Does the court offer any alternative to a conviction for this citation?
  • Was there a crash, an injury or another charge on the same stop?
  • Would a lawyer's advice be worth it for your situation?

If you want legal guidance, speak with a licensed Iowa attorney. We do not recommend a specific one.

What happens if I don't pay the ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. The court can add consequences, and an unresolved citation can affect your license status.

Because Iowa tracks convictions for its habitual-offender and suspension rules, leaving a ticket unresolved can create problems beyond the original fine. We cannot say exactly which steps apply to you, so check the deadlines and instructions printed on the citation.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the clerk of court before the deadline and ask what is available. Do not assume the court will extend the date without being asked.

Respond to the citation using the instructions on it. Silence is rarely the cheaper choice.
