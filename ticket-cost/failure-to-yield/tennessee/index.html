How much is a failure to yield ticket in Tennessee?

Nationally, failure to yield tickets cost $50–$500 by state; in Tennessee the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Tennessee Department of Safety and Homeland Security). That is the only fine figure we can responsibly give, because the court decides the final number.

The $50–$500 range is national. It shows how widely states differ, and it is not a Tennessee price list. Your own total depends on the court that handles your case and what is printed on your citation.

Read the citation first. It will show either a fine amount or a court date, and that is the number or process that applies to you.

Most of the total is usually not the fine itself. County court costs and state litigation taxes make up most of the amount you pay, so two drivers with the same violation can owe different totals.

How is the fine set, and why do totals differ by county?

The exact fine for failure to yield in Tennessee is set by the court within the state's traffic code. Tennessee statutes are available through Justia if you want to read the code yourself.

On top of the fine, you pay court costs and state litigation taxes. County court costs and state litigation taxes make up most of the total, and the county costs vary by court.

This is why a general figure from a website, including ours, cannot replace your citation. The practical ways to find your total are:

  • Check the amount or instructions printed on the citation.
  • Contact the clerk of the court named on the ticket.
  • Use the court's official payment channel if it lists your case.

Ask the clerk what the stated total includes, so you know whether court costs and taxes are already counted.

What does a failure to yield citation mean?

A failure to yield citation says an officer believes you did not give way to another vehicle, a pedestrian or another road user when required. Typical situations include left turns, stop and yield signs, merging and entering a roadway.

It is a moving violation. That matters because moving violations are the kind of conviction that can add points and affect insurance pricing.

If a crash was involved, the situation can be more complicated than a simple ticket. Our figures here cover the ticket itself and do not cover crash-related costs.

How many points does a failure to yield add in Tennessee?

Tennessee uses a points system of 1–8 points by mph over. For example, it assigns 3 points at 6–15 over and 4 at 16–25. Those examples describe speeding tiers, so they do not map directly onto a failure to yield.

We do not have a sourced point value for failure to yield, so we will not guess one. Confirm the points for your specific violation with the Tennessee Department of Safety and Homeland Security or the court handling your case.

The key threshold: 12 points in 12 months = suspension notice.

Because points are cumulative, an earlier ticket can matter. A failure to yield on top of other recent moving violations could bring you closer to that threshold.

Does a failure to yield ticket raise insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, so your own change could be higher, lower or nothing at all.

Several things affect what your insurer does:

  • Whether the ticket ends in a conviction on your record.
  • Your driving history and any recent claims.
  • Your insurer's own rating rules and when your policy renews.

We do not recommend any insurer. If you are worried about a renewal, ask your own insurer how a conviction would be treated before you decide how to handle the ticket.

Can a failure to yield ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but that is decided by the court and not by us. A ticket can be handled by paying it, or by contesting it in court.

Paying generally resolves the citation, but it usually means accepting the violation. That can bring the points and insurance effects described above.

Some courts offer other ways to resolve a ticket, such as a reduced charge or a driver-improvement course. We cannot confirm what your court offers. Ask the clerk what is available for your citation and what it would do to your record and your total cost.

This page is not legal advice. If the stakes are high for you, such as a crash, a license at risk or a commercial license, you may want to speak with a licensed attorney of your own choosing.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the ticket?

Ignoring a citation tends to make things worse. Courts can add penalties, and unresolved tickets can lead to license problems or further court action.

Exact consequences depend on the court. Contact the clerk before your due date if you cannot pay the full amount, and ask what is possible.

Also keep the points threshold in mind: 12 points in 12 months = suspension notice. An unresolved ticket can complicate your record at exactly the wrong time.
