How much is a failure to yield ticket in Kentucky?

There is no single Kentucky price to quote. Nationally, a failure to yield ticket falls in a range of $50–$500 by state, and in Kentucky the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Kentucky Transportation Cabinet).

That means the number printed on your ticket, or the amount the court assigns, is the one that counts. A national range is a rough guide to how much states differ, not a prediction for your case.

The $50–$500 range is a national figure. We do not give an exact Kentucky dollar amount because the court sets it.

The fine is also only part of the bill. Court costs come on top and can change what you actually pay, which is covered below.

How is the fine and court cost set in Kentucky?

In Kentucky, the court sets the exact fine for this violation within the state's traffic code. The statutes are published in the Kentucky Revised Statutes, which you can browse through Justia.

Court costs are set by the district court and usually exceed the fine itself. Two people cited for the same violation can therefore see different totals depending on the court handling the case.

  • Fine: set by the court on the citation.
  • Court costs: set by the district court, and usually larger than the fine.
  • Total: fine plus court costs, so read the full amount due, not just the fine line.

If your citation shows a payment amount, check whether it includes court costs. If it is unclear, the court clerk listed on the citation can tell you what the total covers.

What does failure to yield mean on a Kentucky ticket?

A failure to yield citation generally means an officer says you did not give the right of way to another vehicle, a pedestrian, or another road user when required. Common settings include intersections, stop and yield signs, left turns, merging, and driveways.

The exact wording of the charge appears on your citation. It can matter, because the specific statute cited affects how the court treats the case. Kentucky's statutes are available through Justia.

If a crash was involved, the situation may be more complicated than a ticket alone. Costs, insurance claims and fault findings can all follow, and those depend on facts we cannot assess here.

How many points does a failure to yield ticket carry in Kentucky?

The Kentucky points schedule we reference is tiered by speed: 3 points (10–15 over) or 6 points (16–25 over). A driver who is 26+ over triggers a hearing. Reaching 12 points in 2 years means suspension.

Those tiers are written in terms of speeding, so they do not tell you the point value for a failure to yield conviction. Do not assume your ticket carries a particular number.

To confirm the points for your exact charge, check your citation and your driving record with the Kentucky Transportation Cabinet.

What does apply across the board is the suspension threshold. Points from different tickets add up, so a ticket that looks minor can matter if you already have others on your record within the same 2 years.

Does a failure to yield ticket raise your insurance?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Kentucky quote.

Your own change could be higher, lower, or none at all. It depends on your insurer, your record, and whether the violation is treated as a conviction. We do not recommend any insurer, and we cannot predict what yours will do.

A failure to yield ticket tied to a crash may be weighed differently from one with no collision. If you want to know the effect, ask your insurer how it treats a conviction of this type, and ask when the change would take effect at renewal.

When comparing costs, remember that a premium increase can continue long after the fine is paid, so it may be a larger cost than the ticket.

Can a failure to yield ticket be dismissed or reduced in Kentucky?

Sometimes a court can dismiss, amend or reduce a charge, but outcomes depend on the facts, the court, and the evidence. We cannot say how likely that is in your case, and this site does not give legal advice.

Options people commonly look at include:

  • Paying the citation, which usually resolves the matter but may mean a conviction.
  • Contesting the ticket in court.
  • Asking the court clerk what programs or alternatives the court offers, if any.
  • Speaking with a licensed attorney about your situation.

Because the court sets the fine and court costs, ask the clerk how the total would change under each option. Also ask how each option would be reported on your driving record, since that affects both points and insurance.

What happens if I don't pay a failure to yield ticket?

Ignoring a citation rarely makes it go away. Courts can add consequences when a ticket is neither paid nor answered, and these may include license problems or additional costs.

The specific steps and timing are on your citation and with the court that issued it. Read the instructions on the ticket carefully and contact the clerk as soon as possible if anything is unclear.

Remember that unresolved tickets and added points can combine. Reaching 12 points in 2 years means suspension, so an unpaid or convicted ticket can matter more if your record already has points.

You can check licensing requirements and your record status through the Kentucky Transportation Cabinet.
