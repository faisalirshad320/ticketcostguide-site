How much is a failure to yield ticket in Wyoming?

Nationally, a failure to yield ticket falls in a range of $50–$500 by state. In Wyoming, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Wyoming Department of Transportation).

We do not print a single Wyoming dollar figure here. The amount depends on the court and on the charge written on your ticket. The national range is a benchmark, not a Wyoming price.

Court fees are added per the local schedule. The total you owe can therefore be higher than the fine line alone.

Read the citation first. The amount, the charge and the court's instructions are printed on it or on the notice the court sends.

How is the fine for failure to yield set?

The exact fine for this violation in Wyoming is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The officer's citation identifies the charge, and the court handles the amount from there.

That is why two drivers cited for similar conduct can see different totals. The charge on the ticket, the court that hears it and the local fee schedule can all play a part.

You can read the underlying traffic provisions in the Wyoming statutes on Justia. Statute text tells you what the law requires, but it will not tell you what your particular court will charge.

  • Fine: set by the court on the citation.
  • Fees: added per the local schedule.
  • National context: $50–$500 by state.

How many points does a failure to yield ticket add in Wyoming?

None in the usual sense. Wyoming has no demerit-point system, so there is no point value to look up for this ticket.

That does not mean a conviction has no effect. Suspensions follow repeat convictions and court action. A single ticket is one entry, but a pattern of convictions is what raises license risk.

If you are unsure how your record stands, ask the state's transportation department, which handles driver licensing, or the court that issued the citation. They can tell you what is on file.

No points does not mean no consequences. Repeat convictions and court action are what lead to suspensions in Wyoming.

Does a failure to yield ticket raise insurance in Wyoming?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums ~20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Wyoming-specific one.

Your own change depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the ticket is resolved. Some insurers look at convictions, and some look at crashes tied to the violation. We cannot predict what your company will do.

We do not recommend any insurer. If you want to know the effect on your policy, ask your insurer how it treats a moving-violation conviction before you decide how to respond to the citation.

  • The ~20% figure is an average, not a promise.
  • A conviction is what most often triggers a rate review.
  • Your renewal date and policy terms affect when you would see a change.

Can a failure to yield ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but there is no guarantee. Failure to yield cases often turn on disputed facts, such as who had the right of way and what each driver saw. That can make the case different from a simple speed reading.

Your usual options are to pay, to contest the charge, or to ask the court what it allows. Paying generally resolves the ticket but may mean a conviction. Contesting means presenting your side to the court.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice. If the stakes feel high, for example because of prior convictions or a crash, you may want to speak with a licensed attorney of your own choosing.

Before you pay, check what a conviction would mean for your record and your insurance. Paying is often treated as an admission.

What happens if I don't pay a failure to yield ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. The court can take further action, and in Wyoming suspensions follow repeat convictions and court action.

Unpaid fines can also mean extra court fees under the local schedule. Those costs are on top of the fine set by the court on the citation.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court clerk before the deadline on your citation. Ask what options the court offers. Courts differ, so get the answer from the court that issued your ticket.

  • Find the court name and contact details on the citation.
  • Note any deadline printed on it.
  • Keep a record of anything you pay or send.

What should I do after getting a failure to yield citation?

  1. Read the citation. Confirm the charge, the court and any date or instruction.
  2. Find the amount. The Wyoming court sets it on the citation, and court fees are added per the local schedule.
  3. Gather facts. Write down what happened, and save photos, names and any dash-camera footage while memory is fresh.
  4. Check your record. Repeat convictions are what lead to suspensions, so know your history.
  5. Think about insurance. A conviction can raise premiums, about 20% on average nationally.
  6. Decide how to respond. Pay, contest, or ask the court about options.

If you are unsure about any step, the court clerk can explain procedure. They cannot give legal advice, but they can tell you how the court handles these cases.
