How much is a failure to yield ticket in Missouri?

Nationally, failure to yield tickets run $50–$500 by state. In Missouri, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Missouri Department of Revenue — Driver License Bureau).

We do not publish a single Missouri dollar figure because none is locked in the sources we use. Quoting one would be a guess, and the court that handles your ticket decides the amount.

The national range is a comparison point only. Your own citation or the court clerk gives the number you actually owe.

Court costs are included in the scheduled payable amounts. That means the payable amount shown for your ticket is not a base fine with costs added on top of it.

How is the fine set for failure to yield?

Missouri sets traffic offenses in its state traffic code, and the court sets the amount within that code. You can read the Missouri statutes through Missouri statutes via Justia.

Several things can change what appears on your citation:

  • Which court has your case, since municipal and state courts can handle the same type of violation.
  • Whether the ticket was filed as a municipal or state charge.
  • Whether the court has a scheduled payable amount for the violation.
  • Whether the court sees extra circumstances, such as a crash.

Failure to yield covers situations like not giving the right of way at an intersection, a stop or yield sign, a left turn, or to pedestrians. The citation names the specific offense. Check it, because the wording can affect how the court treats the ticket.

Do not rely on a figure from a different county or from a general website, ours included. Confirm the payable amount with the court named on your citation.

How many points is a failure to yield in Missouri?

A municipal conviction carries 2 points. A state charge carries 3 points. Which one applies depends on how the ticket was filed.

Missouri's point system leads to suspension at 8 points in 18 months (Missouri Department of Revenue — Driver License Bureau). Points from other violations count toward that same total.

Points are added on conviction. Paying the ticket is generally treated as a guilty plea, so it can put points on your record.

If you already have points from earlier tickets, a failure to yield could bring you closer to the suspension threshold. You can check your own record through the Missouri Department of Revenue Driver License Bureau.

The 2-point and 3-point figures reflect the municipal and state distinction. Your citation and the court clerk can tell you which applies to your case.

Does a failure to yield ticket raise insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Missouri-specific one.

Your own change could be higher, lower or none at all. It depends on your insurer, your driving record, how your insurer treats the violation, and whether a crash was involved.

  • A conviction is what usually triggers a surcharge, not just receiving a ticket.
  • Insurers often check your record when your policy renews.
  • An at-fault crash tied to the ticket can matter more than the ticket itself.

We do not recommend insurers or tell you what to do about your policy. If you are concerned, ask your own insurer how it treats a failure to yield conviction.

Can a failure to yield ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome depends on the court, the facts and the prosecutor. We cannot predict it, and nothing here is legal advice.

People generally have a few options after a ticket:

  • Pay it. This usually ends the case, but it is generally treated as a conviction, and points follow.
  • Contest it. You can ask the court for a hearing and present your side.
  • Ask about a reduction. Some courts may amend a charge, which could change the fine or points.
  • Talk to a licensed attorney. A Missouri-licensed attorney can explain what applies to your situation.

The 2-point and 3-point difference makes the charge type worth understanding. Ask the court how your ticket was filed before you decide.

Read the instructions on the citation. They explain how to respond and which court to contact.

What happens if I don't pay a failure to yield ticket?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. Courts can add penalties, and unresolved tickets can lead to licensing problems. The specific consequences depend on the court that has your case.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court clerk before the deadline on your citation. Some courts offer other arrangements, though we cannot say what yours allows.

Remember that points also count toward the Missouri suspension threshold of 8 points in 18 months. An unpaid ticket does not avoid that risk, and it adds new ones.

For license questions, the Missouri Department of Revenue — Driver License Bureau is the official place to check.
