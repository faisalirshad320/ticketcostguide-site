How much is a failure to yield ticket in Maine?

Nationally, failure to yield tickets run $50–$500 by state. In Maine the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Maine Bureau of Motor Vehicles).

We do not list a single Maine dollar figure on this page. The national range is context only, and your own total depends on what the court and the citation say.

The number printed on your citation or court notice is the number that matters. A national range cannot tell you what you owe.

Because surcharges are built into the scheduled amounts, the figure you see should already include them. If a notice shows a different total from what you expected, ask the court clerk to explain how it was calculated.

What counts as failure to yield?

Failure to yield generally means you did not give another road user the right-of-way when the law required it. Common examples include:

  • Pulling out from a stop sign or yield sign in front of other traffic.
  • Turning left across oncoming traffic without waiting for a safe gap.
  • Not yielding to a pedestrian in a crosswalk.
  • Not yielding when merging or entering a roadway from a driveway.

The exact wording of the charge appears on your citation. Maine's traffic code defines each right-of-way rule, and you can browse the state's laws through Maine statutes via Justia.

The specific charge can affect how the court treats the case, so read the description on the ticket carefully before deciding what to do.

How is the fine set in Maine?

In Maine the court sets the exact fine for this violation within the state's traffic code. It is not a number we can look up for you, and it is not a flat figure that applies to every driver.

The scheduled amount includes statutory surcharges. That means the court costs and surcharges are part of the total rather than a separate bill added later.

To find your amount, check these places in order:

  1. The citation itself, which should show the amount or tell you how to find it.
  2. The court notice you receive after the citation is filed.
  3. The clerk of the court named on the ticket.

If the paperwork does not show a clear amount, call the clerk before you pay or send anything in.

How many points does a failure to yield ticket carry in Maine?

Under the state context we rely on, demerit points accrue per conviction, and 12 points in 12 months leads to a suspension. We do not state a point value for failure to yield, because we have no sourced figure for it.

To see how a conviction would affect your record, use the Maine Bureau of Motor Vehicles. It maintains driver records and licensing information (Maine Bureau of Motor Vehicles).

Points attach to a conviction, not to the ticket itself. How you resolve the citation can change what ends up on your driving record.

If you already have other violations on your record, the 12-points-in-12-months threshold matters more. Ask the BMV for a copy of your record so you know where you stand.

Does a failure to yield ticket raise insurance in Maine?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Maine-specific one.

Your own change depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the violation is recorded. A failure to yield that involved a crash may be treated differently from one that did not.

We do not recommend any insurer, and we cannot predict your renewal rate. If you are worried about a rate change, your insurance company or a licensed agent can explain how it rates moving violations.

The extra cost of higher premiums can add up over time and may exceed the ticket itself. Weigh that when you decide how to respond to the citation.

Can a failure to yield ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but we cannot promise an outcome. The usual options are to pay, to contest the ticket, or to ask the court whether other resolutions are available.

  • Pay: This normally resolves the ticket, but it is usually treated as a conviction, which can bring points and an insurance effect.
  • Contest: You can ask for a hearing and present your side. The officer's account and any evidence will matter.
  • Ask the clerk: Court staff can tell you which options exist for your citation and how to request them.

Paying quickly may feel simple, but it can have costs beyond the fine. For a decision about your specific case, you may want to speak with a licensed attorney. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice or recommend attorneys.

State traffic safety rules are summarized by the Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws, which can help with general background.

What happens if I don't pay a failure to yield ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts can add consequences when a ticket goes unanswered, and a license problem can follow.

We do not list a specific late fee or deadline, because we have no sourced figure for either. Your citation or court notice should state the date by which you must respond.

If you cannot pay in full, contact the court clerk before the deadline. Clerks can often explain whether payment options exist, but that depends on the court.

Missing the response date is the most avoidable mistake. Mark it on your calendar the day you receive the ticket.

For license status questions, the Maine Bureau of Motor Vehicles is the official source.
