How much is a failure to yield ticket in West Virginia?

Nationally, a failure to yield ticket runs $50–$500 by state. In West Virginia, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (West Virginia Division of Motor Vehicles).

We do not publish a single West Virginia dollar figure, because none of our sources sets one for every driver. The number printed on your ticket, or the amount the court clerk gives you, is the one that counts.

The $50–$500 range is a national spread across states. It shows how much states differ, not what West Virginia charges.

Your real cost is the fine plus court costs. In West Virginia, court costs often exceed the fine itself, so look at the full amount due.

How is the fine set for failure to yield?

West Virginia sets traffic offenses in its state traffic code. The court handling your case sets the exact fine within that code.

That is why two drivers cited for the same right-of-way violation can owe different amounts. The facts of the stop, the court and the charge written on the citation all matter.

Failure to yield covers several situations, such as not giving way at a stop or yield sign, when turning left, or to pedestrians and emergency vehicles. The charge on your citation tells you which one the officer cited.

You can read the code itself on Justia's West Virginia statutes. Match the section on your citation to the text there.

Why do court costs matter more than the fine?

Municipal and magistrate court costs vary and usually exceed the fine. The fine is only one line of what you may owe.

Costs differ by county and by court. A ticket from a city municipal court may total a different amount than one handled in a county magistrate court.

Before you pay or plan a budget, find out:

  • The fine amount set for your citation.
  • The court costs and any surcharges added on top.
  • The total due and the payment methods the court accepts.

Ask the clerk of the court named on your citation for the full total. We do not list a local court-cost figure because it is not in our sourced facts.

How many points is a failure to yield ticket in West Virginia?

West Virginia's points system assigns 2–5 points by mph over, and 12+ points = suspension. That description is written around speed.

Failure to yield is not a speed offense, so we cannot confirm a specific point value for it from our sourced facts. Check your citation and the West Virginia Division of Motor Vehicles to see whether points apply to your charge.

The point to remember is the threshold. Reaching 12+ points leads to a suspension, so points from any conviction on your record add up.

If you already have other moving violations, ask the DMV how a new conviction would affect your total before you decide how to respond.

Does a failure to yield ticket raise insurance?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums ~20% on average. That is a national figure, not a West Virginia-specific one.

Your own change depends on your insurer, your driving record and whether the violation led to a conviction. A failure to yield is often tied to a collision, and that can matter to an insurer as well.

We do not recommend any insurer. If you are concerned, ask your own insurer how a conviction would be treated at renewal.

Remember that the cost of higher premiums can add up over time and may exceed the fine. That is worth weighing when you decide how to handle the ticket.

Can a failure to yield ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but that is decided by the court and the facts of your case. We cannot predict an outcome or give legal advice.

Your citation lists the court and your options for responding. Generally, you can pay, or you can contest the charge and appear before the court.

Things people commonly look at when deciding:

  • Whether the charge on the citation matches what happened.
  • Whether a conviction would add points toward the 12+ point suspension threshold.
  • Whether a conviction could raise insurance, which is typically ~20% on average nationally.

If the stakes feel high, a licensed West Virginia attorney can advise you on your specific case. We do not recommend any particular attorney.

What happens if I don't pay a failure to yield ticket?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. The court that issued it can take further action, and the total you owe can grow.

Because West Virginia court costs vary and usually exceed the fine, an unpaid ticket can become a larger bill. The specific steps depend on the court handling your case.

A conviction or a failure to resolve the matter can also affect your driving record. With a points system that suspends at 12+ points, a record that keeps growing is a risk.

If you are unsure what to do, contact the clerk of the court on your citation before anything lapses.
