How much is a failure to yield ticket in Louisiana?

Nationally, a failure to yield ticket runs $50–$500 by state. In Louisiana the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Louisiana Office of Motor Vehicles). We do not list a single Louisiana dollar figure because none is fixed in advance for every case.

The national range is a guide to how much fines differ across the country. It is not a quote for a Louisiana ticket. Your citation, or the court that issued it, is the only place to see what you owe.

Look at the citation first. The court named on it sets the amount, and the total you pay may include more than the fine.

Failure to yield covers situations such as not giving the right of way at an intersection, to a pedestrian, or when merging. How the officer describes the conduct on the citation can affect how the court treats it.

How is the fine for failure to yield set in Louisiana?

The fine is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The officer does not choose the dollar amount. The officer writes the citation, and the court decides the amount when the case is resolved.

That is why two drivers cited for similar conduct can see different totals. The court, the parish, and the facts recorded on the citation all matter. If a crash or injury was involved, the citation may read differently from a simple right-of-way violation.

You can read the Louisiana traffic statutes through Louisiana statutes via Justia. The rules on right of way sit in the state's traffic code. Check the exact section printed on your citation, because the wording of the charge is what the court applies.

  • The citation lists the charge and the court.
  • The court sets the fine within the traffic code.
  • Court costs are added on top and vary by location.

Why can the total be higher than the fine?

Local court costs vary by parish and often exceed the fine. So the number printed as the fine is not always what you pay at the window or online.

This catches many drivers off guard. They see a fine amount, then find a larger total once costs are added. Ask the clerk of the court for the full amount due before you pay or plan a payment.

Before paying, confirm with the court whether the figure you were given includes court costs. A fine and a total due are not the same thing.

Because costs differ by parish, the same violation can cost a different total depending on where you were cited. We cannot give you a parish-level number here. The court that handles your case can.

How many points does a failure to yield ticket carry in Louisiana?

Louisiana has no point system. License action follows repeat convictions and specific offenses instead of a running point total.

That does not mean a conviction has no effect on your license. Repeated convictions can lead to action against your driving privileges, and certain offenses can too. A single failure to yield conviction is treated within that framework rather than by adding points.

For questions about your own driving record, the Louisiana Office of Motor Vehicles is the agency that keeps it. You can also compare how states treat points on the Governors Highway Safety Association state laws page.

  • No point values to count or track in Louisiana.
  • Repeat convictions matter for license action.
  • Some specific offenses can trigger action on their own.

Does a failure to yield ticket raise insurance in Louisiana?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Louisiana-specific one.

Your own change depends on your insurer, your driving history, and how the violation is recorded. We do not rate insurers or recommend one, and we cannot predict your premium. Some drivers see no change at renewal, and others see more.

A conviction is what usually matters to insurers. If a ticket is dismissed, or the outcome is not a conviction, the effect on your record may differ. Ask your insurer how it treats a violation like this before you decide how to handle the ticket.

The ~20% figure is a national average for moving-violation convictions. Treat it as a rough benchmark only.

Can a failure to yield ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome is up to the court. Louisiana courts set the amount, and the options available depend on the court, the charge, and your record. We cannot tell you which option fits your case.

Common paths drivers consider include:

  • Paying the ticket. This usually resolves the case but may count as a conviction.
  • Contesting it in court. You present your side, and the court decides.
  • Asking the court about alternatives. Some courts offer other ways to resolve a ticket. Ask the clerk what is available for your charge.

Before choosing, read the citation for deadlines and instructions. Since a conviction can affect insurance and, with repeats, your license, it is worth understanding the outcome before you pay.

What happens if I don't pay a failure to yield ticket?

Ignoring a ticket tends to make things worse. Courts expect a response by the date and method on the citation. Missing it can lead to added consequences, including action against your license.

We do not list specific late fees or deadlines here, because those depend on the court and are not part of the figures we publish. The citation and the court clerk are the right sources for them.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court before the due date. Courts often have processes for questions about payment. Because local court costs can exceed the fine, ask for the full total so you know what you are dealing with.

Do not wait. Contact the court named on your citation as soon as you know you cannot pay or respond on time.
