How much is a failure to yield ticket in Hawaii?

Nationally, failure to yield tickets run $50–$500 by state. In Hawaii, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Hawaii Department of Transportation).

This page does not give a single Hawaii dollar amount, because the figure on your citation is the one that applies to you. Any number you see elsewhere may be out of date or may describe a different state or a different violation.

The national range is a comparison point only. Your Hawaii citation and the court that handles it control what you actually owe.

On top of the fine, court fees and assessments are added by the district court. That means the amount you pay can be higher than the base fine printed on the ticket.

What counts as failure to yield?

Failure to yield generally means you did not give the right-of-way to another driver, a cyclist or a pedestrian when you were required to. Common situations include:

  • Turning left across oncoming traffic
  • Pulling out from a stop sign or driveway
  • Merging without leaving room
  • Not stopping for a pedestrian in a crosswalk
  • Not giving way to an emergency vehicle

The exact wording of the offense and the section of the Hawaii code cited are on your citation. Hawaii statutes are available through Justia if you want to read the text yourself.

If a crash was involved, the officer may have written the ticket with additional details. Those details can matter if you later contest it.

How is the fine set in Hawaii?

The exact fine for this violation in Hawaii is set by the court within the state's traffic code. It is not a fixed number published on a single statewide page that we can quote here.

That is why the citation is the best source. It identifies the violation, the court and the next step you must take.

The district court also adds fees and assessments. These are separate from the fine, so ask the court for the total due before you pay.

  • Fine: set by the court on the citation.
  • Fees and assessments: added by the district court.
  • Total: confirm with the court clerk or the court's payment instructions.

How many points does failure to yield carry in Hawaii?

None, because Hawaii has no demerit-point system. There is no point value to look up for this violation.

That does not mean a conviction is harmless. In Hawaii, repeat convictions and excessive-speeding convictions drive license action directly.

No points does not mean no consequences. A pattern of convictions can lead to license action even without a point total.

If you have other moving violations on your record, a new conviction could matter more than the single ticket suggests. The Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a reference page of state traffic laws for general comparison between states.

Does a failure to yield ticket raise insurance in Hawaii?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Hawaii-specific rate.

Whether your own premium changes depends on your insurer, your driving record and whether the ticket ends in a conviction. We do not recommend or compare insurers.

A failure to yield ticket tied to a crash may be treated differently from one issued with no collision. Your insurer or agent can tell you how it rates violations.

  • Ask your insurer how a conviction would be rated.
  • Ask the court how the case will be recorded if you resolve it.
  • Compare that effect with the cost of the fine and fees.

Can a failure to yield ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome depends on the facts and on the court. This page cannot predict it, and it is not legal advice.

The citation explains how to respond. Typically you can pay, which resolves the ticket, or contest it and have the court hear your case.

Before you decide, consider the full picture:

  • The fine set by the court on the citation
  • Court fees and assessments added by the district court
  • The possible insurance effect of a conviction (about 20% on average nationally)
  • Your record, since repeat convictions drive license action in Hawaii

If the facts are disputed or the stakes are high, you may want to speak with a licensed attorney. We do not recommend a specific one.

What happens if I don't pay?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. The court can add consequences when a ticket goes unanswered, and those can include further costs.

This page does not state specific deadlines or penalty amounts, since none are among our verified figures. Your citation lists the response instructions that apply to you.

If you are unsure what the citation requires, contact the district court named on it before the response date passes.

If you cannot afford the amount, ask the court whether it offers any payment or other options. Courts set their own procedures, so ask rather than assume.

Who sets the failure to yield fine in Hawaii?

Yes. Court fees and assessments are added by the district court. We do not list a Hawaii dollar amount for them, so ask the court clerk for the total amount due on your citation before you pay.

Can I lose my license for failure to yield in Hawaii?

Hawaii has no points system. Repeat convictions and excessive-speeding convictions drive license action directly. A single failure to yield ticket is not described that way, but repeated convictions can lead to action on your license.
