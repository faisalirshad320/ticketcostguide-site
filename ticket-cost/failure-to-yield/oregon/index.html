How much is a failure to yield ticket in Oregon?

Nationally, failure to yield tickets run $50–$500 by state; in Oregon the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Oregon DMV (ODOT)). That national range is a comparison point, not a prediction for your ticket.

This guide does not give a single Oregon dollar amount, because the number printed on your citation is what counts. If the citation lists an amount, that is the figure to plan around.

Quick take: the $50–$500 range is national. Your Oregon amount is the one set by the court on your citation.

Failure to yield covers several situations, such as not yielding at an intersection, to a pedestrian, or to an emergency vehicle. The violation named on your citation can affect how the court treats it, so read that line closely.

How is the failure to yield fine set in Oregon?

The court sets the fine for this violation within the state's traffic code (Oregon statutes via Justia). That is why two drivers cited for similar conduct can see different amounts.

Some enhancements are written directly into statute instead of being left to the court. Zone doubling and the 100+ mph enhancements are two examples. They are tied to speeding conduct, so do not assume they apply to a yield violation. Check your citation to see what was charged.

What to look for on your citation:

  • The exact violation name and statute listed
  • Any dollar amount printed on it
  • The court that will handle the case
  • The date by which you must respond

If anything is unclear, the court clerk can explain how to read the citation. Clerks cannot give legal advice, but they can tell you what the court's process is.

How many points is a failure to yield ticket in Oregon?

None. Oregon has no point system (Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws). You will not see a point value attached to this violation.

That does not mean a conviction is harmless for your license. Suspensions in Oregon follow repeat convictions, for example under habitual-offender rules, and specific offenses such as driving 100+ mph.

No points does not mean no record. A conviction can still count toward repeat-offense rules.

If you already have other convictions, ask the Oregon DMV how your record is treated. The agency publishes driver record information (Oregon DMV (ODOT)).

Does a failure to yield ticket raise insurance in Oregon?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Oregon-specific or insurer-specific one.

Your own change could be higher, lower, or nothing at all. It depends on your insurer, your driving history and whether the ticket ends in a conviction. We do not recommend any insurer and cannot predict your rate.

Practical steps:

  • Ask your insurer how it treats a conviction before you decide how to respond to the ticket
  • Compare the possible premium change with the cost of the fine itself
  • Remember that a conviction, not just a citation, is what usually drives a rate change

For many drivers, the insurance effect over several years can matter more than the one-time fine. That is worth weighing before you pay without thinking it through.

Can a failure to yield ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome is up to the court. Because the court sets the Oregon amount on the citation, a reduced charge or a different result would be decided in that same process.

Common options drivers consider:

  • Paying the ticket, which usually resolves the case but may mean a conviction
  • Contesting the ticket and asking the court to hear the case
  • Asking the court clerk whether any other resolution is available for your citation

Which route fits depends on the facts. Failure to yield often turns on who had the right of way, so the details of the incident matter.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice or recommend attorneys. If the stakes feel high, such as a prior record or a crash, you may want to talk to a licensed Oregon attorney of your own choosing.

What happens if I don't pay a failure to yield ticket in Oregon?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts generally treat a missed response or payment as a separate problem, and the DMV may become involved.

Because Oregon suspensions follow repeat convictions and specific offenses, an unresolved ticket can also leave your record in an uncertain state. The consequences for a given case are set by the court and the statutes, not by this guide.

Do not wait. Contact the court listed on your citation before the response date if you have questions about paying, contesting or timing.

If you cannot pay the full amount at once, ask the court clerk what payment options exist. Do not assume a plan is available until the court confirms it.
