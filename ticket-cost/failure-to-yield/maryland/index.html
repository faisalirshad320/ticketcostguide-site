How much is a failure to yield ticket in Maryland?

Nationally, a failure to yield ticket costs $50–$500 by state. In Maryland, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Maryland Motor Vehicle Administration).

We do not list a single Maryland dollar figure here, because the amount depends on what is written on your citation and which court handles it. Anyone quoting an exact statewide price without pointing to a schedule or your citation is guessing.

The $50–$500 range is a national picture. It shows how widely states differ, and it should not be read as the Maryland price.

Your citation is the best source for your own cost. Look for the amount listed and any instructions on how to pay or how to request a court date.

If the citation lists a prepay amount, that is what the state is asking for if you pay without going before a judge. If you ask for a court date, a different process applies, covered below.

How is the fine set for failure to yield?

In Maryland, the exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. You can browse the code through Maryland statutes via Justia.

Two paths matter most:

  • Paying the citation as written. You accept the amount shown and the outcome that comes with it.
  • Requesting a court date. The prepay amount is replaced by a judge-set fine of up to $500.

That second point is worth thinking about before you decide. Going to court does not guarantee a lower number. The judge sets the fine, and it can be up to $500.

The circumstances of the stop can also matter. A failure to yield that involved a crash or injury is a different situation from a failure to yield with no collision, and the citation will show what you were charged with. We do not give legal advice, so if the details are complicated, consider speaking with a licensed attorney of your own choosing.

How many points is a failure to yield ticket in Maryland?

Maryland's points system, as we have it from our sources, runs in tiers: 1 point (9 or less over), 2 points (10–29 over) and 5 points (30+ over). Eight points leads to suspension, and 12 points leads to revocation.

Those tiers are written in terms of miles "over," which reads like a speed-based scale. We cannot tell you from these figures exactly how many points apply to a failure to yield conviction, and we will not guess.

To find the point value for your specific charge, check the citation and ask the Maryland Motor Vehicle Administration at mva.maryland.gov.

The thresholds still matter for planning. If you already have points on your record, even a small addition moves you closer to the 8-point suspension line or the 12-point revocation line.

Ask the MVA for a copy of your driving record if you are unsure where you stand.

Does a failure to yield ticket raise insurance?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Maryland-specific one.

Your own change could be higher, lower or nothing, depending on your insurer, your record and how the violation is classified. We do not recommend any insurer and cannot predict your rate.

A few points to keep in mind:

  • The fine is a one-time cost. A premium increase can last as long as the insurer counts the conviction.
  • Whether the ticket ends in a conviction can matter more to your rates than the fine itself.
  • If the failure to yield was tied to a crash, the insurance question may also involve a claim, which is separate from the ticket.

If you want an exact answer, ask your insurer how a moving-violation conviction would be treated under your policy. Ask before deciding how to respond to the citation, not after.

Can a failure to yield ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes outcomes change in court, but nothing is guaranteed. We cannot say how likely a dismissal or reduction is in any Maryland court.

Your general options are:

  1. Pay the citation. This is quick, but paying generally means accepting the charge.
  2. Request a court date. A judge sets the fine, which can be up to $500, and you can present your side.
  3. Get advice. A licensed attorney of your choosing can explain how your facts fit the law. We do not recommend a specific attorney.

Before choosing, weigh the full picture: the fine, any points, the 8-point and 12-point thresholds, and the possible insurance effect of about 20% nationally.

Requesting a court date replaces the prepay amount, so you should not expect the number on the citation to stay the same.

The citation itself should say how to respond and where. Follow those instructions exactly, and contact the court clerk if anything is unclear.

What happens if I don't pay a failure to yield ticket?

Ignoring a citation is usually the worst choice. We do not list specific Maryland late fees or deadlines here, because they are not in our locked reference figures, so check your citation and the court for exact consequences.

In general, an unresolved ticket can lead to additional penalties, and it can also affect your driving privileges. Maryland's point thresholds, with suspension at 8 points and revocation at 12 points, show how seriously the state treats a poor record.

If you cannot pay the full amount or cannot make the date, contact the court listed on the citation before the date passes. Clerks can explain what choices exist for your case.

For license questions, the Maryland Motor Vehicle Administration is the agency to contact: mva.maryland.gov.
