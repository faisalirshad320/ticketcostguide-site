How Much Does a Failure to Yield Ticket Cost in Massachusetts?

By Editorial Team, TicketCostGuide · Last reviewed 2026-10-16

Nationally, a failure to yield ticket runs $50–$500 by state (Massachusetts Registry of Motor Vehicles). In Massachusetts, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation, so it depends on your court and citation. Surcharge points and higher insurance can add to the cost.

How much is a failure to yield ticket in Massachusetts?

Our lead figure is this: Nationally $50–$500 by state; in Massachusetts the exact amount is set by the court on the citation — Failure to Yield Ticket cost in Massachusetts (Massachusetts Registry of Motor Vehicles).

The $50–$500 range is a national figure. It shows how widely states differ, and it is not a Massachusetts fine schedule.

We do not publish an exact Massachusetts dollar amount. The number that matters for you is the one printed on your citation.

Your total cost can be more than the fine alone. Surcharges, surchargeable-event points and a possible insurance increase can all add to it.

How is the failure to yield fine set in Massachusetts?

In Massachusetts, the fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The amount appears on the citation you receive.

That means two drivers cited for similar conduct may not see the same number. The court and the specific citation drive the amount.

You can read the state's traffic code through Massachusetts statutes via Justia. We cite the code in general terms only, because we do not state a section-level dollar figure.

If you want to compare, ask the court that issued your citation how the amount on it was set and what it includes.

Are there extra surcharges or court costs?

Yes, some can apply. Our locked state facts say a $50 head-injury surcharge applies to every speeding conviction in Massachusetts.

That fact is stated for speeding. We have not confirmed how it applies to a failure to yield conviction, so check your citation and the court's instructions.

  • Read every line item on the citation or court notice.
  • Ask the court whether any surcharge is added to the fine shown.
  • Keep a copy of what you paid and the receipt.

Do not assume the fine on the citation is the final total until the court confirms it.

How many points does a failure to yield ticket carry in Massachusetts?

Massachusetts uses insurance surcharge points (SDIP) rather than license suspension points for ordinary speeding. The same system is the one to look at for moving violations in general.

We do not state a point value for failure to yield, because we do not have a locked figure for it. Check your citation and the Registry of Motor Vehicles.

The thresholds that matter are these:

  • 3 surchargeable events in 24 months require a driver retraining course.
  • 7 surchargeable events in 3 years means suspension.

Source: Massachusetts Registry of Motor Vehicles. These thresholds count surchargeable events over time, so older tickets can matter.

Does a failure to yield ticket raise insurance in Massachusetts?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Massachusetts rate.

Massachusetts uses SDIP surcharge points, which connect convictions to insurance costs. Your own increase depends on your insurer, your record and the outcome of the ticket.

We do not recommend any insurer. If you are worried about your premium, ask your insurer how a conviction would affect your policy before you decide how to respond.

A fine is paid once. An insurance increase can last, so it may matter more than the fine itself.

Can a failure to yield ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Possibly, but the outcome is up to the court. We cannot give legal advice or say what will work in your case.

In general, drivers who receive a citation can choose to pay it or to contest it. Your citation explains how to respond and where.

  • Pay it: this usually resolves the ticket, but a conviction can bring surcharge points and insurance effects.
  • Contest it: you ask the court to review the citation, and the outcome is the court's decision.
  • Ask questions first: the court clerk can explain the process and deadlines on your notice.

Because paying may count as a conviction, weigh the points and insurance effects before you pay. If you want legal advice, speak with a licensed attorney of your own choosing.

What happens if I don't pay a failure to yield ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts and the Registry of Motor Vehicles can add consequences when a citation goes unanswered.

We do not state specific late fees or deadlines, because we have no locked figures for them. Your citation and the court's notices list the dates and the steps you must follow.

If you cannot pay, contact the court before the deadline and ask what options exist. Doing so early is better than waiting for a notice.

An unresolved ticket can also complicate your driving record, which is the record insurers and the Registry look at.
