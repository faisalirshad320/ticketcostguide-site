How much is a failure to yield ticket in Wisconsin?

Nationally, failure to yield tickets cost $50–$500 depending on the state. In Wisconsin the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Wisconsin Department of Transportation). We do not list a single Wisconsin dollar figure, because the court determines it.

The national range is a guide to scale, not a quote. Your total will also include the statutory surcharge stack, which is added to every forfeiture.

The number on your citation or court notice is the number that matters. Anything else, including this page, is context.

Failure to yield covers several situations, such as not giving right of way at an intersection, to a pedestrian, or when merging. The facts of the stop can affect how the citation is written, so read it closely.

How is the fine set, and what gets added to it?

In Wisconsin the amount is set by the court within the state's traffic code. It is not a single flat number that applies to every driver and every situation.

Wisconsin generally refers to the amount you pay for a civil traffic violation as a forfeiture. The statutory surcharge stack is added to every forfeiture. That means the amount you pay is higher than the base penalty alone.

  • Base amount: set by the court on the citation.
  • Surcharges and court costs: the statutory stack, added to every forfeiture.
  • Possible extras: circumstances such as a crash or a violation in a protected area can change how a citation is handled, so check the paperwork.

Wisconsin traffic statutes are available through Wisconsin statutes via Justia. They are written in legal language, so use them as a reference, not as a calculator for your own total.

How many points does a failure to yield ticket carry in Wisconsin?

Wisconsin's points system, as locked in our sources, runs in tiers: 3 points (10 or less over), 4 points (11–19 over), 6 points (20+ over). A driver who reaches 12 points in 12 months faces suspension.

Those tiers are written in terms of how far over a limit a driver was. They read most naturally for speeding. A yield violation is not measured that way, so we cannot tell you which tier your citation falls into.

To find out how many points your specific citation adds, ask the court clerk or check with the Wisconsin DOT. Do not assume the number from the tiers above.

The suspension threshold matters most if you already have points on your record. Adding even a modest total to existing points can bring you closer to 12 points in 12 months. Your driving record is available through Wisconsin Department of Transportation.

Does a failure to yield ticket raise insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Wisconsin-specific one.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your driving history, and whether the ticket is a conviction on your record. Some insurers weigh a failure to yield more heavily if it involved a crash.

  • The national average is a rough guide. Your quote could be higher, lower, or unchanged.
  • The increase often shows up at renewal, not right away.
  • Comparing the added premium over time to the ticket amount shows the real cost.

We do not recommend any insurer. Your insurance company or a licensed agent can tell you how a conviction would affect your policy.

Can a failure to yield ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes. Whether it can in your case depends on the facts, the court and the prosecutor. We cannot say what is likely for you, and this page is not legal advice.

Options generally open to a driver include:

  • Paying the citation. This usually resolves it quickly, but it can mean a conviction and any points that come with it.
  • Contesting it. You can ask for a court date and present your side of what happened.
  • Asking the court about alternatives. Some courts discuss other resolutions. Ask the clerk what your court offers.

Read the citation for the instructions and deadlines it lists. Because the court sets the amount on the citation, a different outcome would also change what you owe.

If the stakes are high, such as a crash, an injury, or a record that is already close to 12 points in 12 months, consider speaking with a licensed attorney of your choice.

What happens if I don't pay a failure to yield ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts typically add consequences for missed deadlines or unpaid forfeitures, and these can include a license problem.

The citation or court notice tells you what to do and by when. If you cannot meet the deadline, contact the court clerk before it passes and ask what is available.

  • Check the due date and the court named on your paperwork.
  • Keep proof of any payment or court contact.
  • Do not rely on a third-party website for your exact balance.

Because the statutory surcharge stack is added to every forfeiture, the amount owed should be confirmed with the court directly. Ask for the full total, not only the base amount.
