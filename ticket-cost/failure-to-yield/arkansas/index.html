How much is a failure to yield ticket in Arkansas?

Nationally, a failure to yield ticket costs $50–$500 by state. In Arkansas, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Arkansas Department of Finance and Administration — Driver Services).

We do not list a single Arkansas dollar figure here because none is fixed in advance. The number printed on your ticket, or the amount the court clerk gives you, is the one that matters.

The national range is a reference point only. It is not an Arkansas fine schedule, and your Arkansas total could differ from it once court costs are added.

Your citation, or the court that issued it, is the only reliable source for your exact amount.

How is the fine set, and what are court costs?

The fine for failure to yield in Arkansas is set by the court within the state's traffic code. Arkansas statutes are available through Justia if you want to read the traffic provisions yourself.

Court costs are a separate layer. They are added by the district court and vary by county. Two drivers cited for the same behavior in different counties can therefore owe different totals.

When you look at what you owe, separate these items:

  • The fine itself, set by the court on the citation.
  • Court costs added by the district court, which vary by county.
  • Any other charge the clerk lists on your paperwork.

Ask the clerk for an itemized total if your citation shows only one combined number.

What does failure to yield mean on a ticket?

Failure to yield generally means you did not give the right of way when the law required it. Common settings include intersections, left turns, merging, stop and yield signs, and crosswalks.

The exact offense wording and code section are printed on your citation. That wording matters because it tells the court, and later your driving record, what you were cited for.

Right-of-way rules differ in their details from state to state. For a general overview of state traffic laws, see the Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws page.

How many points does a failure to yield add in Arkansas?

Arkansas's points system runs 3–8 points by speed. Reaching 14+ points can trigger suspension.

That locked figure is described by speed, so it does not tell us a specific point value for a failure to yield conviction. We do not guess one. The value that applies to your offense is on your driving record, which you can confirm with Arkansas Department of Finance and Administration — Driver Services.

Points matter because they add up. A single ticket may not threaten your license, but it counts toward the 14+ point level where suspension can be triggered.

Points are tied to a conviction. How your case is resolved can affect what ends up on your record.

Does a failure to yield ticket raise insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Arkansas-specific one.

Your own change could be higher, lower, or nothing at all. It depends on your insurer, your driving history, and whether the violation is reported as a conviction.

We do not recommend or compare insurers. If you want to know how a ticket would affect your policy, ask your insurer or agent directly.

Keep in mind that the fine is a one-time cost. A premium increase can last as long as the violation counts against you, so the total cost can exceed the fine.

Can a failure to yield ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome depends on the facts and on the court. We cannot predict results, and this page is not legal advice.

In general, people who receive a citation can usually do one of these things:

  • Pay the amount and accept the conviction.
  • Contest the ticket and ask the court to hear the case.
  • Ask the court clerk what options exist, such as reduced charges or a different resolution.

Which options exist depends on the district court handling your case and on your record. Ask the clerk what is available, and read the instructions on your citation carefully.

If the stakes feel high, for example because you are close to the 14+ point suspension level, you may want to consult a licensed attorney. We do not recommend a specific attorney.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts generally treat an unaddressed ticket as a failure to resolve the case, and further penalties can follow.

Arkansas also uses a points system under which 14+ points can trigger suspension, so unresolved matters can eventually affect your license. Check the exact consequences for your situation with the issuing court or Arkansas Department of Finance and Administration — Driver Services.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the district court clerk before the deadline on your citation. Do not wait until after it passes.
