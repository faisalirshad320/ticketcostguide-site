How much is a failure to yield ticket in Colorado?

The honest short version: the exact Colorado amount is set by the court on your citation. Nationally, failure to yield fines run $50–$500 by state (Colorado Division of Motor Vehicles). That range is a national spread, not a Colorado price tag.

We do not publish an exact Colorado dollar figure for this violation because the amount depends on the court and the facts written on your ticket. The number printed on your citation, or on the court's notice, is the number that matters.

Two things can push the total above the base fine. A statutory surcharge is added to each penalty, and doubled fines apply in work zones.

Look at your citation first. It shows the charge, the court, and what you are being asked to pay or do next.

How is the fine set for failure to yield?

In Colorado, the fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The code gives the framework, and the court applies it to your citation. You can read the Colorado statutes through Justia.

Several things can affect what appears on your ticket:

  • The charge itself. Failure to yield covers a range of situations, such as yielding to other vehicles at intersections or to pedestrians. The charge written on the citation drives the penalty.
  • Where it happened. A work zone can double the fine.
  • The surcharge. A statutory surcharge is added to each penalty, so the amount you pay is higher than the base fine alone.
  • Whether anyone was hurt. A collision or injury can change how the matter is charged. Read the citation carefully, and consider asking a licensed attorney if the situation is serious.

Because the court sets the amount, two drivers cited for similar conduct can see different totals.

Are there surcharges or doubled fines?

Yes. Our locked state data lists two add-ons: a statutory surcharge added to each penalty, and doubled fines in work zones.

The surcharge means the court total is not just the base fine. When you read your citation or the court's payment notice, check whether the figure shown already includes it.

If your violation happened in a work zone, expect the doubled-fine rule to come into play. Check whether the work zone is noted on the ticket, since that affects the total.

We do not state dollar amounts for the surcharge, because the locked sources do not give one. Ask the court clerk for an itemized total if your notice does not show one.

How many points is a failure to yield in Colorado?

Colorado uses a points system. The state context we rely on describes it as 1–6 points by mph over, with 12 points in 12 months (adult) = suspension.

That scale is described by speed. Failure to yield is not a speeding violation, so we do not assign a point value to it. Do not assume a number from this page.

To find out how many points, if any, your violation carries, check your citation, the court's notice, and the Colorado Division of Motor Vehicles. Points are generally tied to a conviction, so the outcome of your case matters.

The suspension threshold for adults is 12 points in 12 months. Points from other tickets in the same period count toward that total.

Does a failure to yield ticket raise insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Colorado-specific one.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your driving record, and whether the ticket ends in a conviction. We do not recommend any insurer, and we cannot predict what yours will do.

A few practical points:

  • The effect usually follows a conviction, not just the citation.
  • Paying the ticket is generally treated as admitting the violation, so think about that before you pay.
  • Your insurer or agent can tell you how they treat moving violations.

Can a failure to yield ticket be dismissed or reduced?

It may be possible, but outcomes depend on the court and the facts. We cannot tell you whether yours qualifies.

Common options drivers look at include:

  • Paying the ticket. This usually ends the case quickly, but it generally counts as a conviction, with the points and insurance effects described above.
  • Contesting the charge. You can ask the court to hear the case. The court decides based on the evidence.
  • Asking about a reduced charge or alternatives. Some courts discuss other resolutions. Ask the court clerk what your court offers.
  • Talking to a licensed attorney. An attorney can review your situation. We do not recommend a specific one, and this page is not legal advice.

Whatever you choose, follow the instructions on your citation about how and where to respond.

What happens if I don't pay a failure to yield ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts can add consequences when a ticket goes unanswered, and the license record can be affected.

We do not list specific deadlines or penalty amounts here, since the locked sources do not give them. Your citation states your response requirements, and the court can confirm them.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court clerk before the date on your notice. Ask what options exist rather than waiting.

You can also check your driving record status with the Colorado Division of Motor Vehicles.
