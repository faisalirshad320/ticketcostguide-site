How much is a failure to yield ticket in Rhode Island?

Nationally $50–$500 by state; in Rhode Island the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Rhode Island Division of Motor Vehicles). That national range is a spread across states. It is not a Rhode Island price list.

We do not publish an exact Rhode Island dollar figure for this violation. The amount for your ticket is the one the court sets, and it is the number to rely on.

Look at your citation first. The fine, any added fees and the instructions for responding are tied to your specific ticket.

The total you owe can be higher than the base fine because court technology and assessment fees are added. The sections below explain how those pieces fit together.

How is the fine set, and what gets added?

The fine for failure to yield in Rhode Island is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The statutes are published online (Rhode Island statutes via Justia). Which provision applies depends on the facts of the stop, such as a stop sign, a yield sign, a left turn or a pedestrian.

On top of the scheduled fine, court technology and assessment fees are added. These are separate line items, not part of the fine itself.

  • Base fine: set by the court, shown on the citation.
  • Court technology and assessment fees: added to the scheduled fine.
  • Total due: the fine plus those additions.

Because the fees are added, comparing only the base fine across tickets or states can understate what a driver actually pays. Ask the court clerk for the full total if the citation does not show it.

Why do you show a national range if Rhode Island sets its own amount?

The national range of $50–$500 by state gives a sense of scale. Some states sit near the low end and some near the high end, and the exact figure depends on state law and the court.

It should not be read as a prediction for Rhode Island. We have no sourced Rhode Island dollar amount to put in its place, so we say plainly that the court sets it.

The range also does not account for aggravating facts. A failure to yield that is tied to a crash or an injury can be handled differently from a simple citation. The charge on your paperwork matters more than any average.

How many points does a failure to yield add in Rhode Island?

Rhode Island has no running point system for ordinary speeding. Instead, the Traffic Tribunal escalates sanctions, including suspension, for repeat offenses.

That description comes from our state context for speeding. We do not state a point value for failure to yield, because we have no sourced figure for one.

The practical takeaway is that a record of repeated violations is what drives harsher outcomes. A single ticket and a pattern of tickets are treated differently.

To see how a conviction would be recorded on your own driving record, check with the Rhode Island Division of Motor Vehicles.

Does a failure to yield ticket raise insurance?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Rhode Island one.

Your own change could be higher, lower or none at all. It depends on your insurer, your history and whether the ticket ends in a conviction.

We do not recommend insurers and cannot predict your rate. If you are weighing how to respond to a ticket, it is reasonable to ask your insurer how it treats moving violations in general.

  • A conviction is what typically drives the increase.
  • A violation involving a crash may be weighed differently by insurers.
  • The 20% figure is an average across the country.

Can a failure to yield ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Your citation sets out how to respond. Generally, drivers can pay, or contest the ticket and ask for a hearing. The Traffic Tribunal handles traffic matters in Rhode Island.

Whether a ticket is dismissed or reduced depends on the evidence and the court. We cannot say how likely that is, and we do not give legal advice.

  • Paying: usually resolves the ticket but may count as a conviction.
  • Contesting: lets you present your side at a hearing.
  • Getting advice: if the stakes are high, such as a crash or a repeat violation, you may want to talk to a licensed attorney of your choosing.

Because repeat offenses bring escalating sanctions, how a ticket is resolved can matter for later violations.

What happens if I don't pay a failure to yield ticket?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. The court can treat an unanswered citation as a failure to respond, and the consequences are set by the court and the state.

Rhode Island escalates sanctions, including suspension, for repeat offenses. An unresolved ticket can add to your record rather than disappear.

We have no sourced late fee amount or deadline, so we do not state one. Your citation or the court clerk can tell you the deadline and what happens if it is missed.

If you cannot pay the full total, contact the court before the deadline passes and ask what options exist.
