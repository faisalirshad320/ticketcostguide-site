How much is a failure to yield ticket in Georgia?

Nationally, a failure to yield ticket runs $50–$500 by state. In Georgia, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Georgia Department of Driver Services).

That means there is no single Georgia price we can quote. The national range is a rough frame, not a Georgia schedule.

Your own number depends on the court that hears the case. Counties and courts can differ, so the amount on your paperwork or the court's own notice is the one that counts.

Do not rely on a figure from a forum or a friend. Use the amount printed on your citation or confirmed by the court clerk.

The fine is only part of the cost. Points, possible state fees and insurance changes can add to the total over time.

How is the failure to yield fine set in Georgia?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The code gives the court a framework, and the court decides the amount for your case.

The Georgia traffic code is published through sources such as Georgia statutes via Justia. It is the place to read the exact wording of the right-of-way rules.

Things that can affect what you see on the citation:

  • The court that has jurisdiction where the ticket was written.
  • The specific charge listed on the citation.
  • Whether any other violations were written at the same stop.
  • Whether a crash was involved, which can change how a case is handled.

If your citation lists no amount, call the court clerk listed on it. The clerk can tell you what is owed, but cannot give legal advice.

How many points is a failure to yield in Georgia?

Georgia uses a points system that runs 2–6 points depending on how far over the limit a driver was: 2 points for 15–18 over, 3 points for 19–23 over, 4 points for 24–33 over, and 6 points for 34 or more over.

That scale is written around speed. A failure to yield citation does not involve miles per hour over the limit, so we cannot tell you the point value for it from the facts we have. We will not guess one.

The Georgia Department of Driver Services is the right place to confirm how a specific conviction is scored (Georgia DDS).

The threshold that matters most is this: 15 points in 24 months leads to a license suspension. Points from several tickets add up inside that window, so an earlier violation can make a new one more serious.

Is there a $200 Super Speeder fee on a failure to yield ticket?

Georgia adds a $200 Super Speeder state fee on top of the court fine in cases where it applies. Failure to pay it within 120 days suspends the license.

The name points to speeding offenses. It is not a charge we would expect on a plain failure to yield citation, but we cannot confirm every case from our sources.

Check your citation and any notice from the court or DDS. If a $200 fee shows up, find out why it was applied and when it is due.

If a state fee does apply, the 120-day deadline matters. A missed payment can cost you your license even if the court fine is already paid.

Does a failure to yield ticket raise insurance in Georgia?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Georgia-specific one.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your driving record and whether the ticket ends in a conviction. We do not recommend insurers and cannot predict your rate.

A few points worth knowing:

  • The increase usually shows up at renewal, not right away.
  • A crash tied to the ticket may matter more to an insurer than the ticket alone.
  • Your insurer can tell you how it treats a conviction, but may not answer hypotheticals.

Because the premium effect can outlast the fine, it is worth weighing when you decide how to respond to the ticket.

Can a failure to yield ticket be dismissed or reduced in Georgia?

Possibly, but outcomes depend on the facts and on the court. We cannot say how likely it is in your case, and this page is not legal advice.

In general, a driver with a citation can:

  • Pay the fine. This usually resolves the ticket quickly, but paying generally means accepting the conviction and any points that come with it.
  • Contest the ticket. You can ask for a hearing and present your account of what happened.
  • Ask the court what alternatives exist. Some courts offer other ways to resolve a ticket, and others do not. The clerk can tell you what is available.

If a crash, injury or an existing points total is involved, you may want to speak with a licensed attorney. We do not recommend any specific one.

Before paying, think about points and insurance as well as the fine. A lower fine does not always mean a lower total cost.

What happens if I don't pay a failure to yield ticket in Georgia?

Ignoring a citation can make things worse. The specific penalties for an unpaid court fine are set by the court and the state, and we do not list figures for them.

What our sources do tell us is that the $200 Super Speeder state fee, where it applies, must be paid within 120 days or the license is suspended.

More broadly, a license can be suspended once a driver reaches 15 points in 24 months. Skipping a court date or deadline can lead to further action from the court.

If you cannot pay on time, contact the court before the deadline. Clerks can explain what the court accepts and what the next step is.
