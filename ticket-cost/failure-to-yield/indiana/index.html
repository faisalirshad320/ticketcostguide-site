How much is a failure to yield ticket in Indiana?

The lead figure is this: nationally $50–$500 by state; in Indiana the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Indiana Bureau of Motor Vehicles). The national range is a spread across all states. It is not an Indiana price list.

We do not print an exact Indiana dollar amount. The fine is set by the court within the state's traffic code, and the number on your citation or court notice is the one that applies to you.

When you look at your paperwork, check three things:

  • The fine itself.
  • County court costs added to the judgment.
  • Whether the ticket is a payable-by-mail item or requires a court appearance.

The total you owe is the fine plus those costs, so the fine alone understates the bill.

How is the fine for failure to yield set in Indiana?

In Indiana, the court sets the amount within the state's traffic code. The code is available through Indiana statutes via Justia, though the text does not tell you what your particular judgment will be.

That means two drivers cited for the same type of violation can see different totals. The court, the county and the circumstances on the citation all play a part.

The citation is the best source for your number. If it lists a court date instead of a set amount, the court will determine the figure there.

Do not rely on a fine figure from a forum post or another driver's ticket. Use the amount on your own citation or the court's notice.

Why can the total be higher than the fine?

County court costs are added to the judgment and often exceed the fine for low speeds. The same pattern can make the cost line larger than the fine line on other minor moving violations.

These costs vary by county and court. We do not publish a statewide court-cost figure because our sourced data does not give one.

Ways to find your real total:

  • Read the judgment or payment notice from the clerk's office.
  • Ask the clerk of the court handling your case what the total due is.
  • Check whether any payment-plan or deadline rules apply to you.

Ask for the full amount, not just the fine, before you decide how to handle the ticket.

How many points does failure to yield add in Indiana?

Indiana's points system is described as 2–8 points by mph over, and accumulating points triggers BMV probation and suspension. That wording describes a scale tied to speed.

Failure to yield is not a speed offense, so we do not assign a specific point number to it. Our sourced data does not give one, and we will not guess.

To find out how a conviction would be recorded, check your driving record through the Indiana Bureau of Motor Vehicles. The record shows what has been posted and your standing.

The practical risk is accumulation. Points that stack up can lead to BMV probation and then suspension, so one ticket matters more if your record already has others.

Does a failure to yield ticket raise insurance in Indiana?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums ~20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Indiana-specific one.

Your own change depends on your insurer, your history and how the violation is recorded. We do not recommend any insurer, and we cannot tell you what yours will do.

Things that commonly matter:

  • Whether the ticket ends in a conviction.
  • Your prior violations and claims.
  • When your policy next renews.

If the ticket is resolved without a conviction, the record may look different at renewal. Ask your insurer how it treats violations on your policy.

Can a failure to yield ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but we cannot predict it. Outcomes are decided by the court and depend on the facts of the stop and the local rules.

Generally, a driver has these options:

  • Pay the ticket, which usually resolves it but may mean a conviction on the record.
  • Contest it in court and present your side.
  • Ask the court or clerk what resolution options exist in your county.

Because the fine and court costs vary by court, it helps to know the full total before choosing. A lower fine is not always a lower overall cost once county costs are added.

What happens if I don't pay a failure to yield ticket?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. Courts can add consequences when a citation goes unresolved, and the BMV can act on driving records.

Our sourced data does not give exact deadlines or late-fee amounts for Indiana, so we do not list any. Your citation or the court's notice should state the due date and how to respond.

If you cannot pay or cannot make the date, contact the court clerk before the deadline passes. Asking early is usually better than waiting.

Check the deadline on the citation the day you receive it. Missing a court date is a separate problem from the fine.
