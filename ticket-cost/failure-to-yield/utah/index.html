How much is a failure to yield ticket in Utah?

Nationally, failure to yield tickets fall in a range of $50–$500 by state. In Utah, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Utah Driver License Division).

We do not list a single Utah dollar figure because the amount depends on the court and the citation, not on a flat number we can verify. The most reliable number is the one printed on your ticket or shown by the court that received it.

The $50–$500 range is a national spread across states. It is not a Utah fine or a Utah minimum or maximum.

If you cannot find an amount on the citation, the court clerk listed on it can tell you what is owed. Ask whether the figure already includes fees and surcharges.

How is a Utah failure to yield fine set?

Utah sets this fine through the court, within the state's traffic code. The amount appears on the citation rather than in one universal number.

Utah's schedule has zone enhancements and fees built in. That means the total you owe can be higher than a base fine would suggest, depending on where and how the violation happened.

  • The citation: This is your starting point for the amount.
  • The court: It sets the amount within the traffic code.
  • Built-in fees: Zone enhancements and fees are part of the schedule.

For the traffic code itself, Utah statutes are available through Utah statutes via Justia. Reading the statute can tell you what the violation is, but the dollar amount still comes from the court.

How many points does failure to yield add in Utah?

The point figures we can confirm relate to speeding. Utah's scale gives speeding 35–75 points depending on how many mph over the limit you were going.

We do not have a verified point value for failure to yield, so we will not guess. Check with the Utah Driver License Division to see how your specific violation is scored.

The suspension threshold is clear: 200 points in 3 years leads to suspension for drivers age 21 and older. Points from several violations add up toward that total.

Do not apply the speeding point range to a failure to yield ticket. They are different violations, and the scoring may differ.

Does a failure to yield ticket raise insurance in Utah?

It can. Nationally, a moving-violation conviction typically raises premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Utah rate.

Your own change depends on your insurer, your driving history, and whether the ticket ends in a conviction. We do not recommend any insurer, and we cannot predict your quote.

  • A conviction is what usually triggers a rate review.
  • Insurers weigh the violation together with your other history.
  • The effect may show up at your next renewal rather than right away.

If the ticket involved a crash, the insurance effect may involve more than the citation. Your insurer or policy documents are the best source for how they treat it.

Can a failure to yield ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome is up to the court. Our sources do not list a set path for dismissal or reduction in Utah, so we will not promise one.

You generally have a few broad choices once you get a citation:

  1. Pay it. This usually resolves the matter, but a payment may count as a conviction. That can affect points and insurance.
  2. Contest it. You can ask the court how to dispute the citation and what the process looks like.
  3. Ask about alternatives. The court clerk can explain whether any reduced or alternative resolutions are available for your case.

Because paying may affect your record, compare the fine with the possible effect on points and premiums before you decide. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot give legal advice. For advice about your case, speak with a licensed attorney of your choosing.

What happens if I don't pay a failure to yield ticket in Utah?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts commonly add consequences when a ticket goes unanswered, and a driver license problem can follow.

We do not have verified deadlines or late-fee amounts for Utah, so check your citation for the date and instructions. The court listed there controls the timeline.

  • Look for a due date or appearance date on the citation.
  • Contact the court early if you need to ask about your options.
  • Keep proof of any payment or filing.

Since zone enhancements and fees are built into the schedule, an unpaid ticket should be resolved with the court rather than left to sit. Check your driving record with the Utah Driver License Division if you are unsure of its status.
