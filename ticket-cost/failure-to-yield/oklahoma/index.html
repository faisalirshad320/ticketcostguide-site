How much is a failure to yield ticket in Oklahoma?

Nationally, failure to yield fines run $50–$500 by state. In Oklahoma the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Service Oklahoma). We do not publish a single Oklahoma dollar figure because the court, not a fixed statewide price list, decides it.

The number you pay is usually the fine plus court costs and statutory fees. Those extras are added by the municipal or district court handling your case.

The national range is context, not a quote. Your own citation and the court that issued it control what you owe.

What counts as failure to yield?

Failure to yield generally means you did not give another driver, pedestrian or cyclist the right-of-way when the law required it. Common settings include intersections, left turns across traffic, stop and yield signs, merging, and crosswalks.

The citation will name the specific provision you were charged under. Oklahoma's traffic statutes are published online (Oklahoma statutes via Justia), and the charge written on your ticket is the one that matters for fines and points.

Different circumstances can lead to different charges. A yield violation with a crash or injury may be handled differently from one with no collision, so compare the wording on your citation with the statute.

How is the fine set, and what else gets added?

The exact fine for this violation in Oklahoma is set by the court within the state's traffic code. That is why two drivers cited for the same thing can see different totals.

Court costs and statutory fees are added by the municipal or district court. Which court you appear in depends on who wrote the ticket and where it happened.

  • Municipal court: usually handles tickets written under city ordinances inside city limits.
  • District court: usually handles tickets written under state law, often outside city limits.

The citation or the court clerk should show the amount due. If the ticket lists no amount, contact the clerk of the court named on it rather than relying on a general estimate.

How many points does failure to yield add in Oklahoma?

The point figure we can confirm is for speeding: 2 points for ordinary speeding, or more at higher speeds. We do not have a verified point value for failure to yield, so we won't guess one.

What we can say is how the system works. Oklahoma suspends a license at 10 points in 5 years (Service Oklahoma). Points from separate violations add up inside that window.

To find out whether your specific charge carries points, check the citation and ask Service Oklahoma or the court. A reduced or amended charge can change the points outcome, which is worth asking the court about before you plead.

Do not assume that the speeding point figure applies to a yield violation. Confirm the value for your charge.

Does a failure to yield ticket raise insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Oklahoma-specific one.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your driving record, and whether the violation was involved in a crash. A yield ticket tied to an accident may be treated more seriously than one with no collision.

We don't recommend insurers. If you are worried about a rate increase, ask your insurer how a conviction would be treated, and ask the court what happens to the record if you resolve the ticket in different ways.

Over several years, a premium increase can cost more than the fine itself. That is worth weighing when deciding how to respond.

Can a failure to yield ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but there is no guarantee, and this is not legal advice. Your realistic options are usually to pay, to contest the ticket, or to ask the court whether any alternative is available.

  • Pay: this is typically treated as accepting the violation, which can bring points and an insurance effect.
  • Contest: you ask the court to hear the case. You can challenge the facts, such as who had the right-of-way, or procedural problems with the citation.
  • Ask about alternatives: some courts offer options such as a driving course or a reduced charge. Availability varies by court, so ask the clerk.

Right-of-way disputes often come down to evidence: diagrams, photos, witness accounts, and any dash camera footage. If the stakes are high, such as a crash or a license at risk, you may want to speak to a licensed attorney. We do not recommend a specific one.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts can add costs, and an unresolved ticket can lead to further consequences such as a hold on your license or a warrant, depending on the court.

Court costs and statutory fees are added by the municipal or district court, so late handling can raise your total beyond the original amount. The citation lists the response requirements for your case.

If you cannot pay in full, contact the clerk of the court before the deadline on your ticket. Some courts discuss payment arrangements, but this varies by court.

Also keep the point threshold in mind. Reaching 10 points in 5 years means suspension, so an unresolved ticket that turns into a conviction can matter beyond its dollar cost.
