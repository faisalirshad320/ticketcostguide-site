How much is a failure to yield ticket in New Mexico?

There is no single published New Mexico price we can give you. Nationally the range is $50–$500 by state, and in New Mexico the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (New Mexico Motor Vehicle Division).

The national range is useful only as a rough frame. It is not a New Mexico fine, and your ticket could fall anywhere the court's rules allow.

Look at the citation itself. The amount, the court name and the instructions for responding are normally printed on it or on the paperwork that came with it.

Failure to yield covers several situations, such as not giving way at an intersection, to a pedestrian or to another vehicle. The specific wording of the charge on your citation matters, because the court sets the amount for the violation actually charged.

Fees are also added to the penalty assessment, so the total you pay can be higher than the base fine alone. Court cases can exceed the assessment amounts.

How is a failure to yield fine set in New Mexico?

In New Mexico, the exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The code is published by the state and available through New Mexico statutes via Justia.

That means the figure comes from the court handling your citation, not from a general price list. We do not state an exact dollar amount here because the sources we rely on do not give one.

Three things shape what you end up owing:

  • The charge written on the citation. A different charge can mean a different amount.
  • Added fees. Fees are added to the penalty assessment.
  • Whether the case goes to court. Court cases can exceed the assessment amounts.

If you are unsure which amount applies to you, contact the court listed on your citation and ask what the total due is.

How many points does failure to yield add in New Mexico?

The New Mexico points system we have on file is described this way: 3–8 points by mph over. Those tiers are tied to speed, so they read most naturally as a speeding schedule rather than a failure to yield schedule.

We do not have a sourced point value for failure to yield specifically, and we will not guess one. Ask the court or check with the New Mexico Motor Vehicle Division whether points attach to your charge and how many.

The thresholds that matter if points are assessed:

  • 7–10 points in 12 months risks suspension.
  • 12 points = revocation.

Points from different tickets within the same 12 months can add up. A single failure to yield ticket may look minor, but it could push you closer to a threshold if you already have other violations.

Tip: ask the MVD for a copy of your driving record to see what is currently on it before you decide how to respond to the ticket.

Does a failure to yield ticket raise insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums ~20% on average. That is a national figure, not a New Mexico one, and your own change could be higher, lower or nothing.

Failure to yield is generally treated as a moving violation. Whether it affects your premium depends on whether you are convicted, your insurer's rules and your prior record.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend insurers or tell you what any company will do. If you want to know how a ticket might affect your policy, the most reliable step is to ask your insurer how it handles moving violations.

Keep in mind that the ticket's cost does not stop at the fine. An increase of around 20% on a premium, if it applied, would repeat each time you pay.

Can a failure to yield ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but we cannot promise any outcome, and this is not legal advice. What you can do is understand the choices the citation gives you.

Common paths include:

  1. Pay the ticket. This usually resolves the case quickly, but it typically means accepting the violation and any consequences that follow, including possible points and an insurance effect.
  2. Contest it. You can ask for a hearing in the court listed on the citation. Be aware that court cases can exceed the assessment amounts.
  3. Ask the court about alternatives. Some courts may offer options that change how a violation is recorded. Ask the clerk what is available for your charge. We have no sourced list of New Mexico programs, so we do not name any.

If the stakes feel high, for example because of your record or the points thresholds, you may want to speak with a licensed New Mexico attorney. We do not recommend a specific one.

Read the response instructions on the citation first. They tell you where and how to respond.

What happens if I don't pay a failure to yield ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. We do not have sourced figures on late penalties in New Mexico, so we will not quote any.

What we can say in general is that unpaid or unanswered tickets can lead the court to take further action, which may include added costs or consequences for your driving privileges. The details depend on the court and the citation.

Fees are already added to the penalty assessment, and court cases can exceed the assessment amounts. Waiting can leave you facing a larger total than the one you started with.

If you cannot pay in full or have missed a deadline, contact the court on your citation as soon as you can and ask what your options are.
