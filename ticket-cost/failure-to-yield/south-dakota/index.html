How much is a failure to yield ticket in South Dakota?

Nationally, failure to yield tickets cost $50–$500 depending on the state. In South Dakota, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (South Dakota Department of Public Safety).

We do not publish a single South Dakota dollar figure, because the amount can differ by county or court. The national range is a national figure, not a South Dakota price.

Your citation is the best source for your amount. If it lists a fine or a court date, that is what the court expects from you.

Court costs are included in the standard scheduled amounts. That means the scheduled figure you see should already account for them, rather than leaving a separate add-on to discover later. If anything on your paperwork looks unclear, ask the clerk of the court named on the ticket.

How is the failure to yield fine set in South Dakota?

The fine is set by the court within the state's traffic code. South Dakota's laws are published online, and you can browse them through South Dakota statutes via Justia.

The officer writes the violation on the citation. The court then determines the amount, either as a scheduled figure or after a hearing.

Two drivers cited for similar conduct can end up with different totals. The reasons include:

  • the court that handles the case
  • whether the citation lists a scheduled amount
  • whether the case goes before a judge

Because the amount is set at the court level, any online figure that claims to be the single South Dakota price should be treated with caution.

What counts as failure to yield?

In general terms, failure to yield means not giving another driver, a pedestrian or a cyclist the right of way when the law requires it. Common situations include:

  • left turns across oncoming traffic
  • stop and yield signs
  • merging
  • entering a road from a driveway
  • crosswalks

The exact wording and elements of the offense are in South Dakota's codified laws, which you can search through South Dakota statutes via Justia. We do not quote a specific statute number here, so check the citation for the section the officer listed.

If a crash was involved, the situation can be more complicated than a simple ticket. This page covers only the cost of the citation itself.

How many points is a failure to yield ticket in South Dakota?

South Dakota assigns points per conviction toward suspension thresholds. The locked threshold is 15 points in 12 months (South Dakota Department of Public Safety).

We do not state a point value for failure to yield specifically, because our sourced data does not list one. Confirm the value for your violation with the Department of Public Safety or the court.

Points attach to convictions, not to the act of being cited. A ticket that is dismissed does not produce a conviction, so it would not add points.

The 15-points-in-12-months threshold is the one to watch. Several convictions inside a year are what bring a license closer to suspension.

Does a failure to yield ticket raise insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a South Dakota rate.

Your own change could be smaller, larger or none at all. It depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the violation is recorded.

We do not recommend any insurer. If you are worried about a rate change, you can ask your own insurer how it treats a conviction before you decide how to handle the ticket.

The insurance effect usually comes from the conviction on your record, not from the fine. Paying a modest fine can therefore cost less than the premium increase that follows it.

Can a failure to yield ticket be dismissed or reduced?

It may be possible, but outcomes are decided by the court and the facts of your case. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice or recommend attorneys.

In general, people who receive a ticket can:

  • pay it, which usually results in a conviction
  • contest it at a hearing
  • ask the court clerk what options exist for your citation

Paying is quick, but a conviction is what can bring points and insurance effects. Contesting takes more time and effort, and the result is not guaranteed.

Before you decide, read the citation closely. It should state the court, the appearance or payment instructions and the deadline.

If you are unsure which option fits, consider speaking with a licensed South Dakota attorney of your own choosing.

What happens if I don't pay a failure to yield ticket?

Ignoring a citation tends to make things worse. Courts generally expect you to pay, appear or respond by the date on the citation.

Missing that date can lead to added consequences, which may include a hold or suspension on your license. Exact steps depend on the court, so we do not list specific penalties or deadlines here.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the clerk of the court named on the ticket before the deadline. Ask what is available, since policies differ by court.

Remember that suspension thresholds also exist for points. The locked figure is 15 points in 12 months, so unpaid tickets and new convictions can compound.
