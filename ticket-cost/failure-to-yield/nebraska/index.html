How much is a failure to yield ticket in Nebraska?

Nationally, failure to yield tickets run $50–$500 by state. In Nebraska the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Nebraska Department of Motor Vehicles).

We do not publish a single Nebraska dollar figure for this violation. The amount depends on the court and the charge written on your citation.

The $50–$500 range is a national range across states. It is not a Nebraska schedule, so do not treat it as the amount you will owe.

The number on or tied to your citation, plus court costs, is the best guide to what you will pay. If the citation does not list an amount, the court clerk can tell you what applies to your case.

How is the fine for failure to yield set in Nebraska?

In Nebraska the fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The traffic statutes are published online through Nebraska statutes via Justia.

That means two drivers cited for similar conduct can see different totals. The court, the specific charge and the circumstances on the citation all matter.

Failure to yield covers several situations. Examples include not giving right of way at an intersection, a stop sign, a crosswalk or when merging. The charge on your citation tells you which one applies to you.

  • Read the charge description on the citation.
  • Note the court named on it.
  • Check whether the citation lists an amount or tells you to contact the court.

What court costs and surcharges are added?

Court costs are added statewide in Nebraska. They are charged on top of the fine, so the total you pay is higher than the fine alone.

Fines are also doubled in some zones. If your citation was issued in a zone that carries a doubled fine, expect a larger base amount than the same violation elsewhere.

We do not list dollar amounts for court costs here. Our sources do not give a fixed figure, and the amount can differ by court.

Before paying, ask the clerk for the full total including court costs. The fine on the citation may not be the whole bill.

How many points does a failure to yield ticket carry in Nebraska?

The Nebraska points system assigns 1–3 points by mph over. That scale is written around speed, so it fits speeding most directly.

A failure to yield citation does not involve mph over a limit. The points tied to your conviction depend on the offense recorded, so confirm the point value with the Nebraska Department of Motor Vehicles.

The threshold that matters most is this: 12 points in 2 years = revocation. Points from different violations within that window add together.

  • Check your own driving record for current points.
  • Count only points inside the 2-year window.
  • Remember that several smaller tickets can add up toward the 12-point threshold.

Does a failure to yield ticket raise insurance in Nebraska?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums ~20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Nebraska one.

Your own change could be higher, lower or none at all. It depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the violation appears on your record.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer. If you are worried about a rate change, ask your insurer how a conviction would be treated before you decide how to respond to the ticket.

A conviction is what usually affects rates. Paying the ticket is generally treated as a guilty outcome, so consider the insurance effect before paying.

Can a failure to yield ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Possibly, but this depends on the court and your facts. We cannot say whether your ticket qualifies, and this page is not legal advice.

In general, the options on a traffic citation are to pay it, contest it, or ask the court what other resolutions it offers. The citation or the court clerk can explain the steps and deadlines for your case.

  • Pay: usually closes the case but can mean a conviction on your record.
  • Contest: you appear before the court and present your side.
  • Ask the court: the clerk can explain whether any other resolution is available.

If the stakes are high, such as points near the revocation threshold, you may want to speak with a licensed attorney. We do not recommend any specific one.

What happens if I don't pay a failure to yield ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts can add costs or take further action, and the license consequences are handled through the state.

We do not list specific penalty amounts or deadlines because our sources do not give them. Your citation and the court clerk are the right places to confirm what applies.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court before the due date. Ask what is available rather than letting the case go unanswered.

Remember that points count toward the threshold of 12 points in 2 years = revocation. A conviction from an unresolved ticket can add to that total.
