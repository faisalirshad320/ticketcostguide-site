How much is a failure to yield ticket in Illinois?

Nationally, a failure to yield ticket falls in a range of $50–$500 by state. In Illinois the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Illinois Secretary of State).

We do not publish a single Illinois dollar figure for this violation, because the sources we rely on do not give one. The number printed on your ticket or on the court's notice is the one that applies to you.

The $50–$500 range is a national picture across states. It is not a promise about what an Illinois court will set, and it does not include anything the court may add to the final total.

If you want the real number, check the citation itself, the court's website or the circuit clerk's office listed on the ticket.

How is the fine set, and why does it vary by court?

In Illinois the fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. That is why two drivers with similar tickets can see different totals depending on the county and court that handles the case.

The amount can also depend on the facts of the stop. Whether a crash was involved, whether the ticket was issued in a special zone, and how the court treats your record can all matter.

  • Look at the citation for a listed fine or a court date.
  • Check whether the ticket says you must appear or whether you can resolve it without appearing.
  • Ask the clerk what the total due includes before you pay.

Court costs and surcharges are separate from the base fine in many places. We do not list local add-on amounts, because they are not in our locked sources and they vary by court.

How many points is a failure to yield ticket in Illinois?

Illinois tracks convictions through the Secretary of State (SOS). Convictions carry 5–50 point values on the SOS scale (Illinois Secretary of State).

That range covers many kinds of violations, from minor to serious. We do not assign a specific point value to failure to yield here, because the locked figure is a range for the whole scale and not a number for this offense.

Repeat convictions within 12 months trigger suspension. If you already have a recent conviction, a new one deserves extra attention before you decide how to respond.

You can check your own driving record through the Secretary of State. Do that before you decide whether to pay or contest.

Does a failure to yield ticket raise insurance?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Illinois-specific one.

Your own change could be higher, lower or none. It depends on your insurer, your driving history, whether anyone was hurt, and how the ticket is resolved.

  • A conviction on your record is what usually triggers a rate review.
  • Insurers set their own rules, and we do not recommend any insurer.
  • Ask your insurer or agent how a conviction would affect your policy before you decide how to handle the ticket.

Over several years, a rate increase can cost more than the fine itself. That is worth weighing when you decide whether to pay or contest.

Can a failure to yield ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes. Whether it happens depends on the facts, the court and your record, and we cannot predict an outcome.

Common paths drivers ask about include contesting the ticket at a hearing, asking the court about a reduced charge, or asking whether the court offers an option such as supervision or a traffic safety course. Availability varies by court and by your history, so ask the clerk what the court offers.

  • Read the citation for your options and your deadline.
  • Gather what you have, such as photos of the intersection, signs and any dashcam video.
  • Think about whether a crash occurred, since that can change how a court views the case.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the ticket?

Ignoring a ticket tends to make things worse. Courts can add consequences when a ticket goes unanswered, and a missed court date can lead to further action on your license or your case.

The specific steps and deadlines are set by the court and printed on your citation or notice. We do not state timelines here because they are not in our locked sources.

  • Find the court date or response instruction on the ticket.
  • Contact the clerk early if you are unsure what to do.
  • Keep proof of anything you pay or file.

Remember the point rules as well. Repeat convictions within 12 months trigger suspension, so an unresolved or added conviction can matter more than the fine itself.

Does a work-zone or other special zone change the cost?

It can change how a violation is treated, but be careful not to mix up different offenses. Work-zone speeding carries a statutory minimum fine of $375 for a first offense. That figure applies to speeding in a work zone, not to failure to yield.

We include it so you can see that Illinois sets firm minimums for some violations. For failure to yield, the amount is set by the court on the citation.

If your ticket mentions a work zone, a school zone or another special designation, read the charge carefully. Ask the court how it affects the amount and your options.
