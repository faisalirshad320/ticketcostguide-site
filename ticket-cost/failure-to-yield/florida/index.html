By Editorial Team, TicketCostGuide · Last reviewed 2026-10-16

County courts add costs and fees on top of the statutory penalty, so the total you owe can differ by county. Check your citation for the exact figure.

How much is a failure to yield ticket in Florida?

The lead number is a national one: nationally $50–$500 by state; in Florida the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Florida Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles). That range describes how much states differ. It is not a Florida price list.

We do not publish an exact Florida dollar figure because the amount depends on the citation and the court that handles it. Anyone quoting you a single fixed Florida number without seeing your ticket is guessing.

Your citation, or the clerk of the court named on it, is the only place to confirm the amount you owe.

The fine is only part of the cost. Court costs, possible points, and a possible insurance increase all come after it, and each is covered below.

How is the fine set, and why do totals differ by county?

Florida sets penalties for traffic violations within the state's traffic code. The court then fills in the exact amount on the citation. The statutes themselves are available through Florida statutes via Justia.

County courts add costs and fees on top of the statutory penalty. Two drivers cited for the same thing in different counties can therefore see different totals.

School-zone and work-zone amounts are doubled by statute. If your failure to yield happened in one of those zones, expect the amount on the citation to reflect that.

Things worth checking on the citation:

  • The violation charged and the statute listed
  • Whether a school or work zone is marked
  • The total due and the payment deadline
  • The court and county that will handle it

How many points does a failure to yield ticket carry in Florida?

Florida's points system, as summarized in our sources, lists 3 points (15 or less over) or 4 points (16+ over). Those tiers are written around how far over a limit a driver was. They are speed-based wording.

Failure to yield is not a speed offense. We do not state a point value for it from memory. The points assigned depend on the violation charged on your citation and on the conviction outcome.

The official source for point schedules is Florida Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles. Check your driver record there, or ask the clerk what the charge carries.

Threshold to remember: 12 points in 12 months = 30-day suspension.

Can a failure to yield ticket lead to a suspension?

A single ticket is unlikely to reach the suspension threshold on its own. The risk comes from accumulation. Under the locked state facts, 12 points in 12 months = 30-day suspension.

That means points from other moving violations in the same period matter. A failure to yield that adds points to a record already carrying other violations can push a driver closer to the line.

If you are close to the threshold, look at your full record before deciding how to respond to the citation. Our guide does not tell you what to choose. It only shows which numbers matter.

Does a failure to yield ticket raise insurance?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Florida-specific one.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your driving history, and whether the violation ends up as a conviction on your record. Some insurers also weigh whether a crash was involved.

The fine is a one-time cost. A premium increase can last across several renewals, so over time it may cost more than the ticket. Ask your insurer how it treats a violation like this. We do not recommend any insurer.

Can a failure to yield ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes outcomes change, but we cannot predict yours. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice.

In general, a driver who receives a citation can pay it, or ask the court to review it. The citation lists the options and the deadlines that apply to you.

Points and insurance effects often hinge on the final outcome rather than on the original charge. That is one reason to read the citation closely before paying.

For questions about your specific situation, contact the clerk of the court on the citation or a licensed Florida attorney of your own choosing.

What happens if I don't pay a failure to yield ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Unpaid or unanswered traffic tickets can bring added costs and license consequences. The exact steps and timing are set by the court and the state.

Because county courts add costs and fees on top of the statutory penalty, a late or unresolved ticket can end up costing more than the original amount. Confirm the deadline on the citation, and ask the clerk if anything is unclear.

If you cannot pay the full amount, ask the court what options it offers. Do not assume the deadline has passed or can be extended without checking.
