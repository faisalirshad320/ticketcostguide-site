How much is a failure to yield ticket in New Jersey?

Nationally, failure to yield tickets fall in a range of $50–$500 by state. In New Jersey the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (New Jersey Motor Vehicle Commission).

We do not list a single New Jersey fine here, because the amount depends on the court handling your case and the violation as charged. The $50–$500 range is a national figure, not a New Jersey schedule.

Your citation and the court that issued it are the most reliable place to find your actual amount. If the paperwork is unclear, the court clerk can tell you what applies to your case.

The fine is only part of the cost. Points, possible MVC surcharges and insurance changes can add more over time.

How is the fine set for failure to yield?

In New Jersey the fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. That means it is not a flat number printed in one place for every driver.

Several things can shape what appears on your citation or in your court outcome:

  • The specific violation the officer charged.
  • The court that handles the ticket.
  • Whether the case is paid, contested or resolved another way.

New Jersey statutes are available to read through New Jersey statutes via Justia. We point to them for context only, and they are not a substitute for the figures on your own ticket.

How many points is a failure to yield ticket in New Jersey?

New Jersey uses a points system. The locked tiers are 2 points (1–14 over), 4 points (15–29 over) and 5 points (30+ over). Reaching 12 points leads to suspension.

Those tiers are described by how far over a limit a driver was, so they read like speeding tiers. The points for a yield violation depend on how the offense is charged and coded, and we do not assign a number here.

To see how many points your specific charge carries, check your citation and the New Jersey Motor Vehicle Commission, which manages driver records.

Points matter beyond the 12-point suspension line. Once you reach 6+ points within 3 years, a separate surcharge comes into play.

Is there an MVC surcharge on top of the fine?

Possibly. In New Jersey, 6+ points within 3 years adds an MVC insurance surcharge of $150+ (New Jersey Motor Vehicle Commission).

These MVC surcharges at 6+ points are billed annually for 3 years on top of court fines. So a ticket that pushes your record to that level costs more than the fine alone.

  • The court fine is set on the citation.
  • The surcharge is a separate MVC charge tied to your point total.
  • The surcharge repeats each year across the 3-year period.

A single ticket may not reach 6 points by itself. Points already on your record count toward the total, so your history matters.

Does a failure to yield ticket raise insurance?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a New Jersey-specific one.

Your own change could be higher, lower or none, depending on your insurer, your record and how the ticket is resolved. We do not recommend or compare insurers.

The MVC surcharge and an insurance premium increase are different things. The surcharge goes to the state. A premium change comes from your insurance company.

If you want to estimate the effect, take your current premium and think about what a rise of about 20% would mean over a year. Treat that as a rough national benchmark only.

Can a failure to yield ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes a ticket is dismissed or changed to a different charge, but that is decided by the court and the facts of your case. We cannot say how likely it is, and TicketCostGuide does not give legal advice.

Because the fine is set by the court on the citation, the outcome of your case can change the final cost. A different charge may also carry different points.

  • Read your citation for the charge and court information.
  • Ask the court clerk how your case can be resolved.
  • If you want legal advice, you may wish to speak with a licensed New Jersey attorney.

Before you decide how to respond, weigh the fine against the possible points, surcharges and insurance effects described above.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the ticket?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. The exact steps depend on the court, and we do not state specific deadlines or penalty amounts here.

Points are also a risk. Reaching 12 points leads to suspension, and 6+ points within 3 years adds MVC surcharges of $150+ billed annually for 3 years.

The court listed on your citation is the right place to ask what happens if a payment or appearance is missed. Do that early, before the situation grows.
