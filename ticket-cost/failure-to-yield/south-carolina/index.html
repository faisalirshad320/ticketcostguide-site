How much is a failure to yield ticket in South Carolina?

Nationally, failure to yield fines run $50–$500 depending on the state. In South Carolina, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (South Carolina DMV).

We do not publish a single South Carolina dollar figure, because the amount depends on the court handling your case and on what the citation says. The $50–$500 range is a national figure, not a South Carolina schedule.

The number printed on your citation, or given by the court clerk, is the number that counts. Any web page that quotes one fixed South Carolina fine for every driver is guessing.

Remember that the fine is only part of the bill. Statewide assessments and surcharges are added on top, as explained below.

How is the fine set, and what gets added to it?

The fine for this violation in South Carolina is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The code is available through South Carolina statutes via Justia. The court applies it to the facts on your citation.

Statewide assessments and surcharges are added to every fine and usually exceed it. That means the base fine can understate what you actually pay.

When you look at your citation or ask the clerk, check for:

  • The base fine for the charge as written
  • Each assessment or surcharge added to it
  • The total due, and the court that is collecting it
  • Any stated deadline to pay or respond

Because the amount is court-specific, two drivers cited for the same thing can see different totals. Always work from your own paperwork.

How many points is a failure to yield in South Carolina?

South Carolina's points system assigns 2–6 points by mph over. That scale is built around speed, so it does not map neatly onto a yield violation.

We do not state a specific point value for failure to yield, because our sources do not give one for this charge. The South Carolina DMV (South Carolina DMV) is the place to confirm how many points a particular conviction carries.

Two rules from the points system are worth knowing:

  • 12 points = suspension. Reaching that total puts your license at risk.
  • Points halve after 12 months. Older points count for less over time.

If you already have points from earlier tickets, a new one matters more. Check your driving record before deciding how to respond.

Does a failure to yield ticket raise insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a South Carolina one.

Your own result depends on your insurer, your record and how the violation is recorded. We do not name or recommend any insurer, and we cannot predict your rate.

A conviction is what usually triggers a surcharge. For that reason, many drivers weigh the long-term premium effect against the one-time fine, since the premium effect can last longer than the payment itself.

If your policy renews soon, ask your insurer how it treats moving violations. Do this before you decide how to resolve the ticket.

Can a failure to yield ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but it depends on the court and the facts. We cannot say how likely it is in your case, and this page is not legal advice.

In general, drivers with a citation have a few paths:

  • Pay the ticket. This usually resolves the case, but payment typically counts as a conviction.
  • Contest it. You can ask the court for a hearing and present your account of what happened.
  • Ask the court about alternatives. Some courts offer other resolutions, and availability varies by court.

Failure to yield cases often turn on who had the right of way. Notes, photos of the scene and witness details can help if you contest.

For advice on your specific situation, speak with a licensed South Carolina attorney. We do not recommend any particular one.

What happens if I don't pay a failure to yield ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts generally treat an unanswered ticket as a separate problem from the original charge.

Possible consequences include added costs, a hold or suspension on your license, and further action by the court. Exact steps and timelines depend on the court and are not covered by our locked sources.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court clerk before the deadline on your citation. Clerks can explain what options the court offers, such as payment arrangements.

Also keep in mind that statewide assessments and surcharges usually exceed the fine. Ask for the total due, not just the base fine.

Could a failure to yield ticket lead to a suspension?

Only if your points reach the threshold. In South Carolina, 12 points = suspension.

Points halve after 12 months, so older points count for less. A single ticket is less likely to push you over the line than a ticket added to an existing record.

To see where you stand, request your driving record from the South Carolina DMV (South Carolina DMV). Then compare it with the points the new conviction would add.
