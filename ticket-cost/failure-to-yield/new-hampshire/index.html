How much is a failure to yield ticket in New Hampshire?

Nationally the range is $50–$500 by state. In New Hampshire the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (New Hampshire Division of Motor Vehicles).

We do not publish a single New Hampshire dollar figure, because the amount you owe depends on the court handling your case and on the details written on your ticket. The citation, or the court that issued it, is the only reliable source for your number.

Treat the national range as context only. It shows how much states differ, not what New Hampshire will charge you.

Remember that the base fine is not always the full bill. A statutory penalty assessment is added on top, as explained below.

How is the fine set, and what gets added to it?

The exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. Statutes for New Hampshire are available through New Hampshire statutes via Justia.

A 24% penalty assessment is added to every base fine by statute. That means the amount you pay is the base fine plus that percentage.

Here is how to think about it in practice:

  • Find the base fine on your citation or in the court's notice.
  • Ask the court whether the amount shown already includes the assessment.
  • Confirm the total before you pay, so you do not under-pay or pay twice.

Because the court sets the base amount, two drivers cited for the same violation may not see identical numbers. Circumstances noted by the officer and the court's own practice can both play a part.

How many points does failure to yield add in New Hampshire?

New Hampshire's points system assigns 3–4 points by offense (New Hampshire Division of Motor Vehicles). Which number applies to your ticket depends on the specific offense listed on the citation.

Points are added to your driving record after a conviction. They are separate from the fine, so paying the fine does not remove them.

Check the exact violation named on your ticket. Failure to yield can be written in different ways, and the wording may affect how it is scored.

Can failure to yield tickets lead to a license suspension?

Yes, if points add up. Under the state's system, 12 points in 12 months = up to 3-month suspension.

A single failure to yield ticket carries 3–4 points by offense, so it moves you part of the way toward that threshold. Combined with other moving violations inside the same 12 months, it can matter much more.

Count any other tickets from the past year before deciding how to respond. A ticket that looks minor alone can be the one that pushes a record toward suspension.

The threshold is a maximum penalty, described as "up to" 3 months. The Division of Motor Vehicles decides how it applies to your record.

Does a failure to yield ticket raise insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. This is a national figure, not a New Hampshire-specific one.

Your own change could be smaller, larger or zero. It depends on your insurer, your driving history and whether the ticket ends in a conviction.

We do not recommend any insurer or predict your premium. If the question matters to you, ask your insurer how it treats a conviction of this type and whether a different outcome would be treated differently.

Keep in mind that the cost of a higher premium can exceed the fine itself over time, so it is worth weighing when you decide how to respond.

Can a failure to yield ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but we cannot tell you whether yours can be. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice.

In general, a driver cited in New Hampshire has a few paths:

  • Pay the ticket. This usually resolves the case but may mean a conviction, points and the penalty assessment.
  • Contest it. You can ask the court for a hearing and present your account of what happened.
  • Ask the court about alternatives. Some courts discuss reduced charges or other outcomes. Ask the clerk what is available in your case.

Read the instructions on your citation closely. They explain how to respond and by when. Missing the response date can limit your options.

If you are weighing a contest, you may wish to speak with a licensed New Hampshire attorney. We do not recommend a specific one.

What happens if I don't pay a failure to yield ticket?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. Courts can take further action when a citation goes unanswered, and the Division of Motor Vehicles may be notified.

The exact consequences and timing are set by the court and the traffic code, so check your citation and the court's notice. We do not state deadlines here because they are not part of our sourced figures.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court clerk before the response date. Ask what options exist rather than waiting for a notice to arrive.

An unpaid ticket does not make the fine, the 24% penalty assessment or the points go away.
