How much is a failure to yield ticket in Virginia?

Nationally the range is $50–$500 by state. In Virginia the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Virginia Department of Motor Vehicles). The national range is a spread across all states, not a Virginia price.

That means the number printed on your citation or court notice is the one that counts. We do not publish an exact Virginia dollar figure for this violation, because the court sets the amount within the state's traffic code.

The fine is not the whole bill. Fixed processing costs apply to every prepaid ticket. If the case is treated as reckless, court-set fines and fees are added.

Bottom line: use the national range only as a rough frame. Your actual Virginia cost is whatever the court puts on your citation.

How is the failure to yield fine set in Virginia?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within Virginia's traffic code. Virginia statutes are available to read through Virginia statutes via Justia. The statute is the starting point, and the court applies it to your case.

Failure to yield generally means not giving the right of way to another vehicle or a pedestrian when the law requires it. The officer's description on the citation tells you which version of the violation you were charged with.

Two things shape the final cost:

  • How the case is handled. Fixed processing costs apply to every prepaid ticket.
  • Whether it is treated as reckless. Reckless cases add court-set fines and fees, and carry the higher point value.

Read the citation closely. The charge listed on it affects both the amount owed and the points added to your record.

How many points is a failure to yield in Virginia?

Virginia uses a demerit point system with 3–6 points by offense, and 6 points for reckless (Virginia Department of Motor Vehicles). Which value applies depends on the offense you are convicted of, not just the ticket's nickname.

The DMV suspends a license at point thresholds. We do not list the threshold numbers here, so check the DMV directly for the current cutoffs.

Drivers also earn +1 safe point per clean year. A clean year helps offset demerit points over time, which is one reason a single ticket matters less if the rest of your record is clear.

  • Lower-end offenses sit near 3 points.
  • Reckless-type charges sit at 6 points.
  • Each clean year adds +1 safe point.

Points are tied to a conviction. How your case ends can change the points that reach your record.

Does a failure to yield ticket raise insurance in Virginia?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Virginia-specific one (Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws).

Your own change could be higher, lower, or nothing at all. Insurers set their own rates and weigh your history, the charge, and whether anyone was hurt or a crash was involved.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer. If you are worried about a rate change, you can ask your own insurer how a conviction would be treated at renewal.

Over time, the insurance effect can add up to more than the fine itself. That is worth weighing when you decide how to respond to the citation.

Can a failure to yield ticket be dismissed or reduced in Virginia?

Sometimes, but the outcome is decided by the court, not by this guide. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot tell you whether your ticket qualifies for a dismissal or a lower charge.

Your general options usually look like this:

  1. Prepay the ticket. This resolves the case quickly, and fixed processing costs apply. It may also mean a conviction and points.
  2. Contest it in court. The court hears your side, and the fine is set by the court if you are found responsible.
  3. Ask the court clerk about procedure. The clerk can explain deadlines and how your court handles these cases, though not give legal advice.

Because a conviction can bring points and an insurance increase, many drivers talk to a licensed attorney before deciding. We do not recommend a specific attorney.

Prepaying is convenient, but it is a choice with consequences for your record. Read your citation before you pay.

What happens if I don't pay a failure to yield ticket in Virginia?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. The court can add its own fines and fees, and the DMV can act on your driving record. The specifics depend on your court and the instructions on your citation.

We do not state exact deadlines or penalty amounts here, because they are set by the court and the state. Look for the due date or court date printed on the citation, and contact the court clerk if anything is unclear.

A prepaid ticket also carries fixed processing costs, so paying does not mean paying only the fine. If your case is treated as reckless, court-set fines and fees apply on top.

  • Find the court date or payment deadline on the citation.
  • Confirm the charge listed.
  • Decide whether to pay or contest before the deadline passes.
