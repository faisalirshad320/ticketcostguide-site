By Editorial Team, TicketCostGuide · Last reviewed 2026-10-16

A failure to yield ticket in New York has no single price: nationally the range is $50–$500 by state, and in New York the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (New York State Department of Motor Vehicles). Fines vary by court, and an $88–$93 mandatory surcharge comes on top.

How much is a failure to yield ticket in New York?

Nationally, failure to yield fines run $50–$500 by state. In New York the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (New York State Department of Motor Vehicles).

We do not list a single New York dollar figure, because the court decides the fine within the state's traffic code. The number printed on your citation or court notice is the number that matters.

The fine is only part of the cost. A mandatory surcharge, a possible DMV assessment, points and an insurance increase can all follow.

Bottom line: the national range is a reference point, not a New York price. Your citation and the court set your amount.

How is the fine set?

New York traffic violations are handled under the state's traffic code, which is published in the New York statutes via Justia. The code gives the court room to set the fine for a given violation.

That is why two drivers with similar tickets can see different amounts. The court on the citation sets the figure, and it can differ from what a neighboring court sets.

The fine can also depend on the facts of the stop. Whether anyone was hurt, or whether the charge was reduced, can change how a case is handled.

  • Read the citation for the violation charged and the court named.
  • Check the court's notice or website for the amount due.
  • Ask the court clerk if the amount is unclear.

What gets added on top of the fine?

New York adds costs that are not part of the court fine. The $88–$93 mandatory surcharge and the DMV's Driver Responsibility Assessment come on top of the fine (New York State Department of Motor Vehicles).

The Driver Responsibility Assessment is a separate charge from the DMV. It starts at $300+ and is triggered by 6 or more points on your record.

So the amount you pay can be well above the court fine alone. When you budget for a ticket, add the surcharge, and check whether the assessment could apply.

Court fine + mandatory surcharge ($88–$93) + possible Driver Responsibility Assessment ($300+) = your real cost, before insurance.

How many points does a failure to yield ticket carry in New York?

New York's points system, as published, runs on tiers: 3 points (1–10 over), 4 points (11–20), 6 points (21–30), 8 points (31–40) and 11 points (41+). Those tiers are written as miles-per-hour-over bands, so they describe speeding.

We do not state a specific point value for failure to yield, because that figure is not in our locked data. Check the DMV and your citation for the points tied to your exact charge (New York State Department of Motor Vehicles).

Two thresholds matter for any moving violation:

  • 11 points in 18 months leads to a license suspension.
  • 6 or more points also triggers the separate Driver Responsibility Assessment of $300+.

Points from different tickets can add up, so a failure to yield conviction can matter more if you already have points on your record.

Does a failure to yield ticket raise insurance in New York?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a New York quote.

Your own change depends on your insurer, your driving record and whether the ticket ends in a conviction. We cannot predict your rate, and we do not recommend any insurer.

If the violation was a crash-related failure to yield, an insurer may weigh it differently from a ticket with no crash. Ask your insurer how it treats violations on your policy.

The national figure is useful for budgeting. A 20% increase applied to a full year of premiums can exceed the ticket itself.

Can a failure to yield ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but we cannot tell you whether it will happen in your case. Outcomes depend on the facts, the court and the charge.

The citation will explain how to respond, and a court may offer ways to plead or contest it. Because a conviction can add points, and 6+ points can bring the $300+ assessment, the way a case ends can matter as much as the fine.

Common questions to settle before you respond:

  • What exactly is the charge, and what does the court list as the amount?
  • Would a conviction put you near 6 points or the 11-point suspension threshold?
  • Do you have a way to contest the facts, such as photos or a witness?

If you want advice on strategy, a licensed attorney of your choosing can give it. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice.

What happens if I don't pay?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. The court can treat a missed response or payment as a further problem, and the DMV can act on your license.

The costs that sit on top of the fine do not go away either. The $88–$93 surcharge and any Driver Responsibility Assessment of $300+ are still owed if they apply.

If you cannot pay the amount on the citation, contact the court before the due date it lists and ask what is available. Courts differ in what they offer, so ask the one on your citation.

Read every date and instruction on the citation. The court on the ticket is the authority on what you owe and when.

Frequently asked questions

How much is a failure to yield ticket in New York?

Nationally the range is $50–$500 by state. In New York the exact amount is set by the court on the citation, so there is no single fixed price. A mandatory surcharge of $88–$93 is added on top of the court fine, and other costs may apply.

Is there a surcharge on top of a New York traffic fine?

Yes. New York adds a mandatory surcharge of $88–$93 on top of the court fine. The DMV's Driver Responsibility Assessment can also come on top. Both are separate from the fine the court sets, so your total will be higher than the fine alone.

How many points is failure to yield in New York?

Our sourced data gives New York's point tiers as 3, 4, 6, 8 and 11 points, written as speed-over bands, but no specific value for failure to yield. Check your citation and the DMV for the points on your charge. 11 points in 18 months leads to suspension.

What is the Driver Responsibility Assessment in New York?

It is a separate DMV charge of $300+ that is also triggered by 6 or more points. It comes on top of the court fine and the mandatory surcharge. Whether it applies to you depends on the points on your record.

Will a failure to yield ticket raise my insurance?

Possibly. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a New York quote. Your actual change depends on your insurer and driving record, so ask your insurer how it treats the violation.

Can I lose my license for a failure to yield ticket in New York?

A suspension is tied to points: 11 points in 18 months leads to suspension. One ticket may not reach that, but points can add up with others on your record. Check your total points with the DMV.

Who decides the fine for failure to yield in New York?

The court decides. The exact fine is set by the court within the state's traffic code, and the amount appears on your citation or court notice. We do not list one New York figure because the amount comes from the court.
