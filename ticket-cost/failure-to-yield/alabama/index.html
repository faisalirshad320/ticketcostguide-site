How much is a failure to yield ticket in Alabama?

Nationally, failure to yield tickets run $50–$500 depending on the state. In Alabama, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Alabama Law Enforcement Agency (ALEA)).

We do not publish a single Alabama dollar figure, because there is no one number that applies to every driver. The fine depends on the court that hears the case and on the facts written on your ticket.

The $50–$500 range is a national range across states. It is not an Alabama schedule, so do not treat it as the amount you will owe.

The fastest way to learn your own amount is to read the citation itself and then check with the clerk of the court named on it.

How is the fine for failure to yield set in Alabama?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The court that handles your citation decides the amount, so two drivers cited for similar conduct can see different totals.

You can read Alabama's traffic provisions through the state code on Alabama statutes via Justia. The code describes the offense and the range the court works within. It does not replace the amount printed on your ticket.

  • Your citation names the court and usually the appearance or payment instructions.
  • The court clerk can confirm the amount due for your specific charge.
  • Any later change to the charge can change the amount.

Why can court costs be higher than the fine itself?

Municipal and district court costs vary by jurisdiction and often exceed the base fine. That means the number on a payment receipt can be noticeably larger than the fine alone.

When you compare what others paid, ask whether the figure included court costs and surcharges. A neighbor's total from one court may not match yours from another.

Budget for the fine plus court costs. Ask the clerk for the full amount due, not only the base fine.

Because costs differ by court, the same violation can total different amounts in different Alabama jurisdictions.

How many points does a failure to yield ticket add in Alabama?

Alabama's points system is 2 points (25 or less over) or 5 points (more than 25 over). Those tiers are described in terms of speed over the limit, so they do not map neatly onto a right-of-way violation.

We do not state a specific point value for failure to yield. Confirm how points apply to your charge with the court or with Alabama Law Enforcement Agency (ALEA), which handles driver records.

The suspension rule is clearer: 12–14 points in 2 years = 60-day suspension. Points from separate tickets within that window add together, so a prior violation matters.

  • Check your current point total with ALEA before deciding how to respond.
  • Remember the 2-year window when counting earlier tickets.

Does a failure to yield ticket raise insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Alabama-specific one.

Failure to yield is a moving violation, so a conviction can show up on your driving record and may affect what your insurer charges. How much depends on your insurer, your history and the rating rules that apply to you.

We cannot say what any insurer will do. Ask your insurer how it treats a conviction on your record.

The ~20% figure is an average across the US. Your own change could be smaller, larger or none at all.

Can a failure to yield ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Often there are options, but what is available depends on the court and the facts. You can generally pay, contest the charge, or ask the court how a reduction or other resolution works for your case.

Paying the citation usually resolves it, but it can mean a conviction on your record, with the points and insurance effects described above. Contesting it means appearing as the court directs.

  1. Read the citation for the court, the charge and any deadlines it lists.
  2. Contact the clerk to confirm the amount due and the choices the court offers.
  3. Decide whether to pay, contest, or seek advice before the deadline.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice or recommend attorneys. If you want legal advice on your specific case, consult a licensed Alabama attorney of your choosing.

What happens if I don't pay a failure to yield ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes the situation worse. Courts generally treat a missed payment or missed appearance as a separate problem, and added costs may follow.

Because court costs vary by jurisdiction and often exceed the base fine, late or unresolved tickets can grow beyond the amount first listed. The specific consequences depend on the court handling your case.

  • Check the deadline and instructions printed on your citation.
  • Contact the clerk early if you cannot meet the deadline.
  • Keep proof of any payment or appearance.

Unresolved tickets can also affect your license. Ask the court or Alabama Law Enforcement Agency (ALEA) what applies to your record.
