How much is a failure to yield ticket in Mississippi?

Nationally, a failure to yield ticket runs $50–$500 by state. In Mississippi, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Mississippi Department of Public Safety).

We do not publish a single Mississippi dollar figure for this violation. The amount is not a fixed number we can verify for every court, so the citation itself is the most reliable place to look.

Several things can change what you see on that paper or on the court's notice:

  • The court that handles the citation.
  • How the officer wrote up the violation.
  • Whether the stop involved a crash or injury.
  • Whether you have earlier convictions.
  • The mandatory assessments added on top of the fine.

If the citation lists an amount, treat that as the starting point and confirm the total with the court clerk before paying.

How is the fine for failure to yield set in Mississippi?

The fine is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The officer who writes the ticket does not choose the final number in most cases; the court does.

Mississippi statutes are available through Mississippi statutes via Justia. The traffic code is where right-of-way rules and the range of penalties for violating them are found.

Because the amount is court-set, two drivers cited for the same thing can see different totals. The court, the circumstances and the driver's record can all play a part.

Check your citation or the court's notice for the amount due and the deadline. If the paperwork does not show a total, call the clerk of the court listed on it.

Why can the total be higher than the fine?

Mandatory state assessments are added to every fine and often exceed it. That means the number you pay can be noticeably larger than the fine line on the citation.

These assessments are separate from the fine itself. A court cannot usually waive them just because the fine is low.

When you compare costs, look at the full amount due, not only the fine. Ask the clerk for the total including all assessments before you decide whether to pay or contest.

The national $50–$500 range describes typical fines by state. It should not be read as the all-in price in Mississippi, because the assessments are added to the fine.

How many points is failure to yield in Mississippi?

None. Mississippi has no point system (Mississippi Department of Public Safety). A conviction does not add points to your license.

That does not mean a conviction is harmless. Suspensions follow repeat convictions and specific offenses, so a record of violations can still affect your driving privileges.

Keep in mind:

  • There is no point total to track or reduce.
  • Your conviction history still matters to the state and to insurers.
  • A single failure to yield is not described in our sources as a suspension offense by itself, but repeat convictions are the trigger the state points to.

For questions about your own driving record, contact the Mississippi Department of Public Safety directly.

Does a failure to yield ticket raise insurance in Mississippi?

It can. Nationally, moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Mississippi-specific one.

Failure to yield is generally treated as a moving violation. Whether your insurer raises your rate, and by how much, depends on the company, your history and the terms of your policy.

The ~20% figure is an average. Your own change could be higher, lower or none at all. Some insurers look only at convictions rather than citations, so how the case ends can matter.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend insurers or predict your rate. To learn how a conviction would affect your premium, ask your insurer or agent.

Can a failure to yield ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome depends on the court and the facts. We cannot promise a result for any case.

The usual options are:

  1. Pay the ticket. This generally resolves the matter, but payment is typically treated as a conviction.
  2. Contest it. You can ask for a hearing and present your side to the court.
  3. Ask the court about other resolutions. Some courts offer alternatives. Availability varies by court, so ask the clerk what applies to your citation.

Because a conviction can raise insurance costs and count toward repeat-offense rules, it is worth weighing the cost of the fine against those effects before paying.

This is general information, not legal advice. For advice on your situation, consider speaking with a licensed Mississippi attorney of your own choosing.

What happens if I don't pay a failure to yield ticket?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. Courts set deadlines, and missing them can lead to added penalties or other action against your license.

Mississippi suspensions follow repeat convictions and specific offenses (Mississippi Department of Public Safety). Because mandatory state assessments are added to the fine, the amount owed can already be higher than you expect before any extra penalties.

If you cannot pay by the deadline or do not understand the notice:

  • Contact the court clerk listed on the citation before the due date.
  • Ask what payment or scheduling options the court offers.
  • Keep a record of any contact and payment.

We cannot say exactly what penalties a specific court applies. The citation and the court are the reliable sources for that.
