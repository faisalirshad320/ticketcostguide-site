By Editorial Team, TicketCostGuide · Last reviewed 2026-10-16

A careless driving ticket commonly costs $100–$500, though the exact amount depends on the state and the court (Governors Highway Safety Association). It is usually a non-criminal, lesser cousin of reckless driving. Points, surcharges and court costs vary by state and county, so check your citation and the court's fine schedule.

How much is a careless driving ticket?

The lead figure for this offense is: Commonly $100–$500; a lesser, usually non-criminal cousin of reckless driving — US careless/negligent driving penalty range (Governors Highway Safety Association). That comes from state statutes and schedules, last reviewed Oct 2026.

Treat that range as a typical band, not a quote for your ticket. The amount you actually owe depends on your state, the court that hears the case, and whatever add-ons that court applies.

Many courts set fines from a published schedule. Others leave the amount to a judge or magistrate within a statutory range. Either way, the final figure varies by county and court, and we do not publish an exact local amount because we cannot verify one for every court.

Here is what to look for on your citation so you know what you are dealing with:

  • The statute or ordinance number that is cited. Careless, negligent, inattentive and improper driving are sometimes separate offenses with different penalties.
  • Whether a fine amount is printed, or whether the ticket says you must appear in court.
  • The name and address of the court, and the deadline to respond.
  • Whether the ticket lists court costs or surcharges separately from the base fine.

The number printed on a ticket is not always the number you pay. Court costs, surcharges and payment-plan fees can be added, and they vary by county and court.

Why do careless driving fines differ so much by state?

There is no single national careless driving law. Each state writes its own traffic code, and each uses its own name for the offense. You may see "careless driving," "negligent driving," "driving without due care," or something similar.

Because the wording differs, two drivers who did almost the same thing can face different charges in different states. One state may treat the conduct as a basic moving violation. Another may place it in a tier above ordinary traffic infractions.

The Governors Highway Safety Association tracks how states approach traffic safety laws, and it is a useful starting point for seeing that this is a state-by-state question (Governors Highway Safety Association). For the exact wording of a statute, state codes are often posted publicly, including through State codes via Justia.

What we can say with confidence is limited to the locked range of commonly $100–$500 and the examples on this page. Anything else, such as surcharges, court costs, license consequences and payment rules, varies by state.

That matters if you were ticketed away from home. The state that issued the ticket sets the fine and the process, even if you live somewhere else.

  • Look up the exact statute on your citation, not just the name of the offense.
  • Read the court's instructions for out-of-state drivers, if any.
  • Ask the clerk's office what the total amount due is, including costs.

How many points does careless driving carry?

Points depend on the state. Some states do not use a point system at all, and others assign points by offense.

One locked example: New Jersey's careless-driving statute, N.J.S.A. 39:4-97, carries 2 points. We state that figure as it is locked and do not extend it to other states.

For every other state, the point value varies by state. Do not assume that a careless driving ticket in your state carries the same points as New Jersey's.

To find the point value for your own ticket, you can:

  1. Find the statute number on your citation.
  2. Read that statute and the state's point schedule, which is usually published by the motor vehicle agency. State codes can often be found through State codes via Justia.
  3. Ask the court clerk whether a conviction or a plea to a lesser charge would be reported to the licensing agency.

Points usually attach to a conviction, not to the ticket itself. What you plead to, or whether the ticket is dismissed, can change what gets reported. That is a question for the court or a licensed attorney in your state.

Also check whether your state has a separate process, such as a driver improvement course, that affects points. Whether that applies to careless driving varies by state and court.

Does a careless driving ticket raise insurance rates?

It can, but we cannot give you a percentage or a dollar amount. Insurers set their own rates, and the effect depends on the company, your driving record, and how the conviction is recorded in your state.

A careless driving conviction is a moving violation in most places, and insurers generally review moving violations when they price a policy. A lesser charge than reckless driving may be viewed differently from a more serious one, but how it is viewed varies by insurer.

Here is what usually decides whether your premium moves:

  • Whether the ticket ends in a conviction, a dismissal, or a plea to a different charge.
  • Whether the violation is reported to the state licensing agency and shows on your driving record.
  • When your policy renews, since many insurers re-check records at renewal.
  • Whether there was an accident or injury connected to the ticket.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend insurers and does not give insurance advice. If you want to know how a specific outcome would affect your policy, ask your insurer or agent directly.

Safety research gives context on why violations that reflect risky driving get attention from regulators and insurers. The Insurance Institute for Highway Safety publishes research on crash risk (IIHS), though its focus is speed rather than careless driving specifically.

Can a careless driving ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes. The options depend on your state, the court, the facts of your stop, and your record. We cannot say how likely any outcome is.

One locked fact is useful here: careless or negligent driving is a common plea-down from reckless charges. In other words, when a driver is charged with reckless driving, a plea to the lesser careless or negligent offense is a frequent outcome. It is a lower-level charge and, as the locked range shows, it commonly carries a fine of $100–$500 (Governors Highway Safety Association).

Common paths people look into include:

  • Contesting the ticket. You plead not guilty and the prosecution has to prove the charge. Courts differ on how this works.
  • Negotiating a lesser charge. Some prosecutors will discuss an amended charge. Whether that is allowed varies by court.
  • Diversion or a driving course. Some courts offer programs that can lead to a dismissal. Availability varies by state and court.
  • Deferred or conditional outcomes. Some courts hold the case open, and it may be dismissed if conditions are met.

None of this is legal advice. If a conviction would put your license, job or record at risk, consider speaking with a licensed attorney in the state where you were ticketed. We do not recommend specific attorneys.

Whatever you choose, respond to the ticket before the deadline. A missed deadline can remove options that were available at the start.

Is careless driving a criminal charge?

Usually it is not. The locked description is that careless driving is a lesser, usually non-criminal cousin of reckless driving. That is a general pattern, not a rule for every state.

The locked facts also say that some states treat it as criminal when injury is involved. If a crash hurt someone, the same careless driving label can lead to a more serious process than a plain traffic ticket.

This is why the facts around your ticket matter as much as the offense name. A careless driving citation with no crash is different from one issued after a collision with an injured person.

If your ticket involves an injury, take these steps:

  1. Read the citation closely for words like "misdemeanor," "criminal," or "mandatory court appearance."
  2. Look up the statute through the state code to see how it classifies the offense. A public source such as State codes via Justia can help.
  3. Consider talking with a licensed attorney in that state before you enter a plea.

We cannot tell you whether your charge is criminal. That depends on the state's statute and the facts. The only locked statement is that some states treat careless driving as criminal when injury is involved.

Reckless driving is the more serious relative. Moving from a reckless charge down to careless or negligent driving is a common plea-down, so the difference between the two can be significant.

What happens if I don't pay a careless driving ticket?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. What exactly happens varies by state and court, so we do not list specific penalties or deadlines here.

In general, courts treat an unanswered ticket as a failure to respond. Possible consequences, depending on the state, can include additional fees, a hold or suspension on a license, or a warrant in some places. We cannot say which apply to you.

If you cannot pay the full amount, you have options worth asking about before the deadline:

  • Ask the clerk whether the court offers a payment plan.
  • Ask whether the court accepts a request for more time.
  • Ask whether a hearing is available to contest the charge or discuss the amount.

Keep records of anything you send or pay, including receipts and confirmation numbers. If the court posts a notice online, save a copy.

Be careful with websites that offer to handle your ticket. Confirm payment instructions on the official court or state agency site, using the court name printed on your citation. TicketCostGuide cannot take payment or file anything for you.

Paying the ticket is usually treated as a guilty plea or a conviction. If you want to contest it or seek a reduction, do that before you pay.

Frequently asked questions

How much is a careless driving ticket?

Careless or negligent driving commonly carries a penalty of $100–$500 across the US, based on state statutes and schedules last reviewed Oct 2026. Your exact amount depends on your state and the court, and court costs or surcharges can be added. Check your citation or ask the court clerk.

Is careless driving the same as reckless driving?

No. Careless driving is generally a lesser, usually non-criminal cousin of reckless driving. Reckless driving is the more serious charge. Naming and penalties vary by state, so read the statute listed on your citation to see how your state defines each offense.

How many points is careless driving in New Jersey?

New Jersey's careless-driving statute, N.J.S.A. 39:4-97, carries 2 points. That figure applies to New Jersey only. Point values in other states vary by state, so check your state's motor vehicle agency and the statute on your ticket.

Is careless driving a misdemeanor or criminal offense?

It is usually non-criminal, but some states treat careless driving as criminal when injury is involved. Whether your charge is criminal varies by state and facts. Look for words like misdemeanor or mandatory appearance on the citation, and consider asking a licensed attorney.

Can a reckless driving charge be reduced to careless driving?

Yes, careless or negligent driving is a common plea-down from reckless charges. Whether that is offered in your case varies by court and prosecutor. This is not legal advice, so consider speaking with a licensed attorney in the state where you were charged.

Will careless driving raise my insurance?

It can, but the effect varies by insurer, state and your record. We do not have a locked figure for the increase. Whether the ticket ends in a conviction, dismissal or lesser charge can matter. Ask your insurer or agent how a specific outcome would be treated.

What if I ignore a careless driving ticket?

Consequences vary by state and court, and ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. Possible results include added fees or license problems. Contact the court clerk before the deadline on your citation to ask about payment plans, extensions or a hearing.
