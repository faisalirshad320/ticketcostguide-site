How much is a red light ticket in Michigan?

The honest short answer is that no single Michigan number applies to every driver. Nationally, red light tickets run $50–$500 depending on the state, and in Michigan the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Michigan Secretary of State).

That means the figure printed on your ticket, or on the notice from the court, is the one that matters. A neighbor's ticket, an online average or a forum post may not match yours.

Key point: $50–$500 is a national range across states. It is not a Michigan fine schedule, and we do not list a Michigan dollar amount because the court sets it.

Part of what you pay is court cost, not just the fine. District court costs vary by court and make up much of the total.

How is a Michigan red light fine actually set?

In Michigan, the exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The statutes are published online, and you can read the Michigan code through Michigan statutes via Justia.

Two things determine the final bill:

  • The fine, which the court sets on the citation within the traffic code.
  • Court costs, which vary by district court and can make up much of what you owe.

Because costs differ by court, the same violation can total different amounts depending on where the ticket is handled. Check the citation or the court's own notice for the amount due and the court that handles it.

We do not publish court-by-court totals, since the figures we can source do not include them.

How many points does a red light ticket add in Michigan?

Michigan's point system is in §257.320a of the state code. The sourced schedule is 2–4 points by mph over. It is written around speed, so it does not map neatly onto a red light violation.

Whether points are assessed for your citation, and how many, depends on the violation as recorded and the conviction that results. We cannot tell you the number for your case.

The Michigan Secretary of State keeps driver records and applies points, so it is the place to confirm what appears on your record (Michigan Secretary of State).

The threshold to know: 12 points in 2 years = driver reexamination.

Points matter most if you already have other violations on your record. Several tickets in a short window add up faster than any single one.

Does a red light ticket raise insurance in Michigan?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Michigan-specific one.

Your own result depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the ticket is resolved. We do not recommend any insurer and cannot predict your rate.

  • The ~20% figure is an average across the country, so some drivers see less and some see more.
  • A conviction is what insurers typically look at, which is why how the case ends matters.
  • Your policy documents or your insurer can tell you how violations are treated at renewal.

Because the premium effect can last beyond the fine itself, the total cost of a ticket is often larger than the amount you pay the court.

Can a Michigan red light ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes cases end differently from the original citation, but we cannot say how likely that is for you. The citation sets out how to respond and which court handles it.

In general, drivers have these paths:

  1. Pay the ticket. This usually resolves the matter but may mean a conviction on your record.
  2. Contest it. You can ask the court for a hearing and present your side.
  3. Ask the court about other options. The clerk can tell you what the court allows, such as payment arrangements.

Paying and contesting can lead to different outcomes for your record, points and insurance. That is why it helps to read the citation closely before you decide.

What happens if I don't pay a red light ticket in Michigan?

Ignoring a ticket rarely makes it go away. Courts generally follow up on unpaid citations, and costs or consequences can grow if a deadline passes.

The specific deadline and penalties are on your citation or court notice. We do not state them here because they are not part of the figures we can source.

  • Find the court named on the citation and confirm the due date.
  • Contact the clerk if you cannot pay on time or are unsure how to respond.
  • Check your driver record with the Michigan Secretary of State if you are worried about status or points.

Missing a response date can turn a manageable ticket into a larger problem, so acting early is usually cheaper.
