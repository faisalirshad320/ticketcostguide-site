How much is a red light ticket in New Jersey?

Nationally, red light fines fall between $50 and $500 depending on the state. In New Jersey, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (New Jersey Motor Vehicle Commission).

We do not publish a single New Jersey dollar figure here, because the amount is decided by the court handling your case. Quoting a number from a forum or an old article would risk being wrong for your court.

The national $50–$500 range is a national figure. It is not a statement of what any New Jersey court will charge.

The total you actually pay can include more than the fine. Court costs may apply, and if your record reaches a certain point level, the MVC bills a separate surcharge. Both are covered below.

Your citation and any court notice are the documents that show the amount due in your case.

How is the red light fine set in New Jersey?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. New Jersey statutes are available through Justia, and the traffic provisions are in that code.

That means the figure is not a flat statewide price printed on a single list. The court that hears your ticket decides the amount within the range the law allows.

What this means in practice:

  • Two drivers cited for the same violation may be told different amounts by different courts.
  • The number on your citation or court notice is the one that matters.
  • Extra costs and MVC surcharges are separate from the base fine.

If you want the underlying legal text, read the traffic code on the statute site and the MVC's own pages. We are not a law firm and cannot tell you how a court will apply the code to your situation.

How many points is a red light ticket in New Jersey?

New Jersey uses a points system. The scale we have sourced is: 2 points for 1–14 over, 4 points for 15–29 over, and 5 points for 30+ over (New Jersey Motor Vehicle Commission).

Those tiers are written in terms of how far over a limit a driver was, so they read most naturally for speeding. We have not sourced a specific point value for a red light charge, so we will not guess one.

To find the points tied to your own charge, check the MVC's point schedule or the violation listed on your citation, or ask the court clerk.

At 12 points, a license is subject to suspension. Reaching 6+ points within 3 years also adds an MVC insurance surcharge.

Points add up across all violations on your record, not only this one. A red light ticket can matter more if you already have other points.

What MVC surcharges can be added to a red light ticket?

Beyond the court fine, New Jersey adds an MVC surcharge once a driver reaches 6+ points within 3 years. The surcharge starts at $150+ (New Jersey Motor Vehicle Commission).

These surcharges are billed annually for 3 years on top of court fines. So the cost of a ticket that pushes you over the threshold is spread across several bills, not paid once.

  • The surcharge is issued by the MVC, not by the court.
  • It applies based on your total points, not on one ticket alone.
  • We only have the starting figure of $150+; the full amount depends on your record.

If you are close to 6 points, ask the court or the MVC how a conviction would affect your total before you decide how to respond to the ticket.

Does a red light ticket raise insurance in New Jersey?

It can. Nationally, a moving-violation conviction typically raises premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a New Jersey rate.

Whether your own insurer raises your rate depends on its rules, your driving history and whether the ticket ends in a conviction. We do not recommend any insurer and cannot predict your quote.

Two separate costs can show up:

  • Your insurer's premium change, if the conviction appears on your record.
  • The MVC surcharge, if you reach 6+ points within 3 years.

The 20% figure is an average, so some drivers see less and some more. Ask your insurer how it treats a moving violation, ideally before you pay or plead.

Paying a ticket is generally treated as accepting the conviction, which is why it helps to understand the insurance effect first.

Can a red light ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but outcomes depend on the facts and on the court. We cannot promise any result, and we do not give legal advice.

In general, a driver cited for a traffic violation usually has these choices:

  • Pay the ticket. This is usually an admission and typically means the points and any surcharge can follow.
  • Contest it in court. You present your side, and the court decides.
  • Ask the court about alternatives. Some courts discuss amended charges. Ask the clerk what your court allows.

Because the New Jersey fine is set by the court, the amount can change depending on how the case is resolved. A reduced charge may also carry different points.

If the stakes are high, such as a license near 12 points, consider speaking with a licensed attorney of your own choosing. TicketCostGuide does not recommend specific attorneys.

What happens if I don't pay a red light ticket in New Jersey?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. Courts can add penalties, and unpaid matters can affect your license status.

We have not sourced the exact penalties or deadlines for New Jersey, so check the dates and instructions on your citation or court notice. The court clerk can confirm what applies to you.

  1. Read the citation for the court, the response instructions and any dates.
  2. Decide whether to pay or contest before the deadline.
  3. Contact the court if you are unsure or need more time.
  4. Keep copies of everything you send or receive.

A suspension can also come from points. At 12 points, a license is subject to suspension, so an unresolved ticket on top of an existing record deserves attention.
