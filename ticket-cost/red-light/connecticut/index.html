How much is a red light ticket in Connecticut?

Nationally, red light tickets cost $50–$500 depending on the state. In Connecticut, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Connecticut Department of Motor Vehicles).

We do not print a single Connecticut dollar figure here, because the amount that applies to you is the one written on your ticket or court notice. A number from a forum or an old article may not match it.

The $50–$500 range is a national range across all states. It is not a Connecticut fine schedule.

Read the citation closely. The payable total is what you would owe if you pay without contesting, and it already includes the state's fees and surcharges.

How is the red light fine set in Connecticut?

The exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. That is why two people in different situations can see different totals on their citations.

You can read the underlying Connecticut statutes through Connecticut statutes via Justia. The statutes describe the offense and the legal framework. The amount you owe is the one on your citation.

A few practical points follow from this:

  • The citation or court notice is the controlling document for your amount.
  • A national average tells you little about what your court will charge.
  • If the notice and a website disagree, go with the notice and confirm with the court listed on it.

Are court costs and surcharges added to the fine?

State fees and surcharges are built into the payable amount on the ticket. In other words, you do not usually add them on top of the printed total.

This matters when comparing figures. A fine quoted by itself can look lower than what you would pay, because the payable amount includes the state's fees and surcharges.

If your citation lists a base fine and separate add-ons, ask the court clerk how the total was calculated. Do not rely on a calculator or third-party estimate for this.

How many points does a red light ticket add in Connecticut?

The sourced Connecticut context says that speeding adds points under the DMV point system, and that 10 points = 30-day suspension. That is the only point threshold we can state with a source.

We do not give a point value for a red light violation, because that value is not in the sourced facts for this page. Check with the Connecticut Department of Motor Vehicles to find out whether and how your specific violation is recorded on your driving record.

The 10-point, 30-day suspension threshold comes from the DMV point system. Whether your red light citation carries points is something to confirm with the DMV or the court.

If you already have other violations on your record, ask the DMV how they interact with any new conviction.

Does a red light ticket raise insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Connecticut-specific one.

Your own change depends on your insurer, your driving history and whether the ticket ends in a conviction. We do not recommend any insurer, and we cannot predict your quote.

Two things are worth knowing:

  • The effect on premiums usually comes from a conviction on your record, not from receiving the ticket alone.
  • Paying a ticket is generally treated as accepting the violation, so consider the insurance effect before you pay.

For a firm answer, ask your insurer how it treats a moving violation at renewal.

Can a red light ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome depends on the facts and on the court. Because the amount is set by the court on the citation, any change would also come through the court.

In general, you have these options:

  1. Pay the ticket. This closes the matter but usually means accepting the violation.
  2. Contest it. Follow the instructions on the citation to request a hearing, and watch the deadlines it lists.
  3. Ask the court what is available. The clerk can explain procedures, though clerks cannot give legal advice.
  4. Speak with a licensed attorney. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not recommend specific attorneys.

Whatever you choose, keep a copy of the citation and any payment or hearing confirmation.

What happens if I don't pay a red light ticket?

Ignoring a citation tends to make things worse. Courts usually have ways to follow up on unpaid or unanswered tickets, which can include added costs or action involving your license.

We cannot state the exact consequences or deadlines for Connecticut without a sourced figure. Your citation should list the date by which you must respond, and the court can confirm what happens after that date.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court before the deadline and ask what options exist. That is usually better than waiting.
