How much is a red light ticket in Indiana?

Nationally, a red light ticket costs $50–$500 depending on the state; in Indiana the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Indiana Bureau of Motor Vehicles). That is the lead figure for this page, and we do not replace it with a guess.

The $50–$500 range is a national spread across states. It is not an Indiana price list, and your Indiana ticket may fall anywhere the court's authority allows.

The most reliable number for your situation is the one printed on your citation or shown by the court handling your case.

The fine is only part of the bill. County court costs are added to the judgment, which we cover below.

How is the fine for running a red light set in Indiana?

In Indiana, the court sets the exact fine for this violation within the state's traffic code. The amount appears on the citation or in the court's record of your case.

Because the court sets the amount, two drivers cited for the same violation in different counties can end up with different totals. The difference usually comes from local court costs rather than the base fine.

To check the rules yourself, the Indiana traffic code is published through Indiana statutes via Justia. We have not listed specific section numbers here because we only cite what we can source directly.

  • Read the citation for the court name and any listed amount.
  • Contact that court's clerk to confirm what you owe.
  • Ask whether the amount shown includes court costs.

Why can the total be higher than the fine?

County court costs are added to the judgment and often exceed the fine for low speeds. That detail comes from our state context for Indiana, and it is a useful warning for any minor moving violation.

In practice, the number you pay is the fine plus costs. If you only look at the fine, you may underestimate the total.

Costs vary by county and by court, so we do not give a single Indiana dollar amount for them. The court clerk is the best source for the exact total on your case.

When comparing options, compare the total owed, not just the fine line on the citation.

How many points does a red light ticket add in Indiana?

Indiana's points system runs 2–8 points by mph over, and accumulating points triggers BMV probation and suspension. The range is tied to speed over the limit, so it is written with speeding in mind.

We do not state a specific point value for a red light conviction because our sourced facts do not give one. Check with the Indiana Bureau of Motor Vehicles for how your conviction is recorded on your driving record.

What matters most is the accumulation. A single ticket may not cause trouble, but several convictions over time can lead to probation and then suspension.

  • Ask the BMV how many points are currently on your record.
  • Find out whether this violation adds points.
  • Treat each new conviction as moving you closer to probation.

Does a red light ticket raise insurance in Indiana?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Indiana-specific quote.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the conviction is recorded. Some drivers see no change and others see more than the average.

We do not recommend any insurer. If you are worried about cost, ask your own insurer how a conviction would affect your renewal before you decide how to handle the ticket.

Because insurance effects can last longer than the ticket itself, the long-term cost can matter more than the fine.

Can a red light ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome depends on the court and the facts of your case. We cannot say whether yours qualifies, and nothing here is legal advice.

In general, a driver cited for a red light has a few paths:

  • Pay the ticket. This usually resolves the case, but it may mean a conviction on your record, with the points and insurance effects described above.
  • Contest it. You can ask the court for a hearing and present your side.
  • Ask the court about alternatives. Some courts have options that differ from county to county. Ask the clerk what is available.

If the stakes are high, such as many points already on your record, consider speaking with a licensed attorney. TicketCostGuide does not recommend a specific attorney.

Read the citation closely for deadlines and instructions. Missing them can limit your choices.

What happens if I don't pay a red light ticket?

Ignoring a ticket rarely makes it go away. The court generally keeps the case open, and the consequences are set by the court and the state.

Our locked facts note that accumulating points triggers BMV probation and suspension. An unresolved or mishandled ticket can add to that risk if it ends in a conviction on your record.

We do not list specific late fees or deadlines because they vary by court and are not part of our sourced figures. Check your citation and call the court clerk for the exact terms.

If you cannot afford the full amount, contact the court before the due date and ask what is available.
