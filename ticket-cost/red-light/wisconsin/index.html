How much is a red light ticket in Wisconsin?

Nationally, red light tickets run $50–$500 depending on the state. In Wisconsin, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Wisconsin Department of Transportation — DMV).

We do not publish a single Wisconsin dollar amount, because the number that matters is the one printed on your ticket or set by the court handling it. The national range is context only. It is not a Wisconsin quote.

On top of the base amount, the statutory surcharge stack is added to every forfeiture. That means the total you owe is higher than the base figure alone.

Your citation, or the court that issued it, is the only place to confirm what you personally owe. Treat any other number you see online, including the national range here, as an estimate.

How is the fine set for running a red light in Wisconsin?

The court sets the amount on the citation, working within the state's traffic code. The code is available through Wisconsin statutes via Justia.

That is why two drivers cited for the same type of violation can see different totals. The base amount, the surcharges and any added costs all feed into the final figure.

If you want to see how the rules are written, read the traffic code provisions on traffic signals in the statutes. Statutes describe the violation and the penalty framework. The court's own paperwork shows the number you actually pay.

  • Base amount: set by the court on the citation.
  • Surcharges: a statutory stack added to every forfeiture.
  • Total due: the combination of the two, as shown on your paperwork.

What surcharges and court costs get added?

The statutory surcharge stack is added to every forfeiture in Wisconsin. This is the main reason the amount you pay can differ from the base figure.

Our locked sources do not list the individual surcharge amounts, so we do not give dollar values for them. We would rather leave them out than guess.

When you review your citation, look for these items:

  • The base amount for the violation.
  • Any line items labeled as surcharges, costs or fees.
  • The total due and the instructions for paying or contesting.

If the total looks different from what you expected, ask the court clerk to explain each line. That is a question about the paperwork, not legal advice.

How many points is a red light ticket in Wisconsin?

The state points schedule we reviewed lists 3 points for 10 or less over, 4 points for 11–19 over, and 6 points for 20+ over. Reaching 12 points in 12 months means suspension.

Those tiers are written by how far over the limit a driver was. A red light violation does not involve speed in the same way, so we do not claim which tier applies to it. We have no locked fact that assigns a specific point value to a red light conviction.

To confirm the points for your situation, check with the Wisconsin DOT or the court handling your case. Start with the Wisconsin Department of Transportation — DMV.

Points add up. If you already have points on your record, even a small addition matters when the suspension threshold is 12 points in 12 months.

Does a red light ticket raise insurance in Wisconsin?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Wisconsin rate.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your driving record and the details of the violation. We do not recommend any insurer and cannot predict what yours will do.

A few practical points:

  • Premium changes are usually tied to a conviction, so how the ticket is resolved can matter.
  • The increase often shows up at renewal rather than right away.
  • Your insurer or agent can tell you how they treat a moving violation.

When weighing the cost, remember the ticket amount is only part of it. A premium increase of around 20% applied over several renewals can exceed the original forfeiture. That is an average from national data, so your own result may be higher or lower.

Can a red light ticket be dismissed or reduced in Wisconsin?

That is decided by the court, not by this guide. The citation explains your options, which usually include paying or contesting it.

We cannot say how likely a dismissal or reduction is. We also cannot advise whether to contest. For that, you may want to speak with a licensed attorney of your own choosing, or ask the court clerk about procedure.

Before deciding, it helps to gather the following:

  1. A copy of the citation and any court notice.
  2. The total due, including the surcharge stack.
  3. Your current point total, if you know it.
  4. Your insurer's policy on moving violations.

Comparing the total cost, the point impact and the possible insurance effect gives you a clearer picture than the fine alone.

What happens if I don't pay a red light ticket?

The citation and court paperwork explain the consequences and deadlines for your case. We do not state specific deadlines or penalties because our locked sources do not include them.

In general, ignoring a ticket tends to make things worse, not better. Unresolved tickets can lead to added costs or action against a license. Confirm the details with the court that issued the citation.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court clerk. Ask what options exist for your case. Do not rely on a general guide to answer that.

Also remember that accumulating points can lead to suspension: 12 points in 12 months = suspension.
