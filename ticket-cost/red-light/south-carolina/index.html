How much is a red light ticket in South Carolina?

Nationally, red light tickets run $50–$500 depending on the state. In South Carolina the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (South Carolina Department of Motor Vehicles).

We do not publish a single South Carolina dollar figure, because the fine is not a fixed number we can verify. The national range is a national range. It tells you how wide the spread is between states, not what you will owe here.

Your citation is the best source for your own cost. It shows the amount the court has set, and the court clerk can confirm what is owed in total once assessments and surcharges are included.

Quick rule: the fine on the ticket is not the whole bill. Statewide assessments and surcharges are added to every fine and usually exceed it.

How is a red light fine set in South Carolina?

The fine for this violation is set by the court, within the state's traffic code. It is not a figure you can look up in a single chart that applies everywhere.

That means the number on your citation is the starting point. The court, not the officer, is the party that sets the amount, so the clerk's office is where you confirm it.

The state's statutes are available through South Carolina statutes via Justia. If you want to read the traffic code that governs signals and penalties, start there and look for the title covering motor vehicles and traffic.

Reading the code will not give you a different price than your citation does. It can help you understand what the violation is called and what the court is working from.

Why is the total more than the fine on my ticket?

Statewide assessments and surcharges are added to every fine, and they usually exceed it. In practice, the amount you pay can be well above the base fine.

This is why two people comparing notes often disagree. One quotes the fine, the other quotes what they actually paid at the window. Both can be right.

Here is how to check the real total:

  • Look for every line item on the citation or the court notice, not just the first number.
  • Ask the clerk for the full amount due, including assessments and surcharges.
  • Confirm the amount again before you pay online or by mail, so you pay the right total.

We do not list an exact dollar amount for the assessments, because the locked facts we rely on do not give one.

How many points is a red light ticket in South Carolina?

South Carolina's points system assigns 2–6 points by mph over. A driver who reaches 12 points faces suspension, and points halve after 12 months (South Carolina Department of Motor Vehicles).

That description is built around speed. A red light violation has no mph-over figure, so we do not state a point value for it. Whether points apply, and how many, is something to confirm with the DMV or the court before you assume anything.

The 12-point threshold is the number to keep in mind if you already have other violations on your record. Points from different tickets add up toward it.

Check your own record with the DMV. A single ticket may not threaten your license, but a ticket on top of earlier ones can matter.

Does a red light ticket raise my insurance?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a South Carolina rate.

The effect on your own policy depends on your insurer, your driving history and whether the ticket ends in a conviction. We do not recommend or compare insurers, and we cannot predict your premium.

Two practical points:

  • A conviction is what usually triggers the increase, so how the case ends matters.
  • The cost of a higher premium can exceed the ticket itself over time, so it belongs in your decision.

To see what applies to you, ask your insurer how it treats a moving-violation conviction. For general information on state traffic rules, the Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a state laws index.

Can a red light ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but outcomes depend on the court and the facts of your case. We cannot promise a result, and we do not give legal advice.

The options usually look like this:

  • Pay the ticket. This is the quickest route, but it generally resolves the matter as a conviction, with the points and insurance effects described above.
  • Contest it. You can ask the court for a hearing and present your side. The court decides.
  • Ask the court about alternatives. Some courts offer other ways to resolve a ticket. Ask the clerk what your court allows.

Because the fine is court-set, the court is also where you ask what is possible. If the stakes feel high, for example because of prior points, you may want to consult a licensed attorney. We do not recommend a specific one.

What happens if I don't pay a red light ticket?

Ignoring a citation tends to make things worse. We do not list specific deadlines or penalty amounts, because those are not in the facts we rely on. The court notice and the citation will give the dates that apply to you.

In general, an unpaid ticket can lead to added costs, further court action or trouble with your license. With 12 points as the suspension threshold, any outcome that adds points deserves attention.

If you cannot pay the full total, contact the court clerk before the date on your notice. Courts generally respond better to someone who calls early than to someone who stays silent.
