How much is a red light ticket in Oregon?

Nationally, red light tickets fall between $50 and $500 depending on the state. In Oregon, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Oregon DMV (ODOT)).

That means the most reliable number is the one printed on your ticket or on the court's notice. We do not give a single Oregon dollar figure because we have no sourced amount that applies to every citation.

The national $50–$500 range is a comparison across states. It is not the Oregon fine, and your citation may fall anywhere relative to it.

If your ticket shows no amount, contact the court listed on it. The court clerk can tell you what is owed and by when.

How is the fine for running a red light set in Oregon?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The Oregon statutes are published online through Justia (Oregon statutes via Justia).

In practice, that means two drivers cited for the same offense can see different paperwork if the circumstances differ. The citation is the document that controls what you are asked to pay.

  • Read the amount and the court name on the citation.
  • Note the due date and any hearing or response instructions.
  • Check whether the notice describes added enhancements.

For general background on how states treat traffic laws, the Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a state-by-state reference (Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws).

Can zone doubling or other enhancements add to the cost?

Zone doubling and the 100+ mph enhancements are set directly in Oregon statute. They are written into the law itself rather than left to a court's general discretion.

The 100+ mph enhancement concerns speed, so it does not apply to an ordinary red light citation. Zone doubling depends on where the violation happened and on the statute's wording.

If your citation mentions a zone or an enhancement, ask the court which statute it relies on. Then compare that text with the version on Justia (Oregon statutes via Justia).

We do not list court costs or surcharge dollar amounts for Oregon. None are in our sourced figures, so check the citation for any added charges.

How many points does a red light ticket carry in Oregon?

Oregon has no point system. A red light ticket is not assigned a point value that builds toward a threshold.

License suspensions in Oregon instead follow repeat convictions, such as under habitual-offender rules, and specific offenses like driving 100+ mph.

This does not mean a conviction is harmless. A record of repeated moving violations can still matter for your license status, so keep track of your own history.

For your current license status and driving record, the Oregon DMV is the official source (Oregon DMV (ODOT)).

Does a red light ticket raise insurance in Oregon?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Oregon-specific one.

Your own change could be higher, lower or nil. It depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the ticket is recorded.

  • Ask your insurer how it treats a moving-violation conviction on your policy.
  • Ask the court whether paying the ticket counts as a conviction.
  • Compare your renewal quote with and without the violation if you can.

We do not recommend any insurer. The 20% figure is only a rough benchmark for planning.

Can a red light ticket be dismissed or reduced in Oregon?

Sometimes. The options usually include paying, contesting the citation, or asking the court about alternatives. What is available depends on the court and on your citation.

We do not have sourced detail on specific Oregon programs, so we do not list them. The citation should explain how to respond and by when.

  1. Read the citation and note the court and its response instructions.
  2. Decide whether to pay or contest before the deadline.
  3. If you contest, gather what you have, such as photos, notes on signal timing and the location.
  4. Ask the court clerk about any alternatives it offers.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot give legal advice. If the stakes feel high, you may want to speak with a licensed attorney of your own choosing.

What happens if I don't pay a red light ticket in Oregon?

Ignoring a citation tends to make things worse. Courts generally treat a missed deadline as a separate problem. It can lead to added consequences, including license action.

We have no sourced Oregon figures for late fees, so we do not state any. The court or the Oregon DMV can tell you what applies to your case (Oregon DMV (ODOT)).

If you cannot pay by the due date, contact the court before the deadline passes. Courts often have a process for requesting more time or other arrangements.

Keep copies of anything you send, and note the date and the name of any clerk you speak with.
