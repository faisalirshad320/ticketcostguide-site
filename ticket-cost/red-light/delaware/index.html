How much is a red light ticket in Delaware?

Nationally, a red light ticket costs $50–$500 depending on the state. In Delaware, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Delaware Division of Motor Vehicles).

We do not publish a single Delaware dollar figure, because the amount that applies to you is the one printed on your citation or stated by the court. The national range is only a reference point. It is not a Delaware fine schedule.

Bottom line: the number on your citation, plus any court costs and state fees, is what you owe. Nothing on this page replaces it.

If your citation lists no amount, the court handling the case can tell you what applies. Look for the court name and contact details on the ticket.

How is a Delaware red light fine set?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The code gives the court a framework, and the court assigns the amount on your citation. You can read Delaware's statutes through Delaware statutes via Justia.

Court costs and state fees are added to the statutory fine. That means the amount you actually pay can be higher than the base fine alone.

When you compare your ticket to anything you read online, check what is included:

  • Is the figure only the base fine?
  • Does it include court costs and state fees?
  • Is it a national average or a Delaware amount?

Only the court's figure for your case counts.

How many points does a red light ticket add in Delaware?

Delaware's points system runs 2–5 points by mph over. Twelve points in 2 years means a behavior modification course, and 14+ points means a suspension (Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws).

The "mph over" scale describes speed-related violations. A red light violation has no speed measurement, so do not assume a specific point value for it from that scale.

To find out whether your violation carries points, and how many, check these places:

  • The citation itself.
  • The court handling your case.
  • The Delaware Division of Motor Vehicles, which keeps driver records.

The thresholds matter if you already have points. Points from several violations add up toward the 12-point and 14-point marks.

Does it matter if the ticket came from a camera or an officer?

It can. A ticket from an officer at the scene and a ticket mailed after a camera capture are not always handled the same way. Penalties, who is held responsible, and whether the violation reaches your driving record can differ.

We do not list those differences here, because our sources give no figures for them. Read the citation closely. It should say how the violation was recorded and what the next step is.

If the wording is unclear, ask the court named on the ticket. Court staff can explain the process, though they cannot give you legal advice.

Does a red light ticket raise insurance in Delaware?

It may. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Delaware-specific one.

Your own change depends on your insurer, your driving history, and whether the violation ends up as a conviction on your record. We cannot predict your rate and do not recommend any insurer.

Some practical points:

  • The effect often shows up at your next renewal, not right away.
  • A conviction usually matters more than a ticket that is dismissed.
  • Your insurer or a licensed insurance agent can say how your policy treats a violation like this.

Can a red light ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but it depends on the facts and on the court. The citation will explain your choices and any deadline. In general, drivers have these options:

  • Pay the amount on the citation. This usually resolves the case but may mean accepting the violation.
  • Contest the ticket in court. You can present your side to the court.
  • Contact the court with questions. Staff can explain procedures and deadlines.

Think about the cost beyond the fine. Possible points and a possible insurance increase can matter more than the ticket amount.

For advice on your specific case, consult a licensed attorney. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not recommend attorneys.

What happens if I don't pay a red light ticket?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. Unpaid or unanswered citations can lead to added penalties and can affect your driving privileges. The exact consequences depend on the court and the type of citation.

Do not wait until the deadline passes. If you cannot pay or need more time, contact the court named on your citation and ask what it allows.

Missing a deadline can turn a single violation into a larger problem. Check the date on your citation now.
