How much is a red light ticket in Florida?

Nationally, red light tickets run $50–$500 depending on the state. In Florida, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Florida Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles).

We do not publish a single Florida figure for a court-issued citation, because the amount depends on the citation and the court. The number printed on your ticket or on the court's notice is the one that counts.

The one fixed Florida figure we can state is the statutory red-light amount of $158 when it is paid as a camera notice. Court costs and fees can be added to other kinds of citations.

Read the citation itself. The amount due, the court and the payment instructions are all printed there.

How is the fine set: camera notice or officer citation?

There are two common ways a red light violation reaches a driver. One is a notice generated from a camera. The other is a citation written by an officer who saw the violation.

For a camera notice, Florida's statutory red-light amount is $158 when it is paid as a camera notice. That is the only Florida dollar amount we state for this violation.

For a citation, the exact fine is set by the court within the state's traffic code. Two drivers with the same violation can see different totals depending on the county and what the court adds.

  • Camera notice: $158 when paid as a camera notice.
  • Court citation: amount set by the court on the citation.
  • Extras: county courts add costs and fees on top of the statutory penalty.

You can read the statutes themselves through Florida statutes via Justia. Statutes are updated, so confirm against the official version.

Why does the total change by county?

County courts add costs and fees on top of the statutory penalty. That is why a neighbor's total in another county may not match yours, even for the same violation.

We do not list county-by-county amounts. We could not source them to a statute or schedule, and an invented number would be worse than none.

Special zones can change the math. School and work-zone amounts are doubled by statute. If your violation happened in one of those zones, expect a higher figure than in an ordinary intersection.

Rule of thumb: the statutory amount is the base, and county costs and fees come on top. Your citation shows the full total.

How many points does a red light ticket carry in Florida?

Florida's point system, as we have it sourced, is 3 points (15 or less over) or 4 points (16+ over). Those tiers are written by speed band.

Because those tiers are speed-based, they do not map neatly onto a red light violation. We do not claim a specific point value for a red light citation. Check your citation and your driving record with FLHSMV to see what, if anything, was assessed.

The threshold that matters most is this: 12 points in 12 months = 30-day suspension. Points from several violations add together.

  • 3 points: 15 or less over.
  • 4 points: 16+ over.
  • 12 points in 12 months: 30-day suspension.

If you already carry points from other tickets, a new one can matter more than its own size suggests.

Does a red light ticket raise insurance in Florida?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Florida-specific one.

Your own change could be higher, lower or nothing. It depends on your insurer, your record and whether the ticket becomes a conviction on your record. We do not recommend any insurer and cannot predict your rate.

Over time, a premium increase can cost more than the fine. When comparing options, weigh the fine against the possible rate effect.

The 20% figure is a national average for moving-violation convictions. Ask your insurer how it treats this type of violation.

Can a red light ticket be dismissed or reduced?

That is a question for the court that issued your citation. We cannot give legal advice, and we do not recommend attorneys.

In general, a driver who receives a citation has a few paths. Which ones are open depends on the type of notice and the court's rules.

  • Pay it: this usually resolves the matter, but it may count as a conviction. That can bring points and a possible insurance effect.
  • Contest it: you may be able to ask the court for a hearing. The citation or the court's notice should explain how.
  • Ask about other options: some courts offer alternatives. Ask the clerk what is available for your citation.

A camera notice and an officer-issued citation may be handled differently. Read which one you hold before deciding.

For legal questions, a licensed attorney is the right source. This guide only lays out the cost figures.

What happens if I don't pay a red light ticket?

Ignoring a ticket rarely makes it go away. The amount due and the deadline are on your citation or notice, so check those first.

Unpaid tickets can lead to added costs and problems with your driver license. Court costs and fees are added on top of the statutory penalty, and a late balance can grow beyond the original figure.

Points also matter if a conviction results. 12 points in 12 months = 30-day suspension, so a new violation on top of an existing record can have effects beyond the fine.

If you cannot pay by the deadline, contact the court clerk listed on your notice before the deadline passes. The clerk can tell you what is possible.
