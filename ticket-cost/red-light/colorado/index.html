How much is a red light ticket in Colorado?

The amount printed on your citation is the number that matters. Nationally, red light tickets run $50–$500 depending on the state, but in Colorado the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Colorado Division of Motor Vehicles).

We do not publish a single Colorado dollar figure, because the court sets it within the state's traffic code and it can differ from one court to another. The national range is a rough frame of reference only.

The $50–$500 range is a national figure across all states. It is not a Colorado fine schedule.

On top of the fine, a statutory surcharge is added to each penalty. That means the amount you pay can be higher than the fine line alone, so look for the total due on the citation or the court's payment notice.

How is the fine for running a red light set in Colorado?

In Colorado, the fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The court and the citation decide the amount, not a general website or a national average.

The state traffic code is available through Colorado statutes via Justia. It sets the framework courts work within, and the citation shows the amount that applies to your case.

  • The citation: lists the violation and the amount or the court information you need.
  • The court: sets the exact amount within the traffic code.
  • Surcharges: a statutory surcharge is added to each penalty.
  • Work zones: doubled fines apply in work zones.

If your citation lists a court date instead of a fixed amount, contact the court clerk listed on the paperwork to confirm what you owe.

What surcharges and extra costs get added?

Colorado adds a statutory surcharge to each penalty. The surcharge is separate from the fine, so your total cost is the fine plus the surcharge and any court costs the court applies.

We do not list a surcharge dollar amount here, because it is not part of the figures we have confirmed. The court's notice or payment page should show the full amount.

Doubled fines apply in work zones. If your red light violation happened in a work zone, the fine on your citation may be higher than for a similar ticket elsewhere.

Before paying, compare the total due with the fine on the citation. If the two do not match, ask the court clerk what the difference covers.

How many points does a red light ticket add in Colorado?

Colorado uses a points system in which violations carry 1–6 points by mph over. An adult driver who reaches 12 points in 12 months faces a license suspension.

The mph-based scale is written around speed. A red light violation may not be scored the same way, so we do not state a specific point value for it. The Colorado Division of Motor Vehicles is the place to confirm how a particular violation is recorded on your driving record.

  • Points depend on the violation and the outcome of the case.
  • The 12-in-12 threshold counts all violations together, not just this ticket.
  • Check your record with the DMV if you have had other recent tickets.

If you already have points from other violations, a new ticket matters more than it would for a driver with a clean record.

Does a red light ticket raise insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Colorado-specific one, and your own change could be higher, lower or zero.

What your insurer does depends on its own rules, your history and how the case ends. We cannot predict that, and we do not recommend any insurer.

  • A conviction is more likely to affect a premium than a case that ends without one.
  • Insurers often review your record at renewal, so any change may not show up right away.
  • Your insurer or agent can tell you how it treats a moving violation.

Over time, the insurance effect can add up to more than the fine itself, which is worth weighing when you decide how to respond to the citation.

Can a red light ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome is up to the court. Because the court sets the amount on the citation, any reduction or dismissal also goes through the court.

Your options generally include paying the ticket, contesting it, or contacting the court to ask what choices are available. Paying usually resolves the case but may mean accepting the violation.

  • Pay: ends the case, but a conviction may follow.
  • Contest: you ask the court to hear the case.
  • Ask the clerk: the court can explain deadlines and procedures for your citation.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot give legal advice. For advice on your specific case, you would need to speak with a licensed attorney of your choosing.

What happens if I don't pay a red light ticket in Colorado?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. The court can add penalties, and unresolved cases can lead to action against your license or other consequences.

Colorado's points system also matters here: an adult who reaches 12 points in 12 months faces a suspension. Which consequences apply to you depends on your citation and your court.

The safest step is to read the citation for deadlines and contact the court listed on it. The court clerk can confirm what you owe and by when. For license questions, see the Colorado Division of Motor Vehicles.

Do not assume the amount has stayed the same if you miss a deadline. Ask the court for the current total.
