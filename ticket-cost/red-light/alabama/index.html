How much is a red light ticket in Alabama?

Nationally $50–$500 depending on the state; in Alabama the exact amount is set by the court on the citation — Red Light Ticket cost in Alabama (Alabama Law Enforcement Agency (ALEA), last reviewed Oct 2026).

We do not publish a single Alabama dollar figure because the amount is not one fixed number. The court that handles your citation decides it within the state's traffic code.

The $50–$500 range is a national range across states. It is not a quote for Alabama, and your total may fall inside or outside it once court costs are added.

The most reliable number is the one printed on your citation or shown by the court that issued it.

How is the fine and the total cost set?

In Alabama, the court sets the fine for this violation within the state's traffic code. Two people cited for the same violation in different places can be told different totals.

Municipal and district court costs vary by jurisdiction and often exceed the base fine. That means the amount you pay can be well above the fine itself.

When you look at your citation, check for these items:

  • The court name and whether it is a municipal or district court
  • The fine amount, if one is printed
  • Any court costs or surcharges listed separately
  • The payment or appearance deadline

If the total is unclear, the court clerk can tell you what is owed. The Alabama traffic code is available through Alabama statutes via Justia.

How many points does a red light ticket carry in Alabama?

Alabama's points system assigns 2 points (25 or less over) or 5 points (more than 25 over). Those tiers are written in terms of how far over a limit a driver was.

Because of that wording, the tiers do not obviously map onto a red light violation. We do not state a specific point value for a red light ticket. Check how your citation is classified, or ask ALEA or the court, before assuming a number.

The suspension rule is clear: 12–14 points in 2 years = 60-day suspension. A single ticket is unlikely to reach that on its own, but points from other violations in the same 2-year window add up.

Points are tracked separately from the fine. Paying the fine does not remove points that come with a conviction.

Does a red light ticket raise insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums ~20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Alabama-specific or insurer-specific one.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your driving record and how the violation is recorded. Some drivers see no change and others see more than the average.

We do not recommend any insurer. If you want to know the effect on your policy, you can ask your own insurer how it treats a moving-violation conviction.

It is also worth remembering that a premium increase can last longer than the fine itself, so the insurance cost may matter more than the ticket amount.

Can a red light ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Whether a ticket can be dismissed or reduced is decided by the court handling it. We cannot predict the outcome, and this page is not legal advice.

In general, a driver cited in Alabama has these paths:

  1. Pay the citation. This usually resolves the matter but may result in a conviction and any points that go with it.
  2. Contest it. You can ask the court how to plead and how to schedule a hearing, using the instructions on the citation.
  3. Ask the clerk about other options. Courts differ on what they offer, so ask what applies in your jurisdiction.

If you want legal advice, a licensed Alabama attorney is the right person to ask. We do not recommend a specific attorney.

Keep the court's deadline in mind. Choosing an option after the deadline can be harder or impossible.

What happens if I don't pay or respond?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. The court that issued it sets the deadline and the consequences for missing it.

We do not list specific late fees or penalties because they are not part of our sourced figures and they vary by court. The citation and the clerk are the best places to confirm them.

There is also a points angle. If a conviction adds points and your record reaches 12–14 points in 2 years, the result is a 60-day suspension.

If you cannot pay the full amount by the deadline, contact the court before the deadline passes and ask what it allows.
