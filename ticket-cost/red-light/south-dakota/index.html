How much is a red light ticket in South Dakota?

Nationally, red light tickets cost $50–$500 depending on the state. In South Dakota, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (South Dakota Department of Public Safety).

We do not list a single South Dakota dollar figure here, because the amount that applies to you is the one printed on your ticket or set by the court handling your case. The national range is shown only as context.

Read your citation first. The amount, the court, and any instructions on it are what actually govern your ticket.

Court costs are included in the standard scheduled amounts. That means the scheduled figure on a ticket is generally meant to be the full amount, rather than a base fine with separate add-ons stacked on top.

How is a red light fine set in South Dakota?

The exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The ticket is not a fixed number we can quote from a single statewide table.

In practice, that means the amount depends on the court that handles your citation. It can vary by county and court, which is why two drivers cited for the same thing may see different paperwork.

  • Start with the citation. It shows the amount or tells you how to find it.
  • Check the court named on it. That court is the one that sets the amount.
  • Compare with the traffic code. The state's statutes are available through South Dakota statutes via Justia.

If the citation does not show a clear amount, the court clerk is the right place to ask. TicketCostGuide cannot tell you what a specific court will charge.

How many points does a red light ticket add in South Dakota?

South Dakota assigns points per conviction toward suspension thresholds. The locked threshold is 15 points in 12 months.

The figures we have verified do not give a per-violation point value for running a red light, so we do not state one. Points are tied to a conviction, not just to receiving a ticket.

To find the point value for your situation, check with the South Dakota Department of Public Safety, which administers driver licensing, or review the traffic code on Justia.

Key point: the suspension threshold is 15 points in 12 months, and points are counted per conviction.

If you already have other moving violations on your record, a new conviction matters more. Keep track of the dates of each conviction, since the threshold is measured over a 12-month window.

Does a red light ticket raise insurance in South Dakota?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a South Dakota-specific one.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the violation is recorded. Some drivers see no change, and others see more than the average.

  • A conviction is more likely to affect a premium than a ticket that does not result in one.
  • Rates are usually reviewed at renewal, so a change may not show up right away.
  • Only your insurer can tell you how it treats a specific violation.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend insurers. If you are worried about your premium, ask your own insurer how a conviction would be treated before you decide how to respond to the ticket.

Can a red light ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome is decided by the court, not by a guide like this one. We do not have sourced figures on how often tickets are dismissed or reduced in South Dakota, so we will not suggest odds.

The options generally open to a driver are:

  1. Pay the ticket. This usually resolves the case, but it typically means a conviction, which is what points and insurance changes are tied to.
  2. Contest the ticket. You ask the court to hear the case.
  3. Ask the court about alternatives. Some courts may offer other resolutions. Ask the clerk what is available in your case.

This is general information, not legal advice. If the stakes feel high, such as a record that is already close to the 15-point threshold, you may want to speak with a licensed attorney of your own choosing.

What happens if I don't pay a red light ticket?

Ignoring a ticket rarely makes it go away. Courts typically follow up on unpaid citations, and the problem can grow beyond the original amount.

Because the amount and deadlines are set by the court on your citation, the safest step is to read the instructions printed on it. If you cannot find a deadline or are unsure how to respond, contact the court named on the ticket.

If you cannot pay on time, ask the court clerk about your options before the deadline passes, not after.

We do not have verified figures on late fees or license consequences for this violation in South Dakota, so we do not state any. For license questions, the South Dakota Department of Public Safety is the official source.

What should I do after getting a red light ticket?

A few practical steps can keep a small problem small.

  1. Read the citation. Note the amount, the court, and the response instructions.
  2. Decide how to respond. Pay, contest, or ask the court about other options.
  3. Check your record. Know how many points you may already have, given the 15-points-in-12-months threshold.
  4. Think about insurance. A conviction can raise premiums by about 20% on average nationally, so consider that before paying.

For a wider view of how states handle this, the Governors Highway Safety Association state laws page is a useful reference.
