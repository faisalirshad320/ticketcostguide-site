How much is a red light ticket in Illinois?

Nationally, a red light ticket runs $50–$500 depending on the state. In Illinois, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Illinois Secretary of State). This guide does not give a single Illinois dollar figure, because the amount depends on the court and the citation you received.

The national range is a wide band. A ticket in one state can cost a fraction of one in another, and the number printed on your paper citation or court notice is the one that counts.

Bottom line: the national range is $50–$500. Your Illinois amount is whatever the court sets on your citation. We do not estimate it.

Remember that the fine is only one part of the cost. Points on a driving record and a possible insurance increase can add to the total over time.

How is a red light fine set in Illinois?

The exact fine for this violation in Illinois is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The Illinois statutes are available through Illinois statutes via Justia, if you want to read the code yourself.

That means the figure is not a flat statewide price you can look up in one place. The court handling your case sets it, and the citation or court notice shows it.

Illinois does have statutory minimums for some violations. For example, work-zone speeding carries a statutory minimum fine of $375 for a first offense. That is a different violation, and we are not saying it applies to a red light ticket. It shows that the traffic code can set floors for some offenses.

Where to look for your own number:

  • The citation itself, which lists the violation and court information.
  • Any notice you receive from the court.
  • The court clerk, who can tell you what your case involves.

How many points is a red light ticket in Illinois?

The locked state facts describe the system, not a specific red light value. Convictions carry 5–50 point values on the SOS scale, and repeat convictions within 12 months trigger suspension (Illinois Secretary of State).

We do not state an exact point count for a red light conviction. The Secretary of State is the authority for how points are assigned, so confirm there before assuming a number.

Two details are worth understanding:

  • Points attach to convictions. The scale refers to convictions, so the outcome of your case matters.
  • Timing matters. Repeat convictions within 12 months trigger suspension.

If you already have a recent conviction, a new one could matter more than the fine itself. Check your record with the Secretary of State before deciding how to respond.

Does a red light ticket raise insurance in Illinois?

It can. Nationally, moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Illinois rate.

Your own change could be higher, lower or nothing. It depends on your insurer, your driving record and how the ticket is resolved. TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer and cannot predict your quote.

A simple way to think about the cost:

  1. Start with the fine set by the court on your citation.
  2. Add any court costs or surcharges the court notice lists.
  3. Consider a possible premium increase, using ~20% as a rough national reference only.

Because the insurance effect usually lasts longer than the fine, it can end up being the larger part of the cost. Ask your insurer directly how a conviction would be treated.

Can a red light ticket be dismissed or reduced in Illinois?

The locked facts do not list specific dismissal or reduction programs for Illinois, so we will not name any. What we can say is that the amount is set by the court, so the court process is where any change would happen.

General options people usually weigh include:

  • Paying the ticket. This is usually the fastest route, but a conviction can carry points and may affect insurance.
  • Contesting the ticket. This means responding to the court by the deadline on your citation.
  • Asking the court what is available. The clerk can explain the procedures for your case.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice. For advice on your situation, speak to a licensed attorney of your own choosing.

Before choosing, remember the points rule: repeat convictions within 12 months trigger suspension. Paying a ticket is typically treated as a conviction, so consider your record first.

What happens if I don't pay a red light ticket in Illinois?

The locked facts do not list penalties for nonpayment, so we will not quote any amounts or deadlines. Ignoring a ticket generally makes things harder, not easier, because courts typically add consequences when a case is not resolved.

What to do instead:

  • Find the response deadline on your citation or court notice.
  • Contact the court clerk if you are unsure of the amount or the date.
  • Check your driving record with the Illinois Secretary of State if you are worried about points or suspension.

Because the Illinois fine is set by the court on the citation, the court is also the right place to ask about the status of an unpaid ticket.
