How much is a red light ticket in Wyoming?

Nationally, a red light ticket runs $50–$500 depending on the state (Wyoming Department of Transportation). That is a national range, not a Wyoming price. In Wyoming, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation.

The number you owe is the one printed on your citation or shown by the court that handles your case. We do not publish an exact Wyoming dollar figure. The amount is set by the court within the state's traffic code, and a guess could mislead you.

Court fees are added per the local schedule. The total you pay can therefore be higher than the base fine alone.

When you read your citation, look for these items:

  • The fine amount, if one is listed
  • Any court fees or surcharges
  • The court name and how to reach it
  • Instructions for paying or contesting
  • Any date you are required to act by or appear

The national $50–$500 range is context only. Your own citation and the court that issued it control what you actually owe.

How is the fine for running a red light set in Wyoming?

In Wyoming, the exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. It is not a single number we can give you for every driver and every court.

Because the court sets the amount on the citation, two drivers cited for the same offense in different places could see different totals. Court fees are added per the local schedule, and those schedules are local.

The state's traffic code is the legal framework. You can read Wyoming statutes through Wyoming statutes via Justia. Statute text can be technical, and a clerk of the court can tell you how it applies to your citation.

Two practical points follow from this:

  • Treat the citation as the source of truth for your amount.
  • If the amount is unclear, ask the court clerk what the total is, including fees, before you pay.

We are not a law firm and cannot tell you what a court will do in your case.

How many points does a red light ticket add in Wyoming?

Wyoming has no demerit-point system. A red light conviction therefore does not add a set number of points to your license the way it would in a point-based state.

That does not mean a ticket has no license consequences. Suspensions follow repeat convictions and court action. A single ticket is one thing, but a pattern of convictions can lead to a suspension.

If you have moved from another state, do not assume the old rules apply. Many states do use points, and some track out-of-state convictions. The Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a reference page of state traffic laws at GHSA State Laws, which is useful for comparing how states approach traffic enforcement.

No points does not mean no consequences. Repeat convictions and court action are what can lead to a suspension in Wyoming.

To see your own driving record status, contact the state agency that handles driver records. The Wyoming Department of Transportation is the starting point.

Does a red light ticket raise insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Wyoming-specific one.

The real effect on your premium depends on your insurer, your driving history, and how the ticket is resolved. Some insurers weigh a conviction heavily, and others weigh it less. We cannot predict what yours will do, and we do not recommend any insurer.

A few things that commonly matter:

  • Whether the ticket ends in a conviction on your record
  • Your prior tickets or claims
  • When your policy next renews
  • The insurer's own rating rules

The national figure is best used to judge the stakes. A fine you pay once can be smaller over time than a premium increase that lasts across renewals. That comparison is a reason to understand your options before you simply pay.

For a specific answer, ask your insurer how it treats a red light conviction.

Can a red light ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes outcomes other than paying in full are available, but that depends on the court and the facts of your case. We cannot say what a Wyoming court will allow or promise any result.

Your broad options are usually these:

  1. Pay the citation. This is typically treated as accepting the violation. Check with the court what that means for your record.
  2. Contest it. You can ask the court how to dispute the citation and what the process is.
  3. Ask the court what else is offered. Some courts have programs or procedures. The clerk can tell you whether any apply.

Read the citation closely before deciding. Check that the details, such as the location, date and vehicle, are correct. Errors on a citation are worth raising with the court.

This page is general information, not legal advice. If you want advice on your case, you can speak to a licensed attorney of your own choosing.

Weigh the cost of fighting against the possible insurance effect described above.

What happens if I don't pay a red light ticket in Wyoming?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. The details depend on the court, so we do not state specific deadlines or penalty amounts here.

Wyoming has no point system, but suspensions follow repeat convictions and court action. An unpaid or ignored citation can lead to court action, and that is the route to the license problems drivers want to avoid.

Court fees are added per the local schedule, so delays can leave you facing a more complicated bill than the one you started with.

If you cannot pay right away or you are unsure about the deadline, take these steps:

  • Find the court's contact information on your citation.
  • Call or write the clerk and ask about your due date and options.
  • Keep a record of what you were told and when.
  • Act before any date printed on the citation passes.

Courts often prefer that you contact them early rather than say nothing.
