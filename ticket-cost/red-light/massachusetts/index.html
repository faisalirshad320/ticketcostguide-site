How much is a red light ticket in Massachusetts?

The national range for a red light ticket is $50–$500, depending on the state. In Massachusetts, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Massachusetts Registry of Motor Vehicles).

We do not list a single Massachusetts dollar figure, because the amount that matters is the one printed on your ticket or set by the court handling it. The national range is context only. It is not a statement of what you will owe.

Your citation is the best source for your amount. If the number on it is unclear, the court clerk or the Registry of Motor Vehicles can explain what it covers.

Remember that the base fine is not always the whole cost. Surcharges, any insurance effect and possible later consequences can add to the real price of a conviction.

How is the red light fine set in Massachusetts?

In Massachusetts the fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. It is not a figure we can calculate for you from outside the court system.

The state's laws are published and searchable through Massachusetts statutes via Justia. Reading the statute can show how the offense is defined, but your citation shows what the court applied to your case.

Because the court sets the amount, two drivers cited for the same type of violation can see different paperwork. Treat any "standard" figure you see online with caution, including national averages.

  • Start with the amount on your citation.
  • Check the court's instructions for how and where to respond.
  • Ask the clerk if anything on the form is unclear.

Are there extra surcharges or court costs?

One surcharge is named in the facts we rely on: a $50 head-injury surcharge applies to every speeding conviction in Massachusetts.

That fact is stated for speeding convictions. We do not claim it is added to every red light ticket, so check your citation or ask the court whether it appears on yours.

We also do not list other court costs or fees, because no locked figure is available for them. If your paperwork shows additional line items, those are the numbers to rely on.

When comparing what you owe with an online estimate, make sure both include the same items. A base fine and a total with surcharges are not the same number.

How many points does a red light ticket carry in Massachusetts?

Massachusetts uses insurance surcharge points (SDIP) rather than license suspension points for ordinary speeding. That means the points system is tied mainly to insurance surcharges, not to a running suspension total of the kind some states use.

We do not state a specific point value for a red light violation, because it is not among the locked facts. The Registry of Motor Vehicles is the place to confirm how your violation is treated (Massachusetts Registry of Motor Vehicles).

The thresholds that do apply to surchargeable events are:

  • 3 surchargeable events in 24 months require a driver retraining course.
  • 7 surchargeable events in 3 years = suspension.

These thresholds matter if you already have other surchargeable events on your record. One ticket on a clean record is a different situation from a third event within 24 months.

Does a red light ticket raise insurance in Massachusetts?

It can. Nationally, moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Massachusetts-specific one, and your own change could be higher, lower or none.

In Massachusetts, the SDIP surcharge points are the link between a conviction and your insurance. How an insurer applies them depends on your policy, your record and the company.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer and cannot predict your premium. If the effect on your rates matters to you, ask your insurer how a conviction would be treated before deciding how to respond to the ticket.

The 20% figure is an average across the country. Use it to gauge scale, not to estimate your own bill.

Can a red light ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Whether a ticket can be dismissed or reduced is decided by the court, and we cannot say how your case will go. This page is not legal advice.

In general, a person who receives a citation has options on how to respond. The citation explains the available choices and where to send or present your response. Those choices can affect whether a conviction is entered, which in turn affects surcharge points and insurance.

  • Read every instruction on the citation.
  • Note any response deadline printed on it.
  • Keep copies of anything you send or receive.
  • For advice on your specific case, consider speaking with a licensed attorney of your own choosing.

Because points and insurance effects follow a conviction, it is worth understanding the outcome you are choosing before you pay or respond.

What happens if I don't pay a red light ticket?

Ignoring a ticket rarely makes it go away. We do not list specific late penalties or deadlines, because no locked figures cover them, and inventing them would not help you.

The citation and the court's instructions state what you must do and by when. The Registry of Motor Vehicles can tell you how unresolved matters may affect your license or registration (Massachusetts Registry of Motor Vehicles).

Keep in mind the suspension threshold for surchargeable events: 7 in 3 years = suspension. A new event adds to that count if it results in a conviction.

If you cannot pay the amount on the citation, contact the court before the due date rather than waiting.
