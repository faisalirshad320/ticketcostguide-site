How much is a red light ticket in New Mexico?

There is no single statewide price we can quote. Nationally, red light tickets fall in a range of $50–$500 depending on the state. In New Mexico, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation.

Nationally $50–$500 depending on the state; in New Mexico the exact amount is set by the court on the citation — Red Light Ticket cost in New Mexico. (New Mexico Motor Vehicle Division, last reviewed Oct 2026)

The national range is useful only as a rough frame. It blends very different state schedules, so your New Mexico ticket could sit anywhere inside it, or be handled differently.

The number that matters is the one printed on your citation or given to you by the court handling your case. Fees are added to the penalty assessment, so the total you pay can be higher than the base amount alone.

How is the red light fine set in New Mexico?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. That means the court, not a website or a general chart, decides the figure that appears on your citation.

You can read the underlying law in the New Mexico statutes published through Justia. The traffic code is where the court's authority to set the amount comes from.

Two things can change what you owe:

  • Fees added to the penalty assessment. The base amount is not always the whole bill.
  • Court cases. If your matter goes through a court case instead of being resolved at the assessment amount, the total can exceed the assessment amounts.

We do not publish an exact New Mexico dollar figure because we have not confirmed one in our sources. If a site quotes a precise New Mexico red light fine, ask where it came from.

How many points is a red light ticket in New Mexico?

New Mexico's points system runs from 3–8 points, scaled by mph over. That scale is described in terms of speed, so it does not by itself tell you the point value of a red light violation.

To find out how your specific violation is scored, check the citation and the New Mexico Motor Vehicle Division. Do not assume a number from the speeding scale.

The thresholds are what make points matter:

  • 7–10 points in 12 months risks suspension.
  • 12 points means revocation.

Points from several violations add up inside the same 12-month window. A single red light ticket may be minor on its own, but it counts toward the total if you have other recent violations.

If you already have points on your record, find out your current total before deciding how to respond to the ticket.

Does a red light ticket raise insurance in New Mexico?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a New Mexico-specific one.

Your own change could be smaller, larger or nothing at all. It depends on your insurer, your driving record and whether the ticket ends in a conviction.

The distinction between a conviction and another outcome is worth understanding. The premium effect the national figure describes is tied to convictions, so how your case is resolved can matter as much as the fine itself.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer. If you are worried about your rate, ask your insurer how it treats a moving violation on a record.

Over time, the cost of a premium increase can add up to more than the ticket. That is why many drivers look at the full cost, not just the amount due to the court.

Can a red light ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes there are options, but the court controls them. Because the amount is set by the court on the citation, the court is also the place to ask what choices are available in your case.

Typical paths drivers consider are:

  1. Pay the assessment. This resolves the ticket, but paying may carry the same consequences as a conviction, including points and insurance effects.
  2. Contest the citation. You can ask the court to hear your case. Keep in mind that court cases can exceed the assessment amounts.
  3. Ask the court about alternatives. Courts differ in what they offer. The court or clerk can explain what applies to you.

We cannot tell you which option is right for you. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice or recommend attorneys. For a legal opinion on your situation, speak with a licensed attorney of your choosing.

Before choosing, read the citation closely. It lists the court, the instructions for responding and the deadlines that apply to you.

What happens if I don't pay a red light ticket?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. Our sources do not set out New Mexico's exact penalties for non-payment, so we will not guess at specific deadlines or amounts.

What we can say is that fees are added to the penalty assessment, and the court handles the case. An unresolved ticket can leave you with more to deal with than the original amount.

There is also the license risk. If points accumulate, 7–10 points in 12 months risks suspension and 12 points means revocation. Whether an unpaid ticket affects your record depends on how the court and the MVD handle it.

If you cannot pay the full amount or you missed a deadline, contact the court on your citation as soon as possible. Court staff can explain what options exist and what the next steps are.

Keep a copy of your citation and any payment confirmation. They are your proof of how the ticket was handled.
