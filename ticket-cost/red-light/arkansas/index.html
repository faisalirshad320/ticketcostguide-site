How much is a red light ticket in Arkansas?

Nationally, red light tickets fall in a range of $50–$500 depending on the state (Arkansas DFA Driver Services). That range is a national figure, not an Arkansas price.

In Arkansas, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not list a single Arkansas dollar figure because the number you owe is the one printed on your ticket or given by the court that handles your case.

Your citation is the controlling number. If a website quotes one fixed Arkansas fine for every driver, check it against your own paperwork.

The total can also be higher than the fine alone, because court costs are added on top. The next section explains how that works.

How is the fine set, and what are court costs?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The statutes are published online, and you can browse them through the Arkansas statutes via Justia page.

Court costs are added by the district court and vary by county. That means the same violation can produce a different total depending on where you were cited.

When you look at your ticket or court notice, check for these items:

  • The fine amount for the violation.
  • Any court costs listed separately.
  • The court name and county that will handle the case.
  • The date and instructions for paying or appearing.

If the total is unclear, the court clerk's office can explain how the amount was calculated. Clerks can describe procedure, but they cannot give legal advice.

How many points does a red light ticket add in Arkansas?

The sourced point information for Arkansas is: 3–8 points by speed; 14+ points can trigger suspension. The first part is written around speed-based violations, so it does not tell you the exact points for a red light conviction.

We do not guess a red light point value. To find out how a conviction would be scored on your record, check the citation and the Arkansas Department of Finance and Administration's Driver Services pages.

The 14+ point threshold is the part that matters most. Points from several violations add up, and a driver who reaches that level can face suspension.

One ticket is rarely the whole story. Count any other recent violations on your record before deciding how to respond.

Does a red light ticket raise insurance in Arkansas?

It can. Nationally, moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Arkansas-specific one.

Your own change could be larger, smaller or zero. It depends on your insurer, your driving history and whether the ticket ends in a conviction.

The distinction between a ticket and a conviction matters. Insurers generally look at what appears on your driving record, not only at whether you were cited. How a case is resolved can therefore affect what an insurer sees.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend insurers. If you are worried about a rate change, ask your own insurer how it treats moving violations, and compare that with the cost of the ticket itself.

Can a red light ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes the court allows options other than paying in full, but we do not promise any outcome. What is available depends on the court, the county and the facts of your case.

Common routes to ask about include:

  • Paying the ticket. This is usually the fastest route, but it generally means accepting the violation.
  • Contesting the ticket. You can ask the court how to set a hearing and what to bring.
  • Asking about a reduction or alternative resolution. Ask the court clerk whether any are available for your type of citation.
  • Speaking to a licensed attorney. This is a personal decision, and we do not recommend a specific lawyer.

Read the instructions on your citation first. They usually explain how to respond and by when.

What happens if I don't pay a red light ticket?

Ignoring a ticket rarely makes it go away. The court that issued it still has the case, and the amount owed generally does not disappear.

The specific consequences depend on the court and the circumstances. We do not state exact late fees or deadlines here, because those were not part of our sourced figures. Your citation and the court clerk are the reliable places to find them.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court before the deadline and ask what options exist. Courts are often easier to work with before a due date than after it.

Points and suspension are separate from the fine. Reaching 14+ points can trigger a suspension, so an unresolved ticket on top of other violations can add up.
