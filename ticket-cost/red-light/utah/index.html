How much is a red light ticket in Utah?

Nationally, red light tickets fall between $50 and $500 depending on the state. In Utah, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Utah Driver License Division).

We do not list a single Utah dollar amount, because the amount you owe is the one printed on your citation or in the court's notice. A figure quoted on a forum or by a friend may belong to a different court or a different year.

The $50–$500 range is a national spread across states. It is not a Utah price list. Your own citation is the only number that counts for your case.

The sections below explain how the amount is set, what else may be added to it, and what a conviction can mean for points and insurance.

How is the fine set for running a red light in Utah?

Utah does not print one flat fine for every red light ticket. The amount is set by the court within the state's traffic code, and it appears on the citation.

The traffic code is published online. You can read the Utah statutes through Utah statutes via Justia. The text sets the rules, while the court's schedule and the citation give the amount that applies to you.

Two practical points follow from this:

  • Two drivers cited for the same violation can see different totals if the court or the circumstances differ.
  • The amount on the citation is the starting point. Any later change, such as a reduction or an added fee, comes from the court.

If the citation lists a payment amount and you are unsure what it covers, ask the court clerk. The clerk can tell you what the total includes, but cannot give legal advice.

What fees and surcharges get added to the fine?

In Utah, zone enhancements and fees are built into the schedule. This means the amount you see is meant to be the whole amount, not a base fine with a long list of add-ons stacked on top.

A zone enhancement is an increase tied to where the violation happened. If your citation shows a higher amount than you expected, the location may be part of the reason.

Because we only have the locked schedule note and no itemized breakdown, we do not state what share of your total is fine and what share is a fee. Ask the court if the itemization matters to you, for example when comparing a payment to a reduced offer.

Do not assume you can pay a smaller "base fine" and skip the rest. Pay the amount the court lists, in the way the court lists it.

How many points does a red light ticket carry in Utah?

Utah uses a point system run by the state licensing agency, the Utah Driver License Division. The figures we can confirm are about speeding: it carries 35–75 points by mph over on Utah's scale.

We do not have a sourced point value for a red light violation, so we do not state one. Do not assume it matches the speeding range, which is a separate scale tied to speed over the limit.

The threshold that matters is this: 200 points in 3 years = suspension (ages 21+). Points from any violation count toward that total, so a red light conviction can add to a record that already has other points.

  • Check your driving record with the Driver License Division to see your current point count.
  • Ask the Division what a red light conviction adds before you decide how to respond to the ticket.

Does a red light ticket raise insurance in Utah?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Utah-specific one.

Your own change depends on your insurer, your record, and how the ticket is resolved. A conviction is what usually gets reported and rated, so the outcome of the case matters as much as the fine.

We do not recommend any insurer, and we cannot predict your quote. If you are weighing how to handle a ticket, one useful step is to ask your insurer how it treats a moving-violation conviction on your policy.

The fine is a one-time cost. A higher premium can repeat for as long as the conviction affects your rate, so it can outweigh the fine over time.

Can a red light ticket be dismissed or reduced in Utah?

Your options come through the court named on the citation. In general, a driver can pay the ticket, contest it, or ask the court what other resolutions it offers.

Paying usually means accepting the violation. Contesting it means you ask the court to hear the case, and the outcome is up to the court. Whether any reduction or alternative is available depends on the court and your record, and we cannot promise one.

  • Read the citation for the court name, the amount and the response instructions.
  • Contact the court clerk and ask what response options exist for your citation.
  • Consider the point and insurance effects before you pay, since payment is typically treated as a conviction.

What happens if I don't pay a red light ticket in Utah?

Ignoring a citation tends to make things worse. The court can treat a missed response as a failure to appear or pay, and that can bring added consequences on top of the original amount.

Because the amount is set by the court and fees are built into the schedule, an unpaid ticket can change what you owe. We do not state specific penalty amounts or deadlines, since we have no sourced figures for them.

The Driver License Division handles license status, and the 200-points-in-3-years suspension threshold for drivers 21 and older applies to your point total. A ticket you leave unresolved is still a risk to your record.

If you cannot pay the full amount, call the court before the due date and ask what options it has.
