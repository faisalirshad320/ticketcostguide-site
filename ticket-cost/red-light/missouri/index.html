How much is a red light ticket in Missouri?

Nationally, red light tickets run $50–$500 depending on the state (Missouri Department of Revenue). In Missouri, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. The number printed on your ticket, or on the court's payment notice, is the one that applies to you.

We do not publish a single Missouri dollar figure on this page. The amount depends on the court handling your case and how the charge was filed. The national range is a comparison point only.

Court costs are included in the scheduled payable amounts. If the court gives you a scheduled amount, ask whether it is the full total before you assume anything extra is due.

How is the red light fine set in Missouri?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. It is not a flat statewide price you can look up in one place. Missouri statutes are available through Missouri statutes via Justia if you want to read the traffic provisions yourself.

Two things usually shape what you see on paper:

  • The court that has your case, which sets or applies the scheduled payable amount.
  • Whether the charge is a municipal charge or a state charge, which also affects points.

Your citation is the controlling document. If the amount on it is unclear, the clerk of the court named on the ticket can tell you what is owed.

How many points does a red light ticket add in Missouri?

A conviction adds 2 points if it is a municipal conviction, or 3 points if it is a state charge. Which one applies depends on how the ticket was filed, so read the citation for the court and charge listed.

The suspension threshold is 8 points in 18 months. Points from other moving violations count toward the same total. A red light conviction on top of one or two other tickets can bring you closer to that line.

For your own record and point total, the Missouri Department of Revenue Driver License Bureau is the agency to check.

Quick math: 2 or 3 points from this ticket, against a suspension threshold of 8 points in 18 months.

Does a red light ticket raise your insurance in Missouri?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Missouri-specific rate, and your own result may be higher, lower or nil.

What affects it in practice:

  • Whether the ticket ends in a conviction on your record.
  • Your insurer's own rating rules and when your policy renews.
  • Your other violations or claims.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend insurers or predict your premium. If you are worried about the effect, your insurer or agent can explain how they treat a conviction of this type.

Can a red light ticket be dismissed or reduced in Missouri?

Sometimes, but it depends on the court and the facts of your case. We do not have locked data on how often tickets are dismissed or reduced, so we will not guess at odds.

The usual routes are:

  • Contest the ticket and present your side to the court.
  • Ask the court or prosecutor whether any alternative resolution is available for your charge.
  • Pay the ticket, which generally resolves the case but may result in a conviction and points.

Paying is not always a neutral choice. Because points and insurance can follow a conviction, it is worth understanding the consequences before you pay. A licensed attorney can give legal advice; we do not recommend any specific one.

What happens if I don't pay a red light ticket in Missouri?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. Courts can add consequences when a citation goes unanswered, and your license status can be affected. We do not state specific deadlines or penalty amounts here because they are not part of our locked data.

Instead, do this:

  1. Find the court name, date and instructions printed on the citation.
  2. Contact the clerk of that court to confirm what is owed and what options exist.
  3. Respond by the date given, whether you plan to pay or contest.

Unresolved tickets can also interact with the points rule: a suspension follows 8 points in 18 months, so avoid letting problems stack up.

What should I do first after getting a red light ticket?

Start with the citation itself. Note the court, the charge, and any listed amount or appearance instructions. These details decide the fine, the possible points (2 or 3), and your choices.

Then weigh the options in order: confirm the amount with the court, decide whether to pay or contest, and consider the possible effect on your driving record and insurance. If you want to compare how other states handle similar violations, the Governors Highway Safety Association state laws page is a useful starting point.

If the stakes feel high, such as being close to the 8-point threshold, consider speaking with a licensed attorney of your choosing.

Frequently asked questions

How much is a red light ticket in Missouri?

How many points is a red light ticket in Missouri?

A conviction adds 2 points if it is a municipal conviction, or 3 points if it is a state charge. The citation shows which court and charge apply to you. Points count toward the suspension threshold of 8 points in 18 months.

Will a red light ticket raise my insurance in Missouri?

It may. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average, though that is a national figure, not a Missouri rate. Your actual change depends on your insurer, your record and whether the ticket becomes a conviction.

Are court costs added to a Missouri red light fine?

Court costs are included in the scheduled payable amounts. That means a scheduled amount should already account for them. Because the exact fine is set by the court on the citation, confirm the total with the court clerk before paying.

How many points suspend your license in Missouri?

Reaching 8 points in 18 months results in suspension. A red light conviction adds 2 points (municipal) or 3 points (state charge), and points from other violations count toward the same total. The Missouri Department of Revenue can confirm your record.

Can I fight a red light ticket in Missouri?

You can generally contest a citation in court rather than simply paying it. Whether it is dismissed or reduced depends on the court and your facts. Paying may lead to a conviction, points and possible insurance effects, so consider that first.
