How much is a red light ticket in North Dakota?

Nationally, a red light ticket costs $50–$500 depending on the state. In North Dakota the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (North Dakota Department of Transportation).

That national range is context, not a North Dakota price. We do not publish an exact North Dakota dollar figure, because the court on your citation determines it within the state's traffic code.

The most reliable number is the one printed on your ticket or given by the court clerk. If the citation lists no amount, the clerk's office can tell you what applies to your case.

Bottom line: the $50–$500 range is a national benchmark. Your North Dakota amount comes from the court handling your citation.

How is the fine for running a red light set in North Dakota?

The fine is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The code sets the framework, and the court applies it to your citation.

That is why two drivers cited for the same violation can see different paperwork. Details of the case and the court's handling can matter, so compare your own citation rather than relying on a general figure.

You can read the underlying provisions in the North Dakota statutes (North Dakota statutes via Justia). Statutes can be amended, so confirm the current text with the court or the state.

Fines are not the only possible cost. Missed deadlines and higher insurance rates can add to the total, as the sections below explain.

Are there extra court costs or surcharges?

For this violation in North Dakota, the statutory fee is usually the whole amount. There is no large stack of added costs on top of it.

"Usually" matters here. Before you pay, check the total on the citation or ask the clerk whether any other charge applies to your case.

  • Look for a single total amount due on the citation.
  • Ask the clerk to confirm the amount if the ticket is unclear.
  • Keep your payment receipt in case the record is questioned later.

This differs from states where fees pile up and the final bill is far above the base fine. In North Dakota the figure on the citation should be close to the full cost.

How many points does a red light ticket add in North Dakota?

North Dakota assigns points on its own schedule, and the schedule is organized by mph over the limit. Reaching 12 or more points means a suspension.

We do not state an exact point value for a red light violation, because the figures we rely on do not give one. Your driving record and the North Dakota Department of Transportation (North Dakota Department of Transportation) are the places to confirm the points tied to your conviction.

Points matter because they accumulate. One ticket may not threaten your license, but added to earlier violations it could move you toward the 12-point suspension threshold.

Rule to remember: 12+ points = suspension. Ask the state how many points are on your record now.

Does a red light ticket raise insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a North Dakota or insurer-specific one.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the violation is recorded. Some drivers see no change at renewal, and others see more than the average.

Because the effect can last across several renewals, the insurance cost of a conviction can exceed the fine itself. That is worth weighing when you decide whether to pay or contest.

We do not recommend any insurer. If you are worried about your rate, ask your own insurer how a conviction would be treated.

Can a red light ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Possibly, but outcomes depend on the facts and on the court. We cannot predict whether a given ticket will be dismissed or reduced, and this page is not legal advice.

In general, a driver cited for a traffic violation can usually do one of two things:

  1. Pay the citation. This generally resolves the ticket but is usually treated as a conviction, with the points and insurance effects described above.
  2. Contest it. You can ask the court how to request a hearing or discuss the charge. Follow the deadline and instructions on the citation.

If the stakes are high, such as a record close to the 12-point suspension threshold, you may want to speak with a licensed attorney of your own choosing. We do not recommend any specific attorney.

Whatever you choose, act before the deadline on the citation. The court's instructions control the process.

What happens if I don't pay a red light ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts commonly treat a missed deadline as a separate problem, and the outcome can include added consequences beyond the original ticket.

The specifics depend on the court and on the instructions on your citation, so we do not list exact penalties here. Contact the court clerk as soon as you know you cannot meet a deadline.

  • Read the due date and instructions on the citation.
  • Contact the court before the deadline if you need information.
  • Keep copies of anything you send or receive.

Unpaid or unresolved tickets can also affect your license status. Because 12+ points means a suspension, the state's records are worth checking if you have other violations.
