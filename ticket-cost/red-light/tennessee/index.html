How much is a red light ticket in Tennessee?

Nationally, red light tickets run $50–$500 depending on the state. In Tennessee the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Tennessee Department of Safety and Homeland Security).

We do not publish a single Tennessee dollar figure, because the amount on your ticket depends on the court handling it. The national range is only a rough guide to how much states differ.

The number that matters is the one printed on your citation or on the court's notice. Start there, not with a general estimate.

Your total is usually more than a fine alone. County court costs and state litigation taxes make up most of the total, so two drivers with the same violation can owe different amounts in different counties.

How is the fine and total cost set?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The Tennessee traffic code is published online through Tennessee statutes via Justia, where you can read the rules that apply to traffic signals and penalties.

Several pieces make up what you pay:

  • The fine: set by the court on the citation.
  • County court costs: these vary by county and court.
  • State litigation taxes: added on top of the fine.

Court costs and litigation taxes make up most of the total. That is why a ticket can look small on the citation line and still produce a larger bill at the clerk's window.

If the citation lists a total, ask the clerk what it includes. If it lists only a fine, ask what costs and taxes will be added.

How many points does a red light ticket add in Tennessee?

Tennessee's points system runs from 1–8 points, scaled by mph over the limit. Examples: 3 points at 6–15 over, and 4 points at 16–25 over.

That scale is described by speed. It does not give a point value for running a red light, and we will not guess one. Check your citation, the court, or the Tennessee Department of Safety and Homeland Security to see whether and how points apply to your violation.

The suspension threshold is 12 points in 12 months, which triggers a suspension notice. If you already have points on your record, ask how a new conviction would add to them.

Points are tied to a conviction, not just a citation. What happens to your case in court affects what ends up on your driving record.

Does a red light ticket raise insurance in Tennessee?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Tennessee quote.

Your own change could be higher, lower or zero. It depends on your insurer, your driving history and whether the violation ends in a conviction on your record.

  • Insurers set their own rules, so we cannot predict your rate.
  • The effect usually shows up at renewal.
  • A clean record beforehand may soften the impact; prior violations may worsen it.

For a real number, ask your insurer how it treats a moving violation. TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer.

Can a red light ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but that is decided by the court, not by this guide. The citation should explain how to respond, and the court clerk can tell you what choices are available in your county.

In general, drivers with a ticket can:

  • Pay it: this usually resolves the case but may mean a conviction.
  • Contest it: ask for a hearing and present your side.
  • Ask the court about alternatives: some courts offer options that affect the record, but availability varies by court.

Because the conviction can affect points and insurance, think about the long-term cost as well as the amount due today.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice. For advice on your case, consider speaking with a licensed Tennessee attorney of your choosing.

What happens if I don't pay a red light ticket?

Ignoring a ticket rarely makes it go away. Courts set deadlines, and missing them can lead to added costs or other action on your case. The specifics depend on the court and on how the citation was issued.

Read the notice closely for the due date and the response instructions. If you cannot meet the deadline, contact the clerk before it passes and ask what can be done.

Also keep in mind the 12 points in 12 months threshold, which triggers a suspension notice. Unresolved tickets and new convictions can make your record harder to manage.

For the rules that apply to your situation, see the Tennessee Department of Safety and Homeland Security and the court named on your citation.

Is the fine the same in every Tennessee county?

Not necessarily. The court sets the fine on the citation, and county court costs and state litigation taxes make up most of the total. Because court costs vary by county and court, the final amount can differ. Check your citation or ask the clerk.

How many points is a red light ticket in Tennessee?

Tennessee's points system runs 1–8 points by mph over, such as 3 points at 6–15 over and 4 at 16–25. That scale is based on speed, so check your citation or the Department of Safety for how your red light violation is treated.

Under the points rules we cite, 12 points in 12 months results in a suspension notice. If you already have points, a new conviction could bring you closer to that threshold. Ask the court or the Department of Safety about your record.

You can generally respond to a citation through the court named on it, which may include contesting it. The court decides the outcome. TicketCostGuide gives no legal advice, so consider speaking with a licensed Tennessee attorney about your case.
