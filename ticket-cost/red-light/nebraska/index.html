How much is a red light ticket in Nebraska?

Nationally, red light tickets run $50–$500 depending on the state. In Nebraska the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Nebraska Department of Motor Vehicles).

We do not list a single Nebraska dollar figure because we could not source one that applies to every court. The number printed on your citation, or provided by the court clerk, is the one that counts.

The national $50–$500 range is context only. It is not a Nebraska fine schedule, and your Nebraska ticket may fall anywhere the court's authority allows.

The base fine is also not the whole bill. Court costs are added statewide, and fines are doubled in some zones. Both are covered below.

How is the fine for running a red light set in Nebraska?

The exact fine for this violation in Nebraska is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The amount is not fixed by the officer writing the ticket.

In practice, that means the figure appears on the citation or is confirmed by the court handling your case. Two people cited for the same offense in different courts could see different totals once costs are added.

To find your amount, check these in order:

  1. The citation itself, which should list the fine or point you to a payment process.
  2. The website or clerk's office of the court named on the citation.
  3. The statutes, which you can read through Nebraska statutes via Justia, if you want to see the traffic code the court works within.

Statute text can be dense. If something is unclear, the court clerk can explain what is owed, though clerks cannot give legal advice.

Are there extra costs and doubled fines on top of the base fine?

Yes. Court costs are added statewide, so the amount you pay is normally more than the fine alone. We do not state the size of those costs because the sourced data does not give a figure.

Fines are also doubled in some zones. The sourced data does not list which zones apply to red light violations, so do not assume your location is or is not covered. Look at the citation and ask the court if the total seems high.

  • Base fine: set by the court on the citation.
  • Court costs: added statewide.
  • Zone doubling: applies in some zones only.

When comparing what you owe with what you read online, make sure you are comparing the same thing. Many articles quote the base fine only, while the court asks for the total.

How many points does a red light ticket add in Nebraska?

The sourced points system for Nebraska is 1–3 points by mph over the limit, with 12 points in 2 years leading to revocation (Nebraska Department of Motor Vehicles).

That scale is described in terms of speed. A red light violation does not involve mph over the limit in the same way, so we cannot confirm from our data how many points, if any, a particular red light conviction adds.

To confirm points for your exact violation, check the citation, ask the court, or review your record with the Nebraska DMV. Do not rely on a general point total.

What we can say is how the threshold works. Points accumulate on a driving record, and reaching 12 points in 2 years means revocation. Even a violation with a small point value moves you closer to that line if you already have other points.

Does a red light ticket raise insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Nebraska rate.

Your own change could be smaller, larger, or none at all. Insurers use their own rules, and the effect depends on your record, your policy and the insurer.

Some practical points:

  • The increase is usually tied to a conviction, so how the case ends matters.
  • Pending tickets can look different to an insurer than resolved ones.
  • Your insurer or agent is the only reliable source for what your premium will do.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer. Treat the 20% average as a rough sense of scale when you weigh the full cost of a ticket, which includes the premium effect over time as well as the fine.

Can a red light ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Generally, a person who receives a ticket can pay it, or can ask the court about contesting it. Which options are open to you depends on the court and the details of your citation.

Before choosing, consider:

  • Paying usually resolves the matter quickly, but it can mean a conviction, with the points and insurance effects described above.
  • Contesting means appearing before the court, where the facts of your case are considered.
  • Asking the clerk what choices are listed for your citation can clear up what applies to you.

We do not know of a sourced Nebraska rule here that guarantees a reduction, so we do not promise one. If the stakes are high, some drivers speak with a licensed attorney. This guide does not give legal advice or recommend a specific attorney.

Read the citation for any response deadline and follow it. Missing a court date can make the situation more costly.

What happens if I don't pay a red light ticket?

Ignoring a ticket rarely makes it go away. Courts generally expect a response by the date on the citation, and what follows a missed deadline depends on the court and the state's rules.

We do not have sourced Nebraska penalty amounts for late payment, so we do not state any. Contact the court listed on your citation to find out your status.

Because the points system can lead to revocation at 12 points in 2 years, an unresolved ticket is worth handling early, especially if you already have points on your record. The Nebraska DMV is the place to ask about your driving record.
