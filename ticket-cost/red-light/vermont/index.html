How much is a red light ticket in Vermont?

The amount is printed on your citation. Our sourced figure is: nationally $50–$500 depending on the state; in Vermont the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Vermont Department of Motor Vehicles).

The $50–$500 range is a national range across states. It is not a Vermont price list, and we do not present any specific Vermont dollar amount as the fine.

The number on your own citation is the one that matters. If you cannot find it, contact the court named on the ticket and ask what is owed.

Statutory surcharges are included in the waiver amounts. That means the total you are asked to pay should already account for them, rather than being a separate bill on top.

How is the red light fine set in Vermont?

In Vermont, the exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The figure is not a single flat amount we can quote for every driver.

That is why two drivers cited for the same type of violation can see different totals depending on the court and the circumstances recorded on the citation.

The traffic code itself is published through Vermont statutes via Justia. Reading the relevant provisions can show how violations are classified, but the amount you owe is the one the court assigns to your citation.

  • Start with the citation. It lists the violation and the amount.
  • Check the waiver amount. Statutory surcharges are included in it.
  • Ask the court if anything on the paperwork is unclear.

How many points does a red light ticket add in Vermont?

Vermont's points system runs from 2–8 points by mph over. That scale is tied to speed, so it does not map neatly onto a red light violation.

We do not have a sourced point value specific to a red light ticket. Check your citation and the Vermont Department of Motor Vehicles for the points, if any, that apply to your violation.

The suspension threshold is 10 points in 2 years. Points from other violations in the same period count toward that total.

If you already have points on your record, find out how many before deciding how to handle the ticket. A few points that are harmless alone can matter more when added to existing ones.

Does a red light ticket raise insurance in Vermont?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Vermont-specific one.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the violation is recorded. Some insurers look at convictions, others at points, and policies differ in how long they look back.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer. If you are worried about your rate, your insurer or a licensed agent can explain how they treat a conviction.

  • The ~20% figure is an average across the country.
  • Your own change could be higher, lower or none.
  • A conviction is generally what insurers weigh, so how the case ends can matter.

Can a red light ticket be dismissed or reduced in Vermont?

Generally, a driver cited for a red light violation has a few paths. Which are open depends on the court and on the citation.

  1. Pay the waiver amount. This resolves the ticket but usually counts as a conviction. Statutory surcharges are included in the waiver amount.
  2. Contest the ticket. You can ask for a hearing and present your account. The court decides.
  3. Ask about other resolutions. Some courts may offer ways to lower the penalty or avoid points. We have no sourced data on which Vermont courts do, so ask the court directly.

We cannot say how likely any outcome is, and this page is not legal advice. If the stakes are high, such as many existing points, you may want to speak with a licensed attorney of your own choosing.

What happens if I don't pay a red light ticket in Vermont?

Ignoring a traffic citation rarely makes it go away. Courts and motor vehicle agencies generally have tools to enforce unpaid or unanswered tickets, which can include added penalties or action against a license.

We do not have sourced Vermont deadlines or penalty amounts, so we do not state any here. The citation should say how and when to respond.

If you are unsure of your deadline, call the court named on the citation before it passes. An unanswered ticket can cost more than the original fine.

Also keep in mind that a suspension is tied to points. Vermont's threshold is 10 points in 2 years, so an unresolved ticket on top of existing points is worth taking seriously.
