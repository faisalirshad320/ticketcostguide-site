How much is a red light ticket in Rhode Island?

Nationally, red light tickets run $50–$500 depending on the state. In Rhode Island the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Rhode Island Division of Motor Vehicles).

We do not list a single Rhode Island dollar figure here, because the amount you owe is the one printed on your citation or in the court's notice. A number from a forum post or an old article may not match what the court assessed.

The $50–$500 range is a national spread across states. It is not a Rhode Island fine schedule, and your ticket may fall outside it once fees are added.

The total you pay can also include court technology and assessment fees on top of the scheduled fine. Read the full amount due, not just the fine line.

How is the fine for running a red light set?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The code sets the framework, and the court assigns the amount on your citation.

You can read Rhode Island's statutes through Rhode Island statutes via Justia. For the official handling of your case, rely on the citation and the court that issued it.

Two tickets for the same offense can show different totals. Differences can come from the scheduled fine, the added fees, and your driving history.

  • The scheduled fine is set by the court on the citation.
  • Court technology and assessment fees are added to that fine.
  • Repeat offenses can bring escalated sanctions from the Traffic Tribunal.

What extra fees get added to the fine?

Court technology and assessment fees are added to the scheduled fine in Rhode Island. They are separate from the fine itself.

We do not state their dollar amounts, because we have no sourced figure for them. The citation or court notice should show the full total.

If the total looks different from what you expected, compare these items:

  1. The base fine on the citation.
  2. Any court technology fee listed.
  3. Any assessment fee listed.
  4. The final amount due.

If a line is unclear, ask the court clerk what it covers. This is a cost question, not a legal one.

How many points does a red light ticket carry in Rhode Island?

Rhode Island has no running point system for ordinary speeding. Instead, the Traffic Tribunal escalates sanctions, including suspension, for repeat offenses.

That description covers ordinary speeding. We have no sourced point value for a red light violation, so we do not give one. Confirm how your specific violation is recorded on your citation and with the Rhode Island Division of Motor Vehicles.

The practical point is that a single ticket is not tracked the way it is in states with a running point count. A pattern of violations is what draws escalating consequences.

Rhode Island's approach rewards a clean record over time. Repeat violations are where suspension risk comes in.

Does a red light ticket raise insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Rhode Island one.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your record, and how the violation is reported. We do not know your policy terms and cannot predict your rate.

Some things to keep in mind:

  • The effect usually follows a conviction, not just a citation.
  • The ~20% figure is an average, so individual results vary.
  • Rate changes often show up at renewal.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer. For your own rate, ask your insurance company directly.

Can a red light ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Your citation lists your options and how to respond. Generally, you can pay it, or contest it with the court that issued it.

Paying usually resolves the matter but may count as a conviction. Contesting means presenting your case to the court. We do not give legal advice, and we cannot say whether your ticket would be reduced or dismissed.

Before choosing, check these items:

  • The exact fine and fees printed on the citation.
  • Your driving history, since repeat offenses bring escalated sanctions.
  • The possible insurance effect of a conviction, about 20% on average nationally.

If you want advice about your specific case, that is a question for a licensed attorney. We do not recommend one.

What happens if I don't pay a red light ticket?

Ignoring a ticket can make things worse. Rhode Island's Traffic Tribunal escalates sanctions, including suspension, for repeat offenses, so unresolved violations are worth taking seriously.

We do not have sourced late-fee amounts or deadlines, so we do not state any. Your citation or court notice should list the date and the way to respond.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court listed on your citation before the date passes. The Rhode Island Division of Motor Vehicles is the place to check license status.
