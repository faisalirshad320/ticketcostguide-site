How much is a red light ticket in Mississippi?

Nationally, red light tickets cost $50–$500 depending on the state. In Mississippi the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Mississippi Department of Public Safety).

That means there is no single number we can responsibly quote for every driver. The figure that matters is the one printed on your citation or given by the court that handles your case.

The $50–$500 range is a national picture. It shows how widely states differ, and it should not be read as the Mississippi fine or as a Mississippi minimum or maximum.

Bottom line: the national range is $50–$500, and the Mississippi amount is whatever the court sets on your citation. Add the mandatory state assessments on top.

How is a red light fine set in Mississippi?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The code gives the court the authority, and the citation shows the result for your case. You can browse Mississippi's statutes through Justia, though the traffic code is long and the citation is the quicker place to look.

Mandatory state assessments are added to every fine and often exceed it. So the fine alone is rarely the amount you pay.

When you compare a quote or a friend's experience with your own ticket, check whether the number includes those assessments. Two people can describe the same violation and quote very different totals for this reason.

  • The fine: set by the court on the citation.
  • State assessments: mandatory, added to every fine, and often larger than the fine itself.
  • Your total: the fine plus assessments. Ask the court clerk for the full amount due.

We do not publish an exact Mississippi dollar figure here, because the amount is set by the court rather than by a fixed statewide schedule we can verify.

How many points does a red light ticket add in Mississippi?

None, because Mississippi has no point system. Suspensions follow repeat convictions and specific offenses instead.

This differs from states that count points for each violation and suspend a license at a set total. In Mississippi, the risk comes from your overall record: repeat convictions, or certain serious offenses, can lead to a suspension.

A single red light conviction is therefore not tracked as a point value. It can still count as a conviction on your driving record, and that record is what matters if you are cited again.

For the rules on your license status, the Mississippi Department of Public Safety is the agency to check. Do not rely on point totals you see on websites written for other states.

Does a red light ticket raise insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Mississippi-specific one.

Whether your own premium changes depends on your insurer, your driving history, and whether the ticket ends in a conviction. We do not recommend or compare insurers, and we cannot predict what your company will do.

A few practical points:

  • The effect usually follows a conviction, so how the case ends matters.
  • Insurers often review your record at renewal, so a change may not show up right away.
  • If you are worried, ask your insurer how it treats a moving violation before you decide how to resolve the ticket.

The ~20% figure is an average. Individual results can be higher, lower, or nothing at all.

Can a red light ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Often there are options, but they depend on the court that holds your case. The citation should say how to respond, and the court clerk can explain what is available.

Common routes people consider include:

  • Paying the ticket. This usually resolves the case but may mean a conviction on your record.
  • Contesting it. You can ask for a hearing and present your side. The court decides.
  • Asking the court about alternatives. Some courts offer other resolutions. Ask the clerk whether any apply to you.

Because the court sets the amount, it also controls what happens if the case is resolved in a different way. We cannot say which options a given Mississippi court offers.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice. If the stakes are high for your license or job, consider speaking with a licensed Mississippi attorney of your own choosing.

What happens if I don't pay a red light ticket?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. Courts typically treat an unanswered citation as a separate problem, which can mean added costs or action against your driving privileges.

Mississippi suspensions follow repeat convictions and specific offenses, so a missed response is not something to leave to chance. We do not have a verified deadline or penalty figure to quote, and we will not guess at one.

The safe steps are simple:

  1. Read the citation for the court name and response instructions.
  2. Contact the court clerk before any listed date passes.
  3. Ask for the full amount due, including mandatory state assessments.
  4. Keep a record of what you paid or filed.

If your ticket came from a camera rather than an officer, the notice may follow different steps. Read it closely and ask the issuing court or agency how to respond.
