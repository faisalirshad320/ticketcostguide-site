How much is a red light ticket in North Carolina?

Nationally, red light tickets run $50–$500 depending on the state. In North Carolina, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (North Carolina Division of Motor Vehicles).

We do not publish a single North Carolina dollar figure, because the amount you owe is the one tied to your own citation. The national range is a comparison point. It is not a North Carolina fine schedule.

The fine is also not the whole bill. Uniform statewide court costs dwarf the fine on most tickets, so the total you pay can be much larger than the fine line alone.

Look at your citation or court notice first. That document, not a national average, tells you what applies to your case.

How is a red light fine set in North Carolina?

The exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The code itself is part of the North Carolina statutes, which you can read through Justia's North Carolina code.

That means two drivers can hold similar tickets and see different paperwork. The court's assigned amount on the citation is what counts.

If the citation does not show a clear amount, the clerk of court's office is the place to ask. Ask whether the amount can be resolved without a court appearance, and what the total including court costs would be.

We cannot tell you which amount you will be assigned. We can only point you to the document and the official sources that control it.

Why is the total more than the fine?

Uniform statewide court costs dwarf the fine on most tickets. In practice, the costs added by the court can matter more to your final bill than the fine itself.

This is why comparing the fine alone across states or counties can mislead. A ticket with a modest fine can still produce a large total once costs are added.

  • Ask for the total, not just the fine.
  • Check whether the amount on your notice already includes court costs.
  • Keep a record of what you were told and when.

We do not list a dollar amount for the court costs, because we only publish figures we can tie to a cited source.

How many points does a red light ticket add?

The point reference we hold is for typical speeding: license points of 2–3, plus separate insurance (SDIP) points that can raise premiums 30%+ per point tier. We do not have a sourced point value specific to red light violations, so we will not guess one.

Two systems are at work here. License points are tracked by the state's driver licensing agency. Insurance points are a separate scoring system used for premium purposes.

To find out how a red light conviction is treated, check with the North Carolina Division of Motor Vehicles and the statutes on Justia. You can also compare state rules on the Governors Highway Safety Association state laws page.

Do not assume the speeding figures apply to your ticket. Confirm the point treatment for your exact violation before deciding how to respond.

Does a red light ticket raise insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums ~20% on average. That is a national figure, not a North Carolina or insurer-specific one.

Separately, insurance (SDIP) points can raise premiums 30%+ per point tier. The two figures describe different things: one is a broad national average, the other is how a point-based system can stack.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your driving record and how the case ends. A conviction is what typically drives the increase, which is why the outcome of the ticket matters as much as the fine.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend insurers. If you want to know how your own policy reacts, ask your insurer how it treats a moving-violation conviction.

Can a red light ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes the outcome can change, but this page is not legal advice and we cannot say what will happen in your case. Outcomes depend on the facts, the court and the paperwork.

In general, drivers have a few paths:

  • Pay the amount due. This is usually the fastest way to close the case, but it may count as a conviction.
  • Contest the ticket in court. You can present your side and ask the court to decide.
  • Ask the court about alternatives. Ask the clerk's office what options exist for your citation.
  • Speak with a licensed attorney. A lawyer can advise on your specific facts. We do not recommend any specific attorney.

Because the ~20% national premium figure is tied to convictions, the way the case ends can affect insurance as well as the fine. Weigh both before paying.

What happens if I don't pay a red light ticket?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes the situation worse. Unpaid or unanswered citations can lead to added costs, further court action and possible licensing consequences.

The exact steps in North Carolina are set by the courts and the DMV. We do not list penalty amounts, because none are in our sourced figures.

  • Read the court date and response instructions on the citation.
  • Contact the clerk of court's office before the deadline shown on your paperwork.
  • Check your driving record status with the North Carolina DMV.

If you cannot pay the total, ask the court what is available. Doing that before a deadline is better than waiting.
