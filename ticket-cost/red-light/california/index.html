How much is a red light ticket in California?

Nationally, red light tickets run $50–$500 depending on the state. In California the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (California Department of Motor Vehicles).

We do not publish a single statewide dollar figure for California, because the amount you owe depends on the fine the court sets and the add-ons that apply in your county. The $50–$500 range is a national range. It is not a California fine schedule.

The most reliable number is the one printed on your citation or shown on the court's payment page. If the amount on the paper looks much higher than the base fine you expected, the add-ons described below are usually the reason.

Bottom line: the national range is $50–$500, but your California amount is whatever the court lists for your citation.

How is a California red light fine set?

The exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The California statutes are available through California statutes via Justia, and the DMV explains how convictions are recorded on your driving record.

The base fine is only part of the bill. Penalty assessments, county fees and surcharges multiply the base fine roughly 4–5×. That is why two drivers with the same violation can see different totals in different counties.

Here is what that means in practice:

  • The base fine is set by the court within the traffic code.
  • Assessments and fees are added on top of it.
  • The total on your citation or court notice is the amount to plan around.

Because these add-ons vary by county and court, a friend's ticket in another county is a poor guide to what you will owe.

How many points does a red light ticket add in California?

California uses a point system tracked by the DMV. The locked figures we rely on are these: 1 point for ordinary speeding (2 points at 100+ mph), and 4 points in 12 months = suspension under the Negligent Operator Treatment System.

Those figures describe speeding and the suspension threshold. They do not tell us the point value for a red light conviction, so we will not guess one. Your citation, the court, or the California DMV can confirm what applies to your violation.

What matters is the threshold. Points from several violations add up, and reaching 4 points in 12 months triggers suspension under the Negligent Operator Treatment System. If you already have points on your record, a new conviction matters more than it would for a driver with a clean record.

Does a red light ticket raise your insurance?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums ~20% on average. That is a national figure, not a California-specific one, and we cannot tell you what your insurer will do.

The effect depends on whether the ticket becomes a conviction on your record, your insurer's own rules and your driving history. A conviction is what insurers usually react to, so how the ticket is resolved matters.

When you compare the cost of the ticket, include the premium effect, not just the fine. A 20% average increase can add up over the period a violation stays on your record, though how long that lasts varies by insurer.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer or give insurance advice. Ask your own insurer how a conviction would affect your policy.

Can a red light ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome is decided by the court, not by this guide. In general you have three paths: pay the amount on the citation, contest the ticket, or ask the court whether any alternative resolution is available.

Paying is usually treated as accepting the violation, which can mean a conviction on your driving record and the point and insurance consequences described above. Contesting means presenting your case to the court, which can take time and may still end with the full amount owed.

Before you decide, read the citation closely for the following:

  • The court name and the deadline to respond.
  • The total amount, including fees.
  • The instructions for contesting or requesting other options.

We cannot say whether you qualify for a reduction or any program. The court clerk or the California DMV can explain what is available. If you want legal advice, you would need to consult a licensed attorney of your own choosing.

What happens if I don't pay a red light ticket?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes it worse. Courts set deadlines, and missing one can lead to additional penalties and further action on your license or case.

Since penalty assessments, county fees and surcharges already multiply the base fine roughly 4–5×, any extra late charge comes on top of an already larger total. We do not state exact late fees because they depend on the court.

Steps worth taking if you are unsure:

  1. Find the due date and court on your citation.
  2. Contact the court before the deadline if you need clarification.
  3. Check your DMV record afterward to confirm how the ticket was recorded.

Unresolved violations can also contribute to a suspension if points accumulate. Remember that 4 points in 12 months = suspension under the Negligent Operator Treatment System.
