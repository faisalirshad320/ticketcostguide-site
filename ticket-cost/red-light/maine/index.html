How much is a red light ticket in Maine?

Nationally, red light tickets run $50–$500 depending on the state. In Maine, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Maine Bureau of Motor Vehicles).

We do not publish a single Maine dollar figure, because the amount that applies to you is the one written on your citation or court notice. The national range is only a rough frame of reference.

The number on your ticket is the number that matters. A national range cannot tell you what a specific Maine court will set.

If the citation lists a scheduled amount, that is usually the figure to plan around. If it directs you to appear, the court will tell you what is owed.

How is a red light fine set in Maine?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. Maine statutes are available through Maine statutes via Justia if you want to read the text for yourself.

Statutory surcharges are built into the scheduled amounts. That means the total you see on a scheduled fine already reflects those required add-ons, rather than being a base fine with a long list of extras.

Two drivers cited for the same act can still see different outcomes. Details on the citation, the court's process and any reduction or dismissal can change what is actually owed.

  • The citation is your primary source for the amount.
  • The court handling your case controls the final figure.
  • The state traffic code frames what the court may set.

How many points does a red light ticket carry in Maine?

Maine uses demerit points that accrue per conviction. A ticket on its own is not a conviction, so the points question depends on how your case ends.

The threshold to know is this: 12 points in 12 months = suspension. Points from all convictions count toward that total, not only red light violations.

We do not list a specific point value for a red light violation here, because it is not among the figures we can source for this page. Check with the Maine Bureau of Motor Vehicles for how this violation is treated on a driving record.

Paying the ticket is usually treated as accepting the conviction. If points matter to you, understand that before you pay.

Does a red light ticket raise insurance in Maine?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Maine-specific number or a quote from any insurer.

A red light violation is generally a moving violation. Whether your rate changes depends on your insurer, your driving history and whether the ticket ends in a conviction.

We do not recommend insurers or predict what yours will do. If you are worried about a rate change, your insurer or policy documents are the place to ask how they treat moving violations.

  • The ~20% figure is an average across the US.
  • Your own change could be higher, lower or none.
  • Rate effects generally follow a conviction on your record.

Can a red light ticket be dismissed or reduced in Maine?

Sometimes, but we cannot promise an outcome, and this page is not legal advice. Whether a ticket can be reduced or dismissed depends on the facts, the court and the process the citation sets out.

Because the court sets the amount on the citation, the court is also where any request about the ticket is handled. The citation should say how to respond and where.

Before you decide, it helps to gather what you have:

  • The citation itself and any court notice.
  • Your own record of the date, location and conditions.
  • Your driving record, if points or insurance are a concern.

If you want guidance on your specific case, that is a question for a licensed attorney, not for a cost guide.

What happens if I don't pay a red light ticket in Maine?

Ignoring a ticket rarely makes it go away. The court that issued the citation controls what happens next, and the citation or notice explains the required response.

The general risk is that an unresolved ticket can lead to added consequences through the court and the licensing system. We do not state specific penalties or deadlines here, because they are not among the figures we can source for this page.

The safest first step is to read the citation carefully and contact the court named on it. The Maine Bureau of Motor Vehicles is the source for license status questions.

Because points accrue per conviction and 12 points in 12 months = suspension, an unresolved ticket can matter beyond the fine itself.

What are my options after getting a red light ticket?

In broad terms, you can resolve the citation as directed, or you can respond to the court if you disagree with it. Which path fits depends on your situation.

  1. Read the citation and note what it says about the amount and how to respond.
  2. Think about points: a conviction adds demerit points, and 12 points in 12 months = suspension.
  3. Think about insurance: moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average.
  4. Contact the court named on the citation with any procedural questions.
