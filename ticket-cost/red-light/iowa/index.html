How much is a red light ticket in Iowa?

Nationally, a red light ticket costs $50–$500 depending on the state. In Iowa, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Iowa Department of Transportation — Motor Vehicle Division).

We do not publish an exact Iowa dollar figure, because the fine is set within the state's traffic code and the number that applies to you is the one printed on your citation or shown by the court. The national range is only a rough guide to how widely states differ.

Bottom line: the national range is $50–$500, but your Iowa amount is whatever the court sets on your citation, plus a statutory surcharge and court costs.

If your citation shows no amount, the court clerk's office is the place to ask what is owed and what your options are.

How is the fine for running a red light set in Iowa?

The fine is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The statutes are available through Iowa statutes via Justia, which is a useful place to read the underlying rules on traffic signals.

That means two drivers cited for the same violation can see different paperwork, depending on how the court handles the case and what the citation says. We cannot tell you which number applies to you.

Here is what to look for on your citation:

  • The violation described and the statute or ordinance cited.
  • Whether a scheduled fine amount is listed.
  • The court that will handle the case and how to contact it.
  • Any instructions on paying or contesting.

Read these before you decide what to do. The citation itself is the controlling document for your amount.

What extra costs are added to the fine?

A statutory surcharge and court costs are added to every scheduled fine in Iowa. So the amount you actually pay is higher than the base fine alone.

We do not state the size of these add-ons, because we only publish figures we can source. Ask the court clerk for the total, not just the base fine.

When comparing what you owe with a number you saw online, check whether it is the base fine or the full total. Many quoted figures leave out surcharges and costs, which can make a ticket look cheaper than it is.

  • Base fine: set by the court on the citation.
  • Statutory surcharge: added by law.
  • Court costs: added to the scheduled fine.

How many points does a red light ticket carry in Iowa?

Iowa tracks convictions for its habitual-offender and suspension rules rather than a public running point total for ordinary speeding. We therefore do not give a point value for a red light ticket.

In practice, the thing that matters is whether the ticket ends in a conviction. A conviction on your driving record can count toward the state's habitual-offender and suspension rules, while a ticket that does not result in a conviction generally would not.

For the official description of how your record is handled, see the Iowa Department of Transportation — Motor Vehicle Division. For a comparison of how states treat traffic offenses, the Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a state laws reference.

Does a red light ticket raise insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums ~20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Iowa-specific one, and not a prediction for your policy.

Whether your rate changes depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the ticket is resolved. We do not recommend any insurer and cannot say what yours will do.

Two practical points:

  • The insurance effect usually follows a conviction, so how the case ends matters as much as the fine.
  • The cost of higher premiums can exceed the ticket itself over time, so it is worth weighing when you decide how to respond.

Your insurer or agent can explain how your own policy treats a moving violation.

Can a red light ticket be dismissed or reduced?

That depends on the court and the facts of your case. We cannot give legal advice or predict an outcome.

In general, a driver cited for a red light violation has a few paths:

  • Pay the ticket. This usually resolves the matter, but it may count as a conviction on your record.
  • Contest it. You can ask the court to hear the case, following the instructions on your citation.
  • Contact the court. The clerk can explain what the court allows and what the deadlines are for your case.

Because the fine is set by the court and a surcharge and costs are added, it is worth asking about the full amount before deciding. If you want advice on your situation, a licensed attorney is the right person to ask. We do not recommend a specific one.

What happens if I don't pay a red light ticket in Iowa?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. The court that issued the citation controls what happens next, and the consequences can include added costs or action affecting your driving privileges.

We do not list specific deadlines or penalties here, since we only publish figures we can source. The deadlines and instructions on your citation are what count.

If you cannot pay the full amount or cannot meet a deadline, contact the court before the deadline passes rather than after.

The Iowa Department of Transportation — Motor Vehicle Division is the official source for how your driving record and license status are handled.
