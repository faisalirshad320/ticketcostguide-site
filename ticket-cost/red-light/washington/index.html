How much is a red light ticket in Washington?

Nationally, red light tickets cost $50–$500 depending on the state. In Washington, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Washington State Department of Licensing). We do not list a single Washington dollar figure, because the amount printed on your ticket is the one that counts.

The national range is a wide band, and it is not a prediction for Washington. Treat it as context for how much the penalty can differ from state to state.

The number to rely on is the amount on your own citation or on the issuing court's notice. If the two ever seem to disagree, contact the court clerk listed on the ticket.

How is the red light fine set in Washington?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. Washington handles most traffic violations as infractions in courts of limited jurisdiction. Those courts follow the IRLJ (Infraction Rules for Courts of Limited Jurisdiction) schedule.

Statutory assessments are built into the IRLJ schedule amounts. That means the schedule figure is meant to be the full amount, not a base fine with surcharges stacked on top. Even so, read the citation line by line, since it is the document that states what the court wants from you.

  • The violation named on the citation.
  • The amount due, as set by the court.
  • The court and the instructions for responding.

Statutes are available through Washington statutes via Justia if you want to read the traffic code yourself.

How many points does a red light ticket add in Washington?

None. Washington has no point system. There is no running point total that adds up toward a suspension threshold.

That does not make a conviction harmless. Suspensions follow repeat moving-violation convictions within set windows. The risk comes from how many convictions land on your record in a given period, not from a points score.

For your own driving record and any license status questions, the Washington State Department of Licensing is the agency that maintains that information. We are not able to tell you how a particular ticket will affect your license.

No points does not mean no consequences. A conviction still counts as a moving violation, and repeat convictions within the set windows can lead to suspension.

Does a red light ticket raise insurance rates in Washington?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Washington-specific one.

Your own change could be smaller, larger, or nothing at all. It depends on your insurer, your driving history, and how the ticket is resolved. Insurers set their own rules, and we do not recommend any insurer.

  • The ~20% figure is an average across the US, not a promise.
  • The effect usually comes from a conviction, not from receiving the citation alone.
  • Your insurer or agent can tell you how they treat moving violations.

Because the fine is often smaller than a premium increase that lasts several renewals, many drivers weigh the insurance effect alongside the ticket amount.

Can a red light ticket be dismissed or reduced in Washington?

Washington courts allow you to respond to an infraction in more than one way. The citation lists the options and the court you must respond to. We cannot say which option fits your situation, and this page is not legal advice.

In general, the choices on an infraction notice include:

  • Paying the amount, which is typically treated as accepting the infraction.
  • Contesting the infraction and asking the court to hear the case.
  • Asking the court about other responses it offers, if any.

Because the fine is set by the court, any change to the amount would also come from the court. Read the instructions on the citation, and ask the clerk about procedure. For questions about your specific case, a licensed attorney is the right source, and we do not recommend any particular one.

What happens if I don't pay or respond?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts expect a response, and failing to give one can bring added consequences. The exact steps depend on the court and on the instructions printed on your notice.

Because Washington suspensions follow repeat moving-violation convictions within set windows, an unresolved ticket can also interact with your driving record. Check with the issuing court about what happens in your case.

If you are unsure of the deadline or the amount, call the court clerk named on the citation before the response date passes.

We do not state deadlines or late fees here, because those come from the court and the citation rather than from the figures we have sourced.
