How much is a red light ticket in New York?

Nationally, red light tickets run $50–$500 depending on the state. In New York, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (New York State DMV).

We do not print a single New York fine here, because the amount that applies to you is the one on your ticket or court notice. Any site quoting one exact number for every New York driver is simplifying.

The fine is only part of the bill. A mandatory surcharge of $88–$93 is added, and a separate DMV assessment of $300+ can apply if your points reach 6 or more.

Think of the cost in three layers: the court fine, the mandatory surcharge, and any longer-term costs such as the Driver Responsibility Assessment and higher insurance premiums.

How is the fine for running a red light set in New York?

The fine is set by the court within the state's traffic code. It is not a flat number applied the same way everywhere, and it is not something a website can calculate for you.

The amount is shown on the citation or on the court's notice. If the figure is unclear, the court clerk's office that handles your ticket is the place to confirm what is owed.

The text of New York's Vehicle and Traffic Law is available through New York statutes via Justia. It is useful for reading the rules yourself, but the amount you owe still comes from the court handling your case.

  • Where to look: the fine line on your citation or court notice.
  • Who decides: the court, within the state's traffic code.
  • What to avoid: assuming the national range is what you will pay.

What extra costs come on top of the fine?

Two charges sit on top of the court fine in New York. The first is the mandatory surcharge, which is $88–$93.

The second is the DMV's Driver Responsibility Assessment, which starts at $300+. It is a separate charge from the court fine, and it is triggered when you reach 6 or more points.

This is why two drivers with the same fine can end up with very different total costs. Adding up the fine and the surcharge gives you the immediate cost. The assessment, where it applies, is an additional cost on top of that.

Check your notice for how the surcharge is listed. Some drivers see only the fine at first and miss the added charges until they pay.

Details on how the DMV applies its assessment are published by the New York State DMV.

How many points does a red light ticket carry in New York?

New York uses a points system tied to the type of violation. The state schedule we reviewed lists these tiers by how far over the limit a driver was:

  • 3 points (1–10 over)
  • 4 points (11–20)
  • 6 points (21–30)
  • 8 points (31–40)
  • 11 points (41+)

Those tiers are written for speed, so they do not directly tell you the points for a red light violation. Confirm the point value for your exact charge with the New York State DMV or on your court paperwork.

What matters most is the running total. Reaching 11 points in 18 months means a suspension. Reaching 6 or more points also triggers the separate $300+ Driver Responsibility Assessment.

If you already have points from earlier tickets, a red light conviction can push you over one of these thresholds even if the new ticket seems minor.

Does a red light ticket raise insurance in New York?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a New York quote.

Your own change depends on your insurer, your driving record and how the ticket is resolved. We do not recommend any insurer, and we cannot predict what yours will do.

A practical way to estimate the effect is to take your current premium and apply the national average of about 20% as a rough guide. Then compare it with the cost of the ticket itself. The insurance effect often outlasts the fine.

  • Ask your insurer how and when a conviction is reflected at renewal.
  • Keep your court paperwork showing how the ticket was resolved.

Can a red light ticket be dismissed or reduced?

That decision belongs to the court, and outcomes vary by case. We cannot say whether yours is a candidate, and this page is not legal advice.

In general, drivers who receive a ticket can pay it, or respond to the court to contest it or discuss the charge. Your citation or court notice explains the response options and how to make them.

Because a conviction affects points, surcharges and insurance, it helps to read what a plea would mean before you respond. Paying a ticket is usually treated as a plea of guilty, so it is worth knowing that before sending money.

  • Read the response instructions on the citation carefully.
  • Check how a conviction would change your point total.
  • If you want legal advice, you can speak with a licensed attorney of your own choosing.

What happens if I don't pay a red light ticket?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. Courts can add penalties for missed deadlines, and unresolved tickets can lead to action against your license.

The exact consequences depend on the court and on what the notice says. We do not state specific late fees here, because none are in our reviewed figures.

If you have missed a deadline, contact the court that issued the notice as soon as you can. The New York State DMV also publishes information on license status and suspensions.

Remember that the $88–$93 surcharge and any Driver Responsibility Assessment are separate from the fine. Paying only the fine amount may not close out the ticket.
