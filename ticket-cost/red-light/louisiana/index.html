How much is a red light ticket in Louisiana?

Nationally, red light tickets run $50–$500 depending on the state. In Louisiana, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Louisiana Office of Motor Vehicles).

We do not publish a single Louisiana dollar figure, because the amount that matters is the one printed on your ticket or on the court's notice. The national range is context only. It is not a Louisiana fine schedule.

Read the citation first. The amount and instructions on it, or on the court's notice, are what you owe or must respond to.

The fine is only part of the cost. Court costs can be added, and they are covered below.

How is a red light fine set in Louisiana?

The court sets the fine within the state's traffic code. The code is published by the state and available through Louisiana statutes via Justia.

That means two drivers cited for the same violation can see different totals, depending on the court that handles the case and the details of the citation.

Things worth checking on your paperwork:

  • The court name and parish listed on the citation.
  • The violation as it is described.
  • Any stated amount, and whether it is the fine alone or includes costs.
  • The instructions for paying or responding.

If the amount is unclear, the court clerk named on the ticket is the right place to ask. TicketCostGuide cannot tell you what your specific court will charge.

Why can court costs be higher than the fine?

Local court costs vary by parish and often exceed the fine. This is a locked fact from our state context, and it is the main reason a Louisiana ticket can feel more expensive than the fine suggests.

Costs and surcharges are separate from the fine itself. They are set locally, so the same fine can produce a different total in one parish than in another.

When you compare what you owe with a national number, keep in mind that the national range of $50–$500 describes fines. It does not describe what a particular Louisiana parish adds on top.

  • Ask whether the amount you were given is the all-in total.
  • Ask which costs, if any, are added if you pay late or contest the ticket.
  • Keep a receipt or confirmation for any payment.

How many points does a red light ticket add in Louisiana?

Louisiana has no point system. License action follows repeat convictions and specific offenses (Louisiana Office of Motor Vehicles).

So there is no point value to look up for this violation. What matters is your overall record: whether this is a first conviction or one of several, and whether any offense carries its own consequences.

For your own driving record and license status, the Office of Motor Vehicles is the official source. Its site, expresslane.org, is the place to start.

Other states handle this differently, and some do assign points. Do not assume a point count from another state's guide applies in Louisiana.

Does a red light ticket raise insurance in Louisiana?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Louisiana-specific one (Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws).

Your own change could be higher, lower or zero. It depends on your insurer, your history and how the ticket is resolved. We do not recommend any insurer, and we cannot predict your rate.

Practical steps:

  • Ask your insurer how a conviction would be treated before you decide how to respond to the ticket.
  • Compare the possible premium change with the cost of the fine plus court costs.
  • Remember that the outcome of the case, not only the ticket itself, usually drives what appears on your record.

Can a red light ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Often there are choices beyond paying right away, but what is available depends on the court and the citation. We cannot say which options your parish offers.

Common paths people look into:

  • Pay the ticket. This usually resolves the matter quickly, but it may count as a conviction.
  • Contest it. You can ask the court how to respond and whether a hearing is available.
  • Ask about alternatives. Some courts may offer other resolutions. Ask the clerk what, if anything, applies to your case.

Because the court sets the fine and local costs vary by parish, the cost of each option can differ. Ask the court for the full amount before you decide.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice or recommend attorneys. If you are weighing a contested case, consider speaking with a licensed Louisiana attorney.

What happens if I don't pay a red light ticket?

Ignoring a ticket rarely makes it go away. Courts generally expect a response by the date on the citation or notice, and what follows a missed deadline depends on the court.

Louisiana has no point system, but license action follows repeat convictions and specific offenses. A pattern of unresolved or repeated tickets is a poor position to be in.

Costs can also grow. Local court costs vary by parish and often exceed the fine, so a late or contested matter can end up more expensive than a prompt response.

  • Find the response deadline on your citation or notice.
  • Contact the court clerk if you missed it or are unsure.
  • Check your license status with the Office of Motor Vehicles.
