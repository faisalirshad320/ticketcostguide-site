How much is a red light ticket in Kansas?

The exact amount is printed on your citation or set by the court that handles it. We do not publish a single statewide Kansas dollar figure because the court sets it within the state's traffic code.

For context, red light tickets nationally run $50–$500 depending on the state (Kansas Department of Revenue — Division of Vehicles). That is a national range, not a Kansas price list.

Your total is usually the fine plus court costs. Court costs are added by the municipal or district court, so two people with the same violation can owe different totals.

The most reliable number is the one on your citation, or the one the court clerk gives you for your case.

How is a Kansas red light fine set?

In Kansas, the court sets the fine for this violation within the state's traffic code. The statewide rules are the outer frame, and the court that hears your case fills in the amount.

Which court handles your ticket depends on where the violation happened. A municipal court handles many city-issued tickets, while a district court may handle others.

  • Fine: set by the court on the citation.
  • Court costs: added by the municipal or district court.
  • Total owed: the sum the court lists for your case.

If your ticket looks different from a neighbor's or friend's, that is normal. Check the citation for the court name and its payment instructions rather than relying on general estimates.

You can read the underlying traffic code through Kansas statutes via Justia.

How many points does a red light ticket add in Kansas?

None, because Kansas has no demerit-point system. You will not see a point value attached to this ticket.

That does not mean a conviction is harmless. Three moving-violation convictions within 12 months can trigger suspension.

Think of it as a count, not a score. Each moving-violation conviction on your record matters, especially if you collect several in a short window.

Ask the court how a plea or payment will be recorded. Paying a ticket is generally treated as admitting the violation, so understand the record consequences before you pay.

For license status questions, the Kansas Department of Revenue — Division of Vehicles is the agency to check.

Does a red light ticket raise insurance in Kansas?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Kansas-specific rate.

Your own change could be higher, lower or zero. It depends on your insurer, your driving record and how the ticket is recorded.

  • Insurers set their own rules, so results differ between companies.
  • A clean record before the ticket may soften the effect.
  • Several violations close together tend to matter more than one.

We do not recommend any insurer. If you are worried about the effect, ask your insurer how a conviction would be treated at renewal.

Other national road-safety and state-law context is available from the Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws.

Can a Kansas red light ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but there is no guarantee. What is available depends on the court and the facts of your case.

Your usual choices are to pay, to contest the ticket, or to ask the court what other resolutions it offers. Each court sets its own process, so read the citation for deadlines and instructions.

  1. Pay: simple, but it generally means accepting the violation on your record.
  2. Contest: you ask for a hearing and present your side.
  3. Ask the clerk: find out whether the court offers any alternative resolution.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice. If the stakes are high, such as a third conviction in 12 months, consider speaking with a licensed Kansas attorney of your own choosing.

Keep your citation, any photos and your notes about the intersection and signal. Whatever you decide, act before the date on the ticket.

What happens if I don't pay a Kansas red light ticket?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. Courts can add consequences when a citation goes unanswered, and those depend on the court handling it.

Because court costs are added by the municipal or district court, a late or unresolved ticket can leave you dealing with that court directly. Contact the clerk named on the citation as soon as you realize a deadline has passed.

  • Do not assume the ticket will go away.
  • Do not guess at the amount owed; ask the court.
  • Keep proof of any payment or hearing request.

An unresolved ticket can also complicate a later license issue, especially if you already have other moving-violation convictions inside a 12-month window.

Frequently asked questions

How much is a red light ticket in Kansas?

The exact amount is set by the court on your citation, within the state's traffic code. Nationally, red light tickets run $50–$500 depending on the state. Court costs are added by the municipal or district court, so your total depends on which court handles the ticket.

Does Kansas use points for red light tickets?

No. Kansas has no demerit-point system, so a red light ticket does not add points. However, three moving-violation convictions within 12 months can trigger suspension, so the number of convictions on your record still matters.

Will a red light ticket raise my insurance in Kansas?

It might. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average, which is a national figure rather than a Kansas rate. Your actual change depends on your insurer and driving record, so ask your insurer how it treats convictions.

Are there court costs on top of the fine?

Yes. Court costs are added by the municipal or district court. The amount varies by court, so the total you owe can differ from the base fine. Check the citation or ask the court clerk for the full amount before paying.

Can my license be suspended for red light tickets in Kansas?

Three moving-violation convictions within 12 months can trigger suspension. A single red light ticket alone is not described that way in the facts we use, but it counts toward that total if you are convicted. Check your status with the Division of Vehicles.

Can I fight a red light ticket in Kansas?

You can generally contest a citation instead of paying it, and the court can tell you how. Results are not guaranteed. Paying usually means accepting the violation. TicketCostGuide gives no legal advice, so consider a licensed attorney if the stakes are high.
