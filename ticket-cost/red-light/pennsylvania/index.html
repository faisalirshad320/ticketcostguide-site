How much is a red light ticket in Pennsylvania?

Nationally, red light tickets fall in a range of $50–$500 depending on the state. In Pennsylvania, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (PennDOT Driver and Vehicle Services).

We do not publish a single Pennsylvania dollar figure, because the number you owe is the one printed on your citation or court notice. Any exact figure on a third-party site should be checked against that document.

The $50–$500 range is a national range across states. It is not a Pennsylvania fine schedule.

The base fine is only part of what you pay. The next section explains why the total is usually higher.

How is the fine set, and why is the total higher than the base fine?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The amount appears on the citation, and the court handling your case is the final word on what you owe.

Court costs, the EMS surcharge and other statutory fees typically exceed the base fine. That means a ticket with a modest base fine can still produce a total that surprises people.

  • Base fine: set by the court on the citation.
  • Court costs: added by the court handling the case.
  • EMS surcharge and other statutory fees: added under state law.

When you check your balance, look for the grand total rather than the fine line alone. If the notice lists several line items, those are the pieces above. You can read the underlying statutes through Pennsylvania statutes via Justia.

How many points does a red light ticket add in Pennsylvania?

The state context we rely on describes the points system this way: 2–5 points for 11+ mph over, and 6 points triggers a departmental exam/hearing. High-speed convictions add a 15-day suspension at 31+ over.

Those figures are written in terms of speeding. They do not tell us a specific point value for a red light conviction, and we will not guess one. Whether points attach to your red light ticket depends on how it was charged and the conviction on your record.

To find out what, if anything, your ticket adds to your record, check the citation and ask PennDOT Driver and Vehicle Services.

The key threshold to know is 6 points, which triggers a departmental exam/hearing. If you already carry points from other violations, a new conviction matters more.

Does a red light ticket raise my insurance?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Pennsylvania-specific one.

Your own change could be higher, lower or zero. It depends on your insurer, your driving history and whether the ticket ends in a conviction. We do not recommend insurers, and we cannot predict your rate.

  • Ask your insurer how a conviction would be treated at renewal.
  • Compare that with the cost of any option you are considering.
  • Remember that a conviction, not just a citation, is what usually drives pricing.

Because premiums can rise over multiple renewals, a cheaper ticket is not always the cheaper outcome overall.

Can a red light ticket be dismissed or reduced?

You generally have a few paths: pay the amount on the citation, contest the ticket in court, or ask the court what it allows. Paying usually means accepting the violation, with the consequences described above.

Contesting means the court decides the outcome. Whether a reduction or dismissal is possible depends on the facts, the court and the charge. We cannot say what is available in your case.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice or recommend attorneys. For advice on your specific citation, consult a licensed Pennsylvania attorney.

Before deciding, compare the total on the citation (fine plus costs and surcharges) with the possible effect on points and insurance. Both can matter more than the base fine.

What happens if I don't pay a red light ticket?

The citation and any court notice explain the deadline and what the court can do if you miss it. Those instructions are specific to your court, so follow them rather than relying on general rules.

In general, ignoring a ticket tends to make things worse. Added costs and license problems are common outcomes in many states, though the details vary.

  • Read the response instructions on the citation right away.
  • Contact the court listed on the notice if anything is unclear.
  • Keep copies of anything you send or receive.

For official information on license status, use PennDOT Driver and Vehicle Services. For how other states treat traffic laws, see the Governors Highway Safety Association State Laws page.
