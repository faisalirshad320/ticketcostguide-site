How much is a red light ticket in Maryland?

Nationally, red light tickets run $50–$500 depending on the state. In Maryland the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Maryland Motor Vehicle Administration).

This page does not give an exact Maryland dollar amount. The number printed on your own citation, or the one a judge sets, is the one that counts.

The $50–$500 range is a national range. It is not a Maryland price list, and your total may differ depending on how the citation is handled.

If you cannot find an amount on the paperwork, the court named on the citation or the MVA can tell you what applies to your case.

How is the fine set: prepay amount or judge-set fine?

A Maryland citation generally gives you a choice. You can pay the amount listed, or you can ask for a court date.

Requesting a court date replaces the prepay amount with a judge-set fine up to $500. In other words, the number on the citation stops being the final number once a judge decides the case.

  • Prepay: you pay the amount listed on the citation and the matter is usually resolved.
  • Court date: a judge sets the fine, up to $500, within the state's traffic code.

The statutes are published in the Maryland code (Maryland statutes via Justia). The exact fine for this violation is set by the court within that code, so we do not state a fixed figure.

How many points does a red light ticket carry in Maryland?

Maryland's point tiers are: 1 point (9 or less over), 2 points (10–29 over), and 5 points (30+ over). Eight points leads to suspension and 12 points to revocation.

Those tiers are written by how far over a limit a driver was. A red light violation has no speed measurement, so do not assume which tier applies to you.

Check the citation for any listed points. If it is unclear, ask the MVA what will be added to your record (Maryland Motor Vehicle Administration).

Points matter because they add up. Reaching 8 points means suspension, and 12 means revocation.

The type of citation can also matter, for example an officer's ticket versus a camera notice. Your paperwork should say which one you have and what it means for your record.

Does a red light ticket raise insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Maryland-specific one.

Your own change depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the case ends. A conviction is more likely to affect rates than a case that does not end in one.

  • Ask your insurer how it treats a moving violation before you decide how to respond.
  • Compare the fine with a possible premium increase over several years.
  • Remember that paying the prepay amount may mean accepting the violation.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer and cannot predict your quote.

Can a red light ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Possibly, but that is decided by the court, not by this guide. Requesting a court date puts the case in front of a judge, who sets any fine up to $500.

Going to court is a trade-off. The prepay amount is known in advance, while a judge-set fine is not, and it can be up to $500.

  1. Read the citation for deadlines and the court listed.
  2. Decide whether to prepay or request a court date.
  3. If you request a court date, gather your paperwork and any evidence you plan to present.
  4. Keep copies of everything you file.

What happens if I don't pay a red light ticket in Maryland?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Deadlines on the citation are there for a reason, and missing them can lead to further action by the court or the MVA.

Points and license status are also at stake. Eight points means suspension and 12 means revocation, so unresolved violations are worth taking seriously (Maryland Motor Vehicle Administration).

If you cannot pay by the deadline, contact the court on the citation before it passes. Do not wait for a reminder.

What should I check first on my citation?

Start with five things. They tell you which path you are on and what could change the cost.

  • The amount listed, if any, and whether it is a prepay amount.
  • The deadline to pay or request a court date.
  • The court named on the citation.
  • Any points or record consequences described.
  • Whether it was issued by an officer or came from a camera.

Other states' rules can differ. The Governors Highway Safety Association tracks state traffic laws if you want wider context (Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws).
