How much is a red light ticket in Texas?

The honest short answer is that it depends on the court named on your citation. Nationally the range is $50–$500 depending on the state, and in Texas the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Texas Department of Public Safety).

We do not publish a single Texas fine figure because we have not sourced one that applies to every court. Anything you see quoted as "the" Texas red light fine should be checked against your own ticket.

The $50–$500 range is a national figure covering many states. It is not a Texas fine schedule.

The number you actually pay is the fine plus court costs. State court costs are uniform, while municipal add-ons vary by court, so two Texas drivers with the same violation can see different totals.

How is a Texas red light fine set?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The code gives the framework, and the court that handles your citation determines the amount that appears on it. You can browse the statutes through Texas statutes via Justia.

Three pieces usually make up what you owe:

  • The fine, set by the court on the citation.
  • State court costs, which are uniform across Texas.
  • Municipal add-ons, which vary by court.

Because of that last item, the same offense can cost more in one city than in another. The citation, the court's website or the clerk's office will show the total for your case.

If the figure on the ticket and the figure on a payment portal look different, ask the clerk to explain the difference line by line.

How many points does a red light ticket add in Texas?

Texas abolished its driver-point and surcharge program in 2019. That means there is no point value to look up for a red light conviction under that old system.

License action now follows specific offenses and repeat-conviction rules (Texas Department of Public Safety). A single red light conviction is therefore judged under those rules rather than by adding up points.

No points does not mean no consequences. A conviction can still appear on your driving record, and repeat convictions can matter under the state's rules.

If you are worried about your license status, the Texas Department of Public Safety is the place to confirm how your record is treated. We cannot tell you how your particular history will be handled.

Does a red light ticket raise insurance in Texas?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums ~20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Texas-specific one, and it is not a promise about your own policy.

What your insurer does depends on its own rules, your driving history, and whether the ticket ends in a conviction. Insurers set their own rates, and we do not recommend any insurer.

Things that commonly affect the outcome:

  • Whether the ticket results in a conviction on your record.
  • Any other recent tickets or claims.
  • When your policy comes up for renewal.

If you want to know how a ticket could affect your premium, ask your insurer directly how it treats a moving-violation conviction before you decide how to respond to the citation.

Can a red light ticket be dismissed or reduced in Texas?

Sometimes, but we cannot promise it, and we do not give legal advice. What is available depends on the court and the facts on your citation.

Common routes people ask the court about include:

  • Paying the ticket. This usually ends the case, but it generally means accepting a conviction.
  • Contesting it. You can plead not guilty and ask for a hearing or trial.
  • Court programs. Some courts may offer options such as a payment plan, a driving safety course or deferred disposition. Availability varies by court and by citation.

Because municipal add-ons vary by court, the cost of an option can differ too. Ask the court clerk what is offered for your specific citation and what it would cost in total.

If you are unsure whether to pay or contest, consider speaking with a licensed Texas attorney. TicketCostGuide does not recommend specific attorneys.

What happens if I don't pay a Texas red light ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. The court that issued it can take further action, which may add costs or affect your license, depending on the court and the rules that apply to your case.

Because the exact steps vary by court, the best move is to read the instructions on the citation and contact the clerk before the deadline listed there. We do not state a deadline here because yours is printed on your ticket.

If you cannot afford the full amount, ask the court whether it allows payment plans or other arrangements. Many courts prefer that you ask early rather than miss a date.

Texas license action follows specific offenses and repeat-conviction rules (Texas Department of Public Safety), so unresolved tickets are worth taking seriously.

What should I check on my Texas citation?

Before you pay or respond, look at the ticket itself. It holds the details that decide your next step.

  1. The court named. This determines the fine and any municipal add-ons.
  2. The amount listed. Confirm whether it includes court costs.
  3. The response deadline. Missing it can create new problems.
  4. The offense description. Make sure it matches what happened.
  5. Your options. Ask the clerk what the court allows, including any payment plan.

Keep copies of anything you send or receive. If the ticket will not be handled by your own local court, ask the clerk how to respond from out of town.
