How much is a red light ticket in Alaska?

The exact amount is set by the court on your citation. Nationally, red light fines run $50–$500 depending on the state, and in Alaska the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Alaska Division of Motor Vehicles).

We do not list a single Alaska dollar figure on this page. The national range is a comparison point, not a promise of what you will owe.

Your citation is the most reliable source for your amount. If the paper or notice does not show one, contact the court listed on it.

Where the court or county differs, the amount can differ too. Two drivers cited for similar conduct may see different totals depending on where the ticket was issued and what the court adds.

How is the fine for running a red light set?

In Alaska, the court sets the fine for this violation within the state's traffic code. The code itself is published online through Alaska statutes via Justia.

That means the number on your citation reflects the court's schedule and the circumstances written on the ticket. It is not a flat amount we can quote for every driver.

  • The violation: failing to stop for a red signal, as described on the citation.
  • The location: some places, such as work zones and school zones, carry doubled fines.
  • The court: the court named on the citation controls the amount and the payment process.

If you want the underlying rule, search the traffic code text for the section cited on your ticket and compare it with the citation.

Do work-zone or school-zone red light tickets cost more?

Yes. Work-zone and school-zone violations carry doubled fines under the schedule.

If your red light violation took place in or near one of these zones, expect the court amount to reflect that. The citation should say where the violation happened, so check the location field.

Doubling applies to the fine under the schedule. We do not have a locked figure for any other court add-ons, so we do not estimate them here.

If you think the zone was marked incorrectly or the location on the ticket is wrong, that is a question to raise with the court, not something this guide can resolve.

How many points does a red light ticket add in Alaska?

The points system assigns 2–6 demerit points by speed. A suspension applies at 12 points in 12 months.

Those figures are expressed by speed, which is a poor fit for a red light violation that may involve no speed measurement. We cannot tell you how many points, if any, your particular red light citation adds.

To find out, check your driving record or ask the Alaska Division of Motor Vehicles. Camera-based citations and officer-issued citations may be treated differently, so confirm which type you received.

  • Points matter most when you already have other violations in the same 12 months.
  • Reaching 12 points in 12 months leads to suspension.
  • Ask about the point count before you decide to pay or contest.

Does a red light ticket raise insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Alaska-specific rate.

Whether your insurer raises your rate depends on the company, your record and whether the ticket ends in a conviction. We do not recommend any insurer, and we cannot predict your quote.

Some things to look at when you review your options:

  • Whether paying the ticket counts as a conviction on your record.
  • When your policy next renews, since insurers often review records at renewal.
  • What your insurer's own policy documents say about moving violations.

For a general view of how states treat traffic offenses, the Governors Highway Safety Association state laws page is a useful reference.

Can a red light ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome is up to the court. This guide does not give legal advice, and we do not recommend any attorney.

In general, a driver who receives a citation can pay it, or contest it through the court process described on the citation. Paying usually resolves the matter quickly, but it may count as a conviction. Contesting takes more effort and may or may not change the result.

  • Read the citation: it lists the court, the violation and the response instructions.
  • Check the details: location, date, signal type and your vehicle information should all be correct.
  • Ask the court: court staff can explain which options are available for your citation.

If the stakes are high, such as a record that is already close to the suspension threshold, consider speaking with a licensed professional about your situation.

What happens if I don't pay a red light ticket?

An unpaid citation can lead to further consequences from the court and the licensing agency. We do not have locked figures for late fees or deadlines, so we do not state any here.

The citation lists the response instructions and the court's contact details. Missing the response date is a common way a small matter becomes a bigger one.

  • Look at the date on the citation as soon as you receive it.
  • If you cannot pay in full, ask the court whether it offers other arrangements.
  • Keep a record of anything you send or receive.

For license questions, the Alaska Division of Motor Vehicles is the agency to ask.
