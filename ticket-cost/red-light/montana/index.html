How much is a red light ticket in Montana?

Nationally, red light fines run $50–$500 depending on the state. In Montana, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Montana Department of Justice — Motor Vehicle Division).

We do not list a single Montana dollar figure here, because the amount you owe depends on what the court assigns and on any surcharges it adds. The number printed on your citation, or the figure the court gives you when you ask, is the one that counts.

The $50–$500 range is a national spread across all states. It is not a prediction of what a Montana court will assign to your ticket.

If the citation shows no amount, contact the clerk of the court named on it. Ask for the total due, including any surcharges, before you pay or decide to contest.

How is a Montana red light fine set?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. Montana statutes are available through Montana statutes via Justia if you want to read the traffic provisions yourself.

The base fine is only part of the cost. Court surcharges are added to the statutory fine, which means the total you pay is higher than the fine alone.

Because the court sets the amount, two drivers cited for the same violation could see different totals on their citations. Check the document you were handed rather than relying on a general estimate.

  • Statutory fine: set by the court within the traffic code.
  • Surcharges: added on top of the fine.
  • Total due: the combined amount the court tells you to pay.

How many points does a red light ticket carry in Montana?

Convictions carry conviction points toward Montana's habitual-offender threshold. That threshold is 30 points in 3 years, and reaching it means revocation of driving privileges (Montana Department of Justice — Motor Vehicle Division).

We do not state a point value for a red light conviction, because our sourced facts do not give one. The Motor Vehicle Division is the place to confirm how many points apply to your specific conviction.

Points attach to a conviction, not just to being cited. That is why the outcome in court matters, not only the amount you pay.

Keep track of every moving-violation conviction on your record. The 30-points-in-3-years threshold looks at the combined total, not at each ticket alone.

Does a red light ticket raise insurance in Montana?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Montana-specific one.

Your own change depends on your insurer, your driving record and how the company treats the conviction at renewal. We do not recommend any insurer and cannot predict your rate.

The practical point is that the fine is not the whole cost. A conviction can add to your premium over time, which may outweigh the fine itself.

  • Ask your insurer how it treats a moving-violation conviction before you decide how to respond.
  • Compare the possible premium change with the cost of the ticket, not just the fine.
  • Remember the ~20% figure is an average, so your result could be higher or lower.

Can a Montana red light ticket be dismissed or reduced?

You generally have a few options after being cited: pay the amount, or contest the citation in court. What is available to you depends on the court and the details on your citation.

Paying usually resolves the matter quickly, but it can mean accepting a conviction. A conviction is what brings conviction points toward the habitual-offender threshold, so it is worth weighing before you pay.

Contesting means appearing before the court and presenting your side. Whether a case is dismissed or the outcome changes is up to the court, and we cannot predict it.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice or recommend an attorney. For advice on your case, speak with a licensed Montana attorney or ask the court clerk about procedure.

Read the citation closely. It should say which court handles it and how to respond.

What happens if I don't pay a red light ticket in Montana?

Ignoring a citation rarely makes it go away. Courts can add consequences when a ticket goes unanswered, and licensing consequences can follow in some situations.

We do not list specific deadlines or penalty amounts here because our sourced facts do not give them. Your citation and the court clerk are the reliable sources for your response date.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court before the due date. Ask what it offers rather than staying silent.

  • Find the response date on your citation.
  • Contact the issuing court if you have questions about the amount or the date.
  • Confirm any surcharges so you know the full total.

How does Montana compare with other states?

Red light fines vary widely across the country, from about $50 to $500 depending on the state. Montana does not publish a flat figure for this violation in our sources, because the court sets the amount on the citation.

Traffic laws differ by state in other ways too, including how points work and how violations are enforced. The Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws page is a useful starting point for comparing state traffic safety laws.

If you hold a license from another state or are passing through Montana, your home state may have its own rules about out-of-state citations. Check with that state's licensing agency.
