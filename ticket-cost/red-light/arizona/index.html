How much is a red light ticket in Arizona?

Nationally, red light tickets run $50–$500 depending on the state. In Arizona, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Arizona Department of Transportation — Motor Vehicle Division).

That means there is no single Arizona price we can quote. The number printed on your citation, or on the notice from the court, is the figure that matters.

We do not publish an exact Arizona dollar amount because the amount is not fixed in the sources we rely on. Any site that gives you one precise figure for every driver is likely simplifying.

The national $50–$500 range is a national figure. It is not an Arizona schedule, and your own ticket may fall anywhere the court sets it.

How is the fine set for running a red light in Arizona?

The exact fine for this violation in Arizona is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The code gives the framework, and the court handling your case decides the amount that appears on the citation.

You can read the Arizona traffic statutes through Arizona statutes via Justia. We do not cite a specific section number here, since we cannot confirm one from our locked sources.

Because the court sets the amount, two drivers cited for the same act can see different totals. The court, the circumstances and any fees added to the case can all play a part.

  • Read the citation for the stated amount or a court date.
  • Check the court's own notice or website for payment instructions.
  • Ask the court clerk if anything on the paperwork is unclear.

What surcharges and court fees get added?

Court surcharges and fees are added by the justice or municipal court, and amounts vary by court. We have no locked figure for these, so we do not state one.

In practice, this means the base fine is not always the full cost. The total you owe can be higher than the fine alone once the court adds its charges.

When you contact the court, ask for the total due, not only the fine. Ask whether the figure includes surcharges and fees, and whether a different amount applies if you choose a different option.

Two courts in Arizona may add different fees to the same kind of ticket. Always confirm the total with the court that holds your case.

How many points is a red light ticket in Arizona?

Arizona's points system gives 3 points for speeding, and 8+ points in 12 months means suspension or Traffic Survival School (Arizona Department of Transportation — Motor Vehicle Division).

Our locked facts do not list a point value for a red light violation. We will not guess, so check your citation, the court, or the Arizona MVD for the value that applies to your case.

The 8-point threshold is the number to watch. Points from several violations within 12 months can add up, so a red light ticket can matter more if you already have other recent tickets.

  • Find out whether your citation carries points.
  • Count any other violations from the past 12 months.
  • Ask the MVD about your own record if you are close to the limit.

Does a red light ticket raise insurance in Arizona?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Arizona-specific one.

Your own change could be higher, lower or none. It depends on your insurer, your record and whether the ticket ends in a conviction on your driving record.

We do not recommend any insurer or give advice about your policy. If you want to know how a ticket would affect your rate, ask your insurer directly.

The ~20% figure is an average across the US. Do not treat it as a quote for your premium.

Can a red light ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Whether a ticket can be dismissed or reduced is up to the court. Our sources do not list specific Arizona dismissal rules, so we cannot promise any outcome.

Generally, drivers have a few paths. They can pay the amount, contest the ticket at a hearing, or ask the court what other options exist for their case.

Since points and insurance can follow a conviction, it is worth reading the citation closely before you pay. Paying usually settles the matter, and it may also have consequences for your record.

  • Check the deadline on the citation.
  • Ask the court what options apply to your case.
  • Consider speaking with a licensed attorney if you want legal advice. TicketCostGuide does not give legal advice or recommend attorneys.

What happens if I don't pay a red light ticket in Arizona?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. Courts can add to the cost, and the matter can affect your license or driving record. Our locked sources do not give the exact steps or dollar amounts for Arizona, so contact the court for specifics.

Since court surcharges and fees are added by the justice or municipal court, a late or unresolved case may lead to a different total than the one first shown. Ask the court rather than assuming.

If you cannot pay by the due date, contact the court before it passes. Clerks can explain what your case allows.
