How much is a red light ticket in Kentucky?

Nationally, a red light ticket runs $50–$500 depending on the state. In Kentucky the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Kentucky Transportation Cabinet). We do not list a single Kentucky dollar figure because the court, not a fixed public price list, decides it.

That means two drivers cited for the same violation can see different totals depending on the court handling the case. The number printed on your citation, or the figure the court clerk gives you, is the one that counts.

The national range is a national range. It is not a promise that a Kentucky ticket will fall anywhere inside it.

The fine is also only part of the bill. Court costs are added, and they are covered in the next section.

How is a Kentucky red light fine set, and what are court costs?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. Kentucky statutes are published online through Justia, and the Kentucky Transportation Cabinet's licensing pages at drive.ky.gov explain how convictions affect a driving record.

Court costs are set by the district court and usually exceed the fine itself. In practical terms, the amount you pay at the clerk's window can be noticeably larger than the fine line on the citation.

When you look at your ticket, check for:

  • the fine amount, if one is printed
  • whether court costs are listed separately or added later
  • the court name and where to ask questions about the total

If the paperwork does not show a total, ask the district court clerk what the full amount is before you pay.

How many points does a red light ticket carry in Kentucky?

Kentucky's points system, as we have it sourced, works like this: 3 points for 10–15 over, 6 points for 16–25 over, and a hearing is triggered at 26+ over. Reaching 12 points in 2 years leads to suspension.

Those tiers are written in terms of how far over a limit a driver was, so they read as a speed-based schedule. Our sourced facts do not give a separate point value for a red light violation, and we will not guess one.

To find out whether your red light conviction adds points to your record, check the citation and ask the Kentucky Transportation Cabinet through drive.ky.gov.

The suspension threshold is the number to keep in mind if you already have other tickets. Points from separate convictions add together inside the same 2-year window.

Does a red light ticket raise my insurance?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Kentucky-specific one, and your own change depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the violation is recorded.

A conviction is what usually matters for rates. If a case ends in a dismissal, the record may look different from one ending in a guilty plea or a paid ticket, though we cannot say how any particular insurer will treat it.

Two practical points:

  • Paying the ticket is generally treated as an admission, so think about the rate effect before you pay.
  • Ask your insurer how it treats moving violations. We do not recommend any insurer.

Remember that a premium increase can last longer than the fine takes to pay, so the long-run cost may exceed the ticket itself.

Can a red light ticket be dismissed or reduced in Kentucky?

You generally have a few paths: pay the ticket, contest it in court, or ask the court what options it offers. Because the court sets the fine and court costs, the court clerk is the right place to ask what is available for your case.

Paying usually ends the matter quickly, but it is typically a conviction. Contesting means appearing in front of the district court, where you can present your side. Whether a charge is dismissed or reduced is up to the court.

Before choosing, consider:

  1. the total cost, including court costs that usually exceed the fine
  2. your current point total against the suspension threshold of 12 points in 2 years
  3. the likely effect on insurance, about 20% on average nationally

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot give legal advice or recommend an attorney. If the stakes are high, such as a licence at risk, you may want to speak with a licensed attorney of your own choosing.

What happens if I don't pay a red light ticket in Kentucky?

Ignoring a citation rarely makes it go away. The court that issued it controls what happens next, and our sourced facts do not list specific penalties or deadlines, so we will not state any.

What we can say is that court costs are set by the district court and usually exceed the fine, so extra charges from a missed obligation could raise the overall amount. Check the citation for the court date and instructions, and contact the clerk early if you are unsure.

If you cannot pay or cannot appear, contact the court before the date on the citation instead of waiting.

For effects on your licence, see the Kentucky Transportation Cabinet's licensing information at drive.ky.gov. Also compare any points you may receive against the 12-points-in-2-years suspension rule.
