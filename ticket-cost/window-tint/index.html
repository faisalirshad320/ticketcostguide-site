How much is a window tint ticket?

The typical US range for a first-offense window tint ticket is $25–$250 for a first offense; frequently a correctable equipment violation (Governors Highway Safety Association). That is a wide spread, and where you land depends on your state, your county and the court that handles the citation.

Many tint tickets are written as equipment violations, not moving violations. That is why so many of them can be fixed instead of simply paid.

Your citation, or the court's fine schedule, shows the amount you actually owe. Treat the range on this page as a guide, not a quote.

The range covers the base fine for a first offense. Some courts add costs, surcharges or administrative fees on top. Those add-ons vary by state, county and court, and we do not publish a single figure for them.

If your ticket is correctable, the amount you finally pay may be much smaller than the fine printed on the citation. Many states let you remove or replace the tint, get the vehicle inspected, and pay only a small fee.

Read the citation closely for three things:

  • The statute or code section you are charged under.
  • Whether the box for a correctable or "fix-it" violation is checked.
  • The court name, address and the date or deadline to respond.

Those details decide whether you can clear the ticket by fixing the car or whether you need to appear or pay.

Why do window tint fines and rules differ by state?

Each state writes its own tint law. Tint limits are set per window as visible-light-transmission (VLT) percentages that differ by state.

VLT is the share of visible light that passes through the glass and any film on it. A lower percentage means darker tint.

States usually set separate limits for different windows, such as the windshield, the front side windows, the rear side windows and the back glass. A film that is legal on the back windows in one state can be illegal on the front windows in the same state.

Because the percentages differ, a car that is legal where you bought it may not be legal where you drive it. Moving to a new state is a common way people end up with a tint ticket they did not expect.

Other rules vary as well:

  • Whether reflective or mirrored film is allowed.
  • Whether certain colors of tint are banned.
  • Whether sedans and SUVs or vans follow different limits.
  • Whether a sticker or certificate must show the film is compliant.

We do not list a state-by-state table of VLT numbers here, because those numbers need to come directly from current law. You can check the current rules for your state in the state codes (State codes via Justia) or in the summaries of state laws (Governors Highway Safety Association).

Check your state's current statute and the exact window it applies to before you buy or remove film. Dealer and installer advice is not a substitute for the law.

How many points does a window tint ticket add to my license?

Point values for window tint violations vary by state. We do not state a point figure here because none is locked in our sourced data.

Many tint tickets are equipment violations. Equipment violations are often treated differently from moving violations, but this is a state-by-state question. Do not assume your ticket carries no points without checking.

To find out, look at three places:

  1. The citation itself, which may list the statute and any point consequence.
  2. Your state's motor vehicle agency, which publishes its point system.
  3. The court clerk, who can tell you how the violation is classified.

If your ticket is a correctable violation, the court may dismiss it once you show the problem is fixed. A dismissed ticket generally does not end up as a conviction on your record, but the rules depend on the state and court.

It is also worth asking whether paying the fine counts as a conviction. In many places, paying a ticket is treated as admitting the violation. That can matter for your driving record even when the fine itself is small.

Does a window tint ticket raise my insurance?

The effect on insurance varies by insurer and by state. We cannot give a percentage or dollar change, because none is locked in our sourced data.

Insurers generally price risk using your driving record and claims history. Whether a tint ticket appears on that record depends on how your state classifies it and whether it ends in a conviction.

A correctable equipment violation that is dismissed after you fix the tint is less likely to show up as a conviction. A ticket you pay without contesting may be reported as a violation, depending on your state.

Some things that can affect the outcome:

  • Whether the violation was dismissed or ended in a conviction.
  • Whether your state reports equipment violations to insurers.
  • Your insurer's own underwriting rules.
  • Whether you have other recent tickets or claims.

If you are worried about your rates, ask your insurer how it treats equipment violations before you decide how to resolve the ticket. We do not recommend a specific insurer.

Repeat tint violations can also draw more attention, since repeat offenses escalate to larger fines and may build a pattern on your record.

Can a window tint ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Often, yes. Many states issue fix-it tickets that are dismissed on proof of correction for a small fee.

The usual process looks like this:

  1. Remove the illegal film or replace it with film that meets your state's VLT limits.
  2. Get the correction verified. Depending on the state, that may be a law enforcement officer, an inspection station or the court.
  3. Submit proof of correction to the court by the deadline on the citation.
  4. Pay the small dismissal fee if one applies.

The fee, the verifier and the deadline all vary by state and court. Missing the deadline can turn a fixable ticket into a fine, so read the citation carefully.

Keep copies of your receipts, inspection sign-off and any court confirmation. If the court loses your paperwork, these show you complied.

If your ticket is not correctable, you may still be able to ask the court for a reduction or to contest the citation. Whether a court will reduce a fine depends on local practice.

Other grounds to raise might include a measurement problem, a factory-tinted glass question or a valid medical exemption. Whether any of these apply to you is a legal question that depends on your state's law and your facts.

Are there medical exemptions for darker window tint?

Medical exemptions exist in most states. They are meant for people with conditions that make sun exposure harmful, so a darker tint than normal may be allowed.

The details vary by state. Typical features include:

  • A written statement or certification from a licensed medical professional.
  • An application to the state motor vehicle agency or another office.
  • A document or sticker you may need to keep in the vehicle.
  • Limits on how dark the tint can be, even with an exemption.

If you have an exemption, carry the paperwork when you drive. An officer who does not see proof may still write a ticket, and you would then need to show the exemption to the court.

An exemption you obtain after you receive a ticket may or may not help with that ticket. Ask the court clerk how your state handles that situation.

Exemptions generally do not follow you across state lines automatically. If you move or travel, check how the other state treats your documentation.

We cannot say whether you qualify. That depends on your state's statute and your medical situation. State codes are available through State codes via Justia.

What happens if I don't pay or get another tint ticket?

Ignoring a tint ticket usually makes things worse. Courts can add late fees, and some can take further action on your license or registration. The specific steps vary by state.

A fix-it ticket carries an extra risk. If you do not show proof of correction by the deadline, the court may treat the ticket as an unresolved violation and require you to pay the fine.

Repeat offenses escalate to larger fines. If you are stopped again with the same illegal tint, expect the penalty to be higher than the first time, and possibly not eligible for a simple fix-it dismissal.

To avoid that, take these steps:

  • Fix the tint fully, not just partly, before driving again.
  • Keep proof that the vehicle now complies.
  • Respond to the court by the date on the citation.
  • Ask about a payment plan or extension if cost is a problem.

If you are facing a license suspension, a warrant or a repeated violation, consider speaking with a licensed attorney in your state. TicketCostGuide does not recommend specific attorneys.

Frequently asked questions

How much is a window tint ticket?

The typical US range is $25–$250 for a first offense; frequently a correctable equipment violation. The exact amount varies by state, county and court, and court costs or surcharges may be added. Check your citation or the court's fine schedule for the amount that applies to you.

Can I get a window tint ticket dismissed?

Often yes. Many states issue fix-it tickets that are dismissed on proof of correction for a small fee. You generally fix the tint, get the correction verified and show proof to the court by the deadline. Rules and fees vary by state and court.

What is VLT and why does it matter for my ticket?

VLT means visible-light-transmission, the percentage of visible light that passes through a window and its film. Tint limits are set per window as VLT percentages that differ by state. A car with tint that is legal in one state can be illegal in another.

Does a second window tint ticket cost more?

Usually. Repeat offenses escalate to larger fines. The first-offense range is $25–$250, but repeat amounts vary by state. A repeat ticket may also not qualify for the same fix-it dismissal, so check your state's rules and the citation.

Are there medical exemptions for window tint?

Medical exemptions exist in most states. They usually require documentation from a medical professional and may need to be carried in the vehicle. The process, allowed darkness and paperwork vary by state, so check with your state's motor vehicle agency or the court.

Do window tint tickets add points or raise insurance?

It varies by state and insurer. Many tint tickets are equipment violations, which are often handled differently from moving violations, but point rules differ. Ask the court clerk how your ticket is classified and ask your insurer how it treats equipment violations.

What happens if I ignore a window tint ticket?

Ignoring it can lead to a missed fix-it deadline, added fees and possible action on your license or registration, depending on your state. A correctable ticket can become a fine if you do not show proof of correction in time. Respond by the date on the citation.
