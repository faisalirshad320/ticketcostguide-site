How much is a no insurance ticket in Arkansas?

The honest short answer is that the court decides. Nationally, a no insurance ticket runs $100–$1,500+ by state, plus possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences. In Arkansas the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Arkansas Department of Finance and Administration — Driver Services).

We do not publish a single Arkansas dollar figure because the fine is set within the state's traffic code and can differ from one case to the next. The number printed on your citation or the court notice is the one that matters.

The national range is a comparison point only. It is not a statement of what Arkansas charges.

Expect the total you pay to be more than the fine alone, because court costs are added by the district court and vary by county.

How is the fine set, and what are court costs?

Arkansas sets the penalty for this violation in its traffic code, and the court applies it to your case. The citation or a court notice shows what the court is asking for. The state statutes are published through Arkansas statutes via Justia, which is a useful place to read the underlying law.

On top of the fine, court costs are added by the district court and vary by county. Two drivers with the same charge in different counties can owe different totals.

  • Fine: set by the court on the citation.
  • Court costs: added by the district court; they vary by county.
  • Other consequences: nationally, these can include impound, SR-22 and registration consequences.

If the total on your paperwork looks unclear, ask the court clerk for an itemized breakdown. Clerks can explain what is fine and what is cost, though they cannot give legal advice.

How many points does a no insurance ticket carry in Arkansas?

Arkansas uses a points system that runs 3–8 points by speed, and 14+ points can trigger suspension. That scale is described by speed, so it does not tell you how many points, if any, a no insurance charge adds.

We do not state a point value for this violation. Check your citation and the court's disposition, or ask Arkansas Department of Finance and Administration — Driver Services how your record will show the conviction.

Points matter because they add up. If you already have points from other violations, a new conviction brings you closer to the 14+ level where suspension can be triggered.

Does a no insurance ticket raise my insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Arkansas-specific one, and whether a no insurance conviction is treated like a moving violation depends on the insurer and how the offense is recorded.

A lapse in coverage can also affect pricing on its own, since some insurers treat a gap as higher risk. We do not give figures for that because none are in our locked sources.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer. If you are shopping for coverage after a ticket, compare quotes from several companies and ask each how it treats a coverage lapse and a conviction.

Could I also face impound, SR-22 or registration problems?

Nationally, a no insurance ticket can bring more than a fine: possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences. An SR-22 is a certificate of financial responsibility that some drivers must have their insurer file. Whether it applies to you in Arkansas depends on your case.

Registration is the other pressure point. A vehicle registered without valid coverage can run into problems at renewal. Contact the state agency listed at Arkansas Department of Finance and Administration — Driver Services to find out what your record shows and what is required to clear it.

  • Read the citation for any impound or hearing instructions.
  • Ask the court whether proof of coverage affects the outcome.
  • Confirm your registration status before you drive the vehicle again.

Can a no insurance ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes a court will consider a dismissal or reduction, but we cannot say when. Outcomes depend on the facts, the judge and the county. Our locked sources give no Arkansas rule for dismissal, so we will not promise one.

In general, these are the practical steps drivers take:

  1. Read the citation carefully for the court, the appearance instructions and the amount.
  2. Gather proof of any coverage that was in force at the time of the stop, if you had it.
  3. Contact the clerk of the court named on the citation and ask what the court accepts and what it will do with the ticket.
  4. Consider speaking with a licensed Arkansas attorney if the stakes are high. TicketCostGuide does not recommend specific attorneys and does not give legal advice.

Whatever you decide, do it before the date on the citation. Missing it creates a separate set of problems.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the ticket?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. Courts can add costs, and an unresolved case can lead to licensing trouble. We do not state specific deadlines or penalty amounts for Arkansas because they are not in our locked sources.

Points are a related risk. A conviction that adds points moves you closer to the 14+ level that can trigger suspension. A suspension on top of a coverage problem is harder and more expensive to fix than the original ticket.

If you cannot pay the full amount, ask the court clerk about options before the due date on the citation. Some courts offer alternatives, but availability varies by court.

Frequently asked questions

How much is a no insurance ticket in Arkansas?

Who decides the fine for driving without insurance in Arkansas?

The court sets the fine within the state's traffic code, and the amount appears on your citation. The district court also adds court costs, which vary by county. Check the paperwork or ask the clerk for an itemized total instead of relying on a general estimate.

Does a no insurance ticket add points in Arkansas?

Arkansas's points scale is described as 3–8 points by speed, with 14+ points able to trigger suspension. That scale is speed-based, so it does not state a value for no insurance. Check your citation and court disposition, or ask Arkansas Driver Services how it will be recorded.

Are court costs included in the fine?

Not necessarily. Court costs are added by the district court and vary by county, so they come on top of the fine set on the citation. Ask the court clerk for an itemized breakdown so you can see which part is the fine and which part is costs.

Can a no insurance ticket lead to an SR-22 or impound?

Nationally, a no insurance ticket can come with possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences. Whether any of these apply in your Arkansas case depends on the details of the citation and the court. Read the citation closely and ask the court or Driver Services what is required.

What if I can't pay the Arkansas no insurance ticket?
