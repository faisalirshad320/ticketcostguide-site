How much is a no insurance ticket in Michigan?

There is no single published price for a Michigan no insurance ticket. The exact amount is set by the court on the citation, within the state's traffic code.

For context, nationally these tickets run $100–$1,500+ by state, plus possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences (Michigan Secretary of State). That is a national range, not a Michigan quote.

The number that matters is the one printed on your citation or given by the court clerk. Treat any other figure as a rough national reference only.

The fine is only part of what you may pay. District court costs vary by court and make up much of the total, so two drivers with the same violation can owe different amounts.

How is the fine set?

In Michigan, the court decides the fine for this violation within the state's traffic code. The officer's citation and the court's own process determine what you are told to pay.

Several things can shape the final total:

  • The fine the court sets on the citation.
  • District court costs, which vary by court.
  • Any separate consequences tied to your vehicle or license record.

Because court costs differ by court, we do not give a statewide average. Asking the clerk of the court named on your citation is the most reliable way to learn the full amount.

Statutes are available through Michigan statutes via Justia, though a statute does not tell you what your particular court will charge.

What other costs can come with a no insurance ticket?

The national range comes with warnings. Alongside the fine, drivers in many states face possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences (Michigan Secretary of State).

  • Impound: a vehicle may be towed, which can add towing and storage charges. We do not publish those amounts.
  • SR-22: this is a certificate of financial responsibility some drivers must have filed. It is not insurance itself, and the insurer you use may charge for filing it.
  • Registration: a vehicle's registration status can be affected. Contact the Secretary of State to confirm how your record is treated.

These consequences are listed as possible, not automatic. What applies to you depends on your situation and your record.

How many points does a no insurance ticket carry in Michigan?

Michigan's points system, under §257.320a, assigns 2–4 points by mph over. That schedule is built around speeding, so it does not directly answer how a no insurance conviction is scored.

We do not state a point value for no insurance, because the sourced facts do not give one. Check your citation, ask the court clerk, or review your driving record with the Michigan Secretary of State.

The threshold to know: 12 points in 2 years = driver reexamination. If you already have points on your record, any new conviction that adds points matters.

Does a no insurance ticket raise my insurance?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Michigan one.

We cannot say whether a no insurance conviction is rated the same way as a moving violation. Insurers use their own rules, and the effect depends on the company and your history.

A practical point: a driver who has let coverage lapse may also face a harder time finding a policy, and an SR-22 requirement, if one applies, can limit choices. Ask any insurer you are considering how they treat a no insurance conviction. We do not recommend specific insurers.

Can it be dismissed or reduced?

Outcomes are decided by the court, and we cannot predict yours. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice.

Here are general steps people commonly take:

  1. Read the citation closely for the court name, the stated violation and any instructions.
  2. Contact the clerk of that district court to ask what options exist and what the total amount is.
  3. If you had coverage in force, ask the clerk what documentation the court accepts and how it is handled.
  4. If the stakes feel high, such as a prior record, consider speaking with a licensed Michigan attorney.

Do not assume a reduction is available. Whether an option exists is up to the court and the facts of your case.

What happens if I don't pay?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse, but the specific penalties in Michigan are not in our sourced figures, so we do not list them.

Because the national picture includes possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences, an unresolved no insurance matter can affect more than the fine itself. Contact the court on your citation before any deadline passes, and check your record with the Michigan Secretary of State.

If you cannot pay the full amount, ask the clerk what arrangements the court offers. That is a question for the court, not something we can promise.
