How much is a no insurance ticket in Oregon?

The lead figure we can source is national: Nationally $100–$1,500+ by state, plus possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences; in Oregon the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Oregon DMV (ODOT)). That range is a national spread across states. It is not an Oregon price.

We do not publish an exact Oregon dollar amount because the court sets it on the citation, within the state's traffic code. Two people cited for the same thing can see different totals.

The number printed on your citation or on the court's notice is the number that applies to you. Treat any figure you see elsewhere, including the national range here, as context only.

The fine is only part of the cost. Impound, SR-22 and registration consequences can add to it, and so can higher insurance premiums later.

How is the fine set in Oregon?

In Oregon, the court decides the fine for this violation within the state's traffic code. The amount is not a single fixed number that we can quote for every driver and court.

Some add-ons are written directly into statute. Zone doubling and the 100+ mph enhancements are examples, but they apply to speeding offenses. They do not apply to a no insurance citation, so do not expect them on yours.

To see how your total was built, look at these items:

  • The fine or base amount listed on the citation.
  • Any court costs or surcharges the court lists.
  • Any notice of a hearing date or payment deadline.

If the notice does not break the total down, the court clerk can explain what each line covers. Ask for the explanation in writing if you can.

How many points does a no insurance ticket carry in Oregon?

Oregon has no point system. There is no point total to add up or watch for this violation.

That does not mean a conviction has no license consequences. Suspensions follow repeat convictions (for example, habitual-offender rules) and specific offenses like 100+ mph. Oregon tracks your driving history through convictions rather than points.

A single no insurance conviction is not described in our sources as triggering a suspension by itself. Repeat convictions are where the state's rules begin to matter. For your own record, the Oregon DMV is the place to check (Oregon DMV (ODOT)).

The rules sit in the state's traffic code, which you can read through Oregon statutes via Justia. Statute text can be technical, so the DMV or the court clerk can help you find what applies to your citation.

Does a no insurance ticket raise insurance rates?

It can affect what you pay later. Nationally, moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums ~20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Oregon-specific one.

Whether your insurer treats a no insurance conviction the same way is up to that insurer and its rules. We do not give a rate for your situation, and we do not recommend any insurer.

Consider these points:

  • A gap in coverage can matter to insurers separately from the ticket.
  • An SR-22 requirement, where it applies, is one of the consequences listed alongside the national range.
  • Quotes vary by company, so your actual change may be higher or lower than the national average.

When you shop for coverage, tell each insurer accurately about the citation. Compare the quotes you receive in writing.

Can a no insurance ticket be dismissed or reduced in Oregon?

Because the court sets the amount on the citation, the court is also where any change would happen. We cannot say whether a dismissal or reduction is available in your case, and we do not give legal advice.

What you can do is read the citation closely and act on its instructions:

  1. Confirm the violation, the date and the court listed.
  2. Note the payment or hearing deadline.
  3. Ask the court clerk what options exist for your citation, such as responding by mail, appearing in person or requesting a hearing.
  4. Gather any documents that relate to your coverage on the date in question.

If you are unsure about your rights, a licensed attorney can advise you. TicketCostGuide does not recommend a specific attorney.

Do not assume a ticket will go away on its own. Respond to the court by the date on the citation.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore it?

The national range comes with warnings: possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences. Ignoring a citation can make those consequences more likely, not less.

In Oregon, suspensions follow repeat convictions and specific offenses, so a record of several convictions can lead to larger problems over time. Ignoring a court notice can also add steps with the court. Check the citation and the court's notice for the specific consequences it lists.

If you cannot pay the full amount by the deadline, contact the court clerk before the date passes. Ask what options the court offers. Keep a record of who you spoke with and when.

You can also check your driving record and registration status with the Oregon DMV (Oregon DMV (ODOT)).
