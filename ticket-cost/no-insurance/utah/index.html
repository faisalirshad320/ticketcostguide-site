By Editorial Team, TicketCostGuide · Last reviewed 2026-10-16

How much is a no insurance ticket in Utah?

There is no single Utah price to quote. The amount is set by the court on the citation you receive, and that is the number that matters for your case.

For context, the national range is $100–$1,500+ by state, plus possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences (Utah Driver License Division). That range describes the whole country. It is not a prediction for Utah.

Read the citation first. The amount, the court name and the response deadline are printed on it or listed on the court's notice.

Because the court sets the figure, two drivers cited for the same violation can see different totals. Fees and surcharges that are part of the schedule can also change what you owe.

If the citation does not show an amount, contact the court listed on it and ask what is due and by when.

How is the fine set?

In Utah, the exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The code itself is available through Utah statutes via Justia.

Court costs and surcharges work as part of the same picture. Zone enhancements and fees are built into the schedule, so the total on your citation may be more than a base fine alone.

What this means in practice:

  • The citation, not a general guide, shows your actual amount.
  • The court that handles your case controls the amount, so the same offense can differ between courts.
  • Added fees can change the total, so check whether the figure you see already includes them.

We do not publish an exact Utah dollar figure for this violation, because doing so would risk giving you a number that does not match your citation.

How many points is a no insurance ticket in Utah?

The locked facts we rely on do not give a point value specifically for a no insurance citation. We will not guess one.

What they do show is how Utah's point system works in general. Speeding carries 35–75 points by mph over on Utah's scale. Reaching 200 points in 3 years means suspension for drivers aged 21 and over (Utah Driver License Division).

The speeding scale is only a reference for how large point values can be. It does not mean a no insurance ticket carries the same points.

To find out whether your citation adds points to your record, ask the Driver License Division or the court handling the case.

If you already have other violations on your record, ask how they count toward the 200-point threshold before you decide how to respond.

Does a no insurance ticket raise my insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Utah-specific rate.

Whether your particular citation is treated as a moving violation, and how much your premium changes, depends on your insurer and your driving history. We cannot predict either one.

A few practical points:

  • A gap in coverage can be a separate concern for insurers, apart from the ticket itself.
  • Needing an SR-22 can change which insurers will cover you and on what terms.
  • Comparing quotes after a citation is a reasonable step, since insurers weigh these events differently.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer. Ask any company you are considering how it treats this kind of citation.

Can a no insurance ticket lead to impound, SR-22 or registration problems?

Yes, these are possible. The national picture for this violation includes possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences on top of the fine (Utah Driver License Division).

  • Impound: a vehicle may be towed in some situations, which adds costs beyond the fine.
  • SR-22: this is a certificate of financial responsibility that some drivers must have filed after an uninsured-driving violation.
  • Registration: your vehicle registration may be affected until insurance requirements are met.

Which of these applies to you depends on the facts of the stop and on Utah's rules. We do not state costs or time periods for any of them, because the locked facts do not provide them.

Ask the Driver License Division what is required to clear any registration or license issue tied to your citation.

Can a no insurance ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes courts have room to act, but we cannot promise any outcome. This page is not legal advice, and we do not recommend any attorney.

Your general options after a citation are usually to pay it, contest it, or contact the court to ask what it allows. The citation lists the court and the deadline for responding.

Questions worth asking the court clerk:

  • What is the total amount due, including fees and surcharges?
  • Is there a way to show proof of insurance that was in force, if that applies to your situation?
  • What happens to my record if I pay versus contest?

Paying a citation can have consequences for your record and insurance. Before you decide, consider whether you want to speak to a licensed professional about your own case.

What happens if I don't pay a no insurance ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. We do not list specific late fees or timelines, because the locked facts do not include them. The court notice sets the real deadlines for your case.

Possible consequences of not responding include:

  • Additional fees or surcharges added to the amount owed.
  • License or registration problems with the Driver License Division.
  • Added trouble getting or keeping coverage, particularly if an SR-22 is involved.

If you cannot pay the full amount by the deadline, contact the court before the date passes and ask what arrangements exist.

Responding on time and keeping a copy of the citation and any court correspondence is the most reliable way to avoid added costs.
