How much is a no insurance ticket in Texas?

Texas fines $175–$350 for a first no-insurance offense. That is the only Texas dollar range we publish for this violation.

The exact amount for your ticket is set by the court on the citation, within the state's traffic code. Your own number could land anywhere inside that range, and the fine is not the whole bill.

For context, the national range is $100–$1,500+ by state, plus possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences (Texas Department of Public Safety). That is a national figure, not a Texas price.

Court costs come on top of the fine. State court costs are uniform, while municipal add-ons vary by court, so two drivers with the same fine can owe different totals.

How is the fine set for a Texas no insurance ticket?

The Texas traffic code sets the range, and the court handling your case sets the amount that appears on the citation. You can read the code itself through Texas statutes via Justia.

Three things usually shape what you owe:

  • The fine, which falls inside the range the statute allows.
  • State court costs, which are uniform across Texas.
  • Municipal add-ons, which vary by court.

The $175–$350 range applies to a first offense. Repeat offenses are treated differently, and we do not publish a repeat-offense figure because our locked sources do not give one.

Always read the citation and the court's notice. They show the amount that applies to you and where to pay or respond.

How many points does a no insurance ticket add in Texas?

Texas abolished its driver-point and surcharge program in 2019. A no insurance ticket therefore does not add points in the way older guides describe.

License action now follows specific offenses and repeat-conviction rules. A single first offense and a pattern of repeat convictions can have very different outcomes for your license.

If an older article tells you about Texas points or annual surcharges for this ticket, check its date. The program it describes no longer exists.

For license questions about your own record, the Texas Department of Public Safety is the agency to contact.

Will a no insurance ticket raise my insurance rates?

It can. As a national figure, moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums ~20% on average. That number describes moving violations in general, not a no-insurance ticket specifically, and it is not a Texas-specific quote.

A no-insurance citation also shows insurers a gap in coverage. Many insurers weigh a lapse when pricing a new policy, so the practical effect depends on the company and your history.

Compare quotes before assuming the worst. We do not recommend any specific insurer.

The fine is a one-time cost. A higher premium can last for as long as the insurer counts the conviction or the lapse against you.

Can a no insurance ticket mean an SR-22, impound or registration problem?

Possibly. Nationally, the cost of a no insurance ticket can include impound, SR-22 and registration consequences on top of the fine (Texas Department of Public Safety).

  • Impound: a vehicle can be towed, and towing and storage charges are separate from the fine.
  • SR-22: this is a certificate your insurer files to show you carry coverage. Some drivers are required to maintain one after a violation.
  • Registration: problems with coverage can affect your vehicle registration.

We do not give exact Texas dollar amounts for these items because our locked sources do not list them. Ask the court or the Department of Public Safety how they apply to your case.

Other states' rules on these consequences differ, and the Governors Highway Safety Association tracks state traffic laws if you want to compare.

Can a Texas no insurance ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome depends on the court and the facts. We cannot promise a result or give legal advice.

Common questions to raise with the court clerk:

  • Whether proof of coverage that was in force when you were cited can be considered.
  • Whether the court offers any program that changes the fine or the record.
  • Whether a payment plan is available if you cannot pay at once.

Because the exact fine is set by the court on the citation, the court is the best source for what is possible. A licensed Texas attorney can advise on your specific situation, though we do not recommend any particular one.

Do not ignore the citation while you decide. Deadlines are printed on it.

What happens if I don't pay a Texas no insurance ticket?

An unpaid citation can cause bigger problems than the original fine. Courts can add costs, and unresolved tickets can lead to license action under Texas rules.

Our locked sources give no specific late fee amount or deadline, so we do not state one. The notice from your court is the authority on dates and added costs.

If you cannot pay in full, contact the court before the deadline. Ask what your options are in writing where you can.

Remember also that municipal add-ons vary by court, so the total can differ from what a friend paid in another city.
