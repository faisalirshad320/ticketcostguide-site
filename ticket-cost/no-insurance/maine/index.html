How much is a no insurance ticket in Maine?

Nationally, a no insurance ticket costs $100–$1,500+ by state, plus possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences. In Maine the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Maine Bureau of Motor Vehicles).

That national range is a spread across many states. It is not a Maine price, and we do not publish a Maine dollar figure here because the amount depends on the court and the citation.

The number printed on your citation, or the one the court clerk gives you, is the one that counts. Anything else is an estimate.

Costs beyond the fine can matter as much as the fine itself. Impound, SR-22 filing and registration problems can each add expense and paperwork.

How is the fine set?

The fine for this violation in Maine is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The code is available through Maine statutes via Justia, where you can read the wording of the insurance requirement and its penalties.

Statutory surcharges are built into the scheduled amounts. In practice, the total on the citation should already include them, so you should not expect to add them yourself.

Because the amount comes from the court and the citation, two drivers in different places can see different totals. Always confirm with the court named on your ticket.

  • Read the citation for the amount, the court name and the response deadline.
  • Contact the court clerk if the amount is unclear.
  • Do not rely on a figure from a forum or a general website, including ours, for your own case.

How many points does a no insurance ticket carry in Maine?

Maine uses a demerit point system. Points accrue per conviction, and 12 points in 12 months means a suspension.

We do not state a specific point value for a no insurance conviction because that figure is not in our sourced data. Your citation, the court or the Maine Bureau of Motor Vehicles can tell you whether a conviction adds points to your record.

The 12-in-12 threshold matters most if you already have other convictions. A new conviction on top of earlier ones can move you closer to a suspension.

Does a no insurance ticket raise your insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Maine one, and your own change could be higher, lower or none.

A no insurance conviction may be treated differently from a speeding conviction by individual insurers. We cannot say how any particular company will react, and we do not recommend insurers.

A lapse in coverage can also make it harder to find a policy at a good price. Asking prospective insurers how they treat a lapse is a reasonable step.

The 20% figure is an average for moving violations nationally. Treat it as a rough reference, not a quote.

What else can happen besides the fine?

The sourced data points to three consequences beyond the fine: possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences. Whether any of them applies depends on the circumstances and the court.

  • Impound: the vehicle may be towed, which can add towing and storage costs.
  • SR-22: you may be required to file proof of financial responsibility. An SR-22 is a certificate filed by an insurer, not a type of policy.
  • Registration: your registration may be affected until you show valid coverage.

For current requirements, check with the Maine Bureau of Motor Vehicles. For a comparison of how states approach insurance laws, see the Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws page.

Can a no insurance ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome depends on the court and the facts. We cannot say how often it happens in Maine, and this page is not legal advice.

One common situation is a driver who had coverage but could not show proof at the stop. In that case, the citation instructions or the court clerk can explain what proof is accepted and by when.

  1. Read the citation for deadlines and instructions.
  2. Gather proof of coverage that was in force on the date of the stop, if you had it.
  3. Contact the court clerk to ask what options exist.
  4. If you want advice on your specific situation, speak with a licensed attorney of your choosing.

Do not assume an option exists without confirming it with the court.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. The court's response deadline is on the citation, and missing it can lead to added penalties or license consequences.

We do not list specific late fees or penalties for Maine because they are not in our sourced data. The court or the Maine Bureau of Motor Vehicles can explain what applies to your case.

If you cannot pay the full amount, ask the clerk before the deadline whether any arrangement is available. Courts are generally easier to work with before a deadline passes than after.
