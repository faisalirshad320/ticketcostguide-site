How much is a no insurance ticket in North Dakota?

Nationally, a no insurance ticket costs $100–$1,500+ by state, plus possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences (North Dakota Department of Transportation). That range is a national one, not a North Dakota price.

In North Dakota, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single North Dakota dollar figure because the court decides it within the state's traffic code.

Your citation, or the court clerk, is the only reliable source for the amount you owe.

The national range is wide because states treat this violation very differently. Some charge a modest fine and others add extra penalties on top.

How is the fine set?

The fine is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The amount you see on the citation is the one the court assigned for your case.

North Dakota statutes are available through North Dakota statutes via Justia, if you want to read the traffic code yourself. The Department of Transportation is the agency to check for licensing and registration questions (North Dakota Department of Transportation).

Things that can shape the outcome include:

  • What the citation itself lists
  • Whether this is a first or repeat citation
  • How the court handles your case

We cannot tell you how any of these will change your exact amount, because the court sets it.

Are there extra court costs and surcharges?

For North Dakota, the statutory fee is usually the whole amount, with no large cost stack. That means you should not expect a long list of add-on fees stacked on the base fine.

That is different from the "other consequences" in the national range. Impound, SR-22 and registration consequences are separate from the court amount, and they can add real cost.

  • Impound: possible, per the national range
  • SR-22: possible, per the national range
  • Registration: possible consequences, per the national range

We do not have locked dollar figures for these items, so check with the court and the Department of Transportation about what applies to you.

How many points does a no insurance ticket carry in North Dakota?

North Dakota assigns points by mph over on its schedule, and 12+ points means suspension (North Dakota Department of Transportation). That schedule is built around speed, so it does not map neatly onto an insurance violation.

We do not have a sourced point value for a no insurance citation, so we will not guess one. Check the citation and ask the Department of Transportation whether any points apply to your case.

The one threshold to remember: 12+ points = suspension on the ND schedule.

Does a no insurance ticket raise my insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a North Dakota one.

Whether your specific citation counts as a moving violation for your insurer is a question for the insurer. We do not recommend any insurer and cannot predict your rate.

An uninsured driver also faces a practical problem. If a record shows a lapse, you may face the SR-22 requirement the national range mentions, and that is something to raise with the Department of Transportation.

Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a page of state laws that can give wider background (Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws).

Can a no insurance ticket be dismissed or reduced?

It depends on the court. The fine is set by the court on the citation, so any reduction or dismissal would also be a court decision.

Practical steps people commonly take, none of which are legal advice:

  • Read the citation carefully for the listed violation and the court instructions
  • Contact the court clerk and ask what options exist for your case
  • Gather any documents about your coverage and bring them if the court asks
  • Consider speaking with a licensed attorney if you have questions about your rights

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not recommend any specific attorney.

What happens if I don't pay?

We do not have sourced figures for late penalties in North Dakota, so we will not state any. What we can say is that the national range includes possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences, which show that an uninsured citation can have effects beyond the fine.

Ignoring a citation is rarely a good idea. Contact the court named on the citation before the due date to ask about your options.

Also keep in mind the 12+ points = suspension rule on the ND schedule. Whether your citation affects your point total is something to confirm with the Department of Transportation.
