How much is a no insurance ticket in Wisconsin?

Nationally, a no insurance ticket costs $100–$1,500+ by state, plus possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences (Wisconsin Department of Transportation — DMV). That is a national range, and Wisconsin sits somewhere inside the state-by-state spread.

In Wisconsin, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Wisconsin dollar figure because the amount you owe depends on the court handling your case and on the surcharges added to it.

The number printed on your citation, or the figure the court clerk gives you, is the amount that counts. Treat any other number as an estimate.

The fine is only part of the cost. Impound, an SR-22 filing requirement and registration problems can add expense and hassle. The sections below cover each of them.

How is the fine set?

The exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The base amount is not a flat number you can look up in a simple table.

On top of that, the statutory surcharge stack is added to every forfeiture. In practice, the total you pay is larger than the base amount alone. Wisconsin statutes are available through Wisconsin statutes via Justia if you want to read the traffic code yourself.

Because the amount is set by the court on the citation, two drivers with similar tickets can see different totals depending on the court and the details of the case.

  • Base amount: set by the court within the traffic code.
  • Surcharges: the statutory surcharge stack applies to every forfeiture.
  • Total: shown on your citation or by the court.

How many points does a no insurance ticket carry in Wisconsin?

The point schedule in our data is tiered by speed: 3 points (10 or less over), 4 points (11–19 over), and 6 points (20+ over). Those tiers describe speeding, not a no insurance charge.

Our sourced data does not list a specific point value for a no insurance violation. We will not guess one. Your citation and the Wisconsin DOT are the places to confirm whether any points apply to your case.

The threshold to know is that 12 points in 12 months = suspension. If you already have points on your record, any added points matter more.

Ask the court clerk or check with Wisconsin Department of Transportation — DMV about points before you decide how to respond.

Does a no insurance ticket raise your insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Wisconsin-specific one, and individual insurers set their own rates.

Whether a no insurance conviction is treated like a moving violation depends on the insurer. A gap in coverage can also affect the rates you are offered, separate from the ticket itself.

An SR-22 requirement, where it applies, is another factor insurers weigh. We do not recommend any specific insurer. If you need coverage, compare quotes from several companies.

  • National average increase after a moving-violation conviction: ~20%.
  • Your actual change depends on your insurer and your record.

What else can a no insurance ticket lead to?

The national picture includes possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences on top of the fine (Wisconsin Department of Transportation — DMV). Which of these apply in Wisconsin depends on your case.

  • Impound: a vehicle may be towed, which adds towing and storage costs.
  • SR-22: a certificate your insurer files to prove coverage, sometimes required after a violation.
  • Registration: problems with your vehicle registration can follow an uninsured stop.

The Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a state-by-state overview of traffic laws at Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws, which is useful for broader context.

Can a no insurance ticket be dismissed or reduced?

That depends on the court and on the facts of your case. We cannot give legal advice or say what will work for you.

Because the court sets the amount on the citation, the court is the place to ask about your options. Many drivers start by reading the citation closely for the response instructions and the court contact details.

  • Read the citation for the amount, the court and any response instructions.
  • Contact the court clerk to ask what options exist for your type of citation.
  • Gather proof of any coverage you had, if that applies to your situation.
  • If you are unsure, consider speaking with a licensed attorney of your choosing.

Do not assume the ticket will go away on its own. Ignoring it can add cost, as covered next.

What happens if I don't pay?

Our sourced data does not give a Wisconsin-specific schedule of penalties for unpaid tickets, so we will not quote one. The national picture includes possible registration consequences, and an unresolved citation can cause further trouble with the court.

The statutory surcharge stack is added to every forfeiture, so the amount owed is not just the base fine. Check the citation for the deadline and the court's instructions.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court clerk before the deadline and ask what the court allows.

Points matter too. With 12 points in 12 months = suspension, any points you pick up count toward that threshold.
