How much is a no insurance ticket in New Jersey?

Nationally, a no insurance ticket costs $100–$1,500+ by state, plus possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences; in New Jersey the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (New Jersey Motor Vehicle Commission).

That national range is wide because states set their own penalties. It is not a New Jersey price, and we do not publish an exact New Jersey dollar figure on this page.

The number that applies to you is the one on your citation or court notice. If it is unclear, ask the court clerk listed on the ticket.

The fine is only one part of the cost. Court costs, surcharges and what happens to your insurance can add to it, as the sections below explain.

How is the fine set for a no insurance ticket in New Jersey?

In New Jersey, the exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The statutes are available through New Jersey statutes via Justia.

This means two drivers with similar tickets can see different totals. The court looks at the violation as charged and the rules that apply to it.

Here is what to check on your paperwork:

  • The violation description and any statute listed.
  • The amount due, or a note that you must appear in court.
  • The court name, the deadline and how to respond.

Penalties for driving without insurance also differ widely between states, which is why the national figure is a range and not a single price (Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws).

What other costs can come with a no insurance ticket?

The national range comes with a warning: possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences. Those are separate from the fine itself.

  • Impound: a vehicle may be towed in some places, which can bring towing and storage charges.
  • SR-22: this is a filing some states require from insurers after certain violations. It can affect the policies you are offered.
  • Registration: your registration status can be affected until the insurance issue is resolved.

Our locked sources do not say which of these apply in New Jersey for a specific citation. Ask the court or check with the New Jersey Motor Vehicle Commission before assuming either way.

Being insured on the day of the stop and being able to show it are different things. What the court will accept is the court's decision, so ask what it wants to see.

How many points does a no insurance ticket carry in New Jersey?

New Jersey's points system, as locked in our sources, is written in speed tiers: 2 points (1–14 over), 4 points (15–29 over) and 5 points (30+ over). Twelve points means a suspension.

Those tiers describe speeding. Our sources do not list a point value for a no insurance violation, so we do not state one. Look at your citation, or ask the court or the New Jersey Motor Vehicle Commission, whether points are attached to your charge.

Points matter because they feed the surcharge system. If your record reaches 6+ points within 3 years, the MVC adds an insurance surcharge of $150+. That surcharge is billed annually for 3 years on top of court fines.

  • 12 points = suspension.
  • 6+ points within 3 years = MVC surcharge of $150+.
  • Surcharges are billed annually for 3 years.

Does a no insurance ticket raise my insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a New Jersey one, and it is not specific to this violation.

Your own change could be higher, lower or none. It depends on your insurer, your record and how the violation is classified.

There are two separate insurance-related costs to keep apart:

  • Premium changes from your insurer, which are private business decisions.
  • MVC surcharges if your record reaches 6+ points within 3 years, at $150+ per year billed for 3 years.

A gap in coverage may also limit which insurers will quote you. We cannot say which insurer is best for you and do not recommend any.

Can a no insurance ticket be dismissed or reduced in New Jersey?

That is up to the court. Because the court sets the exact amount on the citation, any change to the charge or the fine also comes from the court.

Before you respond, gather what is relevant:

  1. Your citation and any court notice.
  2. Proof of your insurance status on the date of the stop, if you had coverage.
  3. Your registration and vehicle details.

Then contact the court on the ticket and ask what options exist and what the deadline is. If you want legal advice about your situation, speak with a licensed attorney of your choosing. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot advise on your case.

Do not ignore the deadline while you think it over. Missing it can add problems on top of the original charge.

What happens if I don't pay a no insurance ticket?

Our locked sources do not give New Jersey's specific late-payment penalties, so we will not guess. In general, unpaid traffic matters can lead to added court costs and licence or registration problems. The court on your citation can tell you what applies.

Also keep in mind that a no insurance ticket can come with registration consequences and, nationally, possible impound and SR-22 requirements. An unresolved ticket can leave those issues open.

If you cannot pay the full amount, ask the court clerk whether it offers any payment arrangement. Do that before the due date, not after.
