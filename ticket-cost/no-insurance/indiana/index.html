How much is a no insurance ticket in Indiana?

Nationally, a no insurance ticket runs $100–$1,500+ by state, plus possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences. In Indiana the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Indiana Bureau of Motor Vehicles).

That national range is a spread across all states, not an Indiana price. We do not publish a single Indiana dollar figure because the court handling your citation decides it.

Lead figure: Nationally $100–$1,500+ by state, plus possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences; in Indiana the exact amount is set by the court on the citation.

The number on your citation or court notice is the one that matters. If it is blank or unclear, the clerk of the court that issued it can tell you what is owed.

How is the fine set, and why do court costs matter?

The fine for this violation in Indiana is set by the court within the state's traffic code. Indiana statutes are searchable through Indiana statutes via Justia, which is useful if you want to read the underlying language yourself.

County court costs are added to the judgment and often exceed the fine for low speeds. That note comes from the state context for Indiana traffic cases. It shows that the total can be well above the fine alone.

Because costs vary by county, two drivers cited for the same thing in different counties can owe different totals. Use the figure on your own paperwork, not an average.

  • Fine: set by the court on the citation.
  • Court costs: added to the judgment, varying by county.
  • Other consequences: possible impound, SR-22 and registration issues.

What else can a no insurance ticket lead to besides the fine?

The national picture includes more than a fine. Depending on the state and the facts, a no insurance stop can come with impound of the vehicle, an SR-22 requirement and registration consequences.

An SR-22 is a certificate of financial responsibility that an insurer files on your behalf. It is not insurance itself, and it is not required in every case.

Registration consequences can mean that your vehicle registration is flagged or suspended until coverage is shown. The Indiana BMV is the agency that handles registration and licensing records (Indiana Bureau of Motor Vehicles). We do not state which of these applies to a specific driver, because that depends on the citation and the court.

How many points does a no insurance ticket carry in Indiana?

The locked Indiana points fact reads: 2–8 points by mph over; accumulating points triggers BMV probation and suspension. That schedule is tied to speed over the limit, so it describes speeding tickets and does not map directly to a no insurance violation.

We therefore do not claim a specific point value for no insurance. Whether points are assessed at all is something to confirm with the BMV or the court that handles your case.

What the fact does tell you is how the system works in general: points accumulate across violations, and enough of them triggers BMV probation and then suspension. A no insurance ticket is rarely your only record item, so check your driving record as a whole.

  • Points by mph over: 2–8 (speeding-based schedule).
  • Accumulating points can trigger BMV probation and suspension.
  • Your own record is the best guide to where you stand.

Does a no insurance ticket raise my insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Indiana-specific one, and your own change could be higher, lower or none.

Whether a no insurance conviction counts the same as a moving violation depends on the insurer's rating rules. A lapse in coverage can also affect the price you are offered, separate from the ticket itself.

If you currently have no policy, you will be shopping for new coverage, and the quotes you receive depend on the insurer's own underwriting. We do not recommend any insurer. If an SR-22 is required, only an insurer that offers SR-22 filings can supply it.

The national average is useful for budgeting only. Get actual quotes rather than relying on it.

Can a no insurance ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Outcomes are decided by the court, and the rules differ by county and court. Because the Indiana fine is set by the court on the citation, any change to it also comes from the court.

Some courts take into account whether a driver can show valid coverage, or has since obtained it. We cannot say what your court will accept, so ask the clerk what documentation it wants and by when.

Common steps people take include:

  1. Read the citation for the court name, the listed amount and any deadline.
  2. Gather any proof of insurance, including policy documents covering the date of the stop.
  3. Contact the court clerk to ask about your options.
  4. Consider talking to a licensed attorney if the consequences are serious. We do not recommend a specific one.

Remember that court costs are added to the judgment, so a reduced fine may still leave a meaningful total.

What happens if I don't pay a no insurance ticket?

We do not state specific deadlines or penalty amounts because they are not part of our locked facts. In general, ignoring a citation can lead the court to take further action, and the BMV can be involved when licensing is affected.

The state context notes that accumulating points triggers BMV probation and suspension. Unresolved cases can compound problems on your record, so it is better to contact the court early.

If you cannot pay the full amount at once, ask the clerk whether the court offers payment arrangements. Do not assume the amount shrinks if you wait, since county court costs are added to the judgment.
