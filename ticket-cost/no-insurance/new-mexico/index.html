How much is a no insurance ticket in New Mexico?

There is no single New Mexico price we can quote. Nationally the range is $100–$1,500+ by state, plus possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences. In New Mexico the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (New Mexico Motor Vehicle Division).

That means the number printed on your citation, or the number a judge sets, is the one that matters. A national range is only a guide to how wide the spread can be.

Do not treat the national range as a New Mexico fine. It describes all states combined, and the New Mexico amount is decided by the court handling your case.

The fine is also only part of the cost. Fees are added to the penalty assessment, and court cases can exceed the assessment amounts. Add any impound, reinstatement or insurance costs and the total can be well above the base fine.

How is the fine for no insurance set in New Mexico?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The statutes are published online through New Mexico statutes via Justia, and you can read the traffic provisions there.

Two things usually shape what you pay:

  • What the citation says, including any amount listed on it.
  • Whether the matter is handled as a penalty assessment or goes to court.

Our sources state that fees are added to the penalty assessment. They also state that court cases can exceed the assessment amounts. So paying the assessment and appearing before a judge can lead to different totals.

Because the amount is court-specific, we do not publish a New Mexico dollar figure. Read your citation and check the court's instructions for the exact total owed.

How many points does a no insurance ticket add in New Mexico?

The New Mexico points framework in our sources is described by speed: 3–8 points by mph over. It also sets thresholds: 7–10 points in 12 months risks suspension, and 12 = revocation.

Those figures come from the speeding scale. Our sources do not list a separate point value for a no insurance citation, so we do not state one.

To find out whether your citation carries points, check these places:

  • The citation itself and any court paperwork.
  • Your driver record through the New Mexico Motor Vehicle Division.
  • The court clerk handling your case.

If you already have points from other violations, the 12-month thresholds are worth knowing. Points from several tickets in a short period can add up.

Does a no insurance ticket raise my insurance rates?

Often it can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a New Mexico rate.

A no insurance citation is not always treated like a typical moving violation, so the effect on your policy depends on how a given insurer views it. We do not recommend any insurer and cannot predict your quote.

Two other points to know:

  • A gap in coverage can itself affect what insurers offer you when you buy a new policy.
  • Some states require an SR-22, a certificate of financial responsibility, after an uninsured-driver violation. Our sources list SR-22 among possible consequences nationally.

If an SR-22 is required, it adds a cost and a paperwork step on top of the fine. Ask the court or the MVD whether it applies to you.

What else can a no insurance ticket cost besides the fine?

The national range comes with extra consequences: possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences. These can matter as much as the fine.

  • Impound: if a vehicle is towed, towing and storage charges are separate from the citation.
  • SR-22: a filing that may be required before your driving privileges are restored or kept.
  • Registration: the vehicle's registration can be affected until coverage is shown.
  • Court fees: added to the penalty assessment, and court cases can exceed the assessment amounts.

We cannot give a dollar amount for any of these in New Mexico. Each depends on the court, the tow provider and the agencies involved. Check the New Mexico Motor Vehicle Division for registration and filing requirements.

Can a no insurance ticket be dismissed or reduced in New Mexico?

Sometimes courts take a second look at these citations, but we cannot say what a New Mexico court will do in your case. The amount is set by the court on the citation, so the court is the place to ask.

Steps people commonly take:

  1. Read the citation for the court date, the amount and the deadline to respond.
  2. Gather proof of any coverage you had, such as a policy declaration page or an insurer's letter.
  3. Contact the court clerk to ask what the court accepts and what options exist.
  4. Consider speaking with a licensed attorney if the stakes are high. We do not recommend a specific one.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and this is not legal advice. Rules on what a court may reduce or dismiss are in the state's traffic code, available through New Mexico statutes via Justia.

What happens if I don't pay a no insurance ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Court cases can exceed the assessment amounts, and fees are added to the penalty assessment. Waiting can leave you owing more than the original figure.

Missed deadlines can also lead to license or registration problems. Our sources list registration consequences among the possible results of this violation nationally.

If you also carry points, remember the thresholds: 7–10 points in 12 months risks suspension, and 12 = revocation. Whether your citation adds points is something to confirm with the court.

If you cannot pay by the due date, contact the court before the deadline and ask what options it offers. We do not know which options your court provides.
