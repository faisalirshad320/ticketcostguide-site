How much is a no insurance ticket in New Hampshire?

The exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a New Hampshire dollar figure for this violation because the amount depends on the court and the charge written on your ticket.

For context, here is the locked lead figure: nationally $100–$1,500+ by state, plus possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences; in New Hampshire the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (New Hampshire Division of Motor Vehicles). The national range is a spread across all states, not a New Hampshire price.

Read the citation itself. The amount due, the court, and the response instructions are printed on it, and they control what you owe.

Remember that the base fine is not the total. A 24% penalty assessment is added to every base fine by statute, so the amount you pay will be higher than the fine alone.

How is the fine set, and what gets added?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. New Hampshire statutes are available through New Hampshire statutes via Justia, and the section named on your citation tells you which provision you were charged under.

Two layers decide the final number:

  • The base fine, which the court sets on the citation.
  • The 24% penalty assessment, which is added to every base fine by statute.

Because the exact base amount is court-set, two people with the same charge can see different paperwork. Do not rely on a figure from another state or from a general national average to budget for your own ticket.

The national range also mentions possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences. Those are separate from the fine and can add cost and hassle on top of it.

How many points does a no insurance ticket carry?

New Hampshire's point system assigns 3–4 points by offense. Whether your specific charge carries points, and how many, depends on the offense listed on your citation and on the state's records.

The threshold to watch is 12 points in 12 months, which can bring up to a 3-month suspension. If you already have points from other tickets, a new conviction could move you closer to that line.

To see where you stand, you can check your driving record and ask about point treatment through the New Hampshire Division of Motor Vehicles. The court clerk can also tell you what the charge on your citation is.

Points and fines are separate. You can owe a fine whether or not points are assigned.

Does a no insurance ticket raise my insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a New Hampshire-specific one, and what you actually see depends on your insurer, your record and the type of violation.

A no insurance citation is also a sign of a gap in coverage. Insurers often look at coverage gaps when they price a new policy, so a lapse can matter apart from the ticket itself.

The national range also flags SR-22 consequences. An SR-22 is a certificate of financial responsibility that an insurer files on your behalf when a state requires it. Whether New Hampshire would require one in your case is something to confirm with the New Hampshire Division of Motor Vehicles.

We do not recommend any insurer. If your premium changes, compare quotes yourself and ask each company how it treats a recent citation.

Can a no insurance ticket be dismissed or reduced?

The court decides. Some courts may take into account whether you had valid coverage at the time of the stop, or whether you have since obtained coverage, but we do not state a rule for New Hampshire courts here. Ask the court clerk what documents, if any, the court wants to see.

Typical options on any traffic citation include:

  • Paying the amount on the citation, which usually resolves the case but may mean a conviction.
  • Contesting the charge at a hearing.
  • Asking the court whether a reduction or payment arrangement is available.

Which of these are open to you depends on the court and the charge. The response instructions on the citation are the place to start.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice. If the outcome could affect your license or record, you may want to speak with a licensed attorney of your own choosing.

What happens if I don't pay or respond?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. The national range already notes possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences, and an unanswered ticket can add to the trouble.

Courts commonly add penalties or take licensing or registration action when a citation goes unanswered. The exact steps in New Hampshire depend on the court and the instructions on your ticket, so read those instructions carefully.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court clerk before the deadline on the citation and ask what options exist. Remember that the 24% penalty assessment is added to every base fine, so budget for the total, not just the fine.

If you are unsure of your deadline or the court, call the clerk listed on the citation rather than waiting.
