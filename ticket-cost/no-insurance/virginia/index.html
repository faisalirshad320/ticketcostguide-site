How much is a no insurance ticket in Virginia?

The exact amount is set by the court on your citation. We do not publish a single Virginia dollar figure because the amount you owe depends on what the court sets for your case.

For context, no insurance tickets nationally run $100–$1,500+ by state, plus possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences (Virginia Department of Motor Vehicles). That is a national range across all states, not a Virginia fine.

Your citation, the court's notice or the clerk's office is the only reliable place to find the amount you owe in Virginia.

The fine is only one part of the cost. Processing costs, possible vehicle or registration consequences and later insurance changes can add to the total.

How is the fine for no insurance set in Virginia?

In Virginia the court sets the amount within the state's traffic code. The code is published in the Virginia statutes, which you can read through Virginia statutes via Justia.

That means two drivers with the same charge could see different results depending on the court and the facts of the case. Prior history, whether the charge is amended and how the court handles the case can all matter.

There are two common paths. You can prepay if the citation allows it, or you can appear in court. Prepaying usually means accepting the charge, so read the citation before paying.

When a ticket can be prepaid, fixed processing costs apply to every prepaid ticket. Cases that involve reckless driving add court-set fines and fees on top.

What other costs come with a no insurance ticket?

The national range comes with possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences. These are separate from the fine itself.

  • Impound: a vehicle may be towed in some situations, which can bring towing and storage charges.
  • SR-22: this is a certificate of financial responsibility filed by an insurer. Some drivers are asked to carry one after an insurance-related violation.
  • Registration: registration can be affected until the insurance issue is resolved.
  • Processing costs: fixed processing costs apply to every prepaid ticket.

Which of these apply to you depends on your situation. The Virginia DMV is the right place to confirm registration and filing requirements.

How many points does a no insurance ticket add in Virginia?

Virginia's demerit system assigns 3–6 demerit points by offense, with 6 for reckless driving. We do not state a specific point value for a no insurance ticket. Check your citation or ask the DMV how your charge is treated.

The DMV suspends a license when a driver reaches point thresholds. Drivers also earn +1 safe point per clean year, which offsets violations over time.

Points and fines are separate. A court can set a fine while the DMV separately records points on your driving record.

To see where you stand, request your driving record from the Virginia DMV.

Will a no insurance ticket raise my insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Virginia-specific rate, and your own change could be higher or lower.

A lapse in coverage can also make shopping for a policy harder. Some insurers price differently for drivers with a gap in coverage or an SR-22 requirement.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend insurers. Compare quotes from several companies, and ask each one how it treats a conviction and any coverage gap.

Resolving the charge in a way that avoids a conviction, where possible, may limit the effect on your record. Only the court and your insurer can tell you how your own case is handled.

Can a no insurance ticket be dismissed or reduced in Virginia?

Sometimes, but the outcome is up to the court. Because the court sets the amount on the citation, results can vary between courts and cases.

Ideas drivers commonly ask about:

  • Proof of coverage: if you had valid insurance at the time, bring documentation. If coverage was bought afterward, ask the clerk whether the court considers it.
  • Court appearance: you can appear and explain your situation rather than prepaying.
  • Amended charge: a court may sometimes accept a different charge. That is a decision for the court and any prosecutor.

This is general information, not legal advice. For advice on your case, speak with a licensed attorney of your own choosing.

What happens if I don't pay a no insurance ticket?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. Unpaid or unanswered tickets can lead to added court-set fines and fees, and may trigger licence or registration consequences.

Because a no insurance charge already carries possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences, a missed deadline can compound the problem.

  1. Read the citation for the court date or payment instructions.
  2. Contact the court clerk if you are unsure of the amount or deadline.
  3. Pay, or appear in court, before the date on the citation.

If you cannot pay in full, ask the clerk what options the court offers. We do not list specific deadlines here, since they are set on your citation.
