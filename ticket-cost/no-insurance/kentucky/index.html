By Editorial Team, TicketCostGuide · Last reviewed 2026-10-16

Nationally, a no insurance ticket runs $100–$1,500+ depending on the state, plus possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences. In Kentucky, the court sets the exact amount on your citation, and court costs are set by the district court (Kentucky Transportation Cabinet). Amounts can differ by court.

How much is a no insurance ticket in Kentucky?

The amount is set by the court and printed on your citation. We do not state an exact Kentucky dollar figure because the court, not a fixed public price list, sets it within the state's traffic code.

For context, the national picture is $100–$1,500+ by state, plus possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences (Kentucky Transportation Cabinet). That range covers every state, so it is a national reference point and not a prediction for Kentucky.

The fine is only part of what you may owe. Court costs are added separately, as the next section explains.

How is the fine set, and what are court costs?

In Kentucky the court sets the fine within the state's traffic code. The citation, or the court that handles it, is the place to confirm what you owe.

Court costs are set by the district court and usually exceed the fine itself. A ticket whose fine looks modest can therefore carry a much larger total once costs are added.

Because the amount can differ by county or court, a figure quoted by a friend or found online may not match your citation. Always compare against the paperwork you were handed.

  • Fine: set by the court on the citation.
  • Court costs: set by the district court, usually more than the fine.
  • Other consequences: possible impound, SR-22 and registration issues.

The state's statutes are available through Justia. They are the text the courts work within. For official licensing information, see the Kentucky Transportation Cabinet.

How many points does a no insurance ticket carry in Kentucky?

Our sources do not list a specific point value for a no insurance citation. Do not assume a number. Check your citation and the court's instructions, or ask the licensing agency.

The Kentucky points schedule we reference lists 3 points (10–15 over) or 6 points (16–25 over). Those tiers are written for speeding. A speed of 26+ over triggers a hearing.

The suspension rule is 12 points in 2 years. If you already have points on your record, any new conviction that adds points moves you closer to that threshold.

Points are tied to the violation as charged and convicted. Confirm with the court which violation is on your record and whether it carries points.

Driver licensing details are published by the Kentucky Transportation Cabinet. A national comparison of state traffic laws is at the Governors Highway Safety Association.

Does a no insurance ticket raise your insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Kentucky-specific one.

A no insurance citation is not always treated the same as a moving violation. How a given insurer treats it depends on the insurer and on how the record shows the conviction.

A lapse in coverage can also affect the quotes you are offered, since insurers look at coverage history. We do not give advice on which insurer to use.

If a court or the state requires an SR-22, that is a separate filing that proves you carry coverage. It is one of the consequences that can follow a no insurance ticket nationally. Ask the court or the licensing agency whether it applies to you.

Can a no insurance ticket lead to impound, SR-22 or registration problems?

Nationally, yes. The sourced range of $100–$1,500+ comes with possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences. Whether any of these apply in your case depends on the citation and the court.

  • Impound: a vehicle may be towed or held in some situations, which adds towing and storage costs.
  • SR-22: a filing your insurer makes to show you have coverage. It may be required after an uninsured-driving violation.
  • Registration: the vehicle's registration can be affected until coverage is shown.

These extra costs can exceed the ticket. When you ask the court about the total, also ask which of these steps apply.

For the rules that govern each step, use the official Kentucky Transportation Cabinet pages and the statutes via Justia.

Can a no insurance ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes. We can only describe the general routes, not predict an outcome. The court decides what is available on your case.

  1. Read the citation. It shows the charge, the court and the response instructions.
  2. Gather proof of coverage. If you had a valid policy at the time, documents from your insurer may matter to the court.
  3. Contact the court. Ask what options exist, what the total is including court costs, and how to respond.
  4. Consider a licensed attorney. For a complex situation, a licensed attorney can advise you. TicketCostGuide does not recommend specific attorneys.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice. This page explains how costs are generally set so you can ask better questions.

Any reduction in the fine does not automatically reduce court costs, which are set by the district court and usually exceed the fine itself.

What happens if I don't pay or respond?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts can add consequences, and the original amount can grow with additional costs.

Points matter too. The state's rule is that 12 points in 2 years means suspension. Unresolved tickets on top of points can put your license at risk.

If you cannot pay the full amount, ask the court what it offers before the deadline on the citation. Court practices differ by county and court, so ask the specific court handling your case.

Driving while your license is suspended can bring further penalties. For current licensing rules, check the Kentucky Transportation Cabinet.
