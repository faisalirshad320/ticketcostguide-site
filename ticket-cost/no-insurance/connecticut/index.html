How much is a no insurance ticket in Connecticut?

Nationally, a no insurance ticket runs $100–$1,500+ by state, plus possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences (Connecticut Department of Motor Vehicles). That is a national range, not a Connecticut price.

In Connecticut, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Connecticut dollar figure, because the number you owe is the one printed on your ticket or court notice.

Bottom line: use the national range only as a rough sense of scale. Your citation, not this page, tells you what you owe.

The payable amount also includes state fees and surcharges. Those are built into the total on the ticket, so you should not expect to add them on top.

How is the fine for no insurance set in Connecticut?

The fine is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The statutes are published in the Connecticut statutes via Justia, which is where the underlying rules on the violation can be read.

Three things shape the number you see:

  • The court's amount for the violation on your citation.
  • State fees and surcharges, which are built into the payable amount.
  • Any additional consequences tied to the offense, such as impound, SR-22 or registration action.

Because the court sets the amount, two drivers with the same charge should still read their own citations rather than rely on what someone else paid.

A comparison of state approaches is available from the Governors Highway Safety Association, which tracks state highway safety laws.

How many points does a no insurance ticket add in Connecticut?

Our locked reference describes the Connecticut DMV point system this way: points are added for violations (the example given is speeding), and 10 points = 30-day suspension.

That reference does not give a point value for a no insurance conviction, and we will not guess one. Whether points attach to your charge, and how many, is something to confirm with the Connecticut DMV or on your court paperwork.

Why it matters: points stack. A ticket that adds nothing on its own can still matter if your record is already close to the 10-point suspension threshold.

If you have other recent violations, ask the DMV what your current record shows before deciding how to respond.

Does a no insurance ticket raise my insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Connecticut-specific one, and individual results vary by insurer and driving record.

A no insurance ticket also creates a practical problem: you will need to buy a policy, and a lapse in coverage is something insurers see when pricing. TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer, so compare quotes yourself.

If an SR-22 is required, the insurer must file it for you. That requirement is one of the consequences the national picture lists alongside the fine.

What else can happen besides the fine?

The national range comes with extras: possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences. These are listed as possibilities, and which ones apply in Connecticut depends on the citation and the court or DMV action that follows.

  • Impound: the vehicle may be towed, which adds towing and storage costs we cannot quantify here.
  • SR-22: a certificate of financial responsibility that your insurer files with the state.
  • Registration: the vehicle's registration may be affected until the issue is resolved.

These costs can exceed the fine itself, so read every notice you receive from the court and the DMV.

Can a no insurance ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Whether a charge can be reduced or dismissed is up to the court. Our locked facts do not list specific dismissal rules, so we cannot tell you what qualifies.

What you can do is read the citation for the court date, the appearance or payment options, and any instructions about showing proof of coverage. Courts often set out these steps on the notice itself.

  1. Read the citation and note the deadline.
  2. Check what the court says about paying versus appearing.
  3. Gather any documents that relate to your coverage on the date of the ticket.
  4. Contact the court clerk with procedural questions.

What happens if I don't pay a no insurance ticket?

Ignoring a ticket rarely makes it go away. Because the amount is set by the court on the citation, the court is also the place that handles late payment or missed dates.

Unpaid or ignored tickets can lead to further consequences with the court and the DMV. Our locked facts do not give exact late fees or deadlines, so check your citation for dates and amounts.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court clerk before the deadline and ask what options exist. Doing so early is usually better than waiting for a notice.
