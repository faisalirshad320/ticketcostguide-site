How much is a no insurance ticket in Wyoming?

Nationally, a no insurance ticket runs $100–$1,500+ by state, plus possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences. In Wyoming, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Wyoming Department of Transportation).

That national range is a spread across all states. It is not a Wyoming price list, and we do not state a single Wyoming fine here.

The number you owe is the one printed on your citation or given to you by the court clerk. Court fees are added per the local schedule, so two people with the same charge can see different totals.

Treat the national range as context only. Your citation and the court that handles it control the amount you actually owe.

How is the fine set for a no insurance ticket in Wyoming?

The fine is set by the court within the state's traffic code. It is not a fixed figure that applies the same way everywhere.

Several things can shape what ends up on the citation or in the court's order:

  • The court that hears the case and its local fee schedule.
  • Whether this is a first citation or a repeat one.
  • What the court decides after you appear or respond.

Court fees are added per the local schedule. That means the base fine and the added fees may be listed separately, and the total is what you pay to close the case.

Wyoming statutes are published online through Wyoming statutes via Justia. If you want to read the traffic code yourself, start there. Reading the statute does not replace asking the court what it will charge on your specific citation.

How many points does a no insurance ticket add in Wyoming?

Wyoming has no demerit-point system. A no insurance ticket does not add points to your license the way it would in a point-based state.

That does not mean the ticket has no license consequences. Suspensions follow repeat convictions and court action.

In practice, that makes the court record the thing to watch. A single citation handled promptly is a different situation from several convictions, or from a case where the court has taken action because something went unresolved.

  • No points: there is no point total to track or work down.
  • Repeat convictions: these are tied to suspensions.
  • Court action: an unresolved case can bring its own consequences.

Does a no insurance ticket raise my insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Wyoming-specific one.

Your own change could be higher, lower or nothing, depending on your insurer, your driving history and how the violation is recorded. We do not recommend any insurer and cannot predict what yours will do.

A no insurance citation also raises a separate issue: a gap or lapse in coverage. Insurers often look closely at lapses when you apply for a new policy. If an SR-22 is required, that is a certificate your insurer files to show you carry coverage, and it is one of the consequences listed alongside the national range.

If you were uninsured because of a lapse, getting a policy in place quickly matters. Ask the court and the agency named on your paperwork what proof they accept.

Can a no insurance ticket be dismissed or reduced in Wyoming?

Sometimes courts treat these citations differently depending on the facts. The decision belongs to the court, and we cannot promise any outcome.

Questions worth asking the court clerk before your deadline:

  • Was the citation for having no coverage, or for failing to show proof of coverage?
  • If you had a valid policy at the time, what proof does the court accept, and by when?
  • Does the court allow a written response, or does it require an appearance?
  • What would the total be after court fees under the local schedule?

If you had coverage at the time, gather your policy documents before you contact the court. If you did not, be ready to explain your current coverage status.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore a no insurance ticket?

Ignoring the citation tends to make things worse. Wyoming suspensions follow repeat convictions and court action, and an unresolved case can lead to court action.

Nationally, a no insurance violation can also bring impound, SR-22 and registration consequences. Which of these apply in your case depends on the court and the agency involved.

  1. Read the citation for the court name, the deadline and the response options.
  2. Contact the court clerk before the deadline if anything is unclear.
  3. Ask for the total owed, including court fees added per the local schedule.
  4. Keep proof of payment and any proof of insurance you provide.

If you cannot pay the full amount at once, ask the clerk whether the court offers any payment arrangement. Do not assume one exists, and do not wait until after the deadline.

What are my options after getting a no insurance ticket?

Most people have a few paths. The citation should say which ones the court offers.

  • Pay the amount: this usually resolves the case, but it is typically treated as a conviction.
  • Respond to the court: you may be able to contest the citation or ask about the court's process.
  • Show proof of coverage: if you were insured, the court may want to see it.
  • Get coverage in place: this addresses the underlying problem whatever else happens.

Because Wyoming has no demerit-point system, you are not working to avoid points. The focus is on the fine, the court fees, your record and your insurance.

Paying can affect your insurance, since moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. Weigh that against the other options before you decide.
