How much is a no insurance ticket in South Dakota?

The lead figure for this page is: Nationally $100–$1,500+ by state, plus possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences; in South Dakota the exact amount is set by the court on the citation — No Insurance Ticket cost in South Dakota (South Dakota Department of Public Safety).

That range is a national one. It is not a South Dakota price list, and we do not publish an exact South Dakota dollar amount because the court sets it on your citation.

The fine is only part of the cost. Depending on the state and the facts, a no insurance stop can also bring an impound, an SR-22 requirement and registration problems. Those extras can matter as much as the fine itself.

Your citation and the court that issued it are the only reliable source for your exact amount. Treat any single number you see online, including ranges, as a guide only.

How is the fine for no insurance set in South Dakota?

In South Dakota the amount is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The codified statutes are available through South Dakota statutes via Justia, which is where the governing language sits.

Court costs are included in the standard scheduled amounts. In practice, that means a scheduled amount should already account for court costs rather than adding them as a separate line. If your citation lists a figure, read it carefully and ask the clerk what it covers.

Because the court sets the amount, two people cited for the same violation could see different outcomes depending on the court and the circumstances. We do not have tier data to show you, so we do not guess at one.

  • Look at the citation for a stated amount or court date.
  • Ask the court clerk what the amount includes.
  • Check whether any extra requirements, such as proof of coverage, are listed.

How many points does a no insurance ticket carry in South Dakota?

South Dakota assigns points per conviction toward suspension thresholds. The locked threshold is 15 points in 12 months (South Dakota Department of Public Safety).

We do not have a sourced point value for a no insurance conviction, so we do not state one. Whether this specific violation adds points, and how many, is something to confirm with the Department of Public Safety or the court before you assume anything.

The threshold still matters if you have other recent convictions. Points from separate tickets within the same 12-month window count toward the same total.

Does a no insurance ticket raise your car insurance?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a South Dakota quote.

Whether a no insurance conviction is treated the same way as a moving violation depends on the insurer and its rating rules. We cannot say how any particular company will react, and we do not recommend insurers.

A lapse in coverage can also make shopping for a new policy harder. If an SR-22 is required, you would generally need an insurer willing to file it, which can limit your choices.

  • Ask any insurer how a no insurance conviction is rated before you buy.
  • Ask whether a gap in coverage changes your quote.
  • If an SR-22 is required, ask whether the insurer files it.

What are the other consequences besides the fine?

The national figure includes possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences. These are the add-ons that can turn a modest fine into a larger expense.

An impound can mean towing and storage charges that are separate from the court fine. We have no sourced figure for those, so check with the agency that towed the vehicle.

An SR-22 is a form an insurer files to show you carry required coverage. If you are told you need one, the requirement and its length come from the state, so confirm the details with the Department of Public Safety.

Registration consequences can mean your vehicle registration is affected until coverage is shown. Ask the court or the state how to clear this.

Can a no insurance ticket be dismissed or reduced?

That is up to the court. We cannot predict an outcome, and nothing here is legal advice.

In many places, a driver who had valid coverage on the date of the stop may be able to show proof to the court. Whether that leads to a dismissal or a reduced amount is the court's decision, so ask the clerk what documents it accepts and by when.

If you were uninsured, the court may still have discretion within the traffic code, since the amount is set by the court on the citation. Some people choose to speak with a licensed attorney. We do not recommend a specific attorney.

Do not assume a ticket will go away on its own. Contact the court before the date on the citation.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore it?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts can add consequences when a case goes unanswered, and licensing or registration problems can follow.

The national figure already points to registration consequences and possible impound for no insurance cases. We have no sourced figure for late penalties in South Dakota, so confirm them with the court.

Convictions can also add points toward the 15-point-in-12-months suspension threshold. An unresolved case can complicate your record, so deal with it before the deadline.
