How much is a no insurance ticket in Hawaii?

The honest short version is that the exact Hawaii amount depends on the court and the citation you receive. Here is the lead figure we can support:

Nationally $100–$1,500+ by state, plus possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences; in Hawaii the exact amount is set by the court on the citation — No Insurance Ticket cost in Hawaii. (Hawaii Department of Transportation)

That range is national. It is not a Hawaii fine schedule, and we do not present it as one.

Your total bill is usually more than the fine itself. Court fees and assessments are added by the district court.

  • The fine: set by the court on the citation, within the state's traffic code.
  • Court fees and assessments: added by the district court.
  • Possible knock-on costs: impound, SR-22 and registration consequences, plus higher insurance premiums.

Read the citation itself. It is the best guide to what you are being asked to pay or answer for.

How is the fine set for driving without insurance in Hawaii?

In Hawaii the fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. It is not a single flat number printed in a national table.

That is why you will see different dollar figures quoted online. Many of them are national averages or figures from other states, and they may not match what a Hawaii court puts on your citation.

The statutes are published online. You can read the Hawaii code through Hawaii statutes via Justia, and the Hawaii Department of Transportation keeps general information at hidot.hawaii.gov.

If the citation shows an amount, treat that as the starting point. If it directs you to appear in court, the amount may be decided at that stage.

We do not publish an exact Hawaii fine for this violation. If a site gives you one without citing the court or statute, check it against your citation.

What court costs and surcharges are added?

Court fees and assessments are added by the district court. These sit on top of the base fine, so the amount you pay can be higher than the fine alone.

We do not list individual surcharge amounts for Hawaii, because we only publish figures we can source to a locked reference. The court or the citation will show the full amount due.

When you budget for the ticket, think in three layers:

  1. The fine set by the court.
  2. Court fees and assessments added by the district court.
  3. Indirect costs, such as insurance changes, impound and registration consequences.

The third layer is often the hardest to predict. It depends on your insurer, your vehicle's status and what the court orders.

How many points does a no insurance ticket carry in Hawaii?

Hawaii has no demerit-point system. Repeat convictions and excessive-speeding convictions drive license action directly.

So there is no point value to look up for this ticket. The question to ask is how a conviction fits into your driving record and whether you have prior convictions.

Because license action is tied to repeat convictions, a second or later conviction deserves closer attention than a first. The court and the Hawaii Department of Transportation can tell you how a conviction is recorded for your record.

  • No points to count: there is no demerit-point total to track.
  • Repeat convictions matter: they can drive license action directly.
  • Check your record: confirm what is on it before you decide how to respond.

Does a no insurance ticket raise my insurance rates?

It can. Nationally, moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Hawaii-specific one.

We cannot say how much your own premium will change. Insurers use their own rating rules, and they treat a conviction, a lapse in coverage and a claim history differently.

A no insurance ticket also tells a prospective insurer that your coverage lapsed or was missing. Some drivers are asked to file an SR-22 after this kind of violation, which is a certificate of financial responsibility. SR-22 requirements are one of the national consequences linked to this ticket.

If you are shopping for coverage after a ticket, get quotes from more than one company and ask each how it treats a conviction on your record. TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer.

Keeping continuous coverage from now on is the simplest way to avoid repeating this ticket and the costs that come with it.

Can a no insurance ticket be dismissed or reduced in Hawaii?

Because the court sets the amount on the citation, the court is also where any request for a change would be raised. We cannot tell you the outcome in your case, and this page is not legal advice.

Questions drivers commonly ask the court clerk or raise at a hearing include:

  • Was I actually insured on the date on the citation, and can I show proof?
  • Is there an error on the citation, such as the wrong date, vehicle or details?
  • Does the court allow a payment arrangement or other options?
  • How will a conviction be recorded if I pay without contesting?

Paying the citation generally resolves it, but it may also mean a conviction on your record. If you have prior convictions, that matters because repeat convictions drive license action directly in Hawaii.

If you want advice on your specific situation, consider speaking with a licensed attorney. We do not recommend a specific one.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the ticket?

Ignoring a citation rarely makes it go away. Courts can add consequences when a citation is not answered, and the amount you owe may grow once the court adds its fees and assessments.

Nationally, the consequences tied to a no insurance violation can include impound, SR-22 and registration consequences. Which of these applies to you in Hawaii depends on your circumstances and what the court orders.

The practical steps are:

  1. Read the citation for the response instructions and any court date.
  2. Contact the district court listed on the citation if anything is unclear.
  3. Respond by the stated date rather than waiting.

If you cannot pay, ask the court what options exist before the deadline passes.
