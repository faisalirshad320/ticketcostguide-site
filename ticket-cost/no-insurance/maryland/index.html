How much is a no insurance ticket in Maryland?

Nationally, a no insurance ticket costs $100–$1,500+ by state, plus possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences. In Maryland the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Maryland Motor Vehicle Administration).

We do not list a single Maryland dollar figure. The amount can differ by court and by case, and a number copied from a forum or an old article may be wrong.

The national range is a national range. Your own citation is the best guide to what Maryland is asking you to pay.

Read the citation closely. It should show whether there is a prepay amount, whether a court appearance is required, and what your options are.

How is the fine set?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The statutes are published online through Maryland statutes via Justia.

That means the figure on your citation comes from a court process, not from a single flat price that applies to every driver. The national range is wide because states set these penalties differently.

  • The citation shows the amount the court has set for your case.
  • The traffic code sets the outer limits the court works within.
  • Extra consequences, such as registration action, may come on top of the fine.

For the current rules, check the statute text and the Maryland MVA directly. We summarize them but do not replace them.

What changes if I request a court date?

Requesting a court date replaces the prepay amount with a judge-set fine of up to $500. That is the locked Maryland figure we can source for this situation.

In practice, paying the prepay amount and going to court are different paths. If you prepay, you accept the amount on the citation. If you ask for a court date, a judge decides the fine.

Up to $500 is a ceiling for the judge-set fine, not a prediction. The actual amount depends on the court and the case.

Whether a hearing is worth it depends on your situation. TicketCostGuide does not give legal advice, so for case-specific questions consider speaking with a licensed professional of your choosing.

How many points does a no insurance ticket carry in Maryland?

Maryland uses a points system with these tiers: 1 point (9 or less over), 2 points (10–29 over) and 5 points (30+ over). Eight points means suspension, and 12 points means revocation.

Those tiers are described by how far over a limit a driver was. They do not translate directly into a point value for a no insurance citation, and we do not state one.

To find out whether your citation adds points, check the citation itself and ask the Maryland Motor Vehicle Administration. Do not assume the tiers above apply to your case.

  • 8 points = suspension
  • 12 points = revocation

Does a no insurance ticket raise my insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Maryland-specific one.

Whether a no insurance conviction affects your rate depends on the insurer and how it treats the record. We do not recommend any insurer, and we cannot say what your own premium will do.

The national range also lists SR-22 consequences. An SR-22 is a filing some states require to show a driver is carrying coverage. Whether it applies to you is a question for the MVA and the court.

The 20% figure is an average across the US. Your own change could be higher, lower or none.

Can a no insurance ticket be dismissed or reduced?

It depends on the court and the facts. Our locked data does not include dismissal rules or reduction rates, so we do not state any.

What we can say is that a requested court date puts the fine in a judge's hands, with a ceiling of $500 for the judge-set fine. That is different from paying the prepay amount.

  • Read the citation for deadlines and instructions.
  • Ask the court clerk what documents it accepts.
  • Check the traffic code through Maryland statutes via Justia.

We do not give legal advice. If the stakes feel high, a licensed professional can look at your specific situation.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore it?

We cannot give Maryland-specific penalty amounts for unpaid citations, because they are not part of our sourced data. The national range does warn of possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences.

Ignoring a citation tends to make a situation harder, since the court sets the process and the amount. Points can matter too: 8 points means suspension and 12 points means revocation.

If you are unsure what your citation requires, contact the court named on it or the Maryland Motor Vehicle Administration.

Tell the court or the MVA directly if something about your citation is unclear. Guessing can add problems.
