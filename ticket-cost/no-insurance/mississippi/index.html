How much is a no insurance ticket in Mississippi?

The lead figure is national: $100–$1,500+ by state, plus possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences. In Mississippi the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Mississippi Department of Public Safety).

We do not publish a single Mississippi dollar amount. The number on your citation, or the one the court sets, is the one that counts. Treat any exact figure you see elsewhere without a court or statute source with caution.

The total you owe is usually more than the fine alone. Mandatory state assessments are added to every fine and often exceed it.

Read the citation and any court notice for the amount due. A national range tells you the general scale, not what you will pay.

How is the fine for no insurance set in Mississippi?

The exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. That means the statute gives the framework, and the court decides the amount on your citation.

You can read the Mississippi traffic code through the statutes index on Justia. Look in the titles covering motor vehicles and traffic, since the insurance requirement and its penalties sit there.

Because the court sets the amount, two drivers cited for the same thing can see different results. We do not list a formula, since none is part of our sourced figures.

For a comparison of how other states handle insurance and traffic rules, the Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a state laws index.

Why does the total cost more than the fine?

Mandatory state assessments are added to every fine. In many cases these assessments exceed the fine itself.

This is why the number on a citation can differ from what you pay at the clerk's window. The fine is one line, and the assessments are added to it.

  • Ask the clerk for the full amount due, not just the fine.
  • Ask whether the total shown includes all assessments.
  • Keep the receipt and any case number.

We do not state dollar amounts for the assessments. They are not part of the figures we can source, and the court's own total is the reliable one.

How many points does a no insurance ticket add in Mississippi?

Mississippi has no point system. A no insurance ticket does not add points to your driving record, because there is no points tally to add them to.

That does not mean the ticket has no driving-record consequences. Suspensions follow repeat convictions and specific offenses, so a conviction can matter if you have others.

If you want to know where your own record stands, contact the Mississippi Department of Public Safety. Ask what is on your record and whether any suspension is pending.

No points does not mean no consequences. Repeat convictions and certain offenses are what lead to suspensions.

Does a no insurance ticket raise my insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums ~20% on average. This is a national figure and not a Mississippi-specific one.

How an insurer treats a no insurance conviction depends on the insurer and your overall record. A gap in coverage can also affect the rates you are quoted. We do not give a dollar estimate, since none is part of our sourced figures.

The national consequences listed with the fine range include SR-22. An SR-22 is a certificate of financial responsibility filed by an insurer. Whether you would need one is something to confirm with the court or the Department of Public Safety.

We do not recommend specific insurers. If you are shopping for coverage after a ticket, compare quotes from several companies and ask each how a conviction is rated.

Can a no insurance ticket be dismissed or reduced?

The court decides. The fine is set by the court on the citation, so any change to it also goes through the court.

We do not have sourced rules on dismissal or reduction, so we will not claim any. What we can say is how to approach it.

  1. Read the citation for the court name, the date and any instructions.
  2. Contact the clerk and ask what options the court offers for this charge.
  3. If you had coverage in force, gather the documents showing it. If you did not, do not alter or backdate anything.
  4. If you want legal advice, consider speaking with a licensed Mississippi attorney. We do not recommend a specific one.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and this page is not legal advice.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse, not better. Mississippi suspensions follow repeat convictions and specific offenses, and an unresolved case can add to your problems with the court.

The national consequences tied to this violation include possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences. These are reasons to deal with the citation and your coverage promptly.

  • Check the citation for the deadline and the court's contact details.
  • Contact the court clerk before the deadline if you cannot pay the full amount.
  • Get active insurance in place so the problem does not repeat.

We do not state deadlines or late penalties, since those come from the court and the citation itself.
