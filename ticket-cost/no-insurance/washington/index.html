How much is a no insurance ticket in Washington?

Nationally, a no insurance ticket runs $100–$1,500+ by state, plus possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences (Washington State Department of Licensing). That is a national range, not a Washington price.

In Washington, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Washington dollar figure here, because the number printed on your ticket or on the court's notice is the one that counts.

Look at the citation itself. It identifies the violation, the court that will handle it and the amount or instructions for finding it.

The total can differ from the base fine on a published schedule once statutory assessments are included. The next section explains how that works.

How is the fine set for a no insurance ticket in Washington?

Washington traffic infractions are handled in courts of limited jurisdiction, under the Infraction Rules for Courts of Limited Jurisdiction (IRLJ). The IRLJ schedule lists amounts for many common violations.

Statutory assessments are built into the IRLJ schedule amounts. This means the schedule figure is meant to be the all-in amount, rather than a base fine with separate surcharges added on top.

The exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. That is why two people cited in different places, or under different circumstances, may be told different amounts.

  • The citation lists the violation you were charged with.
  • The court handling it sets the amount within the traffic code.
  • The schedule amount already includes statutory assessments.

For the statute text, Washington's codes are available through Justia. If you want to confirm which provision applies to you, compare the citation language to the code.

How many points does a no insurance ticket add in Washington?

Washington has no point system. There is no point count to look up for this ticket, and no point total that builds toward a suspension.

Suspensions instead follow repeat moving-violation convictions within set windows. What counts is how many convictions you have and how close together they fall.

Whether a no insurance citation counts as a moving violation for that purpose depends on how it is classified. The Department of Licensing keeps the driver record, so its site is the place to check your own record and any license action (Washington State Department of Licensing).

No points does not mean no record. A conviction can still show on your driving record and can matter if you are cited again within a set window.

Does a no insurance ticket raise my insurance rates?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Washington-specific one.

Your own change could be smaller or larger. It depends on your insurer, your record and whether the ticket is treated as a moving violation.

A lapse in coverage can also affect how insurers see you, separate from the ticket. Getting a policy in force promptly is usually worth more than any single step in the court process.

We do not recommend any insurer. Compare quotes yourself and ask each company how it treats a coverage gap and a conviction.

What else can happen besides the fine: impound, SR-22 and registration?

The national range comes with a warning: possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences. These can add cost and hassle beyond the fine.

  • Impound: in some situations a vehicle can be towed, and towing and storage are separate costs.
  • SR-22: this is a certificate of financial responsibility filed by an insurer. Some drivers are required to carry one after certain violations.
  • Registration: proof of insurance can be tied to keeping or renewing a registration.

Which of these applies to you depends on your facts and on what the court and the licensing department require. Ask the court clerk, and check the Department of Licensing for license and record questions (Washington State Department of Licensing).

A broader look at how states differ on insurance and traffic laws is on the Governors Highway Safety Association state laws page.

Can a no insurance ticket be dismissed or reduced in Washington?

Sometimes, but we cannot promise it, and this page is not legal advice. Courts decide each case, and the outcome depends on the facts and the judge or court's practices.

Under the IRLJ, people cited for an infraction generally have a choice of responses: pay, ask for a hearing to explain the circumstances, or contest the citation. The instructions on your citation describe the options and the deadline.

Things that people commonly raise with the court include:

  • Whether you had coverage at the time of the stop.
  • Whether you can show proof of that coverage.
  • Whether the citation has an error on it.

Whether any of these changes the result is up to the court. Contact the court clerk for procedure questions. If you want advice on your specific case, consult a licensed attorney, though we do not recommend any specific one.

Read the response deadline on your citation. Missing it can turn a manageable ticket into a bigger problem.

What happens if I don't pay a no insurance ticket?

Ignoring the ticket is usually the costliest choice. Courts generally follow up on unanswered infractions, and the consequences can grow beyond the original amount.

Washington suspensions are tied to repeat moving-violation convictions within set windows. An unresolved ticket can complicate that picture, and it can also affect your license status and registration.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court before the deadline. Many courts have a process for requesting more time or a payment arrangement, but options vary by court.

The citation and the court's notice remain the controlling documents. Follow what they say, and keep copies of anything you send.
