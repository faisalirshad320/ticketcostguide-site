How much is a no insurance ticket in Georgia?

The exact fine is set by the court on your citation. We do not publish a single Georgia dollar amount because the figure depends on the court handling the case and on the details of the violation.

For context, nationally a no insurance ticket costs $100–$1,500+ by state, plus possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences (Georgia Department of Driver Services). That is a national range, not a Georgia fine.

Your citation, or the clerk of the court listed on it, is the authoritative source for what you owe. Amounts can vary by county or court.

The fine is only part of the cost. Reinstatement steps, proof of coverage and higher insurance rates can add to the total over time.

How is the fine set in Georgia?

Georgia sets traffic offenses in its state traffic code, and the court that hears your case decides the fine within that framework. You can read the statutes through Justia's Georgia code.

Because the court sets the amount, two drivers cited for the same thing in different places may see different totals. Court costs and state fees can also be added to the base fine.

One example of a state fee is the $200 Super Speeder fee, which is added on top of the court fine. It is tied to the Super Speeder rule, so check your citation to see whether any state fee applies to your case.

  • The base fine comes from the court on your citation.
  • Court costs and surcharges may be added.
  • The total due is what the clerk shows, not what a general guide estimates.

How many points does a no insurance ticket add in Georgia?

We do not have a sourced point value for a no insurance conviction, so we will not guess one. Check with the court or the Georgia Department of Driver Services about how your specific citation is treated (Georgia DDS).

What we can source is Georgia's points scale for speeding: 2 points for 15–18 mph over, 3 points for 19–23 over, 4 points for 24–33 over and 6 points for 34 or more over. The same data states that 15 points in 24 months leads to suspension.

That scale is for speeding and should not be read as the no insurance value. It does show how points add up toward the suspension threshold if you already have other violations on your record.

Does a no insurance ticket raise your insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, and we do not have a Georgia-specific number.

Whether a no insurance citation counts the same way depends on the insurer and how it rates the violation. A lapse in coverage can also affect the rates you are quoted, separately from the ticket.

The national range for this violation includes possible SR-22 consequences (Georgia DDS). An SR-22 is a certificate your insurer files to show you carry coverage. If one is required, ask the DDS and your insurer what applies to you.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer. Compare quotes yourself and ask each company how it treats your record.

What else can happen besides the fine?

The national range for a no insurance ticket includes more than a fine. Depending on the state, there can be impound, SR-22 and registration consequences on top of the money you owe.

  • Impound: the vehicle may be towed, adding towing and storage costs.
  • SR-22: you may need to file proof of coverage for a period set by the state.
  • Registration: your registration can be affected until coverage is shown.

We do not have Georgia-specific amounts or durations for these, so confirm each one with the DDS or the court.

What happens if I don't pay?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. The court can add penalties, and your license can be put at risk.

For the $200 Super Speeder state fee, failure to pay it within 120 days suspends the license. We cannot say whether that fee applies to a no insurance ticket, so read your citation closely.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the clerk of the court before the due date. Many courts have a process for questions about payment, but terms vary by court.

Can a no insurance ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Some courts look at whether you had valid coverage at the time of the stop and can show proof. Whether that leads to a dismissal or a lower fine is up to the court, and we cannot promise any outcome.

A reasonable first step is to read the citation for the court date and the instructions. Then contact the clerk to learn what the court accepts as proof of insurance and what your choices are.

  • Gather your policy documents and any proof of coverage dates.
  • Ask the clerk what is accepted and by when.
  • Consider talking to a licensed attorney if you want legal advice. We do not recommend specific attorneys.
