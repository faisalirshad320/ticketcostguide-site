How much is a no insurance ticket in Vermont?

The honest short answer is that the exact Vermont amount is set by the court on the citation. Nationally, these tickets run $100–$1,500+ depending on the state, plus possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences (Vermont Department of Motor Vehicles).

That national range is a spread across all states. It is not a Vermont price, and we do not put a Vermont dollar figure on this page that our sources do not support.

Your own citation is the best guide to what you owe. It should show the amount or tell you how to find it, and it may include statutory surcharges.

Use the national range only as context. The number that matters is the one the court sets on your Vermont citation.

How is the fine for no insurance set in Vermont?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The code itself is published in the Vermont statutes (Vermont statutes via Justia).

Several things can affect what appears on your citation:

  • The court handling the case and the amount it assigns.
  • Statutory surcharges, which are included in the waiver amounts.
  • Whether this is a first citation or you have a prior record.
  • Whether other violations were written on the same stop.

Because the amount comes from the court, two drivers cited for the same thing can see different totals. If the citation is unclear, the court clerk's office is the place to ask what the total is and how it was calculated.

Are there extra court costs or surcharges?

Our sourced facts say statutory surcharges are included in the waiver amounts. That means if the citation offers a waiver amount, the surcharges are already part of that total rather than added on top.

We do not list a separate surcharge figure for Vermont because our sources do not give one. Do not assume a number you read on another site applies to your ticket.

If you pay by a different route than the waiver, such as after a hearing, ask the court whether the total changes. The court is the authority on the final amount.

How many points does a no insurance ticket carry in Vermont?

Vermont's points system, as described in our sourced facts, assigns 2–8 points by mph over, and 10 points in 2 years = suspension. Those tiers are tied to speed, so they describe speeding-type violations.

Our sources do not give a specific point value for a no insurance violation. We will not guess one. Check the citation, which may list any points, and confirm with the Vermont DMV (Vermont Department of Motor Vehicles).

The suspension threshold still matters if you already have points. Points from other tickets can stack toward it, so a new citation is worth reading carefully against your record.

Separate from points, the national range notes possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences. Those are not point-based and can affect you even if no points are added.

Does a no insurance ticket raise my insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Vermont-specific one.

Whether a no insurance conviction is treated like a moving violation depends on the insurer and its rules. We cannot say how any specific company will price it.

The mention of SR-22 in the national range is worth understanding. An SR-22 is a certificate of financial responsibility that some states require after certain violations. Whether it applies to your Vermont case is something to confirm with the court or the DMV, not assume.

Drivers often find that a lapse in coverage is itself a pricing factor, separate from the ticket. Ask your own insurer how it views your history. We do not recommend any insurer.

Can a no insurance ticket be dismissed or reduced in Vermont?

Outcomes are decided by the court, and we cannot predict them. What we can describe are the general paths people look at, so you know what to ask about.

  • Read the citation. It explains the charge, the amount and how to respond.
  • Check your coverage records. If you were insured at the time, documentation from your insurer may matter to the court.
  • Ask the court clerk. The clerk can explain deadlines, waiver options and how to contest.
  • Consider a lawyer. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not recommend any attorney, but a licensed Vermont attorney can advise on your situation.

Whether a reduction or dismissal is available is up to the court. Do not rely on a general guide for that decision.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse, but the specific penalties for Vermont are set by the court and the state. Our sources do not give a deadline or a late-fee figure, so we do not state one.

The national range already flags possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences. Unpaid or unresolved citations can add to those risks. Separately, 10 points in 2 years = suspension under the points system described above, so any added points matter.

The safest move is to respond by the date on the citation, even if only to ask the court a question. Contact the court listed on the ticket, and check your registration and license status with the Vermont DMV (Vermont Department of Motor Vehicles).
