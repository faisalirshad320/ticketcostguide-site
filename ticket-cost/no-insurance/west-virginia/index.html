How much is a no insurance ticket in West Virginia?

Nationally, a no insurance ticket costs $100–$1,500+ by state, plus possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences. In West Virginia, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (West Virginia Division of Motor Vehicles).

We do not publish an exact West Virginia dollar figure because the amount depends on the court and the facts of the stop. The national range is a guide to how wide the spread can be, not a prediction for your ticket.

The number on your citation or court notice is the one that counts. If it is unclear, the court clerk can tell you what is owed.

The fine is only part of the bill. Court costs are added on top, and they are covered in the next section.

How is the fine and total set?

West Virginia courts set the fine within the state's traffic code. That means two people cited for the same offense can see different totals depending on the court and the case details.

Municipal and magistrate court costs vary and usually exceed the fine. So the total you pay can be noticeably higher than the fine line alone.

  • Fine: set by the court on the citation.
  • Court costs and surcharges: vary by municipal or magistrate court.
  • Other costs: possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences.

To see the underlying rules, you can read the state code through West Virginia statutes via Justia. Statutes can be dense, and this page is not legal advice.

How many points does a no insurance ticket add?

West Virginia's points system, as summarized in our sourced data, runs 2–5 points by mph over, and 12+ points = suspension. Those point values are tied to speed, so they describe speeding violations.

We have no sourced point value for a no insurance violation, and we will not guess one. Check your citation, or ask the court clerk or the West Virginia Division of Motor Vehicles, whether this charge adds any points to your record.

Points matter because they accumulate. If you already have points from other violations, even a small addition could bring you closer to the suspension threshold.

Does a no insurance ticket raise my insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a West Virginia quote.

Whether this particular ticket counts as a moving violation for your insurer is a question for the insurer. Lapses in coverage can also affect how insurers see you, separate from any ticket.

We do not recommend any insurer. Comparing quotes after a conviction or a lapse is a common step, but your actual change depends on your record and your company's rules.

The ~20% figure is a national average. Your own increase could be higher, lower or nothing.

What are the impound, SR-22 and registration consequences?

The national range comes with warnings: possible impound, SR-22 and registration consequences. These can cost more than the fine itself.

  • Impound: a vehicle may be towed and held, with tow and storage fees owed to get it back.
  • SR-22: a certificate of financial responsibility that some drivers must have filed by an insurer after certain violations.
  • Registration: your vehicle registration may be affected until the insurance issue is resolved.

We do not have sourced dollar amounts for these items in West Virginia, so none are listed here. The West Virginia Division of Motor Vehicles is the place to confirm what applies to your registration and license.

Can a no insurance ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes courts treat cases differently depending on the facts, such as whether coverage was actually in place at the time. We cannot tell you whether that applies to you.

Practical steps to consider:

  1. Read the citation for the court name, the charge and any listed deadline.
  2. Gather any insurance documents that show coverage dates.
  3. Contact the court clerk to ask what options exist and how to respond.
  4. If the stakes are high, consider speaking with a licensed attorney of your own choosing.

TicketCostGuide does not give legal advice and does not recommend specific attorneys. Respond to the court before any deadline on the citation.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts can add costs, and licenses or registrations can be put at risk. The exact steps depend on the court and the West Virginia code.

Since municipal and magistrate court costs vary and usually exceed the fine, an unpaid ticket can grow beyond the original total. We do not have a sourced figure for late fees, so confirm the current amount with the court.

If you cannot pay in full, contact the clerk before the deadline. Some courts offer other arrangements, but availability varies by court.
