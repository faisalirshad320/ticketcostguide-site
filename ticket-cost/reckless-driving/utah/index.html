How much is a reckless driving ticket in Utah?

Nationally, reckless driving fines commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500. It is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail, which makes it a very different matter from a routine speeding ticket (Utah Driver License Division).

In Utah, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Utah dollar figure because the amount you owe depends on what the court assigns to your case.

The national range is a guide to scale, not a quote. Your citation, or the court that issued it, is the only place to confirm what you owe.

Because reckless driving is a criminal charge, the fine is only one part of the cost. Court appearances, time away from work and the effect on your record can matter as much as the amount printed on the citation.

How is a reckless driving fine set in Utah?

Utah does not treat this as a fixed-price ticket. The exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code, and the amount appears on your citation or in your court paperwork.

Zone enhancements and fees are built into the schedule. That means the total can be higher than a base fine if the location or circumstances trigger an enhancement.

The traffic code itself is available through Utah statutes via Justia. Reading the current text is the best way to see how the offense is defined and what range the court works within.

  • Start with the amount and court named on your citation.
  • Check whether any zone enhancement or fee is listed.
  • Ask the court clerk how the total is calculated if the paperwork is unclear.

How many points does reckless driving carry in Utah?

The point figures we have sourced cover speeding, not reckless driving. Speeding carries 35–75 points by mph over on Utah's scale (Utah Driver License Division).

We are not stating a point value for reckless driving, because we have not sourced one. Confirm it with the Driver License Division or the court before relying on any number you see elsewhere.

The threshold that matters for your license is the same regardless of the violation: 200 points in 3 years = suspension (ages 21+). Points from several violations add together inside that window.

A reckless driving conviction can also carry consequences beyond points, since it is a criminal misdemeanor. Points are only one part of the picture.

Does reckless driving raise insurance rates in Utah?

Very likely. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Utah-specific one.

A reckless driving conviction is more serious than many moving violations. Insurers may weigh it more heavily, but we have no sourced figure for how much more, so we do not guess.

  • The effect depends on your insurer, your record and your policy.
  • The increase usually lasts as long as the conviction stays in the insurer's review window.
  • Ask your insurer how it treats the specific charge. TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer.

When estimating total cost, add the premium effect over several renewals to the fine. For many drivers the insurance impact outweighs the fine.

Can a reckless driving ticket be dismissed or reduced in Utah?

Sometimes charges are amended or dismissed, but that decision belongs to the prosecutor and the court. We cannot say how likely it is in your case, and this guide is not legal advice.

Because reckless driving is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail, many people choose to speak with a licensed attorney before the first court date. We do not recommend any particular attorney.

Things worth doing on your own:

  1. Read the citation for the charge, court and any appearance date.
  2. Contact the court clerk to confirm whether you must appear in person.
  3. Keep copies of everything you file or receive.
  4. Look up the statute text through Utah statutes via Justia so you understand the charge as written.

If the charge is reduced, the fine, points and insurance effect may all change. Ask the court to confirm the final charge in writing.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore a reckless driving citation?

Ignoring a criminal citation tends to make things worse. Because reckless driving is a misdemeanor with possible jail, a missed court date can lead to further action by the court.

The specific penalties for missing a date or deadline are set by the court and state law, and we have not sourced exact figures. Check your citation and call the clerk promptly if you have missed a date.

Your driving record is also at stake. Under Utah's points system, 200 points in 3 years = suspension (ages 21+), so unresolved or added violations can bring you closer to that line.

If you cannot pay the full amount, ask the court clerk what options exist. Do not wait until after a deadline passes.
