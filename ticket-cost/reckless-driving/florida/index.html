How much does a reckless driving ticket cost in Florida?

There is no single sticker price. Nationally, fines for reckless driving commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500, and it is treated as a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail (Florida Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles).

In Florida, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Florida dollar figure because the amount depends on the court and the facts of the case.

The national range is a national range. Your Florida amount is whatever the court sets, plus the county costs and fees added on top.

Reckless driving is a different category from an ordinary speeding or equipment ticket. Many people pay a quick civil fine for those. A criminal misdemeanor can mean a court case, a record and potential jail exposure.

The total can also include items that are not the fine itself, covered below.

How is the reckless driving fine set in Florida?

The fine is set by the court within the state's traffic code. That means the number on your citation or court paperwork controls, not a general figure from a website.

Several things can shape what a court does:

  • The facts alleged, such as the driving behavior and any crash or injury
  • Whether the case is handled as a misdemeanor in county court
  • The county where the citation was issued
  • Whether the location was a school or work zone, where amounts are doubled by statute

Florida statutes are available through Florida statutes via Justia, and you can read the reckless driving provision there. Reading the statute does not tell you how a particular judge will rule.

If your citation lists a fine, that is the figure to work from. If it says a court appearance is required, the amount may not be fixed until the case is resolved.

What extra costs get added on top of the fine?

County courts add costs and fees on top of the statutory penalty. These vary by county, and we do not give an exact local figure.

Where the violation happens in a school or work zone, those amounts are doubled by statute. Check whether your citation notes a special zone.

Beyond the court, other costs can follow a conviction:

  • Higher insurance premiums (see below)
  • Time off work for court dates
  • Possible attorney fees if you hire one
  • Costs related to any suspension or license reinstatement

Ask the clerk of the court that handles your citation for the full amount owed, not just the fine line.

The all-in cost of a conviction is often well above the headline fine once these items are added.

How many points does reckless driving add in Florida?

Florida's points system assigns 3 points (15 or less over) or 4 points (16+ over). Those tiers are written in terms of speed over the limit, so they map most directly to speeding violations (Florida Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles).

Our sources do not break out a separate point value for reckless driving, so check your citation and your driving record for how points were assigned. FLHSMV is the agency that keeps the record.

The threshold that matters is cumulative: 12 points in 12 months = 30-day suspension. A single violation with points, added to others from the same year, can push a driver toward it.

Points are only one consequence. A criminal misdemeanor conviction carries its own effects beyond the point count.

To see where you stand, request your own driving record from the state and count points within the 12-month window.

Does reckless driving raise my insurance?

It usually does. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Florida quote.

Your own increase depends on your insurer, your history and the type of conviction. A criminal misdemeanor for reckless driving may be viewed more seriously than a routine ticket, but we have no sourced figure for that difference.

Some points to keep in mind:

  • The increase generally applies at renewal, not the day you are cited
  • A conviction is what typically triggers it, so the case outcome matters
  • Comparing quotes from several insurers can show how each treats the violation

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer. For your actual rate effect, ask your own insurer or agent.

Can a reckless driving ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but we cannot promise an outcome. Because reckless driving is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail, it is handled in court rather than only by mail.

Options people commonly look at include:

  1. Reading the citation closely for errors or missing details
  2. Asking the court clerk what the process and deadlines are
  3. Consulting a licensed attorney about whether a reduction or dismissal is realistic in your case
  4. Appearing as directed and responding to the charge on time

A reduction to a lesser violation can change both the fine and the insurance effect. Whether it is available depends on the facts, the prosecutor and the court.

This page is general information, not legal advice. A licensed attorney in Florida can advise on your specific case.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore it?

Ignoring a criminal citation can make things worse. Courts can add costs, and unresolved cases can lead to license problems or further action.

Because this is a misdemeanor with possible jail, failing to appear when required is a serious matter. Check your citation for the date and instructions, and contact the clerk if anything is unclear.

Also watch the points. If a conviction pushes you to 12 points in 12 months, the result is a 30-day suspension (Florida Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles).

If you cannot pay the full amount at once, ask the court whether it offers a payment arrangement. Do not assume one exists.
