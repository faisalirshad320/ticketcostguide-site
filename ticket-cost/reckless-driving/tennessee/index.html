How much does a reckless driving ticket cost in Tennessee?

Nationally, fines for reckless driving commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500, and the offense is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail (Tennessee Department of Safety and Homeland Security). That is a national range. It is not a Tennessee price list.

In Tennessee, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Tennessee dollar figure because the total depends on the court that hears your case.

Look at your citation and any court notice first. The clerk of the court listed there can tell you the amount owed and the accepted ways to pay.

Remember that the fine is only part of the bill. County court costs and state litigation taxes make up most of the total, so a ticket can cost noticeably more than the fine line alone suggests.

How is the reckless driving fine set in Tennessee?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The Tennessee statutes are publicly available through Tennessee statutes via Justia, and you can read the traffic provisions there.

Unlike some minor violations, a criminal misdemeanor is not usually handled with a fixed fee that you pay by mail. The judge decides the outcome, so the final amount can differ from case to case.

Several things can shape the total:

  • The court and county where the citation was issued.
  • County court costs added to the fine.
  • State litigation taxes added to the fine.
  • The outcome of your case, such as a conviction or a reduced charge.

Because the court sets the amount on the citation, any website that quotes one exact Tennessee figure for every driver is guessing. Ask the court clerk for the number that applies to you.

Is reckless driving a crime in Tennessee?

Yes. Reckless driving is treated as a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail (Tennessee Department of Safety and Homeland Security). That puts it in a different category from a routine speeding ticket.

A criminal charge can mean a court appearance rather than a simple payment. It can also mean a criminal record if you are convicted, which may matter for employment, licensing and background checks.

Possible jail does not mean jail is certain. What a judge actually orders depends on the facts of the case and the court. For questions about your own situation, a licensed Tennessee attorney is the right person to ask. TicketCostGuide does not give legal advice or recommend specific attorneys.

How many points is reckless driving in Tennessee?

Tennessee's points system runs from 1–8 points, scored by mph over the limit. Examples are 3 points at 6–15 over and 4 points at 16–25 over. Reaching 12 points in 12 months triggers a suspension notice.

That scale is built around speed over the limit. Reckless driving is not a pure speed-over-the-limit offense, so we are not stating a point value for it. Your citation and the Tennessee Department of Safety and Homeland Security are the places to confirm how a conviction would be scored (Tennessee Department of Safety and Homeland Security).

Points are tracked on a rolling basis. If you already have points on your record, a new conviction could bring you closer to the 12-points-in-12-months suspension notice.

Points are separate from the fine. You can owe a fine and court costs and also have points added to your driving record.

Does a reckless driving ticket raise insurance in Tennessee?

It often does. Nationally, moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Tennessee quote.

Reckless driving is a more serious conviction than many moving violations, so insurers may view it more seriously. We do not have a sourced Tennessee-specific percentage, so we will not state one.

What affects your premium varies by insurer. Common factors include:

  • Whether the charge ends in a conviction or is reduced or dismissed.
  • Your prior driving record.
  • The company's own rating rules.

The most reliable answer comes from your own insurer or agent. We do not recommend specific insurers.

Can a reckless driving charge be dismissed or reduced?

It can be possible, but the outcome is up to the court and the prosecutor. Because this is a criminal misdemeanor, the case is decided by a judge rather than by a fee schedule.

Common steps people consider:

  1. Read the citation for the court date, the court name and the charge.
  2. Contact the court clerk to confirm how your case will be handled.
  3. Talk with a licensed Tennessee attorney about the facts of your case and your options.
  4. Ask whether any resolution that changes the charge is available in that court.

A reduced charge can change the fine, the points and the effect on insurance. We cannot say what any given court will allow, and these are not legal recommendations.

Do not assume the amount on a notice is final. A different outcome in court can change what you owe.

What happens if I don't pay or miss court?

Ignoring a criminal citation can make things worse. Missing a required court date or leaving a balance unpaid may lead to additional penalties or action against your license. The specifics depend on the court and the state's rules.

With a criminal misdemeanor, a court appearance is often required. Read your citation closely and contact the clerk before the date listed if you have a conflict or a question.

The Tennessee Department of Safety and Homeland Security explains how license actions work (Tennessee Department of Safety and Homeland Security). If you are unsure about a deadline, ask the court rather than waiting.

If you have accumulated 12 points in 12 months, a suspension notice follows under the state's points system.
