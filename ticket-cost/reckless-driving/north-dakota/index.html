How much is a reckless driving ticket in North Dakota?

Nationally, reckless driving fines commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500, and the offense is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail (North Dakota Department of Transportation). In North Dakota, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation.

We do not publish an exact North Dakota dollar figure here, because the amount depends on the court and the facts of your case. The national range is a general guide only, not a North Dakota price.

Read the citation itself. The amount, the court and the appearance instructions printed there are the ones that apply to you.

Reckless driving is treated more seriously than an ordinary moving violation. It is a criminal charge rather than a simple traffic infraction, so the fine is only one part of the possible outcome.

How is the reckless driving fine set in North Dakota?

The fine is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The amount for your case is the one on your citation or the one the judge orders.

Because reckless driving is a criminal misdemeanor, a judge generally has more discretion than with a basic speeding ticket. Courts may look at the circumstances of the stop and the driving record of the person charged.

You can read the state's traffic code through North Dakota statutes via Justia. Statute text can change, so treat any online copy as a reference and confirm current wording with the court.

  • The national range is not a North Dakota schedule.
  • The court on your citation decides the amount.
  • Jail is a possible penalty for a criminal misdemeanor.

Are there extra court costs and surcharges?

For this violation in North Dakota, the statutory fee is usually the whole amount. There is no large stack of added costs on top.

That is helpful when you budget, but it does not make the ticket cheap. The base amount set by the court is still the main cost, and it can be substantial for a criminal misdemeanor.

If anything on your paperwork looks like an extra charge, ask the court clerk what it is for before paying. We do not list any added fee amounts, because none are confirmed in our sources.

How many points is reckless driving in North Dakota?

North Dakota assigns points by mph over on the state schedule, and 12+ points = suspension (North Dakota Department of Transportation).

That schedule is built around speed. We do not state a specific point value for reckless driving, because our sources do not give one. Ask the North Dakota Department of Transportation or the court what points, if any, apply to your conviction.

The suspension threshold is 12+ points. Any points already on your record may count toward that total.

Separate from points, a criminal conviction can bring its own consequences. Confirm with the court what could apply to your license.

Does reckless driving raise insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a North Dakota quote.

Reckless driving is a more serious conviction than many moving violations, so your own change could differ. Insurers set rates using their own rules, and we cannot predict what yours will do.

  • Ask your insurer how a conviction would affect your policy.
  • Compare the possible premium change with the cost of the fine.
  • Remember that the increase can last beyond the day you pay.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer.

Can a reckless driving ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes a charge is reduced or dismissed, but outcomes depend on the court and the facts. We cannot tell you whether that is likely in your case.

Because reckless driving is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail, many people choose to speak with a licensed attorney before deciding how to respond. This is general information, not legal advice or a recommendation of any lawyer.

  • Note your court date and the instructions on the citation.
  • Ask the clerk what options the court offers.
  • Do not pay until you understand what paying means for a criminal charge.

Paying a criminal citation can be treated as a conviction, which is why the decision deserves care.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the ticket?

Ignoring a criminal citation can make things worse. Courts can add consequences when someone misses a required appearance or fails to follow the citation's instructions.

We do not list specific penalties or deadlines, because they are not part of our confirmed figures. Your citation and the court clerk are the right places to confirm them.

If you cannot make the date on your citation, contact the court before it passes.

Unresolved cases can also affect your license. The state's suspension threshold of 12+ points shows that your driving record matters to the Department of Transportation.
