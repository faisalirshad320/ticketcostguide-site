How much does a reckless driving ticket cost in Alabama?

The honest answer is that the exact Alabama amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Alabama dollar figure because it depends on the court and the facts of the case.

For context, nationally fines commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500, and reckless driving is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail (Alabama Law Enforcement Agency (ALEA)). That national range is a benchmark, not a quote for an Alabama court.

The fine is only one part of the bill. Municipal and district court costs vary by jurisdiction and often exceed the base fine.

Read your citation and any court notice carefully. The amount, the court and the appearance requirements printed there control what you actually owe.

Because reckless driving is a criminal matter, the possible consequences go beyond money. A conviction can also bring points, a record and higher insurance costs, covered below.

How is the reckless driving fine set in Alabama?

The exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The officer who writes the citation does not decide the final amount.

The court looks at the charge as written, the outcome of the case and the local costs it adds. Two people charged with the same offense in different jurisdictions can pay different totals.

  • Base fine: set by the court within the state's traffic code.
  • Court costs: vary by jurisdiction and often exceed the base fine.
  • Other consequences: because it is a criminal misdemeanor, jail is possible.

The Alabama traffic code is published online through Alabama statutes via Justia. It is the place to read the offense definition in the state's own words.

If the citation lists a court date or a payable amount, that document is the best guide to your total. The clerk of the named court can confirm what is owed.

How many points does reckless driving add to my Alabama license?

Alabama's points system, as we have sourced it, works in two tiers: 2 points for 25 or less over, and 5 points for more than 25 over. Those tiers are tied to how far over a limit a driver was.

We do not have a sourced figure for how many points a reckless driving conviction carries. Check the offense in the traffic code (Alabama statutes via Justia) or with ALEA, which handles driver records.

The suspension rule is clear: 12–14 points in 2 years = 60-day suspension. Points from several violations within that window add together.

A reckless driving charge that stays on your record can bring you closer to the suspension threshold if you already have points. Ask ALEA for your current record to see where you stand.

Points are separate from the fine and court costs. You can owe money and also carry points from the same citation.

Does reckless driving raise my insurance in Alabama?

It often does. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Alabama-specific one.

Reckless driving is a more serious charge than a basic moving violation, so the effect on your own rate could be different. We cannot predict what any insurer will do, and we do not recommend insurers.

  • The change usually shows up at your next renewal, after the insurer sees the conviction on your record.
  • Each insurer applies its own rules, so the same conviction can cost different amounts with different companies.
  • A conviction typically has more effect on rates than a charge that was dismissed.

To get a real number, ask your insurer how a conviction of this kind would be treated at renewal. Compare that against the other costs of the case.

Can a reckless driving charge be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes charges are dismissed or amended, but that depends on the court, the prosecutor and the facts. We cannot say how likely it is in your case.

It matters because a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail carries more risk than an ordinary traffic fine. The final charge can change the fine, the court costs, the points and the insurance effect.

  • Find out exactly what you are charged with and which court has the case.
  • Note your court date and any appearance requirement on the citation.
  • Ask the court clerk how the court handles payment, pleas and scheduling.
  • Consider speaking with a licensed Alabama attorney about your options.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice or recommend attorneys. A licensed attorney can explain what applies to your situation.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the citation?

Ignoring a reckless driving citation is risky. Because it is a criminal misdemeanor, a missed court date can create further problems on top of the original charge.

Depending on the court, consequences of ignoring a citation can include added penalties, a warrant, or action against your driving privileges. The specifics vary by jurisdiction, so we cannot give one rule for the state.

Court costs already vary by jurisdiction and often exceed the base fine, so delays can make an already large total harder to manage.

If you cannot pay or cannot attend on the date shown, contact the clerk of the court named on the citation before the deadline. Do not wait until after it passes.

Keep copies of the citation, any court notices and proof of anything you pay.
