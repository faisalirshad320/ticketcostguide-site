How much is a reckless driving ticket in Idaho?

Nationally, reckless driving fines commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500. It is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail. In Idaho the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Idaho Transportation Department — DMV).

We do not publish a single Idaho dollar amount, because the court decides within the state's traffic code. The national range gives you a sense of scale, but it is not an Idaho fine schedule.

Your citation and any court notice are the only documents that show what you actually owe. Read them before you pay or respond.

The fine is only one part of the cost. Points, possible court-ordered conditions and higher insurance premiums can add to the total over time.

How is the fine set for reckless driving in Idaho?

Unlike many minor infractions, reckless driving does not come with one fixed penalty printed on a schedule. The court sets the amount within the state's traffic code, and the figure appears on your citation or in the court's order.

That means two drivers charged with the same offense can end up with different totals. The judge or court may look at the facts of the stop, your driving record and what the law allows.

For most infractions, the fixed penalty already includes court costs. Because reckless driving is a criminal misdemeanor rather than a basic infraction, ask the court clerk whether any costs sit on top of the fine.

You can read Idaho's traffic statutes through Idaho statutes via Justia. Statutes can be amended, so check the current text or ask the court which provision applies to your charge.

Is reckless driving a criminal charge in Idaho?

Our sourcing describes reckless driving as a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail. That puts it in a different category from a routine speeding or stop-sign ticket.

A misdemeanor can mean a court appearance instead of a simple mail-in payment. It can also mean a criminal record entry, which matters for jobs, licensing and background checks.

We do not list a jail term for Idaho, because we do not have a locked figure for it. The statute and the court on your citation control what is possible in your case.

  • Check whether your citation requires a court appearance.
  • Note the date, time and location of any hearing.
  • Keep copies of everything you receive.

How many points does reckless driving add in Idaho?

Idaho's points system assigns 3–4 points per moving violation. Reaching 12–17 points in 12 months results in a 30-day suspension (Idaho Transportation Department — DMV).

We do not have a locked figure showing exactly where reckless driving falls within the 3–4 point range. Ask the Idaho Transportation Department or check your driving record after the case closes.

Points matter most when you already have other violations. A single reckless driving conviction plus a few earlier tickets inside a 12-month window can move you toward the suspension threshold.

Points are separate from the fine. Paying the fine does not remove points, and a court outcome can change what is reported.

Does reckless driving raise insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Idaho rate, and it is not specific to reckless driving.

Insurers set their own rates and rules. A misdemeanor-level driving offense may be treated more seriously than a minor ticket, but we do not have a locked figure to say by how much.

If you want to know the effect on your policy, ask your insurer how a conviction would be rated. We do not recommend any specific insurer.

  • Ask when the conviction would appear on your record.
  • Ask how long it could affect your premium.
  • Compare how the outcome changes if the charge is reduced.

Can a reckless driving charge be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes a charge is dismissed or amended, but that depends on the facts, the court and the prosecutor. We cannot say what is likely in your case, and this page is not legal advice.

Because reckless driving is a criminal misdemeanor, many people talk to a licensed attorney before responding. We do not recommend any specific attorney. Your court may also list local resources.

Generally, your options after a citation include:

  • Pay or resolve the citation, if the court allows it. This typically means a conviction.
  • Contest the charge at a hearing.
  • Ask the court or prosecutor about alternatives, such as a reduced charge.

A reduced charge can change the fine, the points and the insurance impact. Ask the court exactly what the new charge would be and how it is reported before you agree to anything.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore a reckless driving citation?

Ignoring a criminal citation can make things worse. The court may treat a missed appearance or unpaid amount as a separate problem, and that can add to your costs.

We do not have locked figures for late fees or warrant procedures in Idaho, so we do not state any. The court named on your citation can tell you the current consequences and deadlines.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court clerk before the due date and ask what arrangements exist.

Unresolved cases can also affect your driving privileges. Check your status with the Idaho Transportation Department — DMV.
