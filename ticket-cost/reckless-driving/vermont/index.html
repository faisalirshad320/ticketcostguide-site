How much is a reckless driving ticket in Vermont?

Nationally, reckless driving fines commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500, and the offense is usually a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail (Vermont Department of Motor Vehicles). That is a national range, not a Vermont price tag.

In Vermont, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Vermont dollar figure because the amount depends on the court and the facts of the case.

Your citation and any court notice are the only documents that show what you owe. Use the national range as context, not as a quote.

Reckless driving is treated far more seriously than an ordinary speeding or equipment ticket. The cost is not only the fine. It can also include surcharges, points, higher insurance and, in some cases, jail exposure.

How is the reckless driving fine set in Vermont?

The fine is set by the court within the limits of Vermont's traffic code. The statutes are published through Vermont statutes via Justia, where you can read the offense definition and its penalty language.

Several things commonly shape the final number:

  • The conduct described on the citation, such as speed, road conditions and whether anyone was endangered.
  • Whether the charge is a civil traffic matter or a criminal one.
  • Your driving history.
  • Whether the court lists a waiver amount or requires an appearance.

Where a waiver amount is listed, statutory surcharges are included in it. Surcharges are added on top of the base fine, so the total can be higher than the fine alone.

If the charge is criminal, you will usually have to appear before a judge rather than simply mail in a payment.

Is reckless driving a criminal charge in Vermont?

Nationally, reckless driving is commonly a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail. That is why it is treated differently from a standard moving violation (Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws).

We cannot tell you how your specific Vermont charge will be classified. The citation, the statute cited on it and the court paperwork will show that.

A criminal charge can mean consequences beyond the fine. These can include a criminal record, possible jail, and effects on employment or professional licensing. Because of this, many people speak with a licensed attorney before deciding how to respond. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not recommend any attorney.

How many points does reckless driving add in Vermont?

Vermont's points system assigns 2–8 points depending on how many miles per hour over the limit a driver was going. Reaching 10 points in 2 years leads to a license suspension (Vermont Department of Motor Vehicles).

We do not state a specific point value for reckless driving, because the locked figures we rely on describe the range, not a value for this offense. Check your citation and your Vermont driving record for the points actually assigned.

Here is why that matters:

  • Points from one conviction can combine with points already on your record.
  • The 10-point threshold counts points within a 2-year window.
  • A suspension can follow even if each individual ticket seemed minor.

Ask the Vermont DMV how your record currently stands before you decide how to resolve the case.

Does reckless driving raise insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Vermont-specific one.

Reckless driving is usually viewed as more serious than a basic moving violation, so some insurers may react more strongly. We have no sourced figure for that, so we do not give one.

What affects your own premium:

  • Your insurer's own rating rules.
  • Your prior record and years of driving experience.
  • Whether the conviction is on your record at renewal.
  • Whether the insurer chooses to surcharge or non-renew.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend insurers. If you want to know how a conviction might affect you, ask your own insurer or agent.

Can a reckless driving ticket be dismissed or reduced in Vermont?

It can happen, but we cannot predict the outcome of any case. Outcomes depend on the evidence, the court and the prosecutor.

People in this position commonly look at a few options:

  • Pay or resolve as charged. This usually results in a conviction and its consequences.
  • Contest the charge. You ask the court to decide whether the state can prove it.
  • Negotiate. In some cases the charge is amended to a lesser offense, which may change the fine, points and insurance effect.

Because reckless driving can be a criminal matter, the stakes are higher than for most tickets. A licensed Vermont attorney can explain the options that apply to your case. This page is general information, not legal advice.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore a reckless driving citation?

Ignoring a citation tends to make things worse. The citation usually lists a court and instructions for responding, and missing that step can lead to additional penalties.

Possible consequences nationally include added fees, a license suspension and, for criminal charges, a warrant for failing to appear. The specifics in Vermont depend on the court and the charge.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court listed on your citation before the deadline. Courts can often explain the options available to you.

Keep copies of everything you receive and note every deadline on the paperwork.
