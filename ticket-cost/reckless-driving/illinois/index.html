How much does a reckless driving ticket cost in Illinois?

Nationally, reckless driving fines commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500, and the offense is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail. In Illinois the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Illinois Secretary of State).

That means we cannot give you one statewide Illinois dollar figure. The number you owe depends on the court handling your case and what it decides.

The $1,000–$2,500 range is a national figure. Treat it as a rough frame of reference, not a prediction of your Illinois fine.

The fine is only one part of the cost. A misdemeanor charge can bring court costs, possible jail exposure, points on your record and higher insurance premiums. The sections below cover each of those.

How is the reckless driving fine set in Illinois?

In Illinois the amount is decided by the court within the state's traffic code and written on the citation. The code gives the court a range to work within, so two drivers can end up with different totals for similar conduct.

Several things can influence where a court lands:

  • The facts described by the officer on the citation.
  • Your driving record and any prior convictions.
  • Whether the case ends in a conviction, a reduced charge or a dismissal.
  • The practices of the particular court or county.

Illinois does set statutory minimums for some violations. For example, work-zone speeding carries a statutory minimum fine of $375 for a first offense (Illinois statutes via Justia). That is a different violation from reckless driving, and we show it only to illustrate that minimums exist in the state's code. It is not a reckless driving fine.

To see the exact language that applies, read the Illinois Vehicle Code through the statute index on Justia and check the citation itself.

Is reckless driving a crime in Illinois?

Reckless driving is treated as a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail. That sets it apart from an ordinary speeding or equipment ticket, which is usually a petty traffic matter.

A criminal charge can mean a court appearance rather than a simple mail-in payment. It can also mean a criminal record if you are convicted.

Jail is a possibility, not a certainty. Whether a court imposes it depends on the case, and we cannot say how likely it is in any given situation.

Because the stakes go beyond a fine, read your citation closely for a court date and any instructions on how to respond. TicketCostGuide does not give legal advice.

How many points does reckless driving carry in Illinois?

Illinois convictions carry point values from 5 to 50 on the Secretary of State scale (Illinois Secretary of State). We do not state a single point value for reckless driving here, because the locked range covers many violation types.

The Secretary of State's office keeps your driving record and applies the suspension rules. Repeat convictions within 12 months trigger suspension.

Practically, this means a reckless driving conviction can matter beyond the fine. If you already have a recent conviction, a new one may put your license at risk.

  • Check your own record with the Secretary of State to see what is on it.
  • Look at the dates of any earlier convictions.
  • Ask the court how a conviction would be reported.

Does reckless driving raise your insurance in Illinois?

Most likely, yes. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Illinois quote.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your record and how the insurer views the offense. A misdemeanor reckless driving conviction is generally treated more seriously than a basic speeding ticket, but we have no sourced figure to put on that difference.

The increase can last well beyond the date you pay the fine, since insurers usually review your record when your policy renews. TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer, so compare quotes yourself and ask your own company how it rates a conviction.

Can a reckless driving ticket be dismissed or reduced?

It can happen, but outcomes depend on the facts and the court. Because this is a criminal misdemeanor, the case goes through the court rather than being handled like a routine ticket.

Common paths people ask about include:

  • Contesting the charge at a hearing.
  • Asking whether the prosecutor will agree to a lesser charge.
  • Asking whether the court offers any alternative disposition that avoids a conviction.

We cannot tell you which of these is available in your court, or whether it would work. Availability varies, and the rules are set by the court and prosecutor.

Since a conviction can mean points, possible suspension and higher premiums, many people talk to a licensed attorney before deciding. We do not recommend a specific attorney.

What happens if I ignore the citation or don't pay?

Ignoring a reckless driving citation is risky. Because it is a criminal matter, missing a court date can lead to further consequences that go beyond an unpaid fine. Exact penalties depend on the court.

On the licensing side, unresolved matters can affect your record with the Secretary of State. Repeat convictions within 12 months can trigger suspension.

  1. Read the citation for the court date and response instructions.
  2. Contact the court named on the citation to confirm what you owe.
  3. Decide whether to contest, negotiate or resolve the case, and act before any deadline.

We do not list deadlines or late fees here because we have no sourced figures for them. The court clerk can tell you the current amounts.
