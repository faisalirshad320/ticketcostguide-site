How much does a reckless driving ticket cost in Missouri?

There is no single Missouri price for reckless driving. Nationally, fines commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500, and it is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail. In Missouri, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Missouri Department of Revenue — Driver License Bureau).

That national range is a guide to how seriously states treat this offense. It is not a Missouri fine schedule, and your own amount may differ from it.

The number that matters is the one printed on your citation or given by the court. Missouri amounts can vary by court, so do not rely on what someone in another county paid.

Court costs are included in the scheduled payable amounts. If your citation lists a payable amount, it should already account for those costs, but check the citation or ask the court clerk to confirm what the total covers.

How is a reckless driving fine set in Missouri?

The fine is set by the court handling your case, within the limits of Missouri's traffic code. The statutes are published online (Missouri statutes via Justia), and they define the offense and the range of penalties a court may impose.

Several things can shape where a court lands:

  • Which court hears it. A municipal court and a state-level court can handle the same kind of case differently.
  • Whether it is a municipal or state charge. This also changes the points that come with a conviction.
  • The facts of the stop. Prosecutors and judges look at what happened, not only the label on the ticket.
  • Your record. A prior history can lead to a different outcome than a first offense.

Because it is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail, the penalty is not limited to a fine. The citation and any court paperwork will show what you are actually facing.

How many points does reckless driving add in Missouri?

A reckless driving conviction adds 2 points if it is a municipal conviction, or 3 points if it is a state charge (Missouri Department of Revenue — Driver License Bureau).

Eight points in 18 months leads to suspension. Points from other moving violations in that window count toward the same total, so a reckless driving conviction can bring you closer to the threshold than a single ticket might suggest.

Points are added when there is a conviction. How the case is resolved can affect whether points are added at all, which is one reason the outcome matters as much as the fine.

To see your current point total, check your driving record through the Department of Revenue's Driver License Bureau.

Does reckless driving raise my insurance?

Often, yes. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Missouri rate, and not a prediction for your policy.

Reckless driving is more serious than a routine moving violation, so your insurer may react more strongly than the average suggests. We cannot say how any particular insurer will treat it, and we do not recommend insurers.

  • Insurers set their own rules for how they treat a conviction.
  • The effect may show up at your next renewal rather than right away.
  • The cost over time can exceed the fine, because the higher premium continues for as long as the insurer counts the conviction.

If you are weighing a plea, factor the likely premium effect into your decision alongside the fine and points.

Can a reckless driving ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes outcomes other than a straight conviction are possible, but this depends on the court, the prosecutor and the facts. We cannot predict them, and nothing here is legal advice.

Because this is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail, many people speak with a licensed attorney before deciding how to respond. TicketCostGuide does not recommend any specific attorney.

Questions worth raising with the court clerk or a licensed professional:

  1. Is this a municipal or a state charge? It changes whether a conviction carries 2 points or 3 points.
  2. Is there a court appearance required, or can the case be resolved another way?
  3. What would a different charge or outcome mean for points on my record?
  4. What is the total amount, and does it include court costs?

Do not assume that paying the ticket is the same as a minor payment. With a criminal misdemeanor, paying can mean accepting a conviction.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore a reckless driving ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts have ways to follow up when a person does not pay or appear, and the specific steps depend on the court. Check your citation for the deadline and instructions.

A missed court date on a misdemeanor can lead to added consequences beyond the original charge. Your driving privilege can also be affected, and the Missouri Department of Revenue handles driver license records and suspensions (Missouri Department of Revenue — Driver License Bureau).

If you cannot pay by the deadline or cannot make the court date, contact the court clerk before the deadline passes. Courts are generally easier to work with before a missed deadline than after.

How does Missouri compare with other states?

Reckless driving is treated as a criminal offense in many states, which is why the national range of up to $1,000–$2,500 and the possibility of jail show up so often. State laws on driving offenses are compiled by the Governors Highway Safety Association (Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws).

Missouri's own amount is set by the court on the citation. Use the national range only as context, not as a quote for what you will owe in Missouri.

Points also differ between states. In Missouri, the figure is 2 points for a municipal conviction or 3 points for a state charge, with suspension at 8 points in 18 months.

Frequently asked questions

How much is a reckless driving ticket in Missouri?

Is reckless driving a misdemeanor in Missouri?

Reckless driving is treated as a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail, which is more serious than a basic traffic infraction. The specific penalty is set by the court. Read your citation closely and consider speaking with a licensed attorney about your situation before you respond.

How many points is reckless driving in Missouri?

Will reckless driving raise my car insurance?

It may. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average, a national figure. Your actual change depends on your insurer and your record, and a reckless driving conviction may be treated more seriously than a routine violation. The change usually appears at renewal.

Do court costs add to the reckless driving fine in Missouri?

Can I lose my license for reckless driving in Missouri?

Points count toward suspension. A conviction adds 2 or 3 points depending on whether it is a municipal or state charge, and 8 points in 18 months leads to suspension. Whether your license is at risk depends on your total points, so check your driving record with the Department of Revenue.
