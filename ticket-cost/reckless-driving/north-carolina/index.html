How much is a reckless driving ticket in North Carolina?

Nationally, reckless driving fines are commonly up to $1,000–$2,500, and the offense is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail. In North Carolina, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (North Carolina Division of Motor Vehicles).

We do not publish a single North Carolina dollar figure, because the amount depends on the court and the facts of the case. The national range is a benchmark only.

The number that matters is the one on your citation or court paperwork. If it is unclear, ask the clerk of court.

The fine is only one part of the bill. Court costs, possible insurance increases and the effect of a criminal record can add up to more than the fine.

How is the reckless driving fine set in North Carolina?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. It is not a fixed number that applies the same way to every driver.

Several things can shape the outcome in practice:

  • The charge written on the citation
  • The facts of the stop, such as speed, road conditions and any crash
  • Your driving history
  • How the case is resolved in court

The statutes are published online through North Carolina statutes via Justia. Statutes are written in legal language and can be amended, so check the current text or ask the court clerk about your specific charge.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot tell you what a judge will order in your case.

Are there court costs on top of the fine?

Yes. Uniform statewide court costs dwarf the fine on most tickets. That is why a total bill can look much larger than the fine alone.

We do not list a North Carolina dollar amount for these costs, because none is in our locked data. Your citation, the clerk's office or the court's payment notice will show the total owed.

  • Ask whether the amount shown includes court costs or only the fine.
  • Keep a receipt for any payment.
  • Check the deadline before paying or responding.

For a criminal misdemeanor, there may also be a required court appearance. Read the citation closely for the date and the instructions.

How many points does reckless driving add in North Carolina?

Our locked data shows two separate point systems. License points are typically 2–3 for typical speeding. Separate insurance (SDIP) points can raise premiums 30%+ per point tier.

The 2–3 figure applies to typical speeding. We do not state a point value for reckless driving. Confirm it with the North Carolina Division of Motor Vehicles or your court paperwork.

License points and insurance points are not the same thing. The DMV tracks license points on your driving record, while insurance points are used in setting your premium.

Do not assume the speeding point range applies to reckless driving. A criminal misdemeanor charge can carry consequences beyond points.

Does reckless driving raise insurance in North Carolina?

It can. Nationally, moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a North Carolina quote.

North Carolina also uses separate insurance (SDIP) points. These can raise premiums 30%+ per point tier.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your record and the outcome of the case. We do not recommend any insurer. Compare your policy terms and ask your insurer how a conviction would be treated.

  • A conviction usually affects rates more than a charge that is dismissed.
  • Renewal timing can affect when you see the change.
  • Multiple violations can compound the effect.

Can a reckless driving ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes cases are dismissed or resolved on a different charge, but the result depends on the facts, the court and the prosecutor. We cannot predict an outcome or give legal advice.

Because reckless driving is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail, many drivers talk to a licensed North Carolina attorney before deciding how to respond. We do not recommend any specific attorney.

Questions worth asking the court or counsel:

  • What exactly is the charge on the citation?
  • Is a court appearance required?
  • What are the total costs, including court costs?
  • How would each possible outcome affect points and insurance?

What happens if I don't pay or don't respond?

Ignoring a criminal citation usually makes things worse. Missing a court date or deadline can lead to further action under the state's traffic code and from the DMV.

We do not list specific penalties or timelines, because those are not in our locked data. Check the citation for deadlines and contact the clerk of court if you are unsure what to do.

If you cannot pay the full amount, ask the clerk what options the court offers. Do this before the deadline, not after.
