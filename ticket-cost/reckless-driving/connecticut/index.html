How much is a reckless driving ticket in Connecticut?

Nationally, reckless driving fines are commonly up to $1,000–$2,500. That is a national range. It is not a Connecticut price list.

In Connecticut, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Connecticut Department of Motor Vehicles). We do not state a Connecticut dollar figure here because the sources we rely on do not publish a single fixed one.

Your citation, or the court that issued it, is the only reliable place to find your exact amount.

Reckless driving is also not a routine ticket. Nationally it is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail, so the fine is only one part of the cost.

How is the reckless driving fine set?

The exact fine for this violation in Connecticut is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The statutes are available through Connecticut statutes via Justia.

The final payable amount can depend on the court's decision and on the facts of your case. Two drivers cited for the same offense can end up with different outcomes.

State fees and surcharges are built into the payable amount on the ticket. If a figure appears on your citation, it is generally meant to be the full amount, not a base fine waiting for extras.

  • The national range gives you a rough sense of scale.
  • The citation or court notice gives you the actual number.
  • A court clerk can tell you what the payable amount includes.

Is reckless driving a criminal charge, and can it mean jail?

Nationally, reckless driving is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail. That sets it apart from most speeding or equipment tickets, which are civil or infraction-level matters.

Whether jail is realistic in a particular case is up to the court. Check the wording of your citation and any court notice for how your charge is classified.

A criminal charge can matter beyond the fine. It may create a court record, so reading the paperwork carefully is worth the time.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot give legal advice. For questions about your specific charge, consider speaking with a licensed attorney of your own choosing.

How many points does reckless driving add in Connecticut?

The point fact we can state from our sources is this: speeding adds points under the DMV point system, and 10 points = a 30-day suspension. That fact is written for speeding, so we do not assume it applies to reckless driving.

We do not give a specific point value for reckless driving. The Connecticut DMV is the place to confirm how a reckless driving conviction is recorded on your driving record (Connecticut Department of Motor Vehicles).

Points and a suspension are separate from the fine. You can pay the court amount and still face consequences on your license.

Does a reckless driving ticket raise my insurance?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Connecticut-specific one.

Reckless driving is usually treated as a more serious violation than an ordinary moving ticket, so your own increase could differ from the average. We cannot predict what your insurer will charge, and we do not recommend any insurer.

  • Ask your insurer how a conviction would be rated at renewal.
  • Compare the possible premium change with the fine when you weigh your options.
  • Keep in mind that the premium effect can last beyond the day you pay.

Can a reckless driving ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Outcomes are decided by the court. A charge can sometimes be contested, and some cases end with a different or lesser charge. We cannot say how likely that is in your case.

The main options are usually:

  1. Pay the amount shown, if the citation allows it, which generally resolves the matter.
  2. Contest the charge and appear in court on the date given.
  3. Ask the court clerk what procedures are available for your charge.

Because this is a criminal misdemeanor nationally, paying without reading the consequences first may not be a good idea. Confirm what a payment means for your record before you send it.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the ticket?

Ignoring a citation rarely makes it go away. Courts generally follow up on missed dates and unpaid amounts, and the exact steps in Connecticut are set by the court and the DMV.

The consequences can reach your driving privileges. Under the DMV point system, enough points can lead to a suspension (10 points = 30-day suspension, stated for speeding).

If you cannot make the date or the payment, contact the court listed on your citation before the deadline passes. Courts are usually easier to work with before a deadline than after.
