How much is a reckless driving ticket in Arizona?

Nationally, reckless driving fines commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500, and the offense is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail (Arizona Department of Transportation — Motor Vehicle Division). That range is a national picture, not an Arizona price list.

In Arizona, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Arizona dollar figure because the amount depends on the court and the facts of the case.

Your citation, summons or court notice is the only reliable source for what you owe. If the amount is not printed there, ask the court clerk.

The fine is also not the whole bill. Court surcharges and fees are added by the justice or municipal court, and those amounts vary by court.

Is reckless driving a criminal charge in Arizona?

Reckless driving is treated as a criminal misdemeanor, not a routine civil traffic infraction. That is why possible jail time appears alongside the fine range.

The difference matters in practice. A criminal charge can mean a mandatory court appearance, a different process from paying a standard citation online, and a criminal record if you are convicted.

Many drivers assume a reckless driving citation works like a speeding ticket. It does not. Read the paperwork closely for a court date and any instructions about appearing in person.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot tell you how your case will be charged or resolved. For the statutory definition, Arizona's traffic code is available through Arizona statutes via Justia.

How is the reckless driving fine set in Arizona?

The exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. There is no single number that applies to every driver.

Several things can sit behind the final amount:

  • The court handling your case (justice court or municipal court).
  • The charge as written on the citation.
  • Surcharges and fees the court adds on top of the fine.

Because surcharges vary by court, two drivers with the same charge can owe different totals. Compare the fine line and the fee lines on your paperwork, and ask the clerk for the full payable amount.

Do not rely on the national range of $1,000–$2,500 as a quote. It shows how serious the offense is treated nationally, not what your Arizona court will impose.

How many points does reckless driving add in Arizona?

Arizona uses a points system. The locked figure we can confirm is 3 points for speeding. We do not have a verified point value for reckless driving, so we are not going to guess one.

The threshold that matters is this: 8+ points in 12 months = suspension or Traffic Survival School.

To see how a conviction would be scored, check your driving record through the Arizona Department of Transportation — Motor Vehicle Division. Also check what the court says about license consequences.

Points are only one consequence. A criminal conviction can carry penalties beyond the points system, so do not treat the point count as the full picture.

Does reckless driving raise insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Arizona-specific one.

A reckless driving conviction is more serious than a typical moving violation, so the effect on your premium could be larger than the average. We cannot give a number, and we do not recommend any insurer.

How much your rate moves depends on your insurer, your driving history and how long the conviction stays on your record. Your insurer can tell you how it treats the violation.

When you weigh the total cost, add any increase in premiums over several years to the fine and court surcharges. For many drivers that long-run cost is larger than the fine.

Can a reckless driving ticket be dismissed or reduced?

It can happen in some cases, but outcomes depend on the court, the facts and the evidence. We cannot predict how any case will turn out.

Because this is a criminal misdemeanor, you generally have more at stake than with a routine ticket. Common questions to raise with the court or a qualified attorney include:

  • Whether the charge as written fits the facts.
  • Whether a different or reduced charge is available.
  • Whether the court offers any program that affects the outcome.
  • How a conviction would affect your license and points.

If points push you toward the 8+ in 12 months threshold, Traffic Survival School is one of the consequences the points system names. Confirm eligibility and details with the MVD or the court.

TicketCostGuide does not give legal advice or recommend an attorney. Decisions about pleading or contesting are best made with a licensed professional.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore a reckless driving citation?

Ignoring a criminal citation can make the situation worse. Courts commonly treat a missed appearance or an unpaid balance as a separate problem, and it can lead to added penalties or a license hold.

Because the court sets the amount and adds surcharges, your balance can differ from what you expect. Contact the clerk before the deadline if you are unsure what you owe or when you must appear.

If you cannot pay in full, ask the court what options it offers. Procedures vary by court, so do not assume the rules from another court apply to yours.

Check the date and time on your citation or summons as soon as you receive it. Missing a required appearance is one of the most avoidable mistakes.
