How much is a reckless driving ticket in Maine?

Nationally, reckless driving fines commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500, and it is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail. In Maine, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Maine Bureau of Motor Vehicles).

We do not publish a single Maine dollar figure because none is fixed in advance. The number on your paperwork reflects the court's decision within the state's traffic code.

The $1,000–$2,500 range is a national figure. Treat it as context for how serious the charge is, not as a quote for a Maine case.

Fines are only one part of the cost. A misdemeanor charge can also bring points, possible jail exposure, higher insurance premiums and time spent in court.

How is the reckless driving fine set in Maine?

The court sets the fine within the limits of the state's traffic code. The amount appears on the citation or in the court's judgment after a plea or finding.

Several things can matter in practice:

  • The facts described in the citation, such as the driving behavior alleged.
  • Your driving record and any earlier convictions.
  • Whether the case ends in a conviction, a reduced charge or a dismissal.
  • The court handling the case, since amounts can differ from one court to another.

Statutory surcharges are built into the scheduled amounts. That means the total you owe is not simply a base fine with a separate pile of add-ons. Read the citation or ask the court clerk what the total payment covers.

You can read the underlying statutes through Maine statutes via Justia. Check that the version you are reading is current, because wording and penalty provisions can change.

How many points does reckless driving add in Maine?

Maine uses a demerit point system in which points accrue per conviction. The locked rule we can state is that 12 points in 12 months leads to a suspension.

We do not list a specific point value for reckless driving here, because our sources do not give one we can verify. Your driving record from the Maine Bureau of Motor Vehicles is the place to see how a conviction was recorded.

Two practical points follow from how the system works:

  • Points attach to convictions, not to the citation alone. The outcome of the case matters.
  • Points from other violations in the same 12 months can combine with this one and move you toward the suspension threshold.

A suspension can also arise through other routes, such as court action after a misdemeanor conviction. Do not assume that staying under 12 points protects your license.

Is reckless driving a criminal charge in Maine?

Nationally, reckless driving is generally a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail. That puts it in a different category from a routine speeding ticket, which is usually a civil traffic matter.

The practical differences can be significant:

  • A conviction can create a criminal record, which may show up in background checks.
  • A judge may have jail available as a penalty, even if it is not imposed in every case.
  • You may need to appear in court instead of simply paying by mail.

For the specific classification and penalty range under Maine law, read the current statutes at Maine statutes via Justia and confirm with the court. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot tell you how the law applies to your situation.

If jail or a criminal record is possible in your case, consider speaking with a licensed attorney of your own choosing before you enter a plea.

Does reckless driving raise your insurance in Maine?

Often, yes. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Maine-specific one.

Reckless driving is usually viewed by insurers as more serious than a minor moving violation, so your own change could be different from that average. We cannot give a Maine percentage, because it depends on your insurer, your history and how the conviction is recorded.

Some things to keep in mind:

  • The increase usually appears at renewal, not the day you are convicted.
  • A surcharge can last for several renewal cycles, depending on the insurer's rules.
  • Comparing quotes after a conviction may show a wide spread between companies.

Over a few years, the added premium can exceed the fine itself. When you weigh the cost of a case, include that long-term effect.

Can a reckless driving ticket be dismissed or reduced in Maine?

It can be possible, but outcomes depend on the facts, the court and the prosecutor. We do not track Maine outcome rates, so we will not suggest a likelihood.

Common paths people ask about include:

  • Contesting the charge and asking the court to hold a hearing.
  • Negotiating with the prosecutor to resolve the case on a lesser charge.
  • Asking the clerk what options and deadlines apply to your citation.

A reduced charge can change both the fine and how the case is recorded, which can affect points and insurance. Because reckless driving is a criminal matter, it is worth understanding the consequences of each plea before choosing one.

TicketCostGuide does not give legal advice or recommend attorneys. A licensed Maine attorney or the court clerk can explain what is available in your case.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the citation?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. A court can treat a missed appearance or unpaid fine as a separate problem, and that can add penalties, affect your license or lead to further court action.

Because reckless driving is a criminal misdemeanor, the stakes of missing a court date are higher than with a simple civil ticket. Read the citation closely for instructions and deadlines.

If you cannot pay the full amount at once, ask the court clerk what options exist, such as a payment arrangement. Do this before the due date, not after.

Remember that the total on the citation includes statutory surcharges built into the scheduled amounts. Paying only part of it usually will not resolve the case.

For license status, you can check with the Maine Bureau of Motor Vehicles.
