How much is a reckless driving ticket in South Dakota?

Nationally, reckless driving fines commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500. It is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail. In South Dakota the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (South Dakota Department of Public Safety).

We do not publish a single South Dakota dollar figure for this offense. The state amount is not a fixed number we can verify, and the national range is only a guide to the general scale.

The national range is not a South Dakota price. Your citation and the court that handles it control what you actually owe.

Your total can also include court costs. Where a scheduled amount applies, court costs are included in that standard amount. Read the citation and any court notice to see what is listed for your case.

How is the reckless driving fine set in South Dakota?

The exact fine is set by the court within the state's traffic code. It is not a flat statewide figure, and it is written on or tied to your citation.

Reckless driving is treated as a criminal misdemeanor, not a routine ticket. That matters because a court has more discretion, and jail is a possible penalty alongside or instead of a fine.

Things that commonly shape the outcome of a criminal traffic case include:

  • the facts described in the citation;
  • your driving record;
  • whether anyone was hurt or property was damaged;
  • the practices of the court that hears the case.

We cannot say how any of these will affect your amount. For the text of the law, see the South Dakota statutes via Justia.

How many points does reckless driving add in South Dakota?

Points in South Dakota are assigned per conviction toward suspension thresholds. The locked threshold we can cite is 15 points in 12 months (South Dakota Department of Public Safety).

We do not state a specific point value for reckless driving. We could not verify one from our sources, and a wrong number is worse than none. Ask the Department of Public Safety or check the current statutes for the value that applies.

Two points are worth knowing:

  • Points follow a conviction, not just a citation.
  • A suspension can be reached by adding up several violations within the 12-month window.

If you already have other convictions on your record, a reckless driving conviction may matter more than it would on a clean record.

Does reckless driving raise my insurance?

Often, yes. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a South Dakota rate.

A reckless driving conviction is more serious than many moving violations because it is a criminal misdemeanor. Insurers may react differently, and some may treat it more severely than the average. We cannot give a verified figure for that.

The ~20% figure is an average across moving violations nationwide. Your own change could be higher, lower or none, depending on your insurer and record.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend insurers. If you are concerned about a renewal, ask your current insurer how a conviction would be treated.

Can a reckless driving charge be dismissed or reduced?

It can happen, but we cannot promise it and we do not give legal advice. Because the charge is a criminal misdemeanor, outcomes are decided by the court and the prosecutor on the facts of your case.

Common paths in criminal traffic cases generally include:

  • pleading to the charge as written;
  • contesting the charge at a hearing;
  • negotiating the charge to a different, lesser offense.

Each path has different consequences for your record, your points and your insurance. A reduced charge may carry a different fine and different points than reckless driving, but the details depend on the court.

Because jail is possible, many people choose to talk with a licensed attorney before deciding. We do not recommend a specific attorney. A court clerk can explain procedure but cannot advise you on strategy.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the citation?

Ignoring a criminal misdemeanor citation can make things worse. Courts can add penalties for missing a required appearance or leaving a fine unpaid, and the matter can affect your driving privileges.

We do not list the exact consequences or deadlines, since they depend on the court and the citation. Read the paperwork for your appearance date and instructions.

  • Note any court date printed on the citation.
  • Contact the court clerk if anything is unclear.
  • Do not assume a fine alone resolves the case, since jail is possible.

Because the exact amount is set by the court on the citation, paying without reading what you are admitting to can also have effects on points and insurance.

What are my options after getting a reckless driving citation?

Start with the citation itself. It tells you the charge, the court and the date you must respond or appear.

  1. Confirm the exact charge written on the citation.
  2. Find the court named on it and note the appearance date.
  3. Ask the clerk what amount, if any, is listed and what costs are included.
  4. Check your points status with the Department of Public Safety.
  5. Consider speaking with a licensed attorney before you enter a plea.

The 15 points in 12 months threshold is worth keeping in mind if you have other recent convictions. For the national picture of how states treat this offense, see the Governors Highway Safety Association state laws pages.
