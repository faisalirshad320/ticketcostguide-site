How much is a reckless driving ticket in Nebraska?

The honest answer is that the amount depends on the court and the citation. Nationally fines commonly up to $1,000–$2,500, and reckless driving is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail (Nebraska Department of Motor Vehicles). In Nebraska the exact amount is set by the court on the citation.

We do not publish an exact Nebraska fine here because it is not fixed in the sources we use. Any dollar figure you see elsewhere without a citation should be checked against your own paperwork.

Key point: the $1,000–$2,500 range is a national figure. Your Nebraska amount is whatever the court sets on your citation, plus court costs.

Remember that the fine is only one part of the cost. Court costs, possible higher insurance premiums and any jail exposure all add to the real price of a reckless driving charge.

How is the reckless driving fine set in Nebraska?

Reckless driving is a criminal misdemeanor, so a court decides the penalty rather than a fixed schedule printed on a ticket. The amount is set within the state's traffic code and written on the citation. You can read the Nebraska statutes through Nebraska statutes via Justia.

Several things can shape what the court does, though we cannot say how any one judge will rule:

  • The facts alleged in the citation and how they are proven or admitted.
  • Whether the charge stays as written or is changed by agreement.
  • Your driving record.
  • Whether the offense happened in a zone where fines are doubled.

Fines are doubled in some zones, and court costs are added statewide. That means the total you owe can be higher than the base fine on the citation.

Are there extra court costs or doubled fines?

Yes. Court costs are added statewide in Nebraska, and fines are doubled in some zones. These are the two add-ons our sources identify.

We do not list a dollar amount for either one, because the locked figures do not include one. Your citation or the court clerk can tell you the total due.

  • Court costs: added statewide on top of the fine.
  • Doubled-fine zones: the fine is increased in certain zones, which can raise the total considerably.

When you compare what you owe with a number you found online, check whether it includes these add-ons. A figure that covers only the fine can understate the real cost.

How many points does reckless driving add in Nebraska?

Nebraska's point system assigns 1–3 points by mph over, and 12 points in 2 years leads to revocation (Nebraska Department of Motor Vehicles). That description is built around speed, so it does not tell you exactly how reckless driving is scored.

We do not state a specific point value for reckless driving. To find out how a conviction is recorded on your license, ask the Nebraska DMV or review the statutes on Justia.

Why it matters: points add up. A conviction that adds to your total brings you closer to the 12-point revocation threshold within 2 years.

Points are a separate consequence from the fine. You can pay the court and still carry the points on your driving record.

Does reckless driving raise insurance rates?

It often does. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure and not a Nebraska-specific one.

Reckless driving is more serious than a routine moving violation, so the effect on your own policy could differ. We cannot predict what your insurer will do, and we do not recommend any insurer.

  • The increase usually comes from a conviction on your record, not just from being cited.
  • Each company rates violations its own way.
  • Some insurers review your record only at renewal.

To understand your own exposure, ask your insurer how a criminal misdemeanor driving conviction would be treated at renewal.

Can a reckless driving charge be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes charges are dismissed, reduced or resolved by agreement, but we cannot say whether that applies to your case. Because reckless driving is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail, the stakes are higher than for a typical ticket.

People in this position generally weigh a few paths:

  1. Plead and pay: you accept the charge, and the court sets the fine plus costs.
  2. Contest the charge: you ask the court to decide whether the case against you is proven.
  3. Seek a negotiated outcome: the prosecutor and the defendant may agree to a different charge.

Each path has different consequences for your record, points and insurance. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice. For a charge with possible jail, many people choose to speak with a licensed attorney before deciding.

Read the citation closely for the court name and appearance instructions. A reckless driving citation can require you to go to court rather than just mail in a payment.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the citation?

Ignoring a criminal citation can make things worse. The court can treat a missed appearance or unpaid fine as a new problem, and the original penalty still applies.

We do not list specific deadlines or late fees, because those are not part of the figures we publish. Your citation and the court clerk are the right places to confirm them.

  • Contact the court named on the citation as soon as you can.
  • Ask what is owed, including court costs.
  • Ask what your options are for appearing or responding.

Unresolved matters can also affect your license standing. Check with the Nebraska DMV about the status of your driving record.
