How much is a reckless driving ticket in Arkansas?

Nationally, fines for reckless driving commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500. It is also a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail. In Arkansas the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Arkansas Department of Finance and Administration — Driver Services).

We do not publish an exact Arkansas dollar figure, because the amount is not fixed in advance. It depends on the court, the facts of the stop and the charge as written.

The national range is a reference point only. Your Arkansas total could fall above or below it, so treat the citation and the court paperwork as the real answer.

Bottom line: the national range tells you the scale of the problem. The Arkansas court tells you your actual number.

Court costs come on top of the fine. The next section explains how those are added.

How is the reckless driving fine set in Arkansas?

The exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The amount appears on your citation or in the court's disposition, not in a single published price list.

Court costs are added by the district court and vary by county. Two drivers with the same charge in different counties can therefore owe different totals.

Several things can affect where a case lands:

  • The charge as written on the citation.
  • Whether the case is handled in district court and by which county's court.
  • Any plea or agreement reached in court.
  • Court costs and surcharges added to the fine.

Because reckless driving is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail, the court process is more serious than for a typical civil traffic infraction. Read the citation for the court name, the date and the instructions on how to respond.

For the underlying law, Arkansas statutes are available through Arkansas statutes via Justia. We do not quote section numbers here because we only state what our sources support.

How many points is reckless driving in Arkansas?

The state context we rely on lists the points system as 3–8 points by speed. It also says 14+ points can trigger suspension.

In plain terms, the number of points depends on the speed involved. A conviction at the higher end counts for more than one at the lower end.

A few practical points:

  • Points are added when a conviction is recorded, not usually when you receive the citation.
  • A point total of 14+ can trigger a license suspension.
  • One conviction at the higher end of the 3–8 range, added to other recent points, moves you closer to that threshold.

To see your current standing, check your driving record through Arkansas Department of Finance and Administration — Driver Services. The agency can confirm how points are recorded on your license.

You can also compare how states treat points through Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws.

Does reckless driving raise insurance rates in Arkansas?

Usually, yes. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Arkansas-specific one.

Reckless driving is a criminal misdemeanor, so insurers may treat it as more serious than many ordinary moving violations. We cannot say how much any particular company will add, because rating rules differ by insurer.

What shapes the effect on your premium:

  • Whether the offense ends in a conviction on your record.
  • Your insurer's own surcharge rules and how long they look back.
  • Your prior driving history and any other recent violations.

The ~20% figure is a national average for moving-violation convictions. Treat it as a rough benchmark, not a quote.

The way to learn your real change is to ask your insurer or compare quotes at renewal. We do not recommend any insurer.

Can a reckless driving ticket be dismissed or reduced in Arkansas?

It can be possible, but outcomes depend on the court, the prosecutor and the facts. We cannot predict a result or tell you what to do in your case.

Because reckless driving is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail, the stakes are higher than for many tickets. Many people choose to speak with a licensed attorney before they respond. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not recommend any specific attorney.

Questions worth asking the court clerk or a licensed professional:

  • What exactly is the charge, and what is the court date?
  • Are there options other than pleading guilty or going to trial?
  • How would a reduced charge change the points recorded on my license?
  • What court costs would be added to any fine?

Since points are set by speed within a 3–8 range, a change in the charge could change the points. Confirm any such effect with the court or the licensing agency before relying on it.

Do not skip the court date while you weigh your options. The next section covers what can follow if you do.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore a reckless driving citation?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Because this is a criminal misdemeanor, missing a court date can bring added legal consequences. Check with the court listed on your citation for exactly what applies to you.

Common risks of not responding include:

  • Additional penalties or costs added by the court.
  • A possible license problem if the court reports non-compliance.
  • Further court action in a criminal case.

Points matter too. If a conviction is recorded and your total reaches 14+ points, a suspension can be triggered.

If you cannot pay the full amount, call the court clerk before the deadline and ask what is available. Court costs are added by the district court and vary by county, so ask for the full total, not just the fine.
