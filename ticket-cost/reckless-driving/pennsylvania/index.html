How much is a reckless driving ticket in Pennsylvania?

Nationally, fines for reckless driving commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500. It is also treated as a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail. In Pennsylvania the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (PennDOT Driver and Vehicle Services).

We do not publish an exact Pennsylvania dollar figure, because the amount depends on the court and on the case. The national range is a reference point, not a quote for your ticket.

The number on your citation or court notice is the one that counts. The national range only shows how serious the charge is considered to be.

Fine amounts are only part of the bill. Court costs, an EMS surcharge and other statutory fees are added, and they typically exceed the base fine.

How is the reckless driving fine set in Pennsylvania?

The court sets the fine within the limits in the state's traffic code. You can read the code through Pennsylvania statutes via Justia.

Because the court sets the amount, two drivers with similar tickets can owe different totals. The court looks at the facts of the case and what the charge says.

Here is what usually makes up the total:

  • Base fine: set by the court on the citation.
  • Court costs: statutory charges for processing the case.
  • EMS surcharge and other statutory fees: added by law on top of the fine.

Court costs, the EMS surcharge and other statutory fees typically exceed the base fine. The base fine alone is therefore a poor guide to what you will owe.

Is reckless driving a criminal charge in Pennsylvania?

Nationally, reckless driving is generally a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail. That is a different category from an ordinary speeding ticket, which is usually a civil-style traffic matter.

A criminal charge can mean a court appearance and a record that follows you. It can also affect things beyond the fine, such as background checks.

We do not state a specific jail term for Pennsylvania here. Read your citation for the exact charge, and check the traffic code on Justia for the penalties that apply.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice. If you face a criminal charge, consider speaking with a licensed attorney of your choosing.

How many points does reckless driving add in Pennsylvania?

We do not list a specific point value for reckless driving. The point information in our sources is tied to speed. For speeding, Pennsylvania's system assigns 2–5 points for 11+ mph over the limit.

Two further rules apply to drivers who build up points or speed at high levels:

  • 6 points triggers a departmental exam/hearing.
  • High-speed convictions add a 15-day suspension at 31+ mph over.

If your reckless driving charge came from a speed-related stop, these thresholds may matter to you. Check your citation and your record with PennDOT Driver and Vehicle Services to see what was actually recorded.

Does reckless driving raise insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Pennsylvania-specific one.

A reckless driving conviction is more serious than a typical moving violation. Your own increase could be higher or lower than the average, depending on your insurer and your driving history.

We do not recommend any insurer. If your policy is up for renewal, you may want to ask your insurer how a conviction would be treated. You can also compare quotes from several companies.

  • The increase usually comes from a conviction, not from the ticket alone.
  • The effect can last for several renewal periods, depending on the insurer.

Can a reckless driving ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes a charge is dismissed or reduced, but this depends on the facts, the court and the prosecutor. We cannot say how likely it is in your case.

The options usually available to a driver are:

  1. Pay the amount on the citation, which generally means accepting the conviction.
  2. Contest the charge at a hearing and present your side.
  3. Ask the court or prosecutor about a reduced charge, where that is allowed.

Paying the fine is not always the cheapest choice. A conviction can raise insurance by about 20% on average, and it may add points or a criminal record.

This is general information, not legal advice. For a criminal misdemeanor, a licensed attorney can explain the choices that apply to you.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts can add costs, and a missed court date can lead to further action on your case.

Because reckless driving is a criminal matter, a missed appearance is more serious than an unpaid parking ticket. The exact consequences depend on the court that issued the citation.

Read the deadline and instructions on your citation. If you are unsure, contact the court listed on the paperwork. If your license is affected, PennDOT Driver and Vehicle Services can tell you the status of your driving record.

Do not rely on a general guide for deadlines. Use the dates on your own citation or court notice.
