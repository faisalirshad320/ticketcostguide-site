How much is a reckless driving ticket in Ohio?

Nationally, reckless driving fines commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500, and the charge is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail (Ohio Bureau of Motor Vehicles). That is a national range, not an Ohio price.

In Ohio, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not print a single Ohio dollar figure, because the court decides it and no fixed number applies to every case.

Your total bill is usually more than the fine alone. Municipal court costs vary by court and often exceed the fine.

The number on your citation or court notice is the only amount that counts for your case. Use the national range here only as a rough sense of scale.

How is the reckless driving fine set in Ohio?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. It is not a flat amount printed on a statewide chart that we can quote.

That means two drivers cited for the same offense in different courts can see different totals. The court handling your case, and the details of the citation, shape what you are asked to pay.

The traffic code itself is part of the Ohio Revised Code, which you can read through Ohio statutes via Justia. If you want the precise wording of the offense and its penalty range, start there.

Because reckless driving is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail, a judge may have more to decide than with an ordinary speeding ticket. The citation or the court's notice will show what is required of you and by when.

Why can court costs be higher than the fine?

Municipal court costs vary by court and often exceed the fine. The fine is the penalty for the violation, while court costs are separate charges the court adds to process the case.

This is why a quoted "fine" can understate what you actually pay. Two courts with the same fine can produce different totals.

  • Look for a line-by-line breakdown on the citation, the court's website or the clerk's notice.
  • Ask the clerk whether the amount shown is the fine only or the fine plus costs.
  • Ask about accepted payment methods and deadlines before you send money.

We do not list an Ohio figure for court costs, because the amount depends on the court.

How many points does reckless driving carry in Ohio?

Our locked data on Ohio's points system covers speeding: 2 points for most speeding convictions, 0 points for small overages in some circumstances, and 4 points at 30+ over. Twelve points in 2 years means a suspension.

The point value for a reckless driving conviction is not part of that data, so we will not guess at it. Confirm the number with the Ohio Bureau of Motor Vehicles or on your court paperwork.

Points matter because they accumulate. The 12-points-in-2-years threshold is the line at which a suspension applies, so earlier points on your record count toward it.

A criminal conviction can carry consequences beyond points, including the possible jail exposure noted above. A court or a licensed attorney can explain how that applies to you.

Does a reckless driving ticket raise insurance in Ohio?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Ohio quote.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your driving record and how the violation is recorded. A criminal misdemeanor conviction may be treated more seriously than a basic speeding ticket, but we have no sourced figure for that difference.

  • Ask your insurer how it treats a reckless driving conviction before you decide how to resolve the case.
  • Compare the possible premium change against the fine and court costs, since the increase can last well beyond the payment date.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer or give insurance advice.

Can a reckless driving ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes cases end in a different outcome, but we cannot say whether yours will. Whether a charge is dismissed or changed is up to the prosecutor and the court, and it depends on the facts.

In general, you have a few paths. You can pay, contest the charge at a court date, or speak with a licensed attorney about your situation.

  1. Read the citation closely for the court name, the charge and any required appearance date.
  2. Ask the clerk whether paying the citation is treated as a plea or a conviction before you pay.
  3. Consider speaking with a licensed Ohio attorney, since this is a criminal charge. TicketCostGuide does not recommend a specific attorney.

This page is general information, not legal advice. Decisions about pleas and defenses should be made with a qualified professional.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the citation?

Ignoring a citation tends to make things worse, not go away. Because reckless driving is a criminal misdemeanor, a missed court date or unpaid amount is a bigger risk than with a minor civil ticket.

We do not have sourced figures for late fees or the specific penalties Ohio courts add. Those are set by the court, so check the notice and contact the clerk directly.

If you cannot pay the full amount or cannot attend on the date given, contact the court before the deadline. Ask what options it offers, rather than waiting for a notice to arrive.
