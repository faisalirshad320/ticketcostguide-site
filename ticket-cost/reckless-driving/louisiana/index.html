How much is a reckless driving ticket in Louisiana?

The lead number is a national one. Reckless driving fines commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500, and the offense is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail (Louisiana Office of Motor Vehicles).

In Louisiana, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Louisiana dollar figure because the court decides it within the state's traffic code.

Treat the national range as a general guide only. Your own number is whatever the court sets for your case, plus local court costs.

Court costs matter as much as the fine. Local court costs vary by parish and often exceed the fine, so the total you owe can be well above the fine line alone.

The citation and the court clerk are the only places to confirm what you owe. A reckless driving charge is more serious than an ordinary speeding ticket, so the paperwork deserves a careful read.

How is the reckless driving fine set in Louisiana?

The fine is set by the court, within the limits of the state's traffic code. It is not a fixed amount printed on a statewide schedule that you can look up and pay without a court's involvement.

That is the main difference from minor infractions. Many simple tickets have a preset payment amount. A criminal misdemeanor like reckless driving usually goes through a court process.

The statutes are published online through Louisiana statutes via Justia. You can read the traffic code there to see how the offense is defined and what penalties the law allows.

Several things can shape the outcome in any court:

  • The facts described on the citation
  • Whether anyone was hurt or property was damaged
  • Your driving record
  • The parish court that hears the case

We cannot predict how a particular court will weigh these. What we can say is that the amount comes from the court and not from a formula we can apply for you.

What are the court costs and surcharges on top of the fine?

Local court costs vary by parish and often exceed the fine. This is easy to miss when you only look at the fine amount.

Because costs differ by parish, two drivers with the same charge can owe different totals. The clerk of the court that handles your citation can tell you the full amount due.

When you compare what you owe, ask for the complete total and not only the fine. Ask what is included and what payment options the court offers.

We do not list a Louisiana court cost figure. The only sourced statement is that costs vary by parish and often exceed the fine.

How many points does reckless driving add in Louisiana?

Louisiana has no point system. License action follows repeat convictions and specific offenses.

That means you will not find a points value to add up for this charge. Instead, the risk to your license depends on your conviction history and on whether the offense itself triggers action.

A reckless driving conviction counts as a conviction on your record, and repeat convictions can lead to license action. The Louisiana Office of Motor Vehicles, reachable through its online portal, is the place to check your license status and record.

A national overview of how states treat traffic offenses is available from the Governors Highway Safety Association. It can help with comparisons, but your own state record is what counts for your license.

Does a reckless driving ticket raise insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure and not a Louisiana-specific one.

Reckless driving is a more serious conviction than many moving violations. Your own change could differ from the average, and it depends on your insurer and your record.

We do not recommend any insurer. If you are worried about your rate, your insurer or agent can explain how a conviction would be treated at renewal.

The outcome of the case can matter here. A conviction is what typically drives a premium change, so the result in court is relevant to your insurance as well as to the fine.

Can a reckless driving charge be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes a charge is changed or resolved in a different way, but we cannot say whether that applies to you. Because reckless driving is a criminal misdemeanor, it is handled as a criminal matter in court.

Common questions to raise with the court or a licensed attorney include:

  • Whether the charge can be amended to a lesser offense
  • Whether any diversion or similar program is available in that parish
  • What a guilty or no-contest plea would mean for your record
  • What the total cost would be, including court costs

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice or recommend any attorney. Because possible jail is part of the penalty, many people choose to talk with a licensed Louisiana attorney before deciding how to respond.

Do not ignore the court date on the citation. Missing it can make the situation worse.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore a reckless driving citation?

We do not have sourced specifics on penalties for non-payment in Louisiana, so we will not guess at them. What we can say is that the offense is a criminal misdemeanor, and courts treat missed appearances and unpaid amounts seriously.

Ignoring a citation can lead to further court action and can affect your license. Repeat convictions and specific offenses already drive license action in Louisiana.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the clerk of the court before the deadline. Ask what options exist for your case.

The citation lists the court and the date you must respond. Keep a copy of it and any receipt or confirmation you get.
