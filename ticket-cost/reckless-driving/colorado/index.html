How much does a reckless driving ticket cost in Colorado?

The lead figure for this page is: Nationally fines commonly up to $1,000–$2,500; a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail; in Colorado the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Colorado Division of Motor Vehicles).

That range is a national one. It is not a Colorado fine schedule, and we do not publish a single Colorado dollar amount for reckless driving.

The Colorado amount is set by the court within the state's traffic code and is written on your citation. Courts can differ, so the number on your paperwork is the one that counts.

Reckless driving is treated as a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail, not a routine traffic infraction. The cost is more than the fine alone.

Beyond the base fine, expect extra costs to be layered on. The next section explains how.

How is the reckless driving fine set in Colorado?

In Colorado, the court sets the exact fine within the state's traffic code. The amount appears on the citation or in the court's instructions.

Because it is a criminal misdemeanor, the outcome depends on the court handling your case. That is why a single statewide price cannot be quoted reliably.

Two locked facts affect the final total:

  • Statutory surcharge: a statutory surcharge is added to each penalty, so the total owed can be higher than the base fine.
  • Work zones: doubled fines apply in work zones.

If your citation lists a dollar amount, ask the court clerk whether it includes the surcharge. Do not assume it does.

You can read the underlying provisions in the Colorado statutes (Colorado statutes via Justia). Statute text is dense, so the citation and the court's own instructions are the better guide to your actual bill.

How many points does reckless driving add in Colorado?

Colorado's points system runs 1–6 points by mph over. Those points are tied to speed over the limit.

Our sources do not give a point value specific to reckless driving, so we will not state one. Your citation, the court record and the state DMV are where to confirm how the conviction is scored (Colorado Division of Motor Vehicles).

The key threshold is this: 12 points in 12 months (adult) = suspension. Points from other violations in the same period count toward that total.

If you already have points on your record, a new conviction could bring you closer to the 12-points-in-12-months suspension threshold. Check your record before deciding how to respond.

Points are separate from the fine. You can pay the fine in full and still face a license consequence.

Does reckless driving raise insurance rates?

Usually, yes. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Colorado quote.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the conviction appears on your record. We do not recommend or rate specific insurers.

Reckless driving is a criminal misdemeanor, so insurers may view it differently from a minor infraction. We have no locked figure for that difference, so treat the 20% as a general reference point only.

To estimate the impact, ask your insurer how a moving-violation conviction would affect your renewal. Compare that with the court's total, including the surcharge, to see the full cost over time.

Can a reckless driving ticket be dismissed or reduced?

It depends on the court and the facts of your case. Our sources do not list guaranteed outcomes, and we cannot promise a dismissal or reduction.

Because reckless driving is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail, the stakes are higher than for a basic ticket. Many people in that position choose to speak with a licensed attorney before they respond. This is not a recommendation of any particular attorney.

General steps that apply to most citations:

  1. Read the citation for the court name, date and any instructions.
  2. Contact the court clerk and ask what options exist for your case and what the total owed would be, including the surcharge.
  3. Check your driving record and your points total.
  4. Decide how to respond before the deadline on the citation.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore a reckless driving citation?

Ignoring a citation is risky, and more so for a criminal misdemeanor. Our sources do not list exact penalties for non-payment, so we will not guess at figures.

The citation and the court's instructions set out what you must do and by when. If you cannot meet a deadline, contact the court clerk before it passes.

Remember that points can also lead to a suspension. Under the locked rules, 12 points in 12 months (adult) = suspension. Missed obligations can make a bad situation worse, so confirm the status of your case directly with the court and the DMV (Colorado Division of Motor Vehicles).

Do not rely on a general guide for deadlines. Use the dates printed on your citation.
