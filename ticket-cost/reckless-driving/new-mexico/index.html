How much is a reckless driving ticket in New Mexico?

There is no single New Mexico price tag we can quote. Nationally, reckless driving fines commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500, and the offense is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail (New Mexico Motor Vehicle Division).

In New Mexico, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. That is why we do not print a state dollar figure here. The national range is context, not a promise of what your court will order.

The number that matters for you is the one on your citation or in the court's instructions. Everything else on this page is background.

Expect the total you pay to include more than the fine itself. The section on court costs below explains why.

How is a reckless driving fine set in New Mexico?

The exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. Statutes are published online, and you can browse New Mexico's code through New Mexico statutes via Justia.

Because reckless driving is a criminal misdemeanor rather than a simple civil-style infraction, it is handled as a court case. The court, not a fixed roadside schedule, decides the amount.

Several things can shape what a court does in a case like this:

  • The facts alleged in the citation.
  • Whether anyone was hurt or property was damaged.
  • Your driving record.
  • How the case is resolved (a plea, a trial, or a reduced charge).

We cannot predict how any of these will affect a specific New Mexico case, and none of them come with a locked dollar figure. Treat the list as the usual factors courts weigh, not as a calculator.

What court costs and surcharges are added?

Fees are added to the penalty assessment. In New Mexico, court cases can exceed the assessment amounts.

In practice, that means the fine is only one part of your bill. If your case goes through court rather than being paid as a standard assessment, the total can be higher than a base penalty would suggest.

We do not list individual fee names or dollar amounts here because we do not have sourced figures for them. Ask the court clerk for an itemized total before you pay or agree to anything.

Tip: when you call the clerk, ask for the fine and all added fees as one total, so you can compare it with the citation.

How many points does reckless driving add in New Mexico?

New Mexico's points system assigns 3–8 points by mph over. That schedule is tied to speed, so a reckless driving citation does not map neatly onto it unless the citation involves speed.

We therefore do not claim a specific point value for reckless driving. Check your citation and the New Mexico Motor Vehicle Division for how your conviction would be scored on your driving record.

The thresholds that matter are these:

  • 7–10 points in 12 months risks suspension.
  • 12 points means revocation.

Points from earlier violations count too. If you already have points on your record, even a lower-scoring conviction could move you toward those thresholds.

Does reckless driving raise insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a New Mexico one, and it covers moving violations in general.

A reckless driving conviction is more serious than many moving violations, so the effect on your own policy could differ. We have no sourced figure for that, and we do not recommend any insurer or predict what yours will do.

If you are concerned, ask your insurer how a conviction is treated at renewal. Ask before you decide how to resolve the case, not after.

Can a reckless driving ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes cases end in a dismissal or a reduced charge, but we cannot say how often that happens in New Mexico, and we have no sourced figures on it. The outcome depends on the facts and on the court.

Because this is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail, the stakes are higher than for a basic speeding ticket. Many people in that position talk to a licensed New Mexico attorney or the court's public resources before responding. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not recommend any specific attorney.

Questions worth asking the court or your own counsel:

  • Do I have to appear in person?
  • What are my options for responding to this citation?
  • Could a different charge be considered?
  • What is the total amount I would owe, fees included?

This is general information, not legal advice.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore it?

Ignoring a criminal misdemeanor citation is risky. We do not have sourced details on New Mexico's specific penalties for missed deadlines, so we will not list them.

What we can say is that the points thresholds apply to your record: 7–10 points in 12 months risks suspension, and 12 points means revocation. Unresolved matters can add to the trouble.

If you cannot pay or cannot make a date, contact the court named on your citation before the deadline. Clerks can explain what options exist for your case.
