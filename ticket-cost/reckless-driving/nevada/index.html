How much is a reckless driving ticket in Nevada?

Nationally, reckless driving fines commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500, and the offense is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail. In Nevada, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Nevada Department of Motor Vehicles).

That means we cannot give you one statewide Nevada price. The national range is a general guide to how serious these fines can be, not a quote for your case.

The fine is also only part of the bill. Administrative fees are added by the court, and those amounts vary by jurisdiction. Your total can therefore be higher than the fine line alone.

Your citation and the court that handles it are the only reliable sources for your actual amount. Do not rely on a national range to budget for payment.

How is the reckless driving fine set in Nevada?

The exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. Nevada statutes are published online, and you can read the traffic provisions through Nevada statutes via Justia.

Because the court sets the amount, two drivers charged with the same offense in different places can see different totals. The court's own administrative fees can also differ from one jurisdiction to another.

Several things can matter in practice, though we do not publish a Nevada formula for any of them:

  • The court that has your case.
  • The facts described in the citation.
  • Whether the charge stays as written or is changed.
  • Added administrative fees.

If the citation does not show a payable amount, the court clerk can tell you what the court requires. A charge that carries possible jail often needs a court appearance rather than a simple payment.

Is reckless driving a criminal charge in Nevada?

Reckless driving is generally treated as a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail. That sets it apart from an ordinary speeding ticket, which many drivers resolve by paying online.

A criminal conviction can have effects beyond the fine. It can create a criminal record, and it can show up on a driving record and affect insurance.

We do not state a jail term for Nevada, because we do not have a locked figure for it. The statute and the court determine what is possible in a given case. You can read the traffic code through Nevada statutes via Justia.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice. If jail is possible in your case, consider speaking with a licensed Nevada attorney of your own choosing.

How many points does reckless driving add in Nevada?

Nevada uses a demerit point system of 1–5 demerit points by mph over. Twelve points in 12 months leads to a 6-month suspension (Nevada Department of Motor Vehicles).

The point range above is described in terms of mph over the limit, which fits speed-related violations. We do not have a locked figure for how many points a reckless driving conviction adds, so we will not guess.

To find out how your conviction would be scored, you can:

  • Ask the DMV how the specific charge on your citation is counted.
  • Ask the court clerk how the conviction is reported to the DMV.
  • Request a copy of your driving record to see your current points.

Points matter most if you already have other violations on your record. The 12-points-in-12-months threshold is cumulative, so older tickets within the window count too.

Does reckless driving raise insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Nevada-specific or insurer-specific one.

Reckless driving is a more serious offense than many moving violations, so some insurers may treat it differently. We have no locked data on how much more, and we do not name or recommend any insurer.

What you can do is ask your own insurer how a conviction would be handled at renewal. Also compare how a conviction differs from a reduced charge, since the outcome in court can change what appears on your record.

Treat the ~20% figure as a rough national average. Your premium could rise by more or less depending on your insurer, your history and the final conviction.

Can a reckless driving ticket be dismissed or reduced?

It can be possible, but outcomes are decided by the court and the prosecutor, not by a published formula. We cannot say how often reductions happen in Nevada.

Common options drivers consider include:

  • Pay or resolve as instructed. This may mean a conviction on your record, with the fine and added fees.
  • Contest the charge. You can ask for a hearing and present your side.
  • Seek a reduced charge. Some drivers ask whether the charge can be changed to a lesser offense, which may affect points and insurance.
  • Consult a licensed attorney. Because jail is possible, many people get individual advice before deciding.

Ask the court about your options before the date on the citation. Reading the traffic code through Nevada statutes via Justia can help you understand what you are charged with.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the citation?

Ignoring a citation for a criminal misdemeanor is risky. Courts generally expect you to respond by the date or follow the instructions on the citation.

Missing that step can lead to added consequences, which can include extra fees or action on your license. The details depend on the court, so we do not list specific penalties or timelines.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court clerk before the deadline. Ask whether payment arrangements exist and what the court requires from you.

Also keep an eye on your license. If points build up, 12 points in 12 months means a 6-month suspension, so one unresolved ticket on top of others can matter.
