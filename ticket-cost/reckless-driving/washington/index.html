How much is a reckless driving ticket in Washington?

Nationally, reckless driving fines commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500. It is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail. In Washington, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Washington State Department of Licensing).

We do not publish a single Washington dollar figure here, because the amount depends on the court and the facts of your case. The national range is a general guide to how serious the charge is, not a quote for your citation.

Read the citation itself. The amount, the court name and any appearance date are what apply to you, not a national average.

Reckless driving is treated very differently from an ordinary speeding or equipment ticket. A criminal charge can carry consequences beyond money, including a record and possible jail.

How is the reckless driving fine set in Washington?

In Washington, the fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The amount is not a fixed number that applies everywhere. You can find the traffic code text through Washington statutes via Justia.

Statutory assessments are built into the IRLJ schedule amounts. That means the listed amount on a schedule already includes those assessments, rather than adding them as separate line items. IRLJ refers to the court rules used for infractions in Washington's courts of limited jurisdiction.

Because reckless driving is a criminal matter, the court looks at the charge and the circumstances rather than simply applying a flat schedule amount. Your citation or court notice is the document that tells you what applies.

  • The court on the citation sets the exact amount.
  • Assessments are built into schedule amounts where a schedule applies.
  • Nothing on this page replaces the figure printed on your paperwork.

Is reckless driving a criminal offense?

Nationally, reckless driving is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail (Washington State Department of Licensing). That puts it in a different category from a standard civil traffic infraction.

A criminal charge usually means a court appearance is expected, not just a payment by mail. It can also mean a criminal record if you are convicted.

Because jail is possible, many people facing this charge talk to a licensed attorney before deciding how to respond. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot give legal advice or recommend anyone.

How many points does reckless driving add in Washington?

None. Washington has no point system. Instead, suspensions follow repeat moving-violation convictions within set windows.

That difference matters. In a points state, you can watch a running total. In Washington, the risk comes from how many moving-violation convictions land on your record inside a given time window.

A reckless driving conviction is a moving-violation conviction, so it can count toward that pattern. For the specific windows and what triggers a suspension, check the Washington State Department of Licensing and the statutes on Justia.

No points does not mean no license risk. Repeat convictions within the set windows can still lead to a suspension.

Does reckless driving raise my insurance in Washington?

It often does. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Washington-specific rate.

Your own change could be smaller or larger. Insurers decide for themselves how to price a conviction, and a criminal reckless driving conviction may be weighed more heavily than a minor ticket. We cannot predict what your insurer will do, and we do not recommend insurers.

  • Ask your insurer how a conviction would be treated before you decide how to respond.
  • Compare the cost of the fine with the cost of higher premiums over several renewals.
  • Remember that the premium effect can last longer than the fine takes to pay.

Can a reckless driving charge be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes charges are dismissed or amended, but the outcome depends on the facts, the evidence and the court. We cannot tell you whether yours qualifies.

Because the exact amount is set by the court on the citation, a change in the charge can change the cost and the record that follows. A conviction on a lesser charge may have different consequences than a conviction for reckless driving.

This is a decision where a licensed attorney can explain your options for your situation. Public defenders may be available in some criminal cases. Ask the court clerk what applies to you.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore a reckless driving citation?

Ignoring it usually makes things worse. Because reckless driving is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail, missing a required court date can lead to further court action.

Do not assume the matter will go away. Check the citation for the court name, the appearance instructions and any deadline. If anything is unclear, contact the court clerk directly.

Unpaid amounts and missed appearances can also affect your driving record and license status. The Washington State Department of Licensing explains how it handles court-reported matters.

Act on the date printed on your paperwork. If you are unsure whether you must appear in person, ask the court before the date passes.
