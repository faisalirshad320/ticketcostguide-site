How much is a reckless driving ticket in West Virginia?

Nationally, fines for reckless driving commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500. That is a national range, not a West Virginia price list. In West Virginia the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (West Virginia Division of Motor Vehicles).

We do not publish an exact West Virginia dollar figure, because the amount depends on the court and the facts of the case. The citation or the court paperwork is the only reliable place to see what you are being asked to pay.

Reckless driving is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail. It is not treated like a routine equipment or parking ticket.

How is a reckless driving fine set in West Virginia?

The fine is set by the court within the limits of the state's traffic code. The statutes are available through West Virginia statutes via Justia. The court handling your case decides the amount that appears on the citation.

Because reckless driving is a criminal misdemeanor, the outcome is not simply a fixed number printed in a schedule. A judge or magistrate may take the circumstances into account, and possible jail is part of the range of outcomes.

Two people charged with the same offense can leave court with different totals. That is why a national range is only a rough guide.

  • The citation lists the charge and the court that will hear it.
  • The court sets the fine within the state's traffic code.
  • Court costs are added on top and vary by court.

Why can court costs be more than the fine?

Municipal and magistrate court costs vary and usually exceed the fine. So the total you owe can be higher than the fine alone suggests.

When you compare numbers online, check whether they include court costs. Many do not, and a headline fine can understate what you pay at the clerk's window.

Costs also differ depending on whether your case is in a municipal court or a magistrate court. The clerk of the court named on your citation can tell you the full amount due, including any costs.

Ask the clerk for the total amount due, not just the fine. Then compare that total with what you were quoted elsewhere.

How many points does reckless driving add in West Virginia?

The state's points system assigns 2–5 points by mph over, and 12+ points = suspension. Those are the figures we can source. We do not state a specific point value for reckless driving, because the points attached to a conviction depend on how the case is resolved and recorded.

Check your driving record or ask the West Virginia Division of Motor Vehicles how a particular conviction would be recorded. Points from different violations can add up toward the suspension threshold.

  • Points: 2–5 points by mph over.
  • Suspension: 12+ points.
  • Reckless driving also carries criminal consequences that are separate from points.

Does reckless driving raise insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a West Virginia quote.

Your own change could be smaller or larger. It depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the conviction is recorded. A criminal misdemeanor may be weighed more heavily than an ordinary moving violation, but we do not have a sourced figure for that.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer. If you want to know how a conviction might affect your policy, ask your own insurer or agent.

Can a reckless driving charge be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes the outcome changes before a conviction is entered, but no outcome is guaranteed. Because this is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail, many people choose to get advice from a licensed attorney. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice or recommend a specific attorney.

Here are general steps worth taking.

  1. Read the citation and note the charge, the court and any date you must appear.
  2. Contact the clerk of the named court and ask what options exist and what the total amount due would be.
  3. Think about the effects beyond the fine: points, insurance and a criminal record.
  4. Do not ignore the citation.

The outcome you reach can change both the amount owed and what appears on your record. Settle those questions before you pay or enter a plea.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the ticket?

Ignoring a criminal misdemeanor citation can make things worse. Because the charge carries possible jail, a missed court date can bring additional consequences. The court handling your case controls what those are.

Unpaid amounts can also affect your driving privileges. With a points system that leads to suspension at 12+ points, a license problem can stack on top of the original charge.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court clerk before any deadline passes. Ask what is possible and get the answer in writing where you can.

Read the citation closely. It tells you which court has your case and what you are required to do.
