How much does a reckless driving ticket cost in Georgia?

Nationally, reckless driving fines commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500, and the offense is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail (Georgia Department of Driver Services). That range is a national picture, not a Georgia price list.

In Georgia, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Georgia dollar figure because the court handling your case decides it.

The number printed on your citation, or the one the judge sets at your hearing, is the one that counts. Check it before you pay anything.

Because this is a criminal charge rather than a simple fine-only ticket, the total cost is more than the fine. Added fees, possible time in court and the effect on your record all matter.

How is a reckless driving fine set in Georgia?

The fine is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The judge or court handling the citation determines the amount, so it can differ from one court to another.

This is why a national range is only a rough guide. Two drivers cited for similar conduct can see different outcomes depending on the court and the facts of the case.

Georgia statutes are available through Justia. They are the place to read the actual wording of the offense and its penalties.

  • The citation lists the charge and the court that will hear it.
  • The court sets the fine, and any separate state fees are added on top.
  • A misdemeanor charge may require a court appearance rather than a simple mail-in payment.

How many points does reckless driving add to a Georgia license?

The point values we have confirmed are the Georgia speeding scale, which runs from 2 to 6 points by mph over the limit: 2 points for 15–18 over, 3 points for 19–23, 4 points for 24–33 and 6 points for 34 or more (Georgia DDS).

We do not list a separate point figure for reckless driving here. To see what a conviction adds to your record, ask the court or check your driving record with Georgia DDS.

The suspension threshold is 15 points in 24 months. Points from several violations within that window add together, so a reckless driving conviction on top of earlier tickets can push a driver closer to it.

Is there an extra state fee on top of the fine?

Yes, in some cases. Georgia adds a $200 Super Speeder state fee on top of the court fine (Georgia DDS). It is separate from what the court charges and is billed by the state.

The fee is tied to the underlying speeding conduct, so whether it applies to you depends on how your citation is written. Check the citation and your DDS record to see if it was assessed.

Timing matters. If the fee is not paid within 120 days, the license is suspended.

Paying the court fine does not pay the Super Speeder fee. They are two separate bills.

Does a reckless driving ticket raise insurance rates?

Most likely, yes. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Georgia quote.

Reckless driving is a more serious offense than a basic moving violation. Your own increase could be higher or lower than the average, and each insurer decides for itself.

We do not recommend any insurer. If you want to know your actual rate change, ask your current insurer how a conviction would be treated at renewal.

  • The effect usually shows up at your next renewal after a conviction.
  • Rate changes depend on your driving history and your insurer.
  • A conviction can stay on your record and affect pricing for some time.

Can a reckless driving charge be dismissed or reduced?

It can happen, but we cannot predict it. Outcomes depend on the facts, the evidence, the court and the prosecutor. Because the charge is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail, many drivers talk to a licensed Georgia attorney before they appear.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice or recommend a specific attorney. We can only point out the questions worth asking.

  • What exactly does the citation charge, and in which court?
  • Do I have to appear in person, or can the court handle it another way?
  • What would a conviction add to my record, and what fees come with it?
  • Is a Super Speeder fee involved?

Do not assume you can simply pay the ticket. For a criminal charge, paying can mean a conviction, with the record and insurance effects described above.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the ticket?

Ignoring it makes things worse. A criminal citation carries a court date, and missing it can cause further problems that we cannot detail here.

For the state fee, the rule is firm: a $200 Super Speeder fee unpaid after 120 days suspends the license (Georgia DDS).

Points can also lead to suspension. Reaching 15 points in 24 months suspends a license, so a new conviction matters more if you already have points on your record.

If you cannot pay by the due date, contact the court before it passes and ask what options exist.
