How much is a reckless driving ticket in California?

Nationally, reckless driving fines commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500, and it is treated as a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail (California Department of Motor Vehicles). That is a national range, not a California quote.

In California, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. The state's reckless statute carries $145–$1,000 and up to 90 days in county jail.

The number on your citation or court notice is the one that counts. This page gives the statutory range and the national context, not a prediction for your case.

The base fine is only part of the cost. Surcharges and fees, covered below, can multiply what you actually pay.

How is the reckless driving fine set in California?

The court sets the fine within the range in the state's traffic code. The statute itself gives a range of $145–$1,000, so two people convicted of the same offense can receive different amounts.

The exact figure depends on the court handling the case and the facts on the citation. TicketCostGuide does not publish an exact California dollar amount because the court decides it.

You can read the statutory language through California statutes via Justia. Statutes can be amended, so compare the text with your own citation.

Why does the total cost end up so much higher than the fine?

Penalty assessments, county fees and surcharges multiply the base fine roughly 4–5×. This is a state-level rule of thumb, and the actual add-ons vary by county and court.

A base fine at the low end of the range can therefore still come to several times that amount. A base fine near the top of the range grows the same way.

  • Ask the clerk for the total due, not only the base fine.
  • Check whether the notice lists the base fine and the add-ons separately.
  • Confirm the payment deadline printed on the notice.

The statutory range does not tell you the final bill. Only the court's figure does.

Is reckless driving a misdemeanor, and can it mean jail?

Yes. Nationally, reckless driving is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail. California's statute carries up to 90 days in county jail alongside the $145–$1,000 fine range.

The words "up to" matter. Jail is a possible outcome, not an automatic one, and the court decides based on the case.

Because it is a criminal charge, it differs from an ordinary infraction. You may have court appearances and a criminal record entry. For advice on your own situation, speak with a licensed attorney. TicketCostGuide does not give legal advice.

How many points is reckless driving in California?

The locked state facts we hold cover speeding: 1 point for ordinary speeding, and 2 points at 100+ mph. We do not state a point value for reckless driving here because we have no verified figure for it.

What we can state is the suspension threshold. Reaching 4 points in 12 months means a suspension under the Negligent Operator Treatment System.

To see how a conviction is scored, check your driving record with the California Department of Motor Vehicles. Points can matter even when the fine itself is modest.

Does reckless driving raise insurance rates?

Likely, yes. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a California quote.

Reckless driving is more serious than an ordinary moving violation, so your own increase could differ. Insurers set their own rules, and TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer.

If your policy is up for renewal, ask your insurer how a conviction would be treated. Compare that with the cost of the fine and surcharges to see the full picture.

Can a reckless driving ticket be dismissed or reduced?

It can happen, but we cannot predict it. Outcomes depend on the facts, the court and the prosecutor, and the charge is a criminal misdemeanor.

Common questions to raise with the court clerk or a licensed attorney include:

  • What are my options if I want to contest the charge?
  • Are any alternative resolutions available in this court?
  • How would a different outcome affect points and my record?

Compare your options on total cost, not only the base fine. Surcharges, possible jail exposure, points and insurance all count. For a state-by-state view of traffic laws, see the Governors Highway Safety Association.
