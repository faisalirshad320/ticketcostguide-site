How much is a reckless driving ticket in Maryland?

The honest answer is that the Maryland amount is set by the court on your citation. We do not publish a single statewide dollar figure because the locked sources do not give one.

For context, nationally reckless driving fines commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500, and the offense is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail (Maryland Motor Vehicle Administration). That range is a national figure. It is not a Maryland price tag.

Your citation and the court that handles it control the amount you owe. Read the citation closely and confirm with the court listed on it.

Fines are only part of the cost. Points, possible license consequences and higher insurance premiums can add to the total over time.

How is the reckless driving fine set in Maryland?

In Maryland the fine is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The code is available through Maryland statutes via Justia.

Because reckless driving is treated nationally as a criminal misdemeanor rather than a simple infraction, it is usually not handled like a routine ticket. The citation will tell you what the charge is and how to respond.

One locked fact matters here: requesting a court date replaces the prepay amount with a judge-set fine up to $500. In other words, once you ask for a court date, the judge decides the fine instead of a preset prepay figure.

  • The national range of up to $1,000–$2,500 describes fines across the country.
  • The "up to $500" figure describes the judge-set fine after you request a court date.
  • Your own citation is the final word on what applies to you.

These figures describe different things, so do not add them together or treat one as a cap on the other. If you are unsure which applies, ask the court clerk.

How many points is reckless driving in Maryland?

The locked Maryland points schedule is tiered by how far over the limit a driver was: 1 point for 9 or less over, 2 points for 10–29 over, and 5 points for 30+ over.

That schedule is written in speed terms. We do not state a specific point value for reckless driving itself, because it is not given in our locked facts. Check the point value on your citation or with the Maryland Motor Vehicle Administration.

The thresholds are clearer:

  • 8 points = suspension
  • 12 points = revocation

Points from several violations can add up. A reckless driving conviction on top of earlier points could move you closer to one of those thresholds.

Does reckless driving raise insurance in Maryland?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Maryland-specific one.

Your own change could be higher or lower. It depends on your insurer, your driving record and how the case ends.

We do not recommend any insurer. If you want to know how a conviction would affect your rate, ask your insurer directly. You can also ask what a reduced or dismissed charge would mean for your policy.

The 20% figure is an average across the country. Treat it as a rough benchmark, not a quote.

Can a reckless driving ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes outcomes change after a court appearance, but we cannot predict any result for your case. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice.

What we can say from the locked facts is how the process changes your costs. Requesting a court date replaces the prepay amount with a judge-set fine up to $500. That puts the decision in front of a judge.

Because reckless driving is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail, the stakes are higher than for a routine ticket. Think carefully before you pay without reviewing your options.

  • Read the citation for the charge, court and any deadline.
  • Ask the court clerk how to request a court date.
  • Consider speaking with a licensed attorney about your situation. We do not recommend any specific attorney.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore a reckless driving citation?

Ignoring a citation is rarely a good idea. Because reckless driving is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail, a missed court date or unpaid obligation can make a situation worse.

We do not list specific penalties for non-payment, since none are in our locked facts. The court named on your citation and the Maryland Motor Vehicle Administration can tell you what applies to you.

If you cannot pay or cannot attend, contact the court before the deadline on the citation. Courts are generally easier to work with when you reach out early.

What is the full cost beyond the fine?

A reckless driving charge can cost more than the fine alone. Consider these pieces:

  • The fine: set by the court on the citation.
  • Points: accumulating to 8 means suspension and 12 means revocation.
  • Insurance: about 20% higher premiums on average for moving-violation convictions, nationally.
  • Criminal record risk: it is a misdemeanor nationally, with possible jail.

We cannot put a single dollar total on these. The national fine range, the judge-set amount and the insurance effect each measure something different.
