How much is a reckless driving ticket in Minnesota?

Nationally, reckless driving fines commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500, and the offense is treated as a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail. That is a national range, not a Minnesota price tag (Minnesota Department of Public Safety).

In Minnesota, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single Minnesota dollar figure because the amount depends on the court and the case.

Your citation or the court that handles it is the only reliable source for what you owe. A statutory state surcharge is added to every payable fine, so the total can be higher than the base fine alone.

Treat any website that quotes one flat Minnesota price for reckless driving with caution, including ours if we ever did so without a source.

How is the reckless driving fine set in Minnesota?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. It is not a fixed number printed on a public fee list that applies the same way everywhere.

Several things can shape the outcome in practice:

  • The court that hears your case and how it handles the charge.
  • The facts written on the citation.
  • Whether the case is resolved by a plea, a trial or a dismissal.
  • Whether any jail exposure applies, since the offense is a criminal misdemeanor nationally.

You can read Minnesota's traffic provisions in the state code, which is available through Minnesota statutes via Justia. Check the current text, because statutes are amended and a free site may lag behind the official version.

What surcharges and court costs are added?

A statutory state surcharge is added to every payable fine in Minnesota. That means the amount you pay is the fine plus the surcharge, and possibly other costs the court lists.

We do not state a dollar amount for the surcharge or any other court cost, because the locked facts we rely on do not give one. Your citation, the court's website or the court clerk can show the full total.

When you compare a quote or a payment notice, ask whether it includes the surcharge. A figure that leaves it out will understate what you owe.

How many points does reckless driving add in Minnesota?

None, because Minnesota has no point system. License action follows the record of convictions and specific high-speed offenses.

This does not mean a conviction has no license consequences. It means the state looks at your conviction record, not at a running point total. A reckless driving conviction becomes part of that record.

For questions about your own driving record or license status, the Minnesota Department of Public Safety, Driver and Vehicle Services is the agency to ask. A court or a licensed attorney can explain how a particular charge may affect you.

Does reckless driving raise your insurance in Minnesota?

Often, yes. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Minnesota-specific one.

Your own change could be smaller or larger. It depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the conviction is recorded. Reckless driving is a more serious charge than a basic moving violation, so do not assume the average will match your case.

  • Ask your insurer how it treats a conviction like this at renewal.
  • Compare quotes if your premium rises after the case closes.
  • Keep your court paperwork showing how the charge was resolved.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer.

Can a reckless driving charge be dismissed or reduced?

It can happen, but we cannot say how likely it is in your case. Outcomes depend on the facts, the court and the evidence.

Because reckless driving is a criminal misdemeanor nationally, the stakes can include jail, not just a fine. That is one reason many people talk to a licensed attorney before they enter a plea. We do not recommend a specific attorney.

Questions worth raising with the court or an attorney:

  • What exactly does the citation charge, and is the wording correct?
  • Does the court allow a plea to a different charge?
  • How would each possible outcome appear on my record?

This page is general information, not legal advice.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the ticket?

Ignoring a criminal misdemeanor citation is risky. Courts can treat a missed appearance or unpaid fine as a separate problem, and that can make your situation worse.

We do not list specific penalties for non-payment in Minnesota, because they are not part of the figures we sourced. Read the citation for the appearance instructions and deadlines, and contact the court if anything is unclear.

Do not pay a reckless driving citation by default without understanding the consequences. Paying can amount to a conviction, which feeds into your record of convictions and can affect insurance.
