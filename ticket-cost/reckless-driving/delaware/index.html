How much does a reckless driving ticket cost in Delaware?

Nationally, reckless driving fines commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500. It is also treated as a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail. That is a national picture, not a Delaware price tag.

In Delaware, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Delaware Division of Motor Vehicles). We do not publish a single Delaware dollar figure because the locked sources we rely on do not give one.

Bottom line: read the citation you were handed. The court-set amount on that document, plus court costs and state fees, is what you are dealing with.

Court costs and state fees are added to the statutory fine. So the amount you actually pay can be higher than the fine line alone.

How is a Delaware reckless driving fine set?

The fine is set by the court within the range the state's traffic code allows. The statutes are available through Delaware statutes via Justia. The judge or court sets the figure that appears on your citation.

Several things can matter in practice:

  • The facts alleged in the citation, such as speed or conduct.
  • Whether the case is handled as a misdemeanor in court rather than paid by mail.
  • Any prior record you may have.

We cannot say how much weight a court gives each factor. The sources we use do not break that down.

Separately from the fine, court costs and state fees are added. Ask the court clerk for an itemized total before you pay.

Is reckless driving a criminal offense in Delaware?

Reckless driving is generally a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail. That sets it apart from an ordinary speeding ticket, which is usually handled as a traffic matter.

A criminal conviction can carry consequences beyond the fine. These may include a record that shows up on background checks. How much that matters depends on your job, licensing and plans.

Because of that, many people want to understand the charge before paying. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot advise you on your case. A licensed Delaware attorney can answer that for your situation.

How many points does reckless driving add in Delaware?

Delaware's points system, as summarized in our sources, assigns 2–5 points by mph over. That description is written around speed. The sources do not state a separate point value for reckless driving.

So do not assume a number. Confirm with the Delaware Division of Motor Vehicles how points apply to your specific charge and conviction.

The thresholds are clearer:

  • 12 points in 2 years = behavior modification course.
  • 14+ points = suspension.

If you already carry points from earlier tickets, a new conviction could move you closer to those thresholds. Check your driving record before your court date.

Does reckless driving raise insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Delaware-specific one.

Reckless driving is more serious than many moving violations, so the effect on your policy could differ. We cannot say by how much. Insurers set their own rules, and we do not recommend any insurer.

  • Ask your insurer how a conviction would affect your renewal.
  • Ask whether a lesser charge would be treated differently.
  • Compare your options at renewal rather than assuming one answer.

The ~20% figure is only a rough benchmark for planning. Your own quote is the number that counts.

Can a reckless driving ticket be dismissed or reduced?

It can sometimes be, but outcomes depend on the facts, the court and the prosecutor. We cannot promise any result, and nothing here is legal advice.

In general, people facing this kind of charge have a few options:

  • Pay and accept the conviction. This usually resolves the case but may add points and affect insurance.
  • Contest the charge. You would appear in court and the state would have to prove its case.
  • Seek a different resolution. Some courts allow negotiated outcomes. Whether yours does is a question for the court or an attorney.

Because this is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail, talking to a licensed Delaware attorney before you enter any plea is worth considering. We do not recommend a specific attorney.

Read the court date and instructions on your citation carefully. Missing a deadline can limit your choices.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. The sources we use do not list the exact penalties for Delaware, so we will not guess at them.

In general, courts can add consequences when a required appearance or payment is missed. Because reckless driving is a misdemeanor, a missed court date is a bigger risk than with a minor ticket.

  • Check the court date and payment instructions on the citation.
  • Contact the court clerk if you are unsure what is required.
  • Do not rely on a mailed reminder arriving.

Court costs and state fees are added to the fine, so late action could also leave you with a total that is harder to plan for.
