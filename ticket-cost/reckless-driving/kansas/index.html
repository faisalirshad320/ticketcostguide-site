How much does a reckless driving ticket cost in Kansas?

Nationally, reckless driving fines commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500. It is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail. In Kansas, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Kansas Department of Revenue — Division of Vehicles).

We do not publish a single Kansas dollar figure, because none exists. The amount depends on the court that hears your case and the charge written on your citation.

The national range is a benchmark, not a Kansas price. Your citation and the court that issued it control what you owe.

The fine is also not the whole bill. Court costs are added by the municipal or district court, so the total you pay can be higher than the fine alone.

How is a reckless driving fine set in Kansas?

The exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. That means there is no fixed statewide price list for you to look up.

Two things usually shape the number you see:

  • The court handling the case. A municipal court and a district court may handle the same kind of charge differently.
  • The charge itself. The wording on the citation matters, and a charge can change if it is amended in court.

Court costs come on top of the fine. They are added by the municipal or district court, and they differ by court.

The citation or the court clerk is the best place to find the amount that applies to you. The Kansas traffic statutes are available through Kansas statutes via Justia if you want to read the code itself.

Is reckless driving a criminal charge, and can it mean jail?

Nationally, reckless driving is treated as a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail. That sets it apart from an ordinary speeding or equipment ticket, which is usually a civil-style traffic matter.

A criminal charge can carry consequences beyond money. It may show up on a criminal record check, and the court process may be more formal than a routine ticket.

Whether jail is realistic in a particular case depends on the court, the facts and the charge as filed. We cannot predict that, and this page is not legal advice.

If your citation lists a misdemeanor or a court appearance, read it closely. Some people choose to speak with a licensed attorney before deciding what to do. We do not recommend any specific attorney.

How many points is reckless driving in Kansas?

Kansas has no demerit-point system. You will not see a point total added to your license for this violation.

Convictions still count, though. Three moving-violation convictions within 12 months can trigger suspension (Kansas Department of Revenue — Division of Vehicles).

A reckless driving conviction is a moving-violation conviction, so it can add to that count. If you already have other recent convictions, the timing matters.

  • Check how many moving-violation convictions you have in the last 12 months.
  • Ask the court how a plea or conviction would be recorded.
  • Contact the Division of Vehicles about your driving record status if you are unsure.

Does a reckless driving ticket raise insurance in Kansas?

It can. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Kansas-specific one.

Your own change could be smaller or larger. Insurers price risk their own way, and a reckless driving conviction is more serious than many moving violations.

The increase usually applies at renewal and can last for several policy periods. We do not state a specific length, because it varies by insurer.

We do not recommend insurers. If you want to understand your exposure, ask your own insurer how a conviction would be treated, or compare quotes with the charge described accurately.

Can a reckless driving ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but it depends on the court, the prosecutor and the facts. We cannot promise any result.

Common paths people ask about include:

  • Contesting the charge at a hearing.
  • Asking the prosecutor about an amended charge.
  • Asking whether the court offers any diversion or similar program.

None of these is guaranteed, and availability varies by court. A reduced charge can also change the cost, since the fine is set by the court on the citation.

Because reckless driving is a criminal matter, the stakes are higher than for a routine ticket. A licensed attorney can explain the options for your situation. This page is general information only.

Do not assume paying the ticket is a neutral choice. Paying or pleading can mean a conviction, which counts toward the three-in-12-months suspension threshold.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore a reckless driving ticket?

Ignoring a criminal citation is risky. Because the exact penalty is set by the court, the court is also the one that decides what follows if you miss a date.

Outcomes courts commonly use for missed appearances or unpaid amounts include added costs, a warrant, or a hold on your license. Which of these applies depends on the court.

A license suspension can also come from convictions. Three moving-violation convictions within 12 months can trigger suspension in Kansas.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court clerk before the due date. Ask what options the court offers. Courts generally respond better to people who contact them early.
