How much is a reckless driving ticket in Rhode Island?

Nationally, fines for reckless driving commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500. It is also treated as a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail. In Rhode Island, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Rhode Island Division of Motor Vehicles).

We do not publish a single Rhode Island dollar figure for this offense. The national range is a benchmark only, and your own number depends on the court and the facts of your case.

Treat the national range as context, not a quote. The amount that applies to you is the one the court sets on your citation.

Reckless driving is a different category from an ordinary speeding ticket. Expect it to be handled as a more serious matter than a routine moving violation.

How is the reckless driving fine set in Rhode Island?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. There is no flat number that applies to every driver. The traffic code sets the framework, and the court sets the amount on the citation.

Several things can shape the outcome in practice:

  • The charge as written on your citation
  • The circumstances described in the police report
  • Your prior driving record, since repeat offenses draw escalating sanctions
  • Any outcome reached through the court process

The statutes are published online through Rhode Island statutes via Justia. Read the exact statute cited on your ticket, because the wording controls. If you want advice on your specific charge, that is a question for a licensed attorney, which this site cannot provide.

What court costs and fees are added to the fine?

Court technology and assessment fees are added to the scheduled fine. This means the total you owe can be higher than the fine line alone.

We do not list dollar amounts for those fees, because they are not part of our locked figures. Look at the total due on your citation or court notice rather than adding numbers from memory or from other states.

  • Fine: set by the court on the citation
  • Court technology fee: added to the scheduled fine
  • Assessment fee: added to the scheduled fine

When you budget for the ticket, plan for the total, not just the fine.

How many points does reckless driving add in Rhode Island?

Rhode Island has no running point system for ordinary speeding. Instead, the Traffic Tribunal escalates sanctions, including suspension, for repeat offenses.

That is the only point-related fact we publish here, and it is stated for ordinary speeding. We do not state a specific point value for reckless driving. Do not assume a point count from another state.

The practical takeaway is that your history matters. A first offense and a later offense can be treated very differently. For license status questions, check with the Rhode Island Division of Motor Vehicles.

Does a reckless driving ticket raise insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Rhode Island quote.

Reckless driving is a more serious offense than a routine moving violation, so insurers may weigh it heavily. We cannot predict what any specific insurer will do, and we do not recommend insurers.

The ~20% figure is an average across the country. Your own change could be lower or higher depending on your insurer, your record and how the case ends.

If you want to know your exposure, ask your insurer how a conviction would be rated before the case concludes. Also keep in mind that the fine is a one-time cost, while a premium increase can last for as long as the insurer counts the conviction.

Can a reckless driving ticket be dismissed or reduced?

It can be possible, but the outcome depends on the court and on the facts. We do not predict results, and nothing here is legal advice.

Because reckless driving can be a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail, the stakes are higher than for a basic traffic infraction. Many people choose to speak with a licensed attorney before deciding how to respond. We do not recommend a specific attorney.

Steps that are generally sensible:

  1. Read the citation carefully, including the statute cited and any court date.
  2. Note every deadline and where you are told to appear.
  3. Gather any documents, photos or records related to the stop.
  4. Ask the court clerk about the process, since clerks can explain procedure but not give legal advice.
  5. Decide on your response before the deadline passes.

A reduced charge could change the fine, the fees and how an insurer views the record. The details of any reduction are set by the court.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore a reckless driving ticket?

Ignoring a citation is risky. Because reckless driving can be a criminal matter, missing a court date can make the situation worse than the original charge.

In Rhode Island, the Traffic Tribunal escalates sanctions, including suspension, for repeat offenses. Unresolved tickets can also add to your record, which is the basis for that escalation.

We do not state specific late fees or deadlines, because those are not in our locked facts. Your citation and the court notice list the dates and amounts that apply to you. If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court before the due date to ask what is available.
