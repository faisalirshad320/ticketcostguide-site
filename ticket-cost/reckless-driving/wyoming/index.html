How much does a reckless driving ticket cost in Wyoming?

The lead figure is a national one: nationally fines commonly up to $1,000–$2,500; a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail; in Wyoming the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Wyoming Department of Transportation). We do not print a single Wyoming dollar amount, because the court decides it.

Treat the national range as background, not a prediction. Your own number depends on the court handling the case and the facts behind the citation.

Court fees are also added per the local schedule. The total on your paperwork can therefore be higher than the fine alone, and it can differ from one court to another.

Reckless driving is not a routine speeding-style ticket. It is treated as a criminal misdemeanor, so the cost is more than money.

How is the reckless driving fine set in Wyoming?

In Wyoming the exact amount is set by the court on the citation, within the state's traffic code. There is no fixed price list we can point to that applies the same way everywhere.

That is why two people cited for the same offense can see different totals. The court, the local fee schedule and the details of the case all play a part.

You can read the statutes yourself through Wyoming statutes via Justia. The Wyoming Department of Transportation site is the starting point for driver-record and licensing questions (Wyoming Department of Transportation).

When you look at your citation, check for these items:

  • The fine amount, if one is printed.
  • Any line for court fees or costs.
  • The court name and its date or deadline.
  • Whether a court appearance is listed as required.

If anything is unclear, the court clerk's office can explain what the paperwork means. The clerk cannot give legal advice, but can explain procedure.

How many points does reckless driving add in Wyoming?

None, because Wyoming has no demerit-point system. Suspensions follow repeat convictions and court action instead.

That does not make a conviction harmless. A conviction is still a court record, and repeat convictions can lead to a suspension. Court action in your case can also affect your license.

If you are used to point systems in other states, do not assume the same rules apply here. There is no point total to count down or watch.

For how convictions are recorded on a driving record and how licensing works, start with the Wyoming Department of Transportation. For a state-by-state view of traffic laws, the Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a reference page.

Does a reckless driving ticket raise insurance rates?

It often does. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums ~20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Wyoming-specific one.

Your own change could be smaller or larger. It depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the violation is recorded.

We do not recommend any insurer. If you are worried about a renewal, you can ask your own insurer how a conviction would be treated before you decide how to handle the case.

The ~20% figure is an average across the US. Use it as a rough planning guide, not a quote.

Remember that the insurance effect comes on top of the fine and court fees. Over time it can be a significant part of the total cost.

Is reckless driving a criminal charge in Wyoming?

Yes. The locked summary describes it as a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail (Wyoming Department of Transportation). That sets it apart from a standard civil-style traffic infraction.

Possible jail does not mean jail is certain. The outcome is up to the court and depends on the case.

Because it is criminal, a conviction can have effects beyond the fine. It becomes part of your record, and a repeat conviction can lead to a suspension.

Because the stakes are higher than with a minor ticket, many people want to understand the charge before they respond. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot advise you on your case.

Can a reckless driving ticket be dismissed or reduced?

It can happen in some cases, but we cannot promise it. Whether a charge is dismissed or changed is decided by the court and the prosecutor, case by case.

The general options people have are:

  1. Pay or resolve the citation as directed, if the court allows it.
  2. Appear and contest the charge.
  3. Ask the court clerk about the process and any deadlines.
  4. Speak with a licensed attorney of your own choosing about the facts of your case.

We do not recommend a specific attorney. Because this is a criminal misdemeanor, it is worth understanding the consequences before you decide how to respond.

Resolving the citation by simply paying may have its own consequences for your record. Read the citation closely before acting.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the citation?

Ignoring a citation is risky, particularly for a criminal misdemeanor. The court can take further action when a deadline or court date is missed.

Because suspensions follow repeat convictions and court action in Wyoming, an unresolved case can lead to bigger problems than the original citation. Court fees are also added per the local schedule, so delays can complicate the total.

We cannot say exactly what a particular court will do. The safest step is to read the date and instructions on the citation and contact the court clerk before the deadline.

If you cannot tell what the citation requires, call the court named on it. Do not wait for the date to pass.
