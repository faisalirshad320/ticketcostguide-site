How much does a reckless driving ticket cost in New Jersey?

Nationally, fines for reckless driving commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500. It is treated as a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail (New Jersey Motor Vehicle Commission).

In New Jersey the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single New Jersey dollar figure, because the amount depends on the court and the facts of the case.

The $1,000–$2,500 range is a national figure. Your own citation and the court that hears it control what you actually owe.

The fine is only part of the cost. Points, an MVC surcharge and higher insurance premiums can add to the total over several years. The sections below cover each one.

How is the reckless driving fine set in New Jersey?

The court sets the amount within the state's traffic code. The code gives the court a range to work within. Where you end up in that range is the court's decision.

You can read the New Jersey statutes through New Jersey statutes via Justia. For how the state describes the offense and its penalties, see the New Jersey Motor Vehicle Commission.

Because the amount is set by the court, it can differ between courts. Do not rely on a number from a different county or a different state.

  • Look at the citation first. It names the charge and the court.
  • Check the court's instructions for the amount due, if any.
  • If a court appearance is required, expect the amount to be decided there.

How many points is reckless driving in New Jersey?

The New Jersey points system lists tiers by how far over the limit a driver was: 2 points (1–14 over), 4 points (15–29 over) and 5 points (30+ over). Those tiers are the points figures we can source here.

The locked tiers are written for speed bands. They do not state a separate point value for a reckless driving conviction, so we do not assign one. Confirm the points for your specific charge with the MVC or the court.

Points matter because 12 points = suspension. Points from separate violations add up on your driving record.

Check your citation and your MVC driver record before assuming a point total. The charge as finally resolved is what counts.

What is the MVC surcharge for points?

New Jersey adds an MVC insurance surcharge when a driver has 6+ points within 3 years. The surcharge starts at $150+.

MVC surcharges at 6+ points are billed annually for 3 years on top of court fines. So a ticket that pushes you past that point threshold costs more than the court fine alone.

  • The surcharge is separate from the court fine.
  • It is billed once a year for 3 years.
  • The $150+ figure is a starting amount, not a cap.

Details on how the MVC bills surcharges are on the New Jersey Motor Vehicle Commission site.

Does a reckless driving ticket raise insurance in New Jersey?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. This is a national figure, not a New Jersey-specific one.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your record and how the case ends. Some drivers see a larger increase and some see less. We cannot predict yours, and we do not recommend any insurer.

Two things can stack. The insurer may raise your rate, and the MVC surcharge may apply if you reach 6+ points within 3 years.

If you are worried about your rate, ask your insurer how it treats this type of conviction. Do that before you decide how to handle the ticket.

Can a reckless driving ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes the court allows other outcomes, but that is up to the court and the prosecutor. We cannot say whether one is available in your case.

Because reckless driving is treated nationally as a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail, the stakes can be higher than for a basic traffic ticket. That is why many people talk to a licensed New Jersey attorney before the court date. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not recommend a specific attorney.

  • Read the citation for the court date and the exact charge.
  • Ask the court what options exist for your charge.
  • Learn how the final outcome affects points, the surcharge and insurance before you agree to anything.

The same charge can cost very different amounts depending on how it ends. Compare the full cost, not just the fine.

What happens if I ignore a reckless driving ticket?

Do not ignore it. Because reckless driving is treated nationally as a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail, missing a court date can make things worse. We do not list specific penalties for missed dates here.

If the ticket requires a court appearance, paying without appearing may not be an option. Read the citation closely and contact the court named on it.

Unresolved violations can also affect your driving record and license status. Remember that 12 points = suspension.

For license questions, start with the New Jersey Motor Vehicle Commission. For legal questions about your case, speak with a licensed attorney.
