How much does a reckless driving ticket cost in Mississippi?

Nationally, reckless driving fines commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500. It is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail. In Mississippi, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Mississippi Department of Public Safety).

We do not publish a single Mississippi dollar figure, because the court decides it and it can differ by county and by court. The national range is a reference point only. It is not a promise of what your court will impose.

The fine is only part of the bill. Mandatory state assessments are added to every fine and often exceed it, so the amount you owe can be well above the fine itself.

The best source for your number is the citation, or the clerk of the court named on it.

How is the reckless driving fine set in Mississippi?

The court sets the fine within the state's traffic code. The officer who writes the ticket does not decide the final amount. A judge or court official does, based on the charge and the outcome of your case.

Mississippi statutes are published online, and you can read the traffic code through Mississippi statutes via Justia. The code sets the framework, and the court applies it to your case.

Several things can affect what ends up on your paperwork:

  • The court that hears the case, which may be a county or municipal court.
  • Whether the charge stays as written or is changed.
  • Your driving history.
  • The mandatory state assessments added on top of the fine.

If your citation shows an amount, check whether it includes the assessments or only the fine.

Is reckless driving a criminal charge in Mississippi?

Nationally, reckless driving is treated as a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail. That makes it different from an ordinary speeding ticket, which is usually a civil or minor traffic matter.

A criminal conviction can show up on a background check. It can also carry consequences beyond the fine, such as a record that employers or licensing bodies may see.

Read the citation closely. It will name the offense, the court and the date you must appear or respond. If the charge is unclear, the statutes at Mississippi statutes via Justia are the place to check the wording.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot tell you how your own case will be treated.

How many points does reckless driving add in Mississippi?

Mississippi has no point system. That means there is no point total that builds toward a suspension the way it does in many other states.

License suspensions in Mississippi instead follow repeat convictions and specific offenses. A reckless driving conviction can therefore matter, especially if it is not your first.

No points does not mean no license risk. Repeat convictions and certain offenses can still lead to suspension.

For the current rules on your driving record and license status, contact the Mississippi Department of Public Safety. The Governors Highway Safety Association also keeps a summary of state traffic laws at GHSA State Laws.

Does reckless driving raise your insurance in Mississippi?

It often does. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Mississippi-specific one.

Your own change could be higher or lower. Insurers set rates using their own formulas, and a reckless driving conviction is usually viewed as more serious than a minor violation.

The increase can last for several renewals, so the long-run cost may exceed the fine. We cannot predict what your insurer will do, and we do not recommend any insurer.

To get a real answer, ask your insurer how a reckless driving conviction would be treated at renewal.

Can a reckless driving ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes charges are dismissed or changed to a lesser offense. Whether that is possible depends on the facts, the court and the prosecutor. We cannot say what is available in your county.

Common steps people consider include:

  • Reading the citation for the court, date and response instructions.
  • Contacting the court clerk to ask how to respond.
  • Asking the court whether any alternative resolution exists.
  • Speaking with a licensed Mississippi attorney about a criminal misdemeanor charge.

Because the charge is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail, many people seek legal advice before they plead. TicketCostGuide does not give legal advice and does not recommend a specific attorney.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore a reckless driving ticket?

Ignoring a criminal citation is risky. Courts can treat a missed appearance or unpaid fine as a separate problem, and that can make the situation worse than the original charge.

Possible consequences vary by court, but may include added costs, a warrant, or license action. Because mandatory state assessments are added to every fine, delay does not make the bill smaller.

If you cannot pay the full amount, or you cannot attend on the date given, contact the court clerk before the deadline on the citation. Courts are generally easier to work with before a deadline passes than after.

For license questions after a conviction, check with the Mississippi Department of Public Safety.
