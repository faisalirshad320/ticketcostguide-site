How much is a reckless driving ticket in Virginia?

Nationally, reckless driving fines commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500, and the offense is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail (Virginia Department of Motor Vehicles). That range is a national figure. In Virginia, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation.

Virginia classifies reckless driving, including 20+ over or 86+ mph, as a Class 1 misdemeanor. The fine can be up to $2,500, and jail is possible.

This page does not give one fixed Virginia fine, because there isn't one. The court decides the amount within the state's traffic code, so check your citation and court paperwork for your own figure.

Fines are only part of the cost. Court-set fees, any insurance increase and the effect of demerit points can add to the total over time.

How is the reckless driving fine set in Virginia?

A judge sets the fine in court, within the limits of Virginia's traffic code (Virginia statutes via Justia). It is not a fixed amount from a printed schedule. Two drivers with the same charge can end up with different totals.

Several things can differ from one case to the next:

  • The specific charge and the facts behind it.
  • The court that hears the case.
  • Any fees the court adds on top of the fine.
  • The outcome of the case, whether a conviction, a reduced charge or a dismissal.

Because reckless driving is a misdemeanor, it is handled as a criminal case. It is not a simple prepaid ticket. The citation will say what the charge is and what the next step is.

What counts as reckless driving in Virginia?

Virginia's traffic code defines the offense, and the statutes are published online (Virginia statutes via Justia). The locked facts for this page confirm that reckless driving includes 20+ over or 86+ mph.

In other words, a speed-related stop can be charged as a criminal offense, not just an ordinary speeding ticket. That changes the cost, the court process and the consequences.

The Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a state-by-state summary of traffic safety laws if you want to compare how other states treat similar conduct (Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws).

Read the charge on your citation carefully. The exact wording and the code section listed matter more than the label people use for it.

What court costs and fees come on top of the fine?

Fixed processing costs apply to every prepaid ticket. Reckless cases add court-set fines and fees.

In practice, the number on your citation or court notice is the one to rely on. This page does not list exact fee amounts, because they depend on the court and the case.

  • Fine: set by the court, with Virginia's Class 1 misdemeanor limit of up to $2,500.
  • Fees: added by the court on reckless cases.
  • Processing costs: fixed amounts that apply to prepaid tickets.

If anything on a court bill is unclear, the clerk's office for your court is the right place to ask for an itemized total.

How many points is reckless driving in Virginia?

Virginia's point system assigns 3–6 demerit points by offense, and reckless driving carries 6 (Virginia Department of Motor Vehicles). That is the top of the range.

The DMV suspends licenses at point thresholds. Drivers earn +1 safe point per clean year, which helps offset demerit points over time.

Points are separate from the fine. You can have a court-set fine, a point entry on your driving record and an insurance change all from one case.

Check your own record with the Virginia DMV to see your current point balance. This page does not state suspension thresholds.

Does reckless driving raise insurance rates?

Often, yes. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Virginia-specific one.

Your own change could be smaller or larger. It depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the violation is recorded. A criminal misdemeanor conviction is more serious than a minor ticket, so the effect may be greater.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend insurers. If you want to know how a conviction would affect your policy, ask your insurer or an independent agent directly.

Over a few years, the insurance change can cost more than the fine itself, so include it when you estimate the total cost.

Can a reckless driving charge be dismissed or reduced?

It can be possible, but it depends on the facts and the court. This page cannot say what outcome is likely in your case. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice.

Because reckless driving is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail, many people choose to get advice from a licensed Virginia attorney before their court date. We do not recommend any specific attorney.

Things to do before court:

  • Read the citation for the charge, court name and date.
  • Do not ignore the court date. A missed appearance can make things worse.
  • Gather any records that relate to the stop.
  • Ask the clerk what the court accepts and what the process is.

A different outcome could change the fine, the points and the insurance effect, so the result matters for the total cost.

What happens if I don't pay or don't show up?

Reckless driving is a criminal charge, so ignoring it carries more risk than ignoring a minor ticket. The court can add consequences when a case is left unresolved, and the DMV can take action on a driving record.

The exact steps depend on the court. Look at your citation or court notice and contact the clerk if you are unsure what is due and when.

If you cannot pay the full amount, ask the court what options it offers before the deadline passes. Courts differ, so do not assume one court's practice applies to another.
