How much is a reckless driving ticket in South Carolina?

Nationally, reckless driving fines commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500, and the offense is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail (South Carolina Department of Motor Vehicles). That is a national range, not a South Carolina price tag.

In South Carolina the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single statewide dollar figure because the court, not a fixed schedule, decides it.

Your citation and any court notice are the only reliable sources for your amount. Treat the national range as a rough ceiling for the fine alone, and remember that surcharges are added on top.

The fine is only one part of the total. Court costs, surcharges, points and insurance can each add to the real cost.

Who decides the fine, and how is it set?

A reckless driving charge is handled as a criminal matter, not a simple civil infraction. That is why a court, rather than a fixed roadside payment, sets the outcome.

The exact fine falls within the limits of the state's traffic code, which you can read through South Carolina statutes via Justia. The figure on your citation or in the court's order controls what you owe.

Several things can influence where a court lands:

  • The facts of the stop and how the driving is described
  • Your driving record
  • Whether anyone was hurt or property was damaged
  • The practices of the particular court that hears the case

These are general factors, not a formula. We cannot tell you how a specific judge will rule.

What court costs and surcharges are added to the fine?

Statewide assessments and surcharges are added to every fine in South Carolina, and they usually exceed the fine itself. A ticket's total can therefore be well above the base fine.

We do not list individual assessment amounts, because the exact total depends on the fine the court sets and on how the assessments are applied to it. The court or clerk can give you the full amount due.

  • Ask the clerk for the total, not only the fine.
  • Check that the total matches what is printed on your paperwork.
  • Keep your receipt after paying.

When you compare costs, always compare totals, since the national fine range does not include these additions.

How many points does reckless driving add in South Carolina?

South Carolina's points system runs from 2–6 points, scaled by mph over for speed-related violations. A suspension is triggered at 12 points, and points halve after 12 months (South Carolina Department of Motor Vehicles).

The sourced facts for this page do not give a reckless-driving-specific point value. Confirm the points that apply to your conviction with the SCDMV or your court paperwork rather than relying on a general figure.

Points matter most when they add up. A reckless driving conviction on top of earlier violations can move you closer to the 12-point suspension threshold.

Since points halve after 12 months, the timing of violations matters. Check your own record with the SCDMV to see where you stand.

Does reckless driving raise car insurance?

Usually, yes. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a South Carolina rate.

Reckless driving is a criminal misdemeanor, so insurers may view it more seriously than an ordinary moving violation. The sourced facts do not quantify that difference, so we will not guess at a number.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your history and how the conviction is recorded. Ask your insurer how it treats this type of conviction, and compare quotes at renewal.

Over several years, a premium increase can cost more than the fine. That makes the outcome of the case, not just the fine, worth thinking about.

Can a reckless driving ticket be dismissed or reduced?

It can be possible, but we cannot say what applies to your case. Reckless driving is a criminal charge, so it moves through the court process instead of being paid like a basic ticket.

Common paths people look into include:

  • Contesting the charge at a hearing or trial
  • Negotiating with the prosecutor on the charge
  • Asking the court about any eligible program or alternative

Whether any of these are open to you depends on the court and the facts. Because the charge carries possible jail, many people choose to speak with a licensed attorney before deciding. TicketCostGuide does not give legal advice or recommend any attorney.

Do not pay the citation until you understand what paying means. Paying can be treated as a conviction, which can bring points and an insurance increase.

What happens if I don't pay or don't show up?

Ignoring a reckless driving citation generally makes things worse. Because it is a criminal matter, missing a court date can lead to further penalties, which may include a warrant or action against your license. The exact steps depend on the court.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the clerk before the due date. Courts often have set processes for payment questions, though we cannot confirm what your court offers.

  • Read the court date and instructions on the citation.
  • Contact the clerk early if you have a conflict or cannot pay.
  • Keep copies of anything you send or receive.

For license questions, check your record and status directly with the South Carolina Department of Motor Vehicles.
