How much is a reckless driving ticket in Oregon?

There is no single price for reckless driving in Oregon. Nationally, fines commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500, and it is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail. In Oregon the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Oregon DMV (ODOT)).

That national range is context, not a promise about what an Oregon court will order. This page does not state an exact Oregon dollar figure, because the amount depends on the court and the facts of the case.

Your citation and any court notice are the only reliable source for what you owe. If the paperwork does not show an amount, ask the court clerk before you pay or plan a budget.

Remember that the fine is only one part of the cost. A criminal conviction can also bring court costs, possible jail, license consequences and higher insurance premiums.

How is the reckless driving fine set in Oregon?

The exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. A judge or the court's process determines the amount, which is why two drivers cited for the same offense can end up with different totals.

Some enhancements are written directly into statute. Zone doubling and the 100+ mph enhancements are set in the law itself, so they are not left to a court's discretion in the same way. You can read the Oregon statutes through Oregon statutes via Justia.

Factors that commonly matter in any state include:

  • The circumstances described in the citation
  • Whether a statutory enhancement, such as a zone or a very high speed, applies
  • Your driving and criminal history
  • Whether anyone was hurt or property was damaged

This list describes general considerations, not a formula. Oregon's total will appear on the citation or in the court's order.

Is reckless driving a crime in Oregon?

Reckless driving is treated as a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail. That puts it in a different category from an ordinary speeding or lane-change ticket, which is usually a civil traffic matter.

The practical differences can be significant:

  • A conviction creates a criminal record, which can show up on background checks.
  • Jail is a possible outcome, though whether it is imposed is up to the court.
  • Court appearances may be required rather than optional.

Because of those stakes, many people consider speaking with a licensed attorney before deciding how to respond. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot give legal advice or recommend a specific attorney.

How many points does reckless driving add in Oregon?

None, because Oregon has no point system. That does not mean a conviction is harmless to your license.

Instead of counting points, Oregon ties suspensions to repeat convictions, such as under habitual-offender rules, and to specific offenses like driving 100+ mph. The Oregon DMV explains driver records and license actions at Oregon DMV (ODOT).

No points does not equal no consequences. A reckless driving conviction on your record can count toward repeat-offense rules later.

If you are comparing Oregon with other states, note that many states do assign points. For a cross-state view of traffic laws, see the Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws page.

Does reckless driving raise my insurance in Oregon?

It usually does. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Oregon-specific one, and your actual change depends on your insurer, your record and how the conviction is classified.

Reckless driving is more serious than a routine moving violation, so a larger effect than the average is possible. We cannot give a precise figure, and we do not recommend any insurer.

Ways to understand your own exposure:

  • Ask your insurer how a criminal driving conviction is rated at renewal.
  • Compare the increase with the fine; over several years, the premium change can exceed the fine itself.
  • Keep in mind that premium changes often appear at your next renewal, not immediately.

Can a reckless driving charge be dismissed or reduced in Oregon?

It can be possible, but outcomes are decided by the prosecutor and the court, and the sources used here do not list specific programs or success rates. We will not guess at them.

Common paths drivers ask about include contesting the charge at trial, negotiating a lesser offense, or asking about diversion options. Whether any of these is available depends on the court and your history.

Because reckless driving is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail, the choice between pleading and contesting carries real consequences. A licensed attorney can explain the options that apply to your case. Read the citation closely for deadlines, since missing one can limit what you can do.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the citation?

Ignoring a criminal citation is risky. Because this is a misdemeanor rather than a simple infraction, you may be required to appear in court, and failing to appear can create additional problems on top of the original charge.

Unpaid amounts and missed court dates can also affect your license. The Oregon DMV is the place to check your driving status and any holds, at Oregon DMV (ODOT).

If you cannot pay the amount in full, contact the court clerk before the due date and ask what options exist. Do not assume a payment plan or extension is available until the court confirms it in writing.
