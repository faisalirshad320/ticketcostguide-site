How much does a reckless driving ticket cost in Michigan?

Nationally, reckless driving fines commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500, and the offense is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail. In Michigan, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Michigan Secretary of State).

That national range is a guide to how seriously states treat this offense. It is not a quote for a Michigan case, and we do not publish a single Michigan dollar figure because the court decides it.

The number that matters is the one printed on your citation or given by the court. Anything else, including this page, is context.

The fine is also only part of the bill. District court costs vary by court and make up much of the total, so two people with similar tickets can owe different amounts.

How is the fine set for reckless driving in Michigan?

The fine is set by the court within the state's traffic code. It is not a fixed number you can look up on a universal schedule the way some minor infractions are.

Several things can shape the outcome, though the sources we use do not assign dollar values to them:

  • The court that hears the case, since district court costs vary by court.
  • The facts of the stop and what the citation actually charges.
  • Whether the case ends in a conviction, a plea to another charge, or a dismissal.

Because reckless driving is treated as a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail, a judge has more discretion than with a basic civil infraction. That is why the total can differ from one courtroom to another.

Read the citation closely. It identifies the charge, the court and the next step. If something is unclear, the court clerk can explain the process, though clerks cannot give legal advice.

Why do court costs matter so much?

In Michigan, district court costs vary by court and make up much of the total. A person who looks only at the fine can underestimate what they will owe.

When you plan for the cost, ask the court for the full amount due, not just the fine. Ask what is included, and whether payment options exist.

  • Fine: set by the court on the citation.
  • Court costs: vary by court.
  • Other costs: insurance changes and any time lost from work or driving.

We do not list a Michigan dollar amount for court costs because the locked sources do not provide one. Any specific number you see elsewhere should be checked against your own court paperwork.

How many points does reckless driving add in Michigan?

Michigan's points system assigns 2–4 points by mph over under §257.320a. The same locked source sets the key threshold: 12 points in 2 years means driver reexamination (Michigan statutes via Justia).

The mph-based scale is written around speed. Because reckless driving charges do not always involve a measured speed, you should confirm how your specific conviction will be scored. Your Michigan driving record, available through the Michigan Secretary of State, is the place to check what was actually recorded.

Points matter most when they stack up. A reckless driving conviction on top of other recent violations can push a driver toward the 12-point threshold within 2 years.

Reexamination does not automatically mean losing a license, but it adds a process and a risk. The Secretary of State handles it, not the district court.

Does reckless driving raise my insurance in Michigan?

It often does. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Michigan quote.

Your own change could be smaller or larger. Insurers weigh your record, your age, your claims history and their own underwriting rules. A criminal misdemeanor conviction may be viewed more seriously than a minor ticket, but we have no locked figure to say by how much.

  • Ask your insurer how a conviction would affect your renewal.
  • Compare quotes before and after, if you can.
  • Remember that the increase can last beyond the year of the ticket.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer. Over several years, the insurance impact can exceed the fine, which is why it belongs in any cost estimate.

Can a reckless driving ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes. Because the charge is a criminal misdemeanor, the case can end in several ways, including a conviction, a reduced charge or a dismissal. Which one applies depends on the facts and the court.

Many people in this position speak with a licensed Michigan attorney before deciding how to respond. We cannot give legal advice or recommend a specific attorney, and a lawyer is not required for every case.

  • Read the citation for the court name and any instructions.
  • Do not ignore the case, even if you plan to contest it.
  • Ask the court about your options and what each could mean for fines, costs and points.

A reduced charge could change the points and the insurance effect, but we have no locked figures for either outcome. Confirm details with the court and the Secretary of State.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore a reckless driving ticket?

Ignoring a criminal misdemeanor citation can make things worse. Courts can add penalties, and the matter can affect your driving record and license status. The specific consequences are set by the court and Michigan law, and we do not have locked dollar figures for them.

If you cannot pay the full amount at once, contact the court before the deadline on your paperwork. Courts often have processes for payment questions, though availability varies by court.

With a possible jail component, a missed court date is a bigger risk than an unpaid civil ticket. If you are unsure of your date, call the court clerk right away.
