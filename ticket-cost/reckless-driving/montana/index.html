How much is a reckless driving ticket in Montana?

Nationally, reckless driving fines commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500. It is treated as a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail, so it is a more serious charge than an ordinary speeding ticket (Montana Department of Justice — Motor Vehicle Division).

That range is a national figure. In Montana, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation, and we do not publish a single Montana dollar amount because it is not fixed in advance.

Your own citation, or the court clerk handling your case, is the only place to confirm what you owe. Any other number you see online is an estimate.

Court surcharges are added to the statutory fine, so the total on your paperwork can be higher than the fine alone. Ask the court for the full amount due, not just the base fine.

How is a Montana reckless driving fine set?

The fine for this violation falls within the state's traffic code, and the court sets the exact amount on the citation. The charge is a criminal misdemeanor, so a judge or court may have a say in the outcome rather than a simple fixed payment schedule.

Several things can matter in practice, though we cannot give Montana-specific dollar effects for any of them:

  • The facts described by the officer on the citation.
  • Whether the court treats the case as a first matter or part of a pattern.
  • Whether the case ends in a conviction, a reduction, or a dismissal.
  • Any surcharges the court adds on top of the fine.

The state traffic code is published through Montana statutes via Justia. Reading the current text there is the best way to see how the offense is defined.

How many points does reckless driving add in Montana?

Convictions carry conviction points toward Montana's habitual-offender threshold. That threshold is 30 points in 3 years, which results in revocation (Montana Department of Justice — Motor Vehicle Division).

The point value assigned to reckless driving specifically is not part of the figures we have confirmed, so we do not state one here. Check your driving record or ask the Motor Vehicle Division how a conviction would be counted.

Points follow a conviction, not just a citation. If a charge is dismissed or reduced to a different offense, the points that result may be different.

Keep in mind that points are separate from any jail exposure or fine. A reckless driving case can affect your record in more than one way.

Does reckless driving raise insurance rates in Montana?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Montana-specific one, and it covers moving violations in general.

A reckless driving conviction is more serious than many moving violations, so the real effect on your policy could be larger or smaller than the average. We have no sourced figure for that, and we will not guess.

What decides your rate is up to your insurer. Factors can include your driving history, how long you have been insured, and the company's own underwriting rules.

  • Ask your insurer how a conviction would be treated before you decide how to handle the case.
  • Compare your renewal quote after the case ends.
  • Note that a dismissal or reduction may be treated differently from a conviction.

Can a reckless driving charge be dismissed or reduced in Montana?

Because reckless driving is a criminal misdemeanor, the outcome is decided in court. Possible results include a conviction, a reduction to a lesser offense, or a dismissal. We cannot say how often each happens in Montana.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice. If jail is possible, many people choose to speak with a licensed Montana attorney before entering a plea. We do not recommend any specific attorney.

Things worth doing before your court date:

  1. Read the citation closely for the charge, court name, and date.
  2. Contact the court clerk to confirm how to respond and what the total amount would be.
  3. Gather any records that relate to the stop.
  4. Do not ignore the deadline on the citation.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore a reckless driving ticket?

Ignoring a criminal misdemeanor citation is risky. The court is likely to treat a missed appearance or unpaid amount as a further problem, and the consequences can build on the original charge. We do not have sourced Montana figures on late penalties, so we do not list any.

Court surcharges are added to the statutory fine, so the amount owed can be higher than the base fine on its own. Ask the clerk how and when payment is due.

If you cannot pay the full amount, or you disagree with the charge, contact the court before the deadline. Courts can usually explain what options exist, even if they cannot give legal advice.

Unresolved cases can also affect your driving privilege. Montana's habitual-offender rule (30 points in 3 years = revocation) shows how seriously the state treats accumulated convictions.
