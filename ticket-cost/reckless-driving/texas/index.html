How much is a reckless driving ticket in Texas?

Nationally, fines for reckless driving commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500, and the offense is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail. In Texas, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Texas Department of Public Safety).

That national range is a general benchmark, not a Texas price. We do not publish a single Texas dollar figure because the court handling your case decides the amount.

The number printed on your citation or court notice is the number that matters. Treat any other figure, including the national range above, as context only.

Reckless driving is not a routine speeding-style ticket. Because it is a criminal misdemeanor, a conviction can bring consequences beyond the fine itself.

How is the reckless driving fine set in Texas?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The statutes are available through Texas statutes via Justia, and the code defines the offense and the limits a court works within.

The total you pay is usually more than the fine alone. Court costs are added on top.

  • Fine: set by the court on the citation.
  • State court costs: uniform across the state.
  • Municipal add-ons: these vary by court, so two people with similar tickets can owe different totals.

Because add-ons differ by court, the safest way to learn your total is to check the citation and then the court's own notice or payment page. Do not rely on a figure quoted for a different city or county.

Is reckless driving a criminal charge in Texas?

Yes. Reckless driving is treated as a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail. That sets it apart from a standard traffic infraction.

In practice, this means you are dealing with a court case rather than a simple ticket payment. The result may appear on a record in ways a minor moving violation would not.

We do not list a jail term here because we have no locked figure for it. The statute and your citation control what applies to you. A licensed Texas attorney can explain how the charge works in your situation, and TicketCostGuide does not recommend any specific attorney.

How many points does reckless driving add in Texas?

Texas abolished its driver-point and surcharge program in 2019. License action now follows specific offenses and repeat-conviction rules.

So there is no point value to look up for reckless driving. What matters is the offense itself and whether you have prior convictions.

If an older website lists points or annual surcharges for Texas, check its date. Those figures describe a program that no longer exists.

For what a conviction could mean for your license, ask the Texas Department of Public Safety or review your driving record directly with them. A repeat conviction can weigh differently than a first one.

Does a reckless driving conviction raise insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Texas-specific one.

Your own change could be smaller or larger. Insurers weigh the violation, your history, your age and their own rating rules.

  • The 20% figure applies to moving violations in general, so a criminal misdemeanor like reckless driving may be treated more seriously by some insurers.
  • Ask your insurer how it rates this type of conviction before you decide how to resolve the case.
  • Compare how outcomes are recorded, since a conviction and a different result may be rated differently.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend specific insurers. A Texas-specific premium figure would need its own source, and we have none to cite.

Can a reckless driving ticket be dismissed or reduced in Texas?

It can sometimes be resolved in different ways, but that depends on the court, the facts and your record. We have no locked data on how often dismissals or reductions happen in Texas, so we make no promises about outcomes.

General options people look at include:

  1. Pay the fine. This usually means a conviction, with the cost and record consequences described above.
  2. Contest the charge. This means going through the court process rather than paying.
  3. Ask about other resolutions. Some courts offer alternatives. Ask the court clerk what your court allows.

Because this is a criminal misdemeanor, many people talk to a licensed Texas attorney before deciding. This is general information and not legal advice.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the citation?

Ignoring a citation does not make it go away. The court case stays open, and the court can take further action. Because the exact fine and any add-ons are set by the court, an unpaid balance can differ from what you first saw on the citation.

We have no locked figures for late fees or deadlines, so we do not state any. Your citation or court notice shows the dates and instructions that apply to you.

If you cannot meet a deadline or pay the full amount, contact the court clerk before the date passes and ask what is available.

Since license action in Texas follows specific offenses and repeat-conviction rules, check with the Texas Department of Public Safety if you are worried about your license status.
