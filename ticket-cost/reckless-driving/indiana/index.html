How much is a reckless driving ticket in Indiana?

Nationally, reckless driving fines commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500. It is treated as a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail, which is why it costs more than an ordinary moving violation (Indiana Bureau of Motor Vehicles).

That range is a national figure. In Indiana, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation, so we do not print a single Indiana dollar amount here.

Your citation and the court that handles it are the only reliable sources for what you owe. Read the paperwork or contact the court clerk named on it.

The fine is also not the whole bill. County court costs are added to the judgment, and the total can be noticeably higher than the fine alone.

How is the reckless driving fine set in Indiana?

The fine is set by the court within the state's traffic code. It is not a fixed number printed on a statewide chart that applies the same way everywhere.

Several things can shape the final figure, though we cannot give exact dollar effects for any of them:

  • The court and county that hear your case.
  • The facts written on the citation.
  • Whether the charge stays as filed or is changed through the court process.
  • County court costs added to the judgment.

Because reckless driving is a criminal misdemeanor, a judge, not a clerk, usually decides the outcome. Jail is possible under the national description of this offense, so the case deserves more attention than a routine ticket.

You can look up how the offense is defined in the Indiana statutes via Justia. Statute text is the legal source, and it should be read alongside the citation you received.

What are the court costs on top of the fine?

County court costs are added to the judgment. For low-speed violations they often exceed the fine itself, so the total can surprise people who only looked at the fine.

The locked figures we use do not include an Indiana dollar amount for these costs. They vary by county and court, and we will not guess at them.

  • Ask the clerk for the total amount due, not just the fine.
  • Ask whether the total includes all court costs.
  • Keep a written record of what you were told and when.

If the total is a hardship, ask the court what payment arrangements it allows. Policies differ by court.

How many points does reckless driving add in Indiana?

The point figure we can source is 2–8 points, set by mph over the limit. Accumulating points triggers BMV probation and suspension (Indiana Bureau of Motor Vehicles).

That scale is described by speed, so it fits speed-related violations most directly. How a reckless driving conviction is scored on your record depends on the conviction and what the BMV applies.

To see how your record actually stands, check your driving record with the Indiana BMV rather than relying on a general point chart.

Points matter because they add up. A single conviction that leads to probation or suspension can cost more over time than the fine, through lost work, transport costs and reinstatement steps.

Does reckless driving raise insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Indiana-specific one.

A reckless driving conviction is more serious than many moving violations because it is a criminal misdemeanor. Your own increase could be larger or smaller than the average, and we have no sourced figure for it.

Insurers set their own rules. We do not recommend any insurer, and we cannot say what yours will do. Your policy documents and your insurer can tell you how convictions are treated.

The increase generally applies over a period of years, not for one billing cycle, so the long-term cost can exceed the fine.

Can a reckless driving ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes a charge is changed or dismissed, but that is decided by the court and the prosecutor on the facts of each case. We cannot predict the outcome, and nothing here is legal advice.

Because this is a criminal misdemeanor, it is worth understanding the stakes before you plead. A conviction can bring a fine, county court costs, possible jail, points and higher insurance.

  • Read the citation carefully for the exact charge and court date.
  • Ask the court clerk what options exist for your charge.
  • Consider speaking with a licensed attorney who can review your case. We do not recommend any specific attorney.

Any change to the charge can affect fine, points and insurance, so ask how an outcome would appear on your driving record before you accept it.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the ticket?

Ignoring a criminal misdemeanor citation is risky. Because the offense carries possible jail, missing a court date can make the situation worse than the original charge.

Accumulating points can trigger BMV probation and suspension, so unresolved or converted charges can affect your license. The BMV is the agency to ask about your license status (Indiana Bureau of Motor Vehicles).

If you cannot pay in full or cannot attend on the date given, contact the court clerk before the deadline, not after.

The specific penalties for non-payment depend on the court, and we do not have sourced dollar figures for late fees, so we do not list any.
