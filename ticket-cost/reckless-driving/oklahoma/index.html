How much is a reckless driving ticket in Oklahoma?

There is no single number to quote for Oklahoma. Nationally, reckless driving fines commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500, and the offense is treated as a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail (Service Oklahoma). That range is a national picture, not an Oklahoma price tag.

In Oklahoma, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. The fine for this violation falls within the state's traffic code, and the court handling your case decides where it lands.

Bottom line: treat the national range as a rough ceiling for planning, then read your own citation and court paperwork for the Oklahoma amount.

Court costs and statutory fees are added on top by the municipal or district court. That means the total you owe can be higher than the fine line alone.

How is the reckless driving fine set in Oklahoma?

The fine is not pulled from a simple lookup chart the way some minor tickets are. Because reckless driving is a criminal misdemeanor, the court has a role in deciding the outcome. The amount is set by the court on the citation, within the limits of the state's traffic code.

Several things can affect what the court does:

  • Which court hears the case: a municipal court or a district court.
  • How the charge is written on the citation.
  • Whether the case ends in a conviction, a plea to a different offense, or a dismissal.
  • Court costs and statutory fees the court adds.

You can read the Oklahoma statutes through Justia to see the traffic code language. Statutes tell you the legal limits, but the number on your paperwork is what you will be asked to pay.

What court costs and fees are added to the fine?

Court costs and statutory fees are added by the municipal or district court. These are separate from the fine itself. They are the reason two people with the same fine can owe different totals.

This page does not state an Oklahoma dollar figure for those add-ons, because the amount depends on the court. Your citation, the court clerk, or the court's payment notice should show the full amount due.

  • Ask whether the amount shown is the fine only or the fine plus costs.
  • Ask what payment methods and deadlines apply.
  • Keep a receipt or confirmation for any payment.

How many points does reckless driving add in Oklahoma?

The point figures we can cite come from Oklahoma's points system as it applies to speeding: 2 points for ordinary speeding, or more at higher speeds. Reaching 10 points in 5 years means a suspension.

Those figures are a guide to how the system scales, not a confirmed point value for reckless driving. The value for your exact charge is something to confirm with Service Oklahoma or in the statutes at Justia.

Why it matters: points from several violations add up. A single charge may not threaten your license, but combined with other tickets within 5 years it can.

Does reckless driving raise insurance rates?

It usually does. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not an Oklahoma-specific one.

Reckless driving is a more serious charge than an ordinary moving violation, so the effect on your own policy could be different. We do not have a locked figure for it, and we do not recommend any insurer.

  • The increase generally depends on a conviction, not just a citation.
  • Your insurer sets its own rules, and they vary by company.
  • Ask your insurer how a conviction would be treated before you decide how to handle the case.

Can a reckless driving ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes the outcome changes, but the result depends on the facts and the court. Because the charge is a criminal misdemeanor, it is worth taking seriously rather than simply mailing in a payment.

Common paths people consider include:

  1. Appearing in court and contesting the charge.
  2. Asking whether the charge can be amended to a lesser offense.
  3. Asking the clerk or prosecutor what resolution options the court offers.
  4. Speaking with a licensed attorney about your situation.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice or recommend an attorney. A lawyer licensed in Oklahoma can explain what applies to your case.

Paying the citation can count as a plea and lead to a conviction. Read the citation carefully before paying anything.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the citation?

Ignoring a criminal misdemeanor citation tends to make things worse. Missing a court date or payment deadline can lead to added penalties, and courts can take further action against people who do not respond. The specific steps depend on the court.

Because possible jail is part of how this offense is described, the safest approach is to find out your court date and the court's contact details right away. The citation should list the court that is handling the case.

  • Note the court name and any deadline printed on the citation.
  • Contact the clerk if anything is unclear.
  • Do not assume that skipping the process will make the charge go away.
