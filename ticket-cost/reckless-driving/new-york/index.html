How much is a reckless driving ticket in New York?

Nationally, reckless driving fines commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500, and the offense is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail. In New York, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (New York State Department of Motor Vehicles).

We do not publish a single New York fine figure for this charge, because the court decides the amount within the state's traffic code. The number on your citation or court notice is the one that matters.

The national range is a benchmark, not a New York price. The fine in your case can differ by court and by the facts of the stop.

The fine is only part of the bill. Mandatory surcharges and a possible DMV assessment are added, and insurance costs can last much longer than the fine itself.

How is the fine for reckless driving set?

A New York court sets the fine within the range the state's traffic code allows. The statutes are available through New York statutes via Justia.

Because reckless driving is a criminal misdemeanor rather than a basic traffic infraction, it is handled differently from an ordinary speeding ticket. A judge, not a fixed price list, usually decides the outcome.

Several things can affect what a court does:

  • The facts described in the citation or charging document.
  • The court and county where the case is heard.
  • Your driving record and any prior convictions.
  • Whether the charge stays as written or is changed through the court process.

Read the citation closely. It identifies the court, the charge and the date you must respond.

What extra fees come on top of the fine?

Two add-ons matter in New York. The first is a mandatory surcharge of $88–$93, which comes on top of the court fine.

The second is the DMV's Driver Responsibility Assessment. It is separate from the court fine and starts at $300+. It is triggered when a driver has 6+ points.

So a New York reckless driving case can involve three separate payments:

  1. The court fine, set by the court on the citation.
  2. The mandatory surcharge of $88–$93.
  3. The Driver Responsibility Assessment of $300+, if point totals reach the 6+ level.

Ask the court clerk what is owed in total and how to pay each part. The DMV assessment is billed by the DMV, so it may arrive separately (New York State Department of Motor Vehicles).

How many points does reckless driving add in New York?

New York's point schedule ties points to how far over the limit a driver was: 3 points (1–10 over), 4 points (11–20), 6 points (21–30), 8 points (31–40) and 11 points (41+).

We do not state a fixed point value for a reckless driving conviction here. Check your charge and the points that apply with the DMV, and look at your court paperwork.

Two thresholds matter whatever the source of the points:

  • 11 points in 18 months leads to a suspension.
  • 6+ points also triggers the separate Driver Responsibility Assessment of $300+.

Points from different tickets within the same 18 months can add together. A reckless driving conviction on top of earlier violations can move a driver toward those thresholds faster than one ticket alone suggests.

Does reckless driving raise insurance rates?

Usually, yes. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a New York quote.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your record and the rules that apply to you. A criminal misdemeanor conviction may be treated more seriously than a basic speeding ticket, so the real increase could be higher or lower than the average. We do not have a New York-specific figure to give.

Insurance cost often outlasts the fine. If your premium rises about 20%, you pay the extra on every renewal while the conviction counts against you.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend insurers. Compare quotes yourself, or ask your current insurer how a conviction would be rated.

Can a reckless driving charge be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes charges are changed or dismissed, but the outcome depends on the court, the facts and the evidence. We cannot predict it, and nothing here is legal advice.

Because this is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail, the stakes are higher than for a routine ticket. Many people talk to a licensed New York attorney before entering a plea. We do not recommend any specific attorney.

Before you appear, it helps to do these things:

  • Read the citation for the exact charge, court and date.
  • Keep any paperwork, photos or witness details from the stop.
  • Ask the court clerk about the process and what a plea would mean.
  • Check your point total with the DMV so you understand the 6+ and 11-point thresholds.

A different final charge can change the fine, the points and the insurance effect. Confirm the details in writing from the court.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the ticket?

Ignoring a reckless driving citation is risky. It is a criminal matter, and the court controls what happens if you miss a date or leave a fine unpaid.

The exact consequences depend on the court and your case, so check your citation and contact the court clerk promptly. Do not assume an unpaid fine will simply go away.

Remember the other charges that can follow a conviction. The $88–$93 surcharge is mandatory, and the DMV's Driver Responsibility Assessment of $300+ is separate from the court fine. At 11 points in 18 months, a license suspension applies.

If you cannot pay in full, ask the court whether any payment arrangement is available. Do that before the due date, not after.

Frequently asked questions

How much is a reckless driving ticket in New York?

Nationally, fines commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500, and reckless driving is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail. In New York the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. A mandatory surcharge of $88–$93 is added on top of the court fine.

Is reckless driving a misdemeanor in New York?

The figures we use describe reckless driving as a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail. That makes it more serious than a basic traffic ticket. Check your citation for the exact charge, and consider speaking with a licensed attorney, since TicketCostGuide does not give legal advice.

What is the mandatory surcharge on a New York ticket?

The mandatory surcharge is $88–$93 and comes on top of the court fine. It is separate from the DMV's Driver Responsibility Assessment. Ask the court clerk for the total owed in your case, since the fine itself is set by the court.

What is the Driver Responsibility Assessment in New York?

It is a separate DMV assessment of $300+ that is triggered when a driver has 6+ points. It comes on top of the court fine and the $88–$93 mandatory surcharge. The DMV, not the court, handles it, so it may arrive as a separate bill.

How many points get your license suspended in New York?

Eleven points in 18 months leads to a suspension. Points are tiered by speed over the limit: 3 (1–10 over), 4 (11–20), 6 (21–30), 8 (31–40) and 11 (41+). Check with the DMV how your specific charge is scored.

Will reckless driving raise my car insurance?

Likely. Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, so your actual change depends on your insurer and record. A criminal misdemeanor may be rated differently, so ask your insurer directly.
