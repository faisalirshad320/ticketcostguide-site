How much is a reckless driving ticket in Wisconsin?

Nationally, reckless driving fines commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500, and the offense is treated as a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail (Wisconsin Department of Transportation). That is a national range, not a Wisconsin price.

In Wisconsin, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation. We do not publish a single statewide dollar figure because the court, not a fixed schedule, decides it.

Read the citation itself. The amount, court date and any required appearance are printed there or come from the court.

On top of the base amount, a statutory surcharge stack is added to every forfeiture. Your total will therefore be higher than the base number, and it can differ by county and court.

How is the reckless driving fine set in Wisconsin?

The court sets the fine within the state's traffic code. The citation is where the amount is recorded, so it is the best starting point for your own case.

Because reckless driving is a criminal misdemeanor rather than a simple civil ticket, a judge or court may weigh more than the base penalty. Jail is possible, and the court process is usually more involved than for a routine moving violation.

The Wisconsin statutes are published online through Wisconsin statutes via Justia. They are the place to read the traffic code that the court applies.

  • The base amount is set by the court on the citation.
  • The statutory surcharge stack is added to every forfeiture.
  • The final total varies by county and court.

How many points does reckless driving add in Wisconsin?

Wisconsin's points system uses these tiers: 3 points (10 or less over), 4 points (11–19 over), and 6 points (20+ over). Twelve points in 12 months leads to suspension.

These tiers are written in terms of how far over a limit a driver was. We cannot tell you from these sources which tier a reckless driving conviction falls into, because that depends on how the conviction is recorded.

Ask the court clerk or check your record with the Wisconsin Department of Transportation to see what was entered.

Points can add up fast. A 6-point entry is half of the 12-point suspension threshold, so a second violation within 12 months matters.

Does reckless driving raise insurance rates in Wisconsin?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Wisconsin-specific one.

Your own change depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the conviction is recorded. A criminal misdemeanor may be treated more seriously than a basic speeding ticket, but we have no locked figure to quantify that.

If your renewal is coming up, ask your insurer how a conviction would be rated. We do not recommend any specific insurer.

Can a reckless driving charge be dismissed or reduced?

It can be possible, but outcomes depend on the facts, the court and the prosecutor. We do not predict results or give legal advice.

Because this is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail, many people consult a licensed attorney before deciding how to respond. TicketCostGuide does not recommend any particular attorney.

  • Read the citation for the court, date and charge.
  • Check whether an appearance is required.
  • Ask the clerk what options the court offers.
  • Consider talking to a licensed Wisconsin attorney.

A reduced charge could change both the fine and the points recorded, so it is worth understanding before you plead.

What happens if I don't pay or don't show up?

Ignoring a citation tends to make things worse. For a criminal misdemeanor, missing a required court date can bring further consequences from the court.

Because the surcharge stack is added to every forfeiture, delays do not make the total smaller. Contact the court named on the citation as soon as you can to confirm what you owe and what you must do.

Driving privileges are also tied to the points system. With 12 points in 12 months, suspension applies, so unresolved matters can compound.

If you cannot tell whether your citation requires a court appearance, call the clerk before the date, not after.

What should I do after getting a reckless driving citation?

Start with the citation. Note the amount if one is printed, the court, the date and whether a mandatory appearance is listed.

Then confirm the total with the court, including the statutory surcharge stack. Keep in mind that the national range of $1,000–$2,500 is not a Wisconsin quote.

  1. Read the citation and note every deadline.
  2. Contact the court clerk about the total owed.
  3. Check your points status with Wisconsin Department of Transportation.
  4. Decide whether to seek legal advice from a licensed attorney.
  5. Ask your insurer about renewal effects.
