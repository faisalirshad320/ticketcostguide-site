How much is a reckless driving ticket in Hawaii?

Nationally, reckless driving fines commonly run up to $1,000–$2,500. It is treated as a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail (Hawaii Department of Transportation).

That range is a national one. In Hawaii the exact amount is set by the court on the citation, so we do not print a single Hawaii dollar figure.

Bottom line: the national range is a planning guide only. The number on your citation or from the court is the one that counts.

Court fees and assessments are added by the district court. Your total can therefore be higher than the fine alone.

How is the reckless driving fine set in Hawaii?

The court sets the fine within the state's traffic code. It is not a fixed amount you can look up in a one-line schedule.

The court decides based on the charge as written on the citation and the outcome of the case. Two people cited for similar driving can end up with different totals.

  • The citation names the charge and the court.
  • The court sets the fine within the traffic code.
  • The district court adds fees and assessments.

You can read the Hawaii statutes through Hawaii statutes via Justia. Check the current wording yourself, because we do not quote section numbers or amounts we have not verified.

Is reckless driving a crime in Hawaii?

Our locked reference describes reckless driving as a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail. That is a different category from an ordinary speeding or equipment ticket.

A criminal charge can mean a court appearance and a record, not just a payment. The practical stakes are higher than the fine alone suggests.

We do not state a jail length for Hawaii because it is not in our locked facts. The court and the statute control that, and the citation will point you to the court handling your case.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice. For questions about your own charge, speak with a licensed attorney of your choosing.

How many points does reckless driving add in Hawaii?

Hawaii has no demerit-point system, so there is no point value to report for reckless driving.

License action works differently. Repeat convictions and excessive-speeding convictions drive license action directly (Hawaii Department of Transportation).

In practice, a conviction history matters more than a running point tally. A prior conviction can change how the state treats the next one.

For comparison with other states, the Governors Highway Safety Association publishes a state-by-state view of traffic laws.

Does reckless driving raise insurance in Hawaii?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Hawaii-specific one.

Your own change could be smaller or larger. It depends on your insurer, your driving record and how the violation is recorded.

  • A conviction is more likely to affect rates than a case that ends without one.
  • Insurers set their own rules, so we cannot predict your quote.
  • Ask your insurer how it treats this type of violation. We do not recommend any specific insurer.

Can a reckless driving ticket be dismissed or reduced?

It can be, but the outcome is up to the court. Because the fine and charge are set through the court process, the result depends on the facts and the record.

Common paths people ask about are contesting the charge, negotiating a different charge, or resolving it at a hearing. We do not promise any of them, and we do not give legal advice on which to pick.

Because this is a criminal misdemeanor with possible jail, many people choose to talk to a licensed attorney before deciding. That choice is yours, and we do not recommend a specific one.

Read the citation closely. It lists the charge and the court, and it is the starting point for any option.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the citation?

Ignoring a criminal citation is risky. The court controls the process, and missing a required step can make things worse than the original charge.

We do not list specific penalties or deadlines for Hawaii because they are not in our locked facts. The citation and the court are the reliable source.

  • Find the court and any instructions on the citation.
  • Contact the court to confirm what is required.
  • Keep copies of everything you file or pay.

Since repeat convictions drive license action directly in Hawaii, an unresolved or repeated case can matter later.
