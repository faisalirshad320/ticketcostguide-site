How much is a seatbelt ticket in New Mexico?

Nationally, seatbelt fines for adults run $10–$200 by state. In New Mexico the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (New Mexico Motor Vehicle Division).

We do not print a single New Mexico dollar figure, because we cannot source one that applies to every case. The $10–$200 range is a spread across states, so it tells you the general scale but not your exact bill.

What you owe is the fine plus any fees added to it. The sections below explain how those pieces fit together and where to look for your own number.

Start with the citation itself. The amount, the court named on it, and the payment or appearance instructions are what control your case.

How is a New Mexico seatbelt fine set?

The exact fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. That means the number comes from the court handling your citation, not from a general price list we can quote.

New Mexico traffic statutes are published online through New Mexico statutes via Justia. If you want the legal text behind a seatbelt citation, the statute or ordinance listed on your ticket is the place to start, and you can look it up there.

The citation may be handled under a state statute or, in some places, a local ordinance. Either way, the court named on the ticket is the authority on what you owe.

For a comparison of how seatbelt laws differ between states, the Governors Highway Safety Association state laws page collects them in one place. It is useful context, but it does not replace the court's figure for your citation.

Are there extra fees on top of the fine?

Yes, in general. In New Mexico, fees are added to the penalty assessment, and court cases can exceed the assessment amounts.

In practice, that gives you two possible paths:

  • Paying the assessment: the amount shown on the citation, with fees added as described there.
  • Going through the court: a case that goes before a judge can end up costing more than the assessment amounts.

We do not have a sourced dollar figure for the fees, so we do not give one. The citation or the court clerk's office can tell you the total owed before you pay.

Ask for the full total, not just the base fine. Fees are added to the assessment, so the two numbers can differ.

How many points does a seatbelt ticket carry in New Mexico?

The New Mexico points system we have sourced is described as 3–8 points by mph over. That wording ties the point values to speed, so it does not clearly tell you how many points, if any, a seatbelt citation adds.

We are not going to guess. Whether your citation carries points is something to confirm with the New Mexico Motor Vehicle Division or the court named on the ticket.

The overall thresholds are worth knowing, because points from any violation can add up:

  • 7–10 points in 12 months risks suspension.
  • 12 points = revocation.

If you already have points on your record, ask the MVD how a new citation would count before you decide how to respond.

Does a seatbelt ticket raise insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a New Mexico rate, and it applies to moving-violation convictions in general.

We do not have a sourced figure for how insurers treat a seatbelt citation specifically. Whether it is reported or counted as a moving violation can vary, so we cannot say your premium will or will not change.

Your policy documents and your insurer are the reliable source on that question. TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer or give insurance advice.

The ~20% figure is a national average for moving-violation convictions. Your own change could be higher, lower, or none.

Can a seatbelt ticket be dismissed or reduced?

That is decided by the court, not by us. Because the exact amount is set by the court on the citation, the court is also where you would ask about your options.

Common steps people take include:

  1. Read the citation for the court, the listed violation, and the response instructions.
  2. Contact the court clerk to confirm the total owed and what choices are available.
  3. Decide whether to pay the assessment or have the matter heard by the court.

Keep in mind that court cases can exceed the assessment amounts. Weigh that before choosing to contest the citation.

This page is not legal advice. If you want advice about your specific case, that is a question for a licensed attorney, and we do not recommend any particular one.

What happens if I don't pay a seatbelt ticket?

We do not have sourced figures for late penalties or deadlines, so we do not state any. The citation lists the response instructions, and those are the ones that apply to you.

Ignoring a citation can lead to additional consequences, and since fees are added to the penalty assessment, delay can only make it harder to know your true total. The safest approach is to contact the court named on the citation as soon as you can.

If you are unsure about status or any hold on your driving record, the New Mexico Motor Vehicle Division is the agency to ask.
