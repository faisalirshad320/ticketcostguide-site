How much is a seatbelt ticket in Tennessee?

Nationally, seatbelt fines for adults range from $10 to $200 depending on the state (Tennessee Department of Safety and Homeland Security). In Tennessee, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation.

We do not publish a single Tennessee dollar figure, because the amount you owe depends on the court that handles your case. The national range is a comparison. It is not a promise of what you will pay.

The fine is only part of the bill. County court costs and state litigation taxes make up most of the total.

The number printed on your citation, or given by the court clerk, is the number that counts. Any general range is only a guide.

How is a Tennessee seatbelt fine set?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. Tennessee statutes are available through Tennessee statutes via Justia if you want to read the text yourself.

Two things shape the final total:

  • The fine itself: set by the court on the citation.
  • Court costs and taxes: county court costs and state litigation taxes make up most of the total.

Because court costs are county-based, two drivers cited for the same violation in different counties can see different totals. That is why a statewide flat price is not reliable.

Seatbelt rules also differ between states, including who must buckle up and how the fine is treated. The Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws page tracks these differences across the country.

Where do I find the exact amount I owe?

Start with the citation itself. It normally lists the court, the violation and instructions for paying or appearing.

If the amount is not printed, contact the clerk of the court named on the citation. Ask for the total due, not only the base fine, so that court costs and taxes are included.

  1. Find the court name and county on the citation.
  2. Contact that court's clerk, or check the court's official page.
  3. Ask for the full amount due and the accepted payment methods.
  4. Write down any deadline given to you, and keep your receipt.

The Tennessee Department of Safety and Homeland Security is the state agency for driver records and licensing. Its site is at tn.gov/safety.

How many points is a seatbelt ticket in Tennessee?

Tennessee's points system runs from 1 to 8 points, and the schedule is described by miles per hour over the limit. For example, 3 points apply at 6–15 over and 4 points at 16–25 over. Twelve points in 12 months leads to a suspension notice (Tennessee Department of Safety and Homeland Security).

That schedule is built around speeding. The facts we have do not assign a point value to a seatbelt violation, so we do not state one.

To find out whether your citation adds anything to your driving record, check the citation, ask the court clerk, or review your record with the state's driver services.

Do not assume points apply or do not apply. Confirm it with the court or the state before relying on either.

Does a seatbelt ticket raise insurance in Tennessee?

Nationally, moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Tennessee-specific one.

Whether a seatbelt ticket affects your rate depends on several things:

  • How your insurer treats this type of violation.
  • Whether the ticket results in a conviction on your record.
  • Your overall driving history.

We do not recommend any insurer and cannot say what your policy will do. If you are concerned, ask your insurer or agent how a seatbelt citation is handled under your policy.

Can a Tennessee seatbelt ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Options depend on the court and on the facts of your citation. We cannot promise any result.

In general, people with a ticket can:

  • Pay it, following the instructions on the citation.
  • Contest it, by following the court's process for a hearing.
  • Ask the clerk what options the court offers for this type of violation.

Paying usually resolves the case but may be treated as a conviction. Contesting takes time and a court appearance. Ask the court how each choice would be recorded.

What happens if I don't pay a seatbelt ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts set deadlines, and missing one can lead to further action in your case.

The specific consequences depend on the court. Because county court costs make up most of the total, delays can also complicate the amount you owe.

If you cannot pay by the deadline or cannot attend the date, contact the clerk before the deadline passes. Ask what the court allows and get the answer in writing where possible.

Separately, a suspension notice in Tennessee is tied to reaching 12 points in 12 months. Confirm with the court or the state how your citation affects your record.
