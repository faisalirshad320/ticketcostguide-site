How much is a seatbelt ticket in West Virginia?

Nationally, adult seatbelt fines run $10–$200 depending on the state. In West Virginia, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (West Virginia Division of Motor Vehicles).

We do not print a single West Virginia dollar figure here, because we cannot source one that applies to every court. The national range is context only. It is not a statement of what you will owe in West Virginia.

The most reliable number is the one printed on your citation or shown on the court's payment notice. Read that before you pay or respond.

The fine is only part of the bill. Court costs are often added, and they can change the total noticeably. The next sections explain how.

How is the seatbelt fine set in West Virginia?

The fine for this violation falls within the state's traffic code, and the court sets the amount on the citation. That means two drivers cited for the same thing could see different totals depending on the court that handles the case.

The traffic code itself is published online. You can look up West Virginia statutes through West Virginia statutes via Justia, though the version on a third-party site may not reflect the latest amendments.

For a quick cross-state comparison of how seatbelt laws differ, the Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws page is a useful overview. It is a summary, not the text of the law.

  • Check the citation for the stated amount and the court name.
  • Check the court's instructions for how and when to respond.
  • Confirm anything unclear with the court clerk directly.

Why can the total cost more than the fine?

Municipal and magistrate court costs vary and usually exceed the fine. So the amount you actually pay may be noticeably higher than the fine line alone.

This is why a national range such as $10–$200 can mislead. It describes fine levels across states, not the all-in total for a particular West Virginia case.

When you review a notice, look for separate lines. Some courts list the fine, the court costs, and any other charges individually. Others show one combined figure.

If the notice shows only one total, ask the clerk what portion is the fine and what portion is court costs. Do not assume the two are the same.

We do not state any specific court-cost amount, because the amounts vary by court and we have no sourced figure to give.

How many points does a seatbelt ticket carry in West Virginia?

The point system we can source is described as 2–5 points by mph over, with 12+ points leading to suspension. That scale is built around speed, so it reads as a speeding scale rather than a seatbelt one.

For that reason, we do not claim a specific point value for a seatbelt violation. Whether and how points apply to your citation is something to confirm with the West Virginia Division of Motor Vehicles or the court.

  • 12+ points = suspension is the threshold on the locked schedule.
  • Points from different violations add up on one record.
  • A seatbelt ticket's effect on your record depends on how it is treated under West Virginia rules.

If you already have points from other tickets, it is worth checking your record before deciding how to respond.

Does a seatbelt ticket raise insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure. It is not specific to West Virginia, to any one insurer, or to seatbelt tickets in particular.

Whether a seatbelt citation affects your premium depends on how your insurer treats it and whether it shows as a conviction on your record. TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer and cannot predict your rate change.

The ~20% figure is an average. Your own change could be higher, lower, or zero.

The most direct way to find out is to ask your insurer how a seatbelt citation is handled at renewal. You can also ask the court whether a given outcome would count as a conviction.

Can a seatbelt ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Courts differ in how they handle seatbelt citations, and we have no sourced figure or rule to say what is available in your county. The citation and the court's instructions are the starting point.

Some people contact the court to ask what response options exist, such as paying, contesting, or requesting a hearing. The court clerk can describe the process, though clerks generally cannot give legal advice.

  • Read the response instructions and deadline on the citation.
  • Ask the court what options are available for your specific citation.
  • Keep copies of anything you submit.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice. If you want advice on contesting a ticket, that is a question for a licensed attorney of your own choosing.

What happens if I don't pay a seatbelt ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse, not better. Courts can add consequences when a citation goes unanswered, and those consequences vary by court.

We cannot cite specific penalty amounts or deadlines for West Virginia, so we do not list them. The citation and the court's notice should state what is required and by when.

Unpaid or unanswered tickets can also affect your driving record. Since 12+ points = suspension on the points schedule, any added penalties on top of existing points are worth checking with the West Virginia Division of Motor Vehicles.

If you cannot pay the full amount or cannot meet a deadline, contact the court before the deadline passes and ask what it allows.
