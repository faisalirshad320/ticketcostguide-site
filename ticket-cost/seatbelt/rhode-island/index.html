How much is a seatbelt ticket in Rhode Island?

Nationally, seatbelt fines for adults run $10–$200 depending on the state. In Rhode Island the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Rhode Island Division of Motor Vehicles).

We do not publish a single Rhode Island dollar figure here. The amount that applies to you is the one printed on, or tied to, your citation.

Treat the $10–$200 range as a national yardstick only. It is not a Rhode Island fine schedule.

On top of the scheduled fine, court technology and assessment fees are added. Your total is therefore the fine plus those fees, and it can exceed the base amount.

How is the fine set, and what gets added?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. Rhode Island statutes are published online, including through Rhode Island statutes via Justia.

Two parts make up what you owe:

  • The scheduled fine: the amount the court sets for the violation.
  • Added fees: court technology and assessment fees, which are added to the scheduled fine.

We do not state the size of those fees. If the total on your paperwork looks higher than the base fine, these additions are the likely reason.

Rules for seatbelts differ from state to state, and the Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a comparison of them at GHSA State Laws.

How many points does a seatbelt ticket carry in Rhode Island?

Rhode Island has no running point system for ordinary speeding. Instead, the Traffic Tribunal escalates sanctions, including suspension, for repeat offenses.

That statement describes the state's general approach, and we have no point value for a seatbelt violation. We do not invent one.

The practical takeaway is that repeat violations matter more than any single ticket. A first citation and a pattern of citations can be treated very differently.

For questions about your own record, the Rhode Island Division of Motor Vehicles is the official place to start.

Does a seatbelt ticket raise insurance rates?

Nationally, moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Rhode Island one.

We cannot say whether your insurer will treat a seatbelt citation as a moving violation, or whether it will change your rate at all. That depends on the insurer, your policy and your driving record.

  • Read your policy or ask your insurer how it treats seatbelt citations.
  • Remember that the ~20% figure is an average. Your own change could be higher, lower or zero.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer or give insurance advice.

Can a seatbelt ticket be dismissed or reduced?

The court that handles your citation decides whether a ticket is dismissed, reduced or upheld. We cannot predict that outcome, and this page is not legal advice.

Steps that are generally sensible:

  1. Read the citation closely and note what it says about paying or contesting.
  2. Follow the instructions and deadlines printed on it, since the court controls those.
  3. If you want to contest it or have questions about your rights, consider speaking with a licensed professional of your own choosing.

Because the amount is set by the court on the citation, any change to it would also come from the court. We do not state what reductions are available.

What happens if I don't pay a seatbelt ticket?

Ignoring a citation is rarely a good idea. We do not list specific late penalties, because the locked facts we rely on do not give them.

What we can say is that Rhode Island's Traffic Tribunal escalates sanctions, including suspension, for repeat offenses. Unresolved tickets can add to that pattern.

Check your citation for how to pay or respond, and contact the court named on it if anything is unclear.

For license-status questions, use the Rhode Island Division of Motor Vehicles.
