How much is a seatbelt ticket in New Hampshire?

Nationally $10–$200 for adults by state; in New Hampshire the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (New Hampshire Division of Motor Vehicles). The national range is a spread across all states, not a New Hampshire price.

We do not publish a single New Hampshire dollar figure, because the amount that applies to you is the one printed on your citation or confirmed by the court handling it.

Best source for your exact amount: the citation itself, or the clerk of the court named on it.

Remember that a 24% penalty assessment is added to every base fine by statute. The total you owe can therefore be higher than the base fine alone.

How is a New Hampshire seatbelt fine set?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. That is why two drivers cited for the same violation can see different paperwork depending on the court and the citation.

New Hampshire statutes are available through New Hampshire statutes via Justia. They are the place to read the actual seatbelt provisions and the rules for penalty assessments.

The 24% penalty assessment works like this: it is calculated on the base fine and added by statute. It is not an optional fee, and a court clerk does not normally waive it on their own.

  • Base fine: set by the court on the citation.
  • Penalty assessment: 24% added to every base fine.
  • Your total: the base fine plus the assessment.

How many points is a seatbelt ticket in New Hampshire?

New Hampshire's points system assigns 3–4 points by offense. Whether and how this violation is scored depends on the offense listed on your citation and on the state's rules, so check with the court or the New Hampshire Division of Motor Vehicles.

The threshold that matters is 12 points in 12 months, which can lead to a suspension of up to 3 months. Points from different violations are added together, so a minor ticket can matter if you already have others.

We cannot tell you your current point total. The DMV holds your driving record and is the right place to request it.

Does a seatbelt ticket raise insurance in New Hampshire?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a New Hampshire rate, and it is not specific to seatbelt tickets.

Whether your insurer sees or rates the ticket depends on the insurer, your record and the way the case ended. We do not recommend insurers or predict what yours will do.

  • Ask your insurer how it treats this kind of citation.
  • Ask the court whether the outcome will be recorded as a conviction.
  • Compare your renewal notice with your prior one.

Can a seatbelt ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Your options are set by the court handling the citation. Usually you can pay, contest the citation, or ask the court about other resolutions it allows.

Each choice has trade-offs. Paying generally resolves the matter but may count as a conviction. Contesting takes time and may require a court appearance.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice. For advice on your case, speak with a licensed attorney of your choosing.

Before deciding, read the citation closely for the response instructions and the date by which you must act. The court clerk can explain procedure, though not legal strategy.

What happens if I don't pay a seatbelt ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts can add consequences when a citation goes unanswered, and your driving privileges can be affected.

The specific steps depend on the court and the New Hampshire traffic code, which you can read through New Hampshire statutes via Justia. The citation lists your response instructions.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court clerk before the deadline and ask what the court allows. Do not wait until after the deadline to ask.
