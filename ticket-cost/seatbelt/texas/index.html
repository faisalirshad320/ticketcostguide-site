How much is a seatbelt ticket in Texas?

Nationally, seatbelt fines range from $10 to $200 for adults, depending on the state. In Texas, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Texas Department of Public Safety).

The Texas seatbelt fine is $25–$50. That is the only Texas dollar range we can source, and your own citation is the final word on what you owe.

The $10–$200 range is a national spread across all states. Do not read it as the Texas fine. For Texas, use the $25–$50 range and the amount printed on your citation.

Your total can still differ from the base fine because of municipal add-ons, which vary by court.

How is the fine set, and why can the total differ?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The statute text is available through Texas statutes via Justia, and the court that handles your citation applies it.

Two layers sit on top of the base fine:

  • State court costs. These are uniform across the state.
  • Municipal add-ons. These vary by court, so two drivers cited for the same violation in different places can owe different totals.

We do not publish an exact add-on figure for any Texas court because our sourced facts only say that these add-ons vary. Check the citation, the court's website or the clerk's office for the amount due.

How many points does a seatbelt ticket carry in Texas?

Texas abolished its driver-point and surcharge program in 2019. A seatbelt ticket therefore does not add points under a point system, because that system no longer exists.

License action now follows specific offenses and repeat-conviction rules. In practice, the question to ask is whether the offense or your record triggers any of those rules, not how many points you have.

The Texas Department of Public Safety (Texas Department of Public Safety) is the agency to contact about your driver record and license status.

Does a seatbelt ticket raise your insurance in Texas?

Nationally, moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Texas-specific one.

Whether your own premium changes depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the conviction is classified. We do not know how any particular insurer treats a seatbelt conviction, and we do not recommend insurers.

  • Ask your insurer how it treats this type of conviction.
  • Check your policy documents for surcharge rules.
  • Remember that Texas has no state point surcharge program, but insurers set their own pricing.

Can a seatbelt ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Options depend on the court. Some courts offer ways to resolve a citation other than a straight payment, but availability, eligibility and any extra cost vary by court and by your record.

Because the fine and add-ons vary by court, the best first step is to read your citation and contact the court clerk. Ask what responses the court accepts and what each would cost.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot give legal advice. If you want advice on contesting a citation, speak with a licensed Texas attorney of your own choosing.

Paying the citation is generally treated as a conviction. A conviction is what insurers typically look at, so weigh your options before you pay.

What happens if I don't pay a seatbelt ticket in Texas?

Ignoring a citation usually makes the situation worse. Courts can add costs, and an unresolved citation can lead to further enforcement steps, depending on the court.

Because municipal add-ons vary by court, a late or unresolved ticket may cost more than the original amount. We cannot give you a specific deadline or penalty figure from our sourced facts, so check the due date and instructions printed on your citation.

  • Find the court named on the citation.
  • Confirm the due date and accepted ways to respond.
  • Contact the clerk if you are unsure or need more time.

What should I check before I pay or respond?

Look at the citation first. It names the court and shows the amount that court set within the state's traffic code.

  1. Confirm the fine is in line with the Texas $25–$50 range.
  2. Ask the court which add-ons apply, since municipal add-ons vary by court.
  3. Ask which ways of responding the court accepts.
  4. Check with your insurer about the possible effect of a conviction.

For a broader comparison, the Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a state-by-state summary of seatbelt laws (Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws). Use it for context, not as a source for your Texas fine.
