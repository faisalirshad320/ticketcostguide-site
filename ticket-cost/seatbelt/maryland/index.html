How much is a seatbelt ticket in Maryland?

Nationally, adult seatbelt fines run $10–$200 by state. In Maryland, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Maryland Motor Vehicle Administration).

We do not publish a single Maryland dollar figure on this page. The amount that applies to you is the one printed on your citation or shown by the court handling your case.

The $10–$200 range is a national range. It is not a Maryland price list, so use it only as a rough sense of scale.

Your own citation is the best source. It should list the violation, the court and the way to respond.

How is the seatbelt fine set in Maryland?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. You can read the code itself through Maryland statutes via Justia.

There are two broad paths, and they affect the amount differently:

  • Pay the amount on the citation. This is the prepay route. The amount comes from the court and is printed on the ticket.
  • Request a court date. This replaces the prepay amount with a judge-set fine of up to $500.

The up-to-$500 ceiling is the most a judge can set after you request a court date. It is not the typical seatbelt amount, and it is not a prediction of what you would owe.

Because the court sets the figure, two people with the same violation can see different notices. Check the paperwork for your own case rather than relying on a general number.

How many points does a seatbelt ticket add in Maryland?

Maryland's points system uses these tiers: 1 point (9 or less over), 2 points (10–29 over), and 5 points (30+ over). Eight points leads to suspension, and 12 points leads to revocation.

Those tiers are written in terms of how far over a limit a driver was. That wording fits speeding, so it does not map neatly onto a seatbelt violation.

We therefore do not state a point value for a seatbelt citation. Whether your citation carries points, and how many, should be confirmed with the Maryland Motor Vehicle Administration or the court.

  • Look at the citation for any mention of points.
  • Ask the court clerk how the violation is recorded.
  • Check your record through the Maryland Motor Vehicle Administration.

The suspension and revocation thresholds matter mainly if you already have points. Adding any points to an existing record brings those lines closer.

Does a seatbelt ticket raise insurance in Maryland?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Maryland rate and not a promise about your policy.

Whether a seatbelt citation affects your premium depends on how it is recorded and on your insurer's own rules. We do not recommend any insurer, and we cannot say what yours will do.

If you want a firm answer, you can:

  • Ask your insurer how it treats this type of citation.
  • Ask the court how the case would be recorded if you pay or if you contest it.
  • Request your driving record from the Maryland Motor Vehicle Administration to see what is listed.

The 20% figure is an average for moving-violation convictions nationwide. Your result could be higher, lower or no change at all.

Can a Maryland seatbelt ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Some options exist, but the outcome is up to the court. We can describe the routes without predicting a result.

  • Pay it. This settles the citation at the amount the court set. Check how paying is recorded first.
  • Request a court date. A judge can then set the fine, up to $500. That could end up higher or lower than a prepay amount.
  • Ask about the record. Before choosing, ask the clerk how each choice affects your driving record.

Requesting a court date gives you the chance to explain your case. It also replaces the prepay amount, so weigh that before you decide.

What happens if I don't pay a seatbelt ticket in Maryland?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. The court can treat an unanswered ticket as a separate problem, and the consequences depend on the court and the case.

We do not list specific penalties or deadlines here, because they are not part of our verified figures. The deadline and response options appear on the citation itself.

  • Read the response instructions on the ticket right away.
  • Contact the court named on the citation if anything is unclear.
  • Keep proof of any payment or response you make.

If you want to contest the ticket, respond in time to request the court date. Waiting can leave you with fewer choices.
