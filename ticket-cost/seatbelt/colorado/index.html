How much is a seatbelt ticket in Colorado?

Nationally, adult seatbelt fines run $10–$200 by state. In Colorado, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Colorado Division of Motor Vehicles).

We do not publish a single Colorado dollar figure, because the amount you owe is the one printed on your citation or shown by the court handling your case. The national range tells you what is typical across the country. It does not tell you what a Colorado judge or clerk will assess.

Read the citation first. The amount, the court name and the response instructions are all printed there, and they control what you owe.

How is the seatbelt fine set in Colorado?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The code gives the framework, and the court applies it to your citation.

That is why two people can compare notes and see different totals. The base fine may differ by court, and added charges change the final bill.

You can read the Colorado statutes through Colorado statutes via Justia. For a national comparison of how states handle seatbelt rules, see the Governors Highway Safety Association state laws page.

  • The citation shows the amount the court has assessed.
  • The court clerk can confirm the total owed.
  • The national $10–$200 range is context only.

Are there extra court costs or surcharges on a seatbelt ticket?

Yes. A statutory surcharge is added to each penalty. This means the number you pay can be higher than the base fine alone.

Doubled fines also apply in work zones. Whether that rule reaches a seatbelt citation depends on the facts of your stop and the wording of your citation, so check the paperwork rather than assuming either way.

When you budget for a ticket, think in terms of three layers:

  1. The base fine set by the court.
  2. The statutory surcharge added to the penalty.
  3. Any enhancement, such as a doubled fine where it applies.

We do not state a dollar value for the surcharge. If the citation or court notice does not itemize it, ask the clerk for the full amount due.

How many points does a seatbelt ticket carry in Colorado?

The state's points system assigns 1–6 points by mph over, and 12 points in 12 months (adult) means suspension. That mph scale describes speeding violations.

Our locked data does not give a point value for a seatbelt violation. We will not guess one. Confirm with the Colorado Division of Motor Vehicles or the court before assuming points do or do not apply.

Why it matters: points from any violation count toward the 12-in-12 suspension threshold for adults. A seatbelt ticket on its own is unlikely to be the issue, but a driver already carrying points should check their record.

You can ask the DMV what your record currently shows and how any conviction would be recorded.

Does a seatbelt ticket raise my insurance?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Colorado or seatbelt-specific number.

Whether a seatbelt citation affects your rate depends on how your insurer treats it and whether it is reported as a conviction. We do not recommend any insurer and cannot predict your quote.

  • Ask your insurer how it treats a seatbelt conviction.
  • Ask the court whether paying results in a conviction on your record.
  • Compare the possible premium change with the ticket cost before deciding how to respond.

A small fine can matter less than a multi-year premium change. That is worth weighing before you pay without reading the options.

Can a seatbelt ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Some courts offer ways to resolve a citation besides paying it as written. Which options exist, if any, depends on the court that issued the ticket.

We cannot say whether your court allows a reduction, dismissal or alternative. The citation and the court clerk are the right places to start. TicketCostGuide does not give legal advice or recommend attorneys.

  1. Read the citation for the response instructions and court name.
  2. Contact the court clerk and ask what response options are available.
  3. Ask whether paying counts as a conviction.
  4. If you are unsure, consider getting advice from a licensed attorney of your choosing.

Do not assume a ticket will be reduced. Ask before you decide.

What happens if I don't pay a seatbelt ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts can add consequences when a ticket goes unanswered, and the statutory surcharge and other costs still apply.

Our locked data does not give deadlines or late-fee amounts, so we do not state any. Check the response date on the citation and call the court if you cannot meet it.

Unresolved violations can also create trouble with your driving record. The Colorado Division of Motor Vehicles is the place to confirm your status.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court clerk before the response date and ask what is available.
