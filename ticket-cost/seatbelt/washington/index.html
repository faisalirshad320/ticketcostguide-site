How much is a seatbelt ticket in Washington?

Nationally, adult seatbelt fines run $10–$200 by state. In Washington the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Washington State Department of Licensing).

We do not publish a single Washington dollar figure on this page. The number that matters is the one printed on your ticket or shown on the court's payment record.

The national range is a comparison across states. It is not a Washington fine amount.

If your citation lists an amount, that is what the court expects you to pay unless you contest the ticket or the court changes it. If the amount is unclear, contact the court that issued the citation and ask for the total due.

How is the seatbelt fine set in Washington?

Washington traffic infractions are handled under the Infraction Rules for Courts of Limited Jurisdiction (IRLJ). The court sets the amount on the citation within the state's traffic code.

Statutory assessments are built into the IRLJ schedule amounts. That means the figure you see is generally the total for the infraction, not a base fine with separate line items you would need to add up yourself.

Because the court sets the amount, two people cited for the same violation could see different paperwork depending on the court and the details of the citation. For the underlying text, you can search the Washington codes through Washington statutes via Justia.

  • Read the amount printed on the citation.
  • Check the court's payment or hearing instructions.
  • Ask the clerk if you are unsure what the total includes.

How many points does a seatbelt ticket add in Washington?

Washington has no point system. There is no point value to look up for a seatbelt ticket.

Instead, license suspensions follow repeat moving-violation convictions within set windows. The practical risk is a pattern of convictions over time, not a points total building up.

Whether a particular seatbelt infraction counts toward that kind of pattern is a question for the Washington State Department of Licensing or the court. You can start at Washington State Department of Licensing. We do not state the exact windows here because they are not part of the figures we have verified for this page.

No points does not mean no record. Your driving record can still show the outcome of a ticket.

Does a seatbelt ticket raise insurance rates?

Nationally, moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Washington-specific or seatbelt-specific one.

Whether your insurer treats a seatbelt infraction as a rate-affecting violation depends on the company, your policy and your record. We do not give insurer-specific guidance or recommend any insurer.

If you are worried about a premium change, you can ask your insurer how it treats this kind of infraction, and when it reviews your record. Paying quickly or contesting the ticket can lead to different outcomes on your record, so it helps to know the result before you decide.

Do not assume the 20% figure will apply to you. It is an average across many drivers and violations.

Can a seatbelt ticket be dismissed or reduced?

You generally have a choice of responses on a Washington infraction: pay it, contest it, or ask the court about other options. The citation lists the choices and the deadline for responding.

Courts decide whether to dismiss or change an amount. We cannot say what a particular court will do, and this page is not legal advice.

  • Check the response deadline on the citation and do not let it pass.
  • Ask the court which response options it offers, such as a hearing.
  • If you contest the ticket, bring any records that support your account.
  • If you want legal advice, speak with a licensed attorney of your own choosing.

Because the amount is set by the court on the citation, ask about the total before assuming that a different response will lower it.

What happens if I don't pay a seatbelt ticket in Washington?

Ignoring an infraction rarely makes it go away. Courts can treat an unanswered citation as a failure to respond, which can lead to added consequences beyond the original amount.

Washington suspensions follow repeat moving-violation convictions within set windows. An unresolved ticket can complicate your record, so it is better to respond before the deadline on the citation.

We do not list exact late fees or collection steps here because those amounts are not part of the figures we verified. Your court can tell you what applies to your case.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court clerk before the due date and ask what options exist.
