How much is a seatbelt ticket in Mississippi?

Nationally, seatbelt fines for adults run $10–$200 by state. In Mississippi the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (Mississippi Department of Public Safety).

We do not list a single Mississippi dollar figure because the amount on your ticket comes from the court that handles it, within the state's traffic code. The national range is context only. It is not a quote for your case.

The fine is not the full bill. Mandatory state assessments are added to every fine and often exceed it, so the total you owe can be well above the fine line.

The most reliable number is the one printed on your citation or shown by the court clerk. If the citation lists only a court date, ask the clerk what the total due would be.

How is the seatbelt fine set in Mississippi?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. It is not a figure chosen by the officer who writes the ticket.

That is why two people cited for the same violation in different places could see different totals on paper. The code sets the framework and the court sets the amount on the citation.

To see the legal text, you can read the Mississippi statutes through Justia. The Governors Highway Safety Association also keeps a summary of seatbelt laws by state at GHSA State Laws, which is useful for comparing how states treat the violation.

  • Read the citation for the violation description and the court named on it.
  • Check whether it lists an amount or tells you to appear.
  • Contact the court clerk with your citation number for the total.

Why can the total be higher than the fine?

Mandatory state assessments are added to every fine in Mississippi, and they often exceed the fine itself. This is common for minor traffic violations, which is why a low-looking fine can still produce a larger bill.

We do not publish a dollar amount for these assessments, because we have no locked figure for them. What matters is that you should ask for the all-in amount, not just the fine.

When you call the clerk, ask: "What is the total due, including all assessments, if I pay by the date on the citation?"

Paying only the fine amount and skipping the assessments can leave a balance open. Get the total in writing or from the court's official payment channel.

How many points does a seatbelt ticket add in Mississippi?

Mississippi has no point system. Suspensions follow repeat convictions and specific offenses rather than a running point total.

So there is no point value to look up for a seatbelt ticket. The consequences that matter are the money you owe and how convictions stack up over time.

If you have other recent convictions, it is worth understanding how repeat convictions are treated, because that is where suspensions come from. The Mississippi Department of Public Safety (DPS) is the agency to ask about your driving record and license status.

Does a seatbelt ticket raise insurance in Mississippi?

Nationally, moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Mississippi or seatbelt-specific one.

Whether a seatbelt citation affects your own rate depends on your insurer's rules and your record. We cannot predict that, and we do not recommend any insurer.

  • Ask your insurer how it treats a seatbelt conviction, if at all.
  • Ask the court whether a conviction is recorded if you pay.
  • Check your own policy documents for surcharge language.

Treat the 20% as a rough benchmark for moving violations in general. It is not a promise about your premium.

Can a seatbelt ticket be dismissed or reduced?

That is up to the court that issued the citation. Because the court sets the amount, it is also the place to ask what options exist for your ticket.

We cannot say that any particular outcome is available in your case, and this page is not legal advice. What you can do is gather facts and ask the right questions.

  1. Read the citation closely for errors in the date, location, vehicle or violation description.
  2. Note the court date and any deadline to respond.
  3. Ask the clerk what your options are, such as paying, contesting or appearing.
  4. If you want advice on strategy, speak with a licensed Mississippi attorney of your own choosing.

If you plan to contest, do not let the response date pass. Missing it can create a separate problem.

What happens if I don't pay a seatbelt ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. The court can add consequences beyond the original amount, and the details depend on the court and the Mississippi traffic code.

We have no locked figures for late penalties, so we do not state any. Mississippi's suspensions follow repeat convictions and specific offenses, so an unresolved ticket combined with a poor record can matter more than a single ticket alone.

If you cannot pay the full amount by the deadline, contact the court clerk before the date passes and ask what arrangements are possible.

Keep proof of anything you pay, including the receipt, the date and the citation number.
