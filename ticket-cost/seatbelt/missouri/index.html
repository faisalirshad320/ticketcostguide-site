How much is a seatbelt ticket in Missouri?

Nationally, seatbelt fines run $10–$200 for adults by state; in Missouri the exact amount is set by the court on the citation — Seatbelt Ticket cost in Missouri (Missouri Department of Revenue — Driver License Bureau). We do not give a single Missouri dollar figure, because the amount depends on the court that handles your case.

The $10–$200 range is a national spread across states. It is useful context, but it is not a Missouri fine schedule.

The number you actually owe appears on your citation or in the court's payable-amount listing. If neither shows an amount, contact the court clerk named on the ticket.

Bottom line: the national range is $10–$200 for adults. Missouri's amount is whatever the court sets on your citation.

How is a Missouri seatbelt fine set?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. Missouri's statutes are available through Missouri statutes via Justia, and the state's seatbelt requirements are also summarized by the Governors Highway Safety Association.

Because the court sets the amount, it can differ by county or by municipal court. Two drivers cited for the same thing in different places may see different totals.

That is why a statewide figure would mislead you. The reliable number is the one printed on your own citation.

Also check which court the ticket names. A municipal court and a state-level court may handle the case differently, and that can affect both the amount and the points.

Do court costs and surcharges add to the fine?

In Missouri, court costs are included in the scheduled payable amounts. If your citation lists a payable amount, that figure is meant to be the total you pay to resolve the ticket without a court appearance.

Do not assume extra fees will be stacked on top of a scheduled amount. Still, confirm with the clerk what the listed total covers, especially if you pay through a third-party online portal.

If your case goes to a hearing instead of a scheduled payment, the court sets the amount. In that situation, ask the clerk what the total will include before you commit.

  • Look for a "payable amount" or "total due" line on the citation.
  • Confirm the payment method the court accepts.
  • Keep the receipt or confirmation as proof of payment.

How many points does a seatbelt ticket add in Missouri?

Missouri's points system uses 2 points for a municipal conviction and 3 points for a state charge. Which one applies depends on how the ticket was filed and which court handles it.

Reaching 8 points in 18 months leads to a suspension. Points from several violations in that window add together, so a seatbelt conviction counts alongside any others.

Check your citation to see whether it is a municipal or a state charge, and confirm with the court or the Missouri Department of Revenue Driver License Bureau how the conviction will be recorded on your record.

Points depend on the conviction, not just the ticket. A ticket you have not yet resolved is different from a conviction entered on your record.

Does a seatbelt ticket raise your insurance?

Nationally, moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national average, not a Missouri-specific or seatbelt-specific figure.

Whether your insurer reacts at all depends on how it treats the violation, how it checks your record, and when your policy renews. Some insurers weigh a seatbelt conviction differently from a speeding conviction.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer. If you are worried about a rate change, ask your own insurer how it treats this kind of conviction before deciding how to resolve the ticket.

Can a Missouri seatbelt ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Your options are set by the court that issued the citation, so the citation and the court clerk are the first places to look. Common paths in traffic courts generally include paying the scheduled amount, contesting the charge, or asking the court about alternative resolutions.

Paying the ticket usually resolves it, but it can also mean accepting a conviction and any points that come with it. Contesting means appearing in court or following the court's process for a plea.

Whether a reduction or dismissal is available depends on the court and the facts. We cannot tell you what a particular court will allow, and this page is not legal advice.

  • Read the citation for the court name, deadline and instructions.
  • Ask the clerk what options the court offers for this violation.
  • Consider speaking with a licensed attorney if you are unsure how a conviction would affect you.

What happens if I don't pay a seatbelt ticket?

Ignoring a citation rarely makes it go away. Courts can treat unpaid or unanswered tickets as a failure to respond, which can lead to added consequences, including license problems.

The specific penalties and timing depend on the court and are listed on your citation. We do not state deadlines here because they are set by the issuing court.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the clerk before the deadline passes and ask what the court allows. Courts are generally easier to work with before a missed deadline than after.

Always act on the deadline printed on your own citation. It controls, not any general guide.
