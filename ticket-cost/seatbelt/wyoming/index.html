How much is a seatbelt ticket in Wyoming?

Nationally, a seatbelt ticket for an adult runs $10–$200, depending on the state (Wyoming Department of Transportation). In Wyoming, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation.

This page does not state a specific Wyoming dollar figure. The amount that applies to you is the one printed on your citation or confirmed by the court. The national range is context, not a Wyoming price.

Your total can also be higher than the fine alone, because court fees are added per the local schedule.

Bottom line: read your citation first, then confirm the full amount owed with the court clerk before you pay.

How is a Wyoming seatbelt fine set?

The exact fine for this violation in Wyoming is set by the court within the state's traffic code. That means the number is not a single price shared by every court. It comes from the court that handles your citation.

The traffic code itself is published in the Wyoming statutes (Wyoming statutes via Justia). If you want to read the underlying rule, that is the place to start. Statute text can be updated, so treat any copy as a reference and confirm current wording with the court.

For a state-by-state view of how seatbelt laws differ, the Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a summary (Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws). That is useful for comparison, but it does not replace your citation.

To find the amount that applies to you:

  1. Check the citation for a listed fine or a court appearance notice.
  2. Look for the issuing court's name and contact details.
  3. Ask the clerk what the fine and added court fees come to in total.
  4. Ask what payment or response deadlines apply.

What court costs and surcharges are added?

Court fees are added per the local schedule. This is the main reason two people with the same violation can see different totals.

We do not list a dollar amount for these fees, because the figure depends on the court that handles your case. The clerk's office can tell you the combined amount owed.

When you compare the number on your citation with what you are asked to pay, check for these items:

  • The base fine set by the court.
  • Court fees added under the local schedule.
  • Any extra charge for late payment, if you miss a deadline.

If the totals do not match, ask the clerk to explain the difference line by line.

How many points does a seatbelt ticket carry in Wyoming?

Wyoming has no demerit-point system. A seatbelt ticket does not add points to your record in the way it would in a point-based state.

That does not mean convictions never matter. Suspensions follow repeat convictions and court action, so a pattern of violations can still affect your license.

If you move between states, remember that other states may use points. A conviction in Wyoming does not tell you how another state or an insurer will treat your record.

No points in Wyoming is not the same as no consequences. Repeat convictions and court action are what lead to suspensions.

Does a seatbelt ticket raise my insurance?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a Wyoming one, and not specific to seatbelt tickets.

Whether a particular insurer counts a seatbelt conviction as a moving violation, and how much it changes your rate, depends on the company and your policy. Your driving history, your current rate, and your insurer's own rules all play a part.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend insurers. To find out how a conviction could affect you, ask your insurer or agent directly. Ask before you decide how to respond to the citation, not after.

  • Ask whether a seatbelt conviction is surcharged under your policy.
  • Ask how long a conviction stays on your rating record.
  • Ask whether an existing clean record changes the outcome.

Can a Wyoming seatbelt ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Your options are set by the court that issued the citation, so the citation and the clerk are your best sources. In general, a driver can pay the ticket, contest it, or contact the court to ask what other choices exist.

Paying usually closes the matter, but it can also mean a conviction on your record. A conviction is what insurers typically look at, so consider that before you pay.

Contesting means asking the court to hear the case. A court may dismiss or change a ticket for reasons that depend on the facts and the court's rules. We cannot say whether that applies to you.

Questions to ask the clerk:

  • What is the full amount owed, including court fees?
  • What is the deadline to pay or respond?
  • How do I contest the citation, and what does that involve?
  • Does the court offer any alternative to a conviction for this violation?

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot give legal advice. If you want advice on your specific case, consider speaking with a licensed attorney in Wyoming.

What happens if I don't pay a seatbelt ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts can add steps and charges when a deadline passes, and the citation may require you to respond by a specific date.

In Wyoming, suspensions follow repeat convictions and court action. An unresolved ticket that leads to court action can therefore put your license at risk, even if the original violation seems minor.

If you cannot pay the full amount by the deadline, contact the court before the date passes. Clerks can explain what the court allows and what happens next.

Do not wait for a second notice. Check your citation for the response deadline and act before it.
