How much is a seatbelt ticket in New Jersey?

Nationally, adult seatbelt fines range from $10–$200 depending on the state. In New Jersey, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (New Jersey Motor Vehicle Commission).

We do not publish a single New Jersey dollar figure on this page, because the amount is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The number printed on your citation, or the figure the court gives you, is the one that applies to you.

The $10–$200 range is a national spread across states. It is not a New Jersey fine schedule, so do not treat either end of it as your expected bill.

Court costs and other add-ons can change the total you owe. Ask the court for the full amount before you pay.

How is the seatbelt fine set in New Jersey?

New Jersey's traffic code sets the framework, and the court decides the exact fine on the citation. That is why two drivers cited for the same thing can see different numbers depending on the court and the circumstances.

Because the amount varies by court, a figure you hear from a friend or find in an old forum post may not match yours. Statutes and court practice also change over time.

To see the current rules, you can read the state's traffic code through New Jersey statutes via Justia. The Motor Vehicle Commission is the state agency that handles driver records and points, at nj.gov/mvc.

For a national comparison of how states treat seatbelt laws, the Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a state-by-state reference.

How many points does a seatbelt ticket carry in New Jersey?

The state points system we have on file works in tiers: 2 points (1–14 over), 4 points (15–29 over), and 5 points (30+ over). Those tiers are written in terms of how far over a limit a driver was, so they describe speeding-type violations.

We cannot confirm from our locked sources that a seatbelt violation adds points to a record. Check the citation itself, ask the court clerk, or look at your record through the Motor Vehicle Commission.

Here is why the point system still matters to you:

  • 12 points = suspension.
  • 6+ points within 3 years adds an MVC insurance surcharge of $150+.

If you already have points from other tickets, even a small change in your total can move you closer to those thresholds.

What are the MVC surcharges on top of the fine?

Surcharges are separate from the court fine. At 6+ points within 3 years, the Motor Vehicle Commission adds an insurance surcharge of $150+ (New Jersey Motor Vehicle Commission).

These MVC surcharges are billed annually for 3 years on top of court fines. That means a surcharge can cost more over time than the one-time ticket payment.

  • Court fine: set by the court on the citation.
  • MVC surcharge: $150+ once you reach 6+ points within 3 years.
  • Billing: annually for 3 years.

If your seatbelt ticket does not add points, this surcharge would not be triggered by that ticket alone. It matters only if your total points reach the threshold.

Does a seatbelt ticket raise insurance rates?

Moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums by about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a New Jersey-specific one.

Whether a seatbelt citation counts as a moving violation for your insurer depends on the company and on how the violation is recorded. We do not have a locked source that answers this for New Jersey insurers.

If you want to know your own risk, you can ask your insurer how it treats a seatbelt conviction. TicketCostGuide does not recommend any specific insurer.

The ~20% average is a national benchmark for moving-violation convictions. Your own change could be higher, lower, or none at all.

Separately, the MVC surcharge described above is a state charge, not an insurance premium. It applies only at 6+ points within 3 years.

Can a seatbelt ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Court options depend on the court and on the facts of the stop, and we do not give legal advice. In general, a driver who receives a citation can pay it or contest it in court.

Questions worth raising with the court clerk:

  • What is the total amount due, including any court costs?
  • What are the options for responding to this citation?
  • Does this violation add points to my record?

If you want legal advice about a specific citation, a licensed New Jersey attorney of your own choosing can give it. We do not recommend any individual attorney.

Since the fine is set by the court on the citation, the court is also where any question about the amount gets answered.

What happens if I don't pay a seatbelt ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts can add costs or take further action on unpaid tickets, and the details depend on the court that issued yours.

We do not have a locked figure for late fees in New Jersey, so we do not state one. Read the instructions on the citation and contact the court listed on it.

Remember that more problems on a record can raise your point total. At 12 points the state system calls for suspension, so it pays to know where your record stands. You can check through the Motor Vehicle Commission.
