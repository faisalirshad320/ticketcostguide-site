How much is a seatbelt ticket in North Dakota?

Nationally, adult seatbelt fines run $10–$200 depending on the state. In North Dakota, the exact amount is set by the court on the citation (North Dakota Department of Transportation).

That is why this page does not quote a single North Dakota dollar figure. The national range is a comparison across states, not a price list for North Dakota.

The number printed on your citation, or the one the court gives you, is the amount that applies to your case.

If you want to see how North Dakota compares with other states, the Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a state-by-state summary of seatbelt laws (Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws).

How is a North Dakota seatbelt fine set?

The fine for this violation is set by the court within the state's traffic code. The statutes themselves are published online (North Dakota statutes via Justia).

In practice, that means you will not find a different price from one website to another that you can rely on. The citation, or the court that handles it, is the source that matters.

  • Look at the citation for an amount or a court instruction.
  • Check which court is listed and how it handles payment.
  • If anything is unclear, contact the court clerk. Clerks can explain procedure but cannot give legal advice.

TicketCostGuide does not guess at the figure. If a source does not give an exact North Dakota amount, we say so.

Are there extra court costs or surcharges?

For this violation, the statutory fee is usually the whole amount. There is no large cost stack on top of it.

That is different from some other kinds of tickets, where surcharges and court costs can multiply the base fine. For a seatbelt ticket, the number on the citation is generally the number to plan around.

"Usually" matters here. If the court adds a charge, it should appear in the paperwork you receive. Ask the clerk if the total does not match the citation.

Other costs, such as time off work to appear in court, are outside the fine itself and vary by person.

How many points does a seatbelt ticket carry in North Dakota?

The locked North Dakota information says points are assigned by mph over on the ND schedule, and that 12+ points leads to suspension. That description is built around speed, so it does not give a point value for a seatbelt violation.

For that reason, we do not state a seatbelt point number. Whether a seatbelt conviction adds any points to your record is something to confirm with the North Dakota Department of Transportation (North Dakota Department of Transportation).

  • 12+ points = suspension on the North Dakota schedule.
  • Points are tied to the violation and the schedule, so check how your specific citation is classified.
  • Your driving record shows what has actually been recorded.

Rules can change. Check the statutes (North Dakota statutes via Justia) or the state agency for the current version.

Does a seatbelt ticket raise insurance in North Dakota?

Nationally, moving-violation convictions typically raise US premiums about 20% on average. That is a national figure, not a North Dakota quote.

Whether a particular seatbelt ticket affects your premium depends on how your insurer treats it and on whether it ends up as a conviction on your record. TicketCostGuide does not recommend insurers or predict what any company will charge.

  • The 20% figure is an average across the US, so your result could be higher, lower or zero.
  • An insurer usually learns about a ticket through your driving record, often at renewal.
  • Your policy documents or your insurer can tell you how violations are treated.

If you are weighing whether to pay or contest, the possible effect on premiums is worth considering alongside the fine itself.

Can a seatbelt ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Some tickets are dismissed or reduced, but this depends on the court and the facts. We have no locked source that gives North Dakota-specific rules, so we do not list guaranteed outcomes.

What you can do is read the citation closely and find out what choices the court offers. Typical options on traffic citations include paying, contesting, or asking the court about alternatives.

  • Check the citation for the court name, the date and the instructions.
  • Ask the clerk what options the court allows for your violation.
  • Keep copies of anything you submit.

This page is general information, not legal advice. For advice about your own case, you would need to speak to a licensed attorney.

What happens if I don't pay a seatbelt ticket?

Ignoring a ticket generally makes the situation worse. The specific consequences in North Dakota depend on the court and the statutes, and we do not have locked figures for penalties.

What we can say is that the fine is set by the court, so the court is where to go if you cannot pay on time or have questions about deadlines.

If you believe you cannot meet the deadline on your citation, contact the court before it passes rather than after.

Because the North Dakota schedule can lead to suspension at 12+ points, it is also wise to know where your record stands. The state DOT is the place to ask about your own record (North Dakota Department of Transportation).
