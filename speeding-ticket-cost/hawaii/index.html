How much is a speeding ticket in Hawaii?

The fine is set by the district court. For ordinary speeding it is commonly in the $55–$200 range before fees (Hawaii Department of Transportation). That range is a typical band, not a fixed schedule, so your own ticket may land at a different point within it or outside it.

The amount on your citation or on the court notice is the number that matters. The exact figure can vary by court, and we do not publish a per-mile-over price list because we have no sourced one.

The $55–$200 range is before fees. Court fees and assessments are added by the district court, so the amount you actually pay is higher than the base fine.

The statutes behind these penalties are HRS §291C-102 and §291C-105, available through Justia's Hawaii code.

What is excessive speeding in Hawaii and what does it cost?

Hawaii treats 'excessive speeding' as a separate and much more serious offense. It applies at 30+ over the limit or 80+ mph. The penalty is $500–$1,000, with possible license suspension and jail under §291C-105 (Hawaii Department of Transportation).

This is the sharp edge of Hawaii's speeding law. It converts a ticket into a criminal matter, which changes what is at stake well beyond the dollar amount.

  • Fine: $500–$1,000 under the excessive-speeding provision.
  • License: suspension is possible.
  • Jail: possible under the statute.

If your citation mentions excessive speeding, read it closely and consider getting qualified legal advice. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot advise you on your case.

What fees and surcharges are added to the fine?

Court fees and assessments are added by the district court. We do not have a sourced dollar amount for these, so we do not state one.

The amount varies by court, and the total due is shown on the citation or the court's notice. Treat the base fine as the starting point and expect the final bill to be higher.

  • Base fine: commonly $55–$200 for ordinary speeding.
  • Court fees and assessments: added by the district court.
  • Excessive speeding: $500–$1,000, with the same court-added fees applying on top.

If the total on your paperwork looks different from the range above, the paperwork controls. Ask the court clerk to explain how it was calculated.

How many points does a speeding ticket add in Hawaii?

None. Hawaii has no demerit-point system. There is no point total to build up or watch.

License consequences come from the convictions themselves. Repeat convictions and excessive-speeding convictions drive license action directly.

No points does not mean no consequences. A conviction can still lead to higher insurance costs, and an excessive-speeding conviction can bring license suspension.

If you are comparing states, check how others handle points. The Governors Highway Safety Association's state laws page is a useful starting reference for state traffic-safety laws.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance in Hawaii?

It can. Published market analyses show that a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal.

That is a national average, not a Hawaii figure. Your own change depends on your insurer, your driving record and the type of violation.

Because the increase applies at renewal and can recur, it can cost more over time than the fine itself. A $55–$200 ticket with a premium increase of roughly 20–25% on a yearly policy may add up quickly.

We do not recommend specific insurers. If you are worried about your rate, ask your own insurer how it treats a conviction.

Can a Hawaii speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

The district court handles speeding tickets, so any request to contest or reduce one goes through the court. The citation itself should explain your options and how to respond.

We have no sourced data on how often tickets are dismissed or reduced in Hawaii, so we do not give odds. Outcomes depend on the facts and on the court.

The stakes differ by tier:

  • Ordinary speeding: a fine commonly in the $55–$200 range before fees.
  • Excessive speeding: a criminal matter with $500–$1,000 in fines and possible suspension and jail.

Because excessive speeding is criminal, many drivers facing it look for qualified legal advice. We cannot recommend a particular attorney.

What happens if I don't pay a Hawaii speeding ticket?

Ignoring a ticket does not make it go away. The district court sets the consequences for non-payment, and we have no sourced list of them to quote here.

Leaving a ticket unresolved can mean further court action and added costs, and it can put your license at risk. Court fees and assessments are already added to the base fine, so delay is unlikely to lower the total.

If you cannot pay or cannot make your court date, contact the court named on your citation as soon as possible and ask what options exist. Do not rely on a guess about deadlines. Read your paperwork.

What is excessive speeding in Hawaii?

Does Hawaii use a point system for speeding tickets?
