How much is a speeding ticket in Kansas?

The Kansas figure to start from is this: uniform fine schedule amounts by mph over, commonly about $45–$300, plus court costs of roughly $100+ that vary by court (Kansas Department of Revenue — Division of Vehicles, last reviewed Oct 2026).

That means the number printed on your ticket is rarely the whole bill. The fine reflects how far over the limit you were. The court costs are layered on top and depend on which court handles the case.

The schedule is tied to K.S.A. §8-1336 (uniform fine schedule), which you can find in the state code via Justia. Because the fine rises with mph over, a few miles over the limit sits toward the low end of the range. A much higher speed sits toward the top.

We do not list an exact fine for each mph bracket. The locked range is the only fine figure we publish. Check your citation or the court's posted schedule for the exact amount on your ticket.

What is the difference between the fine and court costs?

The fine is the penalty for the violation. In Kansas it comes from the uniform fine schedule, so the same offense at the same mph over is meant to carry the same fine statewide.

Court costs are separate. They are added by the municipal or district court that handles your ticket, and they are roughly $100+ and vary by court.

This is the key point for budgeting: the uniform schedule sets the fine, but court costs are where totals diverge. Two drivers cited for the same speed can owe different totals if their cases are heard in different courts.

  • Fine: commonly about $45–$300, driven by mph over.
  • Court costs: roughly $100+, driven by the court.
  • Total: the two added together, so expect the bill to exceed the fine alone.

We cannot give an exact court-cost figure for a given county or city. That amount varies, and the court clerk is the best source for it.

What drives the total on a Kansas speeding ticket?

Based on the locked facts, two things drive what you pay: how fast you were going over the limit, and which court processes the ticket.

The first factor moves the fine within the commonly cited $45–$300 range. The second moves the court-cost portion, which is roughly $100+ and varies by court.

A third factor is your record. Kansas has no demerit-point system, but three moving-violation convictions within 12 months can trigger suspension. A ticket that is your third in a year carries a different risk than a first one, even if the fine is the same.

Finally, there is the longer-term cost. Insurers often reprice after a conviction, which can add more over time than the ticket itself (see below).

How many points does a speeding ticket add in Kansas?

None. Kansas has no demerit-point system, so a speeding conviction does not add points to your license in the way it would in many other states.

That does not mean convictions are ignored. Three moving-violation convictions within 12 months can trigger suspension. The count of convictions matters here, not a point total.

  • No points are assigned per offense.
  • The suspension trigger is three moving-violation convictions within 12 months.
  • Speeding is a moving violation, so it can count toward that tally.

For license rules and your own record, the Kansas Department of Revenue — Division of Vehicles is the agency to check. For a cross-state comparison of traffic laws, the Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a state laws resource.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance in Kansas?

It can. Published market analyses indicate a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal.

That is a national average, not a Kansas figure. Your own change could be higher, lower or nothing, depending on your insurer, your record and how your policy is rated.

The effect usually shows up at renewal rather than right away. Because the increase can apply to a full policy term, it can add up to more than the fine and court costs combined.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend or compare insurers. If you want to know how a ticket would affect your rate, ask your own insurer or agent.

Can a Kansas speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome depends on the facts and the court. We do not publish dismissal rates or promise any result.

Because the fine follows a uniform schedule, a reduction usually means the charge changes, for example to a different offense or a lower speed bracket, rather than the court simply picking a smaller number. Whether that is offered varies by court and prosecutor.

Practical steps that cost nothing:

  • Read the citation for the court name, the date and the listed mph over.
  • Contact the court clerk to ask what the total is, including court costs, and what response options exist.
  • Keep in mind the three-convictions-in-12-months suspension trigger when deciding how to respond.

What happens if I don't pay a Kansas speeding ticket?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. Courts generally treat an unanswered citation as a failure to comply, which can lead to added consequences beyond the original fine and court costs.

The exact steps differ by court, and we do not state specific deadlines or penalty amounts because they are not in our locked data. The citation and the court clerk are the reliable sources for your due date and options.

  • Find the court listed on your ticket.
  • Ask what is owed in total, fine plus court costs.
  • Ask what choices you have before the due date passes.

A paid or resolved ticket can still count as a conviction, so consider the effect on the three-in-12-months suspension trigger and on your insurance renewal.

Frequently asked questions

Are court costs included in the Kansas speeding fine?

No. The uniform schedule sets the fine. Court costs are added separately by the municipal or district court, roughly $100+ and varying by court. That is why two drivers with the same fine can owe different totals. Ask the court clerk for the full amount.

Does Kansas use points for speeding tickets?

No. Kansas has no demerit-point system, so speeding does not add points. However, three moving-violation convictions within 12 months can trigger suspension. The number of convictions in that window is what matters, so check your record with the Division of Vehicles.

Can I lose my license for speeding tickets in Kansas?

Three moving-violation convictions within 12 months can trigger suspension. Kansas has no points system, so the count of convictions is the trigger. Speeding is a moving violation and can count toward it. Check your driving record with the Kansas Division of Vehicles.
