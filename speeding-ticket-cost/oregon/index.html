How much is a speeding ticket in Oregon?

Oregon sets speeding fines by violation class. The presumptive amounts are $115 for Class D, $165 for Class C, $265 for Class B and $440 for Class A, which covers 30+ over (Oregon DMV (ODOT)).

Those are the fine amounts the court starts from. Your citation shows which class your violation falls into, and that class is the biggest driver of what you owe.

Two things can raise the number. Many offenses in work and school zones are doubled, and 100+ mph carries a $1,150 presumptive fine and mandatory suspension.

Your citation is the best guide to your own total. Other court charges can vary by county and court, and we do not publish an exact local figure for them.

How does Oregon's presumptive fine system work?

Oregon uses a presumptive-fine system under ORS §811.109 / §153.019 (ORS §811.109 / §153.019 — state code via Justia). The judge starts from a fixed statewide number tied to the violation class.

This means the same class of violation begins at the same amount no matter which court hears it. A driver in one county and a driver in another face the same starting point for the same class.

The classes run from D at the low end to A at the top:

  • Class D: $115
  • Class C: $165
  • Class B: $265
  • Class A (30+ over): $440

The statute is the place to read the exact wording. We summarize it here and do not replace it.

Are speeding fines higher in work and school zones?

Yes, for many offenses. Fines are doubled in work and school zones, and that doubling is set directly in statute rather than left to a local court to decide (ORS §811.109 / §153.019 — state code via Justia).

The word "many" matters. Doubling applies to many offenses, not necessarily every one, so check the statute and your citation to see whether it applies to your violation.

We do not list a doubled dollar amount for each class because the locked sources we rely on do not give one. Treat the doubled figure as a multiple of the class amount shown on your citation.

In practice, a ticket that would sit at a lower class can cost much more when it is issued in a marked zone. Slowing down near schools and road work is the simplest way to avoid that.

What happens if I'm caught going 100+ mph in Oregon?

Driving 100+ mph carries a $1,150 presumptive fine and mandatory suspension (Oregon DMV (ODOT)). This is the top of the scale and well above the $440 Class A amount for 30+ over.

The enhancement is set directly in statute. That means the suspension is not a matter of the judge's discretion in the way a lower-class fine might feel.

If you are facing this level of charge, the consequences go beyond the fine. Losing your license affects work, commuting and insurance, so read your paperwork closely.

How many points does a speeding ticket add in Oregon?

None. Oregon has no point system. Instead, suspensions follow repeat convictions, such as under habitual-offender rules, and specific offenses like 100+ mph.

That changes how you should think about a single ticket. A lone conviction at a lower class does not add to a running point total. The risk grows as convictions pile up.

Not having points does not mean a conviction is harmless. It still goes on your record, it can matter for repeat-offense rules, and it can show up when your insurer reviews your driving history.

For the rules on suspensions and your own record, the Oregon DMV (ODOT) is the authority.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance in Oregon?

It often does. Published market analyses suggest a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal.

That is a national average, not an Oregon figure. Your own change could be higher, lower or nil depending on your insurer, your record and the violation.

Because insurance can add up over several renewals, it may cost more than the fine itself. A $115 ticket and a 20–25% rise on a yearly premium are very different kinds of cost.

We do not recommend any insurer. To learn what a conviction would do to your own rate, ask your insurer directly. For comparisons of state rules, the Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws page is a useful reference.

Can an Oregon speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Possibly, but we cannot tell you how your case will go. Because Oregon uses presumptive fines, the judge starts from a fixed statewide number for the class (ORS §811.109 / §153.019 — state code via Justia).

What you pay therefore depends mostly on the class of the violation. Contesting the charge, or the class it was written under, is the main way the starting number could change.

Before you decide, read the citation for the violation class, any zone listed and the court's instructions. Pay attention to the response deadline printed on it, since missing it can create further problems.

If you are unsure, a licensed attorney can advise you. We do not recommend a specific one.
