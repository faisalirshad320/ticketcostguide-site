How much is a speeding ticket in Nevada?

Since 2023 most minor traffic offenses are civil infractions with penalties up to $500 set by the court — commonly $50–$305 by mph over, plus fees — Nevada civil infraction penalty range (Nevada Department of Motor Vehicles).

That means the fine itself is not one flat number. The faster you were going compared with the posted limit, the higher the penalty is likely to sit inside that range.

The fine is only part of the bill. Administrative fees are added by the court, and the amounts vary by jurisdiction. We do not publish an exact fee for any county because we could not source one.

Bottom line: expect a penalty in the $50–$305 range for a typical speeding ticket, plus court-added fees that differ by county or court. The ceiling for the penalty is $500.

Your citation and the court that handles it are the best sources for your exact total. If the ticket lists an amount, that is the number to plan around.

What drives the total cost of a Nevada speeding ticket?

Several things move the final amount up or down. Some are on the ticket itself and some show up later.

  • Miles per hour over the limit. The commonly cited $50–$305 range is tied to mph over, so a larger overage generally means a higher penalty.
  • The court and county. Administrative fees are added by the court and vary by jurisdiction, so the same speed can produce a different total in different places.
  • Court discretion. Penalties are set by the court, up to $500 for a civil infraction.
  • Points on your record. Points do not add to the fine, but they can affect your license and, indirectly, your insurance.

The statute behind the civil infraction framework is NRS §484B.600 / §484A.7043, available through Justia's Nevada code. Reading the current text is the most reliable way to see how the law is worded.

Other costs, such as time off work or a lawyer if you choose to hire one, are personal and are not part of the sourced figures on this page.

Is a speeding ticket a crime in Nevada now?

For most minor traffic offenses, no. Since 2023 they have generally been treated as civil infractions rather than criminal misdemeanors, with penalties set by the court (Nevada Department of Motor Vehicles).

The change is often called decriminalization. It matters mainly for what happens if a ticket goes unpaid, which is covered below.

Not every traffic matter is a minor one. This page covers ordinary speeding tickets, and we do not sourced figures for more serious offenses. If your citation lists something other than a standard speeding violation, check the citation and the court's instructions.

The relevant provisions are NRS §484B.600 / §484A.7043 (Justia). TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and this is not legal advice.

How many points does a speeding ticket add in Nevada?

A speeding conviction adds 1–5 demerit points, depending on mph over (Nevada Department of Motor Vehicles). A bigger overage generally means more points.

The key threshold is 12 points in 12 months, which results in a 6-month suspension. A single ordinary speeding ticket will not reach that level on its own, but it moves you closer, especially if you already have other violations on your record.

  • 1–5 points per speeding conviction, by mph over.
  • 12 points in 12 months = 6-month license suspension.

To see how many points you currently have, check with the Nevada DMV directly. We cannot see your record, and we do not give figures beyond those above.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance rates?

It often does. Published market analyses indicate that a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal.

Important: 20–25% is a national average, not a Nevada figure. Your own change could be higher, lower or nil depending on your insurer, your record and how far over the limit you were.

Insurers decide for themselves how to treat a conviction, so TicketCostGuide does not predict what yours will do. If you want to know, ask your insurer or compare quotes at renewal.

Because the increase usually shows up at renewal rather than right away, the full cost of a ticket can arrive months after you pay the fine.

Can a Nevada speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but we cannot promise it and we do not recommend any attorney or service. Because penalties are set by the court, the court is where any request for a different outcome is handled.

Practical steps people commonly take include the following.

  1. Read the citation carefully for the court name, the response instructions and any deadlines.
  2. Contact the court clerk and ask what options exist for your type of ticket, including whether you can contest it or ask about alternatives.
  3. Gather anything relevant, such as photos or records, if you plan to contest.
  4. Consider whether you want legal advice from a licensed professional.

Whether any option reduces points, fees or the penalty depends on the court. We do not have sourced figures for savings, so we do not state any.

What happens if I don't pay a Nevada speeding ticket?

Decriminalization ended arrest warrants for ordinary unpaid tickets — collections and license holds replaced them.

That does not make an unpaid ticket harmless. Ignoring it can lead to the debt being sent to collections and to a hold on your license, which can be harder to deal with than the original fine.

  • No arrest warrant for an ordinary unpaid ticket.
  • Collections can be used to pursue the amount owed.
  • License holds can limit your ability to drive legally or renew.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court listed on your citation before the due date and ask what is available. We do not have sourced details on payment plans, so we do not describe them.

Frequently asked questions

How many points is a speeding ticket in Nevada?

Are court fees included in the Nevada speeding fine?

No. The penalty range of $50–$305 by mph over is separate from fees. Administrative fees are added by the court, and the amounts vary by jurisdiction. Check your citation or ask the court clerk for the exact total owed.

Can I go to jail or get a warrant for not paying a Nevada speeding ticket?

For ordinary unpaid tickets, decriminalization ended arrest warrants. Collections and license holds replaced them. Ignoring a ticket can still cause real problems, so contact the court listed on your citation if you cannot pay.
