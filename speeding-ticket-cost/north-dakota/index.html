How much is a speeding ticket in North Dakota?

North Dakota is among the lowest-cost states in the nation for speeding. The fixed statutory fees run roughly $5–$100 by mph over, with a higher scale on non-interstate roads and for 16+ over on highways (North Dakota Department of Transportation).

The fee table is written directly into state law, N.D.C.C. §39-06.1-06, which you can read through Justia's North Dakota code. Because the amounts come from the statute and not from a local schedule, totals are small and uniform.

Bottom line: the fee depends mainly on how many mph over the limit you were going and on the type of road. Your citation should show the amount that applies to your stop.

This page gives the locked range only. We do not list an exact dollar figure for each mph bracket, because the statute is the authority and your citation is the document that matters.

What drives the total on a North Dakota speeding ticket?

Three things matter most, and all of them come from the statute rather than from a judge's discretion.

  • Mph over the limit: the fee rises as the speed over the limit rises, within the roughly $5–$100 range.
  • Type of road: the statute uses a higher scale on non-interstate roads.
  • Very high speeds on highways: a higher scale also applies for 16+ over on highways.

Because these inputs are fixed, two drivers cited for the same speed over on the same type of road should see the same statutory fee. That makes North Dakota easier to predict than states where each county or court adds its own costs.

If you want to see how other states set their fee structures, the Governors Highway Safety Association state laws page is a useful starting point for comparing general traffic laws.

Are there surcharges or extra fees on top of the fine?

Usually not in any large way. The statutory fee is usually the whole amount, so there is no large cost stack on top of it.

That is a real difference from many states, where a base fine can be followed by a long list of court costs, assessments and add-ons that multiply the total. In North Dakota the fee table in N.D.C.C. §39-06.1-06 is set directly in statute, which keeps totals small and uniform.

We do not publish a dollar amount for any extra charge, because the locked sources do not list one. If your citation or the court shows an additional line item, ask the court clerk what it is for.

Tip: compare the amount on your citation with the statutory fee scale before you pay, and check that the road type and mph over match what was written.

How many points does a speeding ticket add in North Dakota?

Points are assigned by mph over on the North Dakota schedule. A faster speed means more points on your driving record.

The key threshold is that 12+ points = suspension. We do not list the point value for each speed bracket here, since the sources we are allowed to cite do not give a bracket-by-bracket breakdown. The North Dakota Department of Transportation is the place to confirm how many points apply to your specific speed (North Dakota Department of Transportation).

Points matter more than the fee itself, because the fee is small but a suspension has consequences well beyond it. A driver who already has points from earlier violations should check their record before assuming one more ticket is minor.

Does a speeding ticket in North Dakota raise insurance?

It can. A speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal, according to published market analyses. That is a national average, not a North Dakota figure.

Your own change could be higher, lower or nonexistent. It depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the conviction is recorded. We do not recommend any insurer and cannot predict what yours will do.

The practical point: a low statutory fee does not mean a low total cost. The renewal increase can outweigh the fee, so it is worth asking your insurer how a conviction would be treated.

Remember that the 20–25% figure is an average across the country. Use it as a rough benchmark for planning, not as a quote.

Can a North Dakota speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Since the fee is set directly in statute, the amount for a given speed over and road type is fixed. A reduction would generally mean a different charge or a different speed bracket, not a discount on the same one.

We cannot say how any particular court handles requests, and TicketCostGuide does not give legal advice. Your citation lists how and where to respond, and the court clerk can explain the options in your case.

If you are weighing whether to contest the ticket, a licensed attorney can advise you on your situation. We do not recommend any specific attorney.

  • Read the citation for the response instructions.
  • Check the mph over and road type it lists against the statute.
  • Consider the points and the insurance effect, not just the fee.

What happens if I don't pay a North Dakota speeding ticket?

Ignoring a citation is rarely a good idea. Unpaid or unanswered tickets can lead to further court action, and that can create costs and problems larger than the original fee.

The sources we cite do not give specific deadlines, late charges or penalties, so we do not state any. Your citation and the court named on it are the authority on timing and next steps.

Because the statutory fee is small, paying or responding on time is usually the cheapest path. If you cannot pay, contact the court clerk before any deadline passes and ask what options exist.

Also keep in mind that points can add up. With 12+ points = suspension, a conviction that stays on your record matters more than a few dollars of fee.
