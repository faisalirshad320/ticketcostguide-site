How much is a speeding ticket in Oklahoma?

Oklahoma statutory speeding fines are commonly $10–$205 by mph over the limit, plus court costs and fees that vary by court and typically exceed the fine (Service Oklahoma). That second part matters. The fine is often only the smaller piece of what you pay.

The fine itself comes from 47 O.S. §11-801, the state speeding statute, which you can read through Justia. The statute ties the fine to how far over the limit you were driving.

Bottom line: the fine range is $10–$205, but the amount on your citation or at the court clerk's window can be higher once court costs and fees are added.

We do not publish an exact per-mph schedule or a single "typical" total, because the court costs vary by court and we cannot source one statewide number.

What drives the total on an Oklahoma speeding ticket?

Three things shape what you pay: how fast you were going over the limit, which court hears the ticket, and the costs and fees that court adds.

  • Speed over the limit. The statutory fine rises with mph over, within the commonly cited $10–$205 range.
  • The court. Court costs and statutory fees are added by the municipal or district court, so the same fine can produce different totals in different places.
  • Where you were stopped. A ticket written inside city limits may go to a municipal court, while one written elsewhere may go to a district court.

Because costs and fees typically exceed the fine, a ticket at the low end of the fine range can still cost noticeably more than the fine alone suggests.

The most reliable number is the one printed on your citation or provided by the court clerk. If the citation lists only a fine, ask the court what costs and fees will be added.

Is there a lower bracket for 1–10 mph over?

Yes, in some cases. By statute, 1–10 mph over on some highways falls into a lower "fine-only" bracket under 47 O.S. §11-801 (Justia).

That wording is narrow. It applies to some highways, not every road, and we do not list which ones. Your citation and the statute text are the places to confirm whether your stop qualifies.

"Fine-only" describes the statutory fine bracket. It does not mean court costs and fees disappear, since those are added by the court and vary by court. Ask the clerk what the full amount due is.

Tip: if your ticket was for a small amount over the limit, read the citation closely. The listed offense and the road type can affect which bracket applies.

How many points does a speeding ticket add in Oklahoma?

Ordinary speeding carries 2 points, and higher speeds can carry more. Reaching 10 points in 5 years leads to suspension (Service Oklahoma).

Points matter because they accumulate. One ticket at 2 points is a small step toward the threshold, but several tickets in a short period can add up quickly, especially if some were at higher speeds.

  • Ordinary speeding: 2 points.
  • Higher speeds: more than 2 points.
  • Suspension threshold: 10 points in 5 years.

We do not list the exact point value for each speed bracket, because it is not part of the figures we have verified. Service Oklahoma's driver services pages are the place to confirm how a specific violation is scored and what your current point total is.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance in Oklahoma?

It can. Published market analyses suggest a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal. That is a national average, not an Oklahoma figure.

Your own change could be smaller, larger, or none at all. It depends on your insurer, your driving record, how fast you were going, and whether the ticket results in a conviction on your record.

We do not recommend any insurer or give advice on shopping for coverage. If you want to know how a ticket would affect your policy, your insurer or agent can tell you how they treat convictions at renewal.

Keep the math in mind: a premium increase applies at renewal and can add up over time, so the fine and court costs may not be the full cost of a ticket.

Can an Oklahoma speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but outcomes depend on the court and the facts of your case. We cannot predict whether a particular ticket will be dismissed or reduced, and this page is not legal advice.

What we can say is that the court handling your ticket, municipal or district, sets the process and adds its own costs and fees. The citation should tell you which court that is and how to respond.

  • Read the citation for the court name, the listed offense, and any response instructions.
  • Contact the court clerk to ask what options exist and what the total amount due would be under each.
  • If you are unsure about your rights, consider speaking with a licensed attorney of your choosing.

If a different outcome changes the offense or the points, it can also change what happens with your record and with insurance. That is worth asking about before you decide how to respond.

What happens if I don't pay an Oklahoma speeding ticket?

Ignoring a ticket generally makes things worse, not better. The court that issued it can take further action, and your record can be affected.

We do not list specific deadlines, late fees, or penalties, because those are set by the court and are not among the verified figures on this page. Your citation should show the date and instructions for responding.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court clerk before the deadline and ask what options are available. Courts handle this differently, so asking early is the safest step.

Remember that points can also build toward the suspension threshold of 10 points in 5 years, so an unresolved ticket is not only a payment issue.
