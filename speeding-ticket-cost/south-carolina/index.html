How Much Does a Speeding Ticket Cost in South Carolina? Fines, Surcharges and Points

By Editorial Team, TicketCostGuide · Last reviewed 2026-10-16

South Carolina's statutory base fines are $15–$25 (10 or less over), $25–$50 (10–15), $50–$75 (15–25) and $75–$100 (25+ over). Mandatory assessments and surcharges roughly triple real totals (South Carolina Department of Motor Vehicles). Confirm your exact total with the court handling your ticket.

How much is a speeding ticket in South Carolina?

The statutory base fine is small. It runs from $15–$25 for 10 or less over the limit up to $75–$100 for 25+ over (South Carolina Department of Motor Vehicles).

Those numbers are not what most drivers pay. Mandatory assessments and surcharges are added to the base fine, and together they roughly triple real totals.

The statute's small numbers are misleading. The assessment stack is the real bill.

The base fine is set by S.C. Code §56-5-1520 (state code via Justia). The amount on your ticket or court notice is the amount that counts, so check it before you budget for the cost.

The sections below break the cost into three parts: the base fine, the assessments, and the longer-term costs of points and insurance.

What are the base fines by mph over the limit?

South Carolina uses a tiered system under S.C. Code §56-5-1520 (state code via Justia). The more you are over the limit, the higher the base fine.

  • 10 or less over: $15–$25
  • 10–15 over: $25–$50
  • 15–25 over: $50–$75
  • 25+ over: $75–$100

Each tier is a range, not a single number. We do not publish an exact figure for any tier because the amount within the range is not fixed by the figures we have sourced.

These are statutory base fines before assessments. They are the starting point for the total, not the finish.

Why is the total so much higher than the base fine?

Statewide assessments and surcharges are added to every fine, and they usually exceed it. That is why real totals roughly triple the statutory figure.

Here is how that plays out in practice. A ticket in the lowest bracket can have a base fine of only $15–$25, yet the amount due is a multiple of that once the add-ons are included.

We do not list a dollar amount for each individual assessment. The sourced material gives the overall effect, not a line-by-line schedule, and we will not guess at numbers.

  • Ask the court or read your ticket for the total due.
  • Look for a breakdown that separates the base fine from the assessments.
  • Do not compare your total against the base fine alone.

If your total looks far above the statutory range, that is expected. The base fine is only one part of the bill.

How many points does a speeding ticket carry in South Carolina?

A speeding conviction adds 2–6 points to your driving record, depending on how many mph over the limit you were going. Faster speeds carry more points.

Twelve points means suspension. Points halve after 12 months, which lowers the total on your record as time passes.

We do not assign an exact point value to each mph bracket here. The sourced range is 2–6 points, and the South Carolina Department of Motor Vehicles is the place to confirm the value for a specific violation (South Carolina Department of Motor Vehicles).

Points are a separate cost from the fine. You can pay the ticket in full and still carry the points.

If you already have points from earlier violations, a new ticket matters more. Check your current record before assuming one ticket is a small matter.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance in South Carolina?

It can. Published market analyses show that a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal.

That is a national average, not a South Carolina figure. Your own increase depends on your insurer, your record and the rest of your rating profile.

The premium effect can outlast the fine. A one-time ticket cost may be followed by higher premiums at each renewal while the conviction counts against you.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend insurers or give insurance advice. If you want to know how a conviction would affect your own policy, ask your insurer or a licensed agent.

Can a speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but outcomes depend on the facts of your case and the court that handles it. We do not publish odds or promise any result.

Because the base fine moves in tiers by mph over the limit, the bracket on your ticket matters. A ticket recorded in a lower bracket carries a lower base fine range and a lower points range.

For questions about contesting a ticket, plea options or how a conviction would affect your record, talk to a licensed attorney. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not recommend specific attorneys.

  • Read the ticket carefully for the court and instructions.
  • Contact the court if you are unsure what is due.
  • Keep records of anything you pay or file.

What happens if I don't pay a speeding ticket?

Ignoring a ticket is risky. An unresolved ticket can lead to added problems with the court and with your driving privileges.

We do not list specific late penalties, deadlines or suspension timelines here, because they are not part of the figures we sourced. Your ticket and any court notice are the best guide to what you must do and by when.

Remember that points also matter. Twelve points means suspension, so unresolved or repeated violations can add up on your record.

If you cannot pay the full total, contact the court before the due date and ask what options exist. For legal questions, speak with a licensed attorney.
