How much is a speeding ticket in California?

California sets the base fine by how far over the limit you were driving. The base fines are $35 (1–15 over), $70 (16–25 over), $100 (26+ over) and $200 (100+ mph).

Those base numbers are not what most drivers pay. Mandatory penalty assessments raise real totals to roughly $238, $367, $490 and $900 under the Judicial Council's Uniform Bail and Penalty Schedule (California Department of Motor Vehicles).

The underlying speed rules are in Cal. Veh. Code §22350 / §22349 (Justia), which cover the basic speed law and the maximum speed limit.

The amount printed on your citation or court notice is the number to rely on. County fees and surcharges vary by county and court, so two drivers with the same ticket can see slightly different totals.

Why is the total so much higher than the base fine?

The base fine is only the starting point. California adds penalty assessments, county fees and surcharges on top of it.

As a rule of thumb, those additions multiply the base fine roughly 4–5×. That is why a $35 base fine becomes a total of roughly $238, and a $100 base fine becomes roughly $490.

Individual add-ons differ by county and court, and we do not publish a line-by-line amount for each one. The published totals for each tier in the Uniform Bail and Penalty Schedule are a better guide than the multiplier alone.

  • Base fine: set by the speed bracket.
  • Penalty assessments: mandatory and added to the base fine.
  • County fees and surcharges: vary by county and court.

If you only budget for the base fine, you will likely underestimate the bill by a wide margin.

What drives the total cost of a California speeding ticket?

Several factors move the final number, and they stack on one another.

  • How fast you were going. The bracket sets the base fine: 1–15 over, 16–25 over, 26+ over, or 100+ mph.
  • Assessments and surcharges. These multiply the base fine roughly 4–5× and vary by county and court.
  • Points on your record. Points do not add to the fine, but they can lead to a suspension if they accumulate.
  • Insurance at renewal. A conviction can raise premiums, often by more than the fine itself over time.

The last two items are often the larger long-term cost. A moderate ticket that stays on your driving record can outlast the one-time payment.

Other states handle speeding penalties differently. The Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a reference on state traffic laws (Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws) if you want to compare.

How many points does a speeding ticket add in California?

An ordinary speeding conviction adds 1 point to your driving record. Speeding at 100+ mph adds 2 points.

Points matter because of the Negligent Operator Treatment System. Reaching 4 points in 12 months means a suspension under that system.

One ordinary ticket on its own is well below that threshold. The risk comes from combining it with other violations in a short period.

Check your own record with the California Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV) before deciding how to handle a ticket. If you already have recent points, a new conviction matters more.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance rates?

Often, yes. Published market analyses put the typical increase from a speeding conviction at roughly 20–25% at renewal.

That is a US national average, not a California-specific figure. We do not have a California-only number, and your own increase could be higher or lower.

The size of any increase depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the violation is recorded. Your renewal notice and your insurer are the reliable sources for your own rate.

Because the surcharge can last across multiple renewals, the insurance effect may exceed the fine itself. That is worth weighing when you decide whether to contest a ticket.

Can I contest a California speeding ticket by mail?

Yes. California offers Trial by Written Declaration, which lets you contest a ticket by mail without a court appearance.

This can be useful if travelling to court is hard or costly. The court's instructions on your notice explain how to request it and what deadlines apply.

Contesting does not guarantee a lower cost or a dismissal. A conviction brings the full total with assessments, plus the point and insurance consequences described above.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot give legal advice. If the stakes are high, such as a 100+ mph ticket or a record already close to the 4-point threshold, consider speaking with a licensed California attorney.

What happens if I don't pay a California speeding ticket?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. Courts can add costs and take further action, and the specifics depend on the county and court.

Do not assume a missed deadline simply disappears. Read the notice for the due date and the options the court offers, including contesting or asking about payment arrangements.

If you are unsure what a notice means, contact the court clerk named on the citation. For driver's license questions, the California Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV) is the official source.
