How much is a speeding ticket in Mississippi?

The fine is set by the justice court or municipal court that hears your case, within limits written into state law. Totals with state assessments commonly run $100–$250 (Mississippi Department of Public Safety).

That range is a planning figure, not a guarantee. The number printed on or tied to your citation is the one that counts.

The speeding statute is Miss. Code §63-3-501 (Justia). It sits within the state's traffic code, which the courts apply when they set a fine.

Where the amount depends on the county or court, this page gives only the published range. We do not list exact local fines because they are not part of the sourced figures we can verify.

If you want the precise amount, check the citation itself or contact the clerk of the court named on it. Clerks can tell you what is owed and which payment options the court accepts.

What drives the total cost of a Mississippi speeding ticket?

Mandatory state assessments are added to every fine and often exceed it. In practice, the assessment stack, not the fine itself, drives most of the total.

This is why two drivers who receive similar tickets can see different bottom-line amounts. The court, the county and the way the case is resolved can all change what is added.

  • The base fine: set by the court within statutory limits.
  • State assessments: required on every fine and frequently larger than the fine.
  • Local court costs: these vary by county and court.
  • How the case ends: a conviction, a reduced charge or a dismissal leads to different costs.

Read the paperwork closely. Ask whether the amount shown includes the assessments or only the fine, because the difference can be large.

The published range of $100–$250 already includes state assessments, so you should not add them on top of it (Mississippi Department of Public Safety).

How many points does a speeding ticket add in Mississippi?

Mississippi has no point system. Suspensions follow repeat convictions and specific offenses instead.

That means you will not see a running point total on your record the way you would in many other states. It does not mean tickets are harmless to your license.

Repeat convictions can lead to a suspension, and certain offenses can trigger one on their own. Because the rules turn on your conviction history, a single ticket today can matter more if you already have others.

No points does not mean no consequences. A conviction still goes on your driving record, and the record is what insurers and licensing authorities look at.

For the current licensing rules, check with the Mississippi Department of Public Safety (DPS). You can also compare state approaches in the Governors Highway Safety Association's overview (GHSA State Laws).

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance in Mississippi?

It often does. Published market analyses indicate a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal.

That is a national average, not a Mississippi figure. We do not have a sourced Mississippi-specific number, so treat 20–25% as a rough guide only.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the ticket is resolved. Some drivers see no change, and others see more.

  • The increase usually shows up at your next renewal, not immediately.
  • A conviction on your record is what insurers generally react to.
  • Ask your insurer how it treats a ticket on your policy. We cannot recommend any specific insurer.

Because the premium effect can last longer than the fine, it is worth weighing it when you decide how to handle a ticket.

Can a Mississippi speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes. The court that hears your case decides the outcome, and practices differ between justice courts and municipal courts.

A ticket can end in a conviction, a reduced charge or a dismissal. Each result carries a different cost and a different effect on your record.

Common points people ask the court or clerk about:

  1. What the total amount is, including state assessments.
  2. Whether you must appear in person or can resolve the ticket another way.
  3. What options the court offers for contesting the citation.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot give legal advice or recommend an attorney. If the stakes feel high, such as repeat convictions, you may want to talk to a licensed Mississippi attorney.

Because the assessment stack often exceeds the fine, a reduced fine does not always lower your total as much as you might expect. Ask the clerk to show the full amount for each outcome.

What happens if I don't pay a Mississippi speeding ticket?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. Courts can add consequences when a citation goes unresolved, and a license suspension is among the outcomes drivers worry about.

We do not list specific deadlines or penalty amounts, because they are not in the sourced figures we can verify. The citation and the court clerk are the right places to confirm them.

Steps worth taking soon after you receive a ticket:

  • Read the citation for the court name and instructions.
  • Contact the clerk to confirm the total amount owed.
  • Decide whether to pay or contest, and act within the time the court gives you.

Paying a ticket is generally treated as accepting the conviction. If you want to contest it, do that before paying.

If you cannot afford the full amount, ask the court whether it offers any payment arrangement. Do not assume one exists.
