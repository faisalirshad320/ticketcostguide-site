How much is a speeding ticket in Louisiana?

The lead figure for Louisiana is this: set by the local city, parish or district court within statutory limits — totals vary widely by parish and commonly run $100–$300 with costs — Louisiana speeding fine range with costs (Louisiana Office of Motor Vehicles).

That is a range, not a fixed price. Two drivers cited for similar speeds can pay different totals because they were handled by different courts.

The range includes costs. In Louisiana the base fine is only part of the bill, and the amount you actually pay can be driven as much by the court's added costs as by the fine.

Parish-level variation is larger in Louisiana than in most states. Treat any single number you see online, including ours, as a guide and check your citation for the real amount.

The statute behind speed limits is La. R.S. 32:61. It sets the framework, and the court that hears your case sets the figure within it.

What drives the total cost of a Louisiana speeding ticket?

Because we only publish figures we can source, we cannot give you an exact amount for a given mph bracket or parish. We can explain what moves the total.

  • The court that hears the case. City, parish and district courts each set amounts within statutory limits.
  • Local court costs. These vary by parish and often exceed the fine.
  • The nature of the offense. How the officer wrote the citation and how far over the limit you were can change where the case lands within the court's range.
  • Your record. Repeat convictions can lead to license action, which is a separate cost from the fine.

The practical effect is that the number printed on your citation, or the figure the clerk quotes, is the one that counts. Our range is a way to check whether a quote looks ordinary or unusual.

If your total is well outside the commonly cited range, ask the clerk what makes up the amount. Fine and costs are usually separate line items.

Local court costs vary by parish and often exceed the fine. When comparing quotes, compare the total with costs, not the fine alone.

How many points does a speeding ticket add in Louisiana?

Louisiana has no point system. License action follows repeat convictions and specific offenses.

That differs from states that assign a numeric value to each violation. In Louisiana, the question is less "how many points" and more "how many convictions, and for what."

A single speeding conviction is therefore not tracked as a point total. A pattern of convictions, or a specific serious offense, is what can put your license at risk.

For the rules that apply to your license status, the Louisiana Office of Motor Vehicles is the official source. We do not give legal advice, so for questions about your own record, check with the OMV or a licensed professional of your choosing.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance in Louisiana?

It can. Published market analyses indicate that a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal.

That is a national average, not a Louisiana figure. Your own change could be higher, lower or nil depending on your insurer, your driving history and how the ticket is resolved.

Timing matters too. A premium change usually shows up when your policy renews, not on the day you pay the ticket.

  • The conviction, not the citation alone, is what typically feeds rating.
  • Each insurer weighs violations differently.
  • A clean prior record may soften the effect, though we cannot quantify that for Louisiana.

Because the premium effect can outlast the fine itself, it is worth thinking about the long-run cost and not only the amount due to the court. TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer. Ask your own insurer how a conviction would be treated.

Can a Louisiana speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome depends on the court and the facts of your case. We do not have sourced data on how often reductions or dismissals happen, so we will not guess.

What we can say is that the court handling your citation decides what you owe within statutory limits. That means options, and the paperwork for them, are local.

  1. Read the citation closely for the court, the listed charge and any instructions.
  2. Contact the clerk of that court and ask what options exist and what the total with costs would be.
  3. Ask whether a conviction would be recorded, since insurance and license action follow convictions.
  4. If the stakes are high, consider speaking with a licensed professional of your choosing.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice or recommend attorneys. This page explains costs, not how to fight a ticket.

Because parish variation is large, a process that works in one parish may not apply in another.

What happens if I don't pay a Louisiana speeding ticket?

Ignoring a citation tends to make a ticket more expensive and more complicated. The details depend on the court that issued it, and we do not have a sourced figure for late costs, so we will not quote one.

In general, unpaid or unanswered citations can lead to additional court costs and possible action on your license. The notice on your citation and the clerk of your court are the right places to confirm what applies.

  • Check the court name and contact details on the citation.
  • Ask the clerk what is owed and what the deadlines are.
  • Keep proof of any payment or response.

Since local court costs often exceed the fine, added costs can matter. The Louisiana Office of Motor Vehicles is the official source for questions about license status.

Frequently asked questions

How much is a speeding ticket in Louisiana?

Totals commonly run $100–$300 with costs. The local city, parish or district court sets the amount within statutory limits, so totals vary widely by parish. Local court costs often exceed the fine itself, so check your citation or ask the clerk for the full total.

Does Louisiana have a point system for speeding tickets?

No. Louisiana has no point system. License action follows repeat convictions and specific offenses rather than a running point total. For questions about your own license status, the Louisiana Office of Motor Vehicles is the official source.

Why do speeding ticket costs differ so much between parishes?

Each city, parish or district court sets its amount within statutory limits, and local court costs vary by parish. Those costs often exceed the fine. Parish-level variation is larger in Louisiana than in most states, so two similar tickets can total very different amounts.

Will a speeding ticket raise my insurance in Louisiana?

It may. Published market analyses show a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal. That is a national average, not a Louisiana figure. Your own change depends on your insurer and your driving history.

What is La. R.S. 32:61?

It is the Louisiana statute on speed limits, available through Justia's version of the state code. It sets the legal framework, while the local court handling your citation sets the fine and costs within statutory limits.

Are court costs included in the $100–$300 range?

Yes. The commonly cited $100–$300 range is the fine with costs. Local court costs vary by parish and often exceed the fine, so the base fine alone may be well below the total you owe. Ask the clerk for an itemized amount.
