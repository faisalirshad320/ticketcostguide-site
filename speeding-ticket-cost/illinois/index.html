How much is a speeding ticket in Illinois?

For the lowest tier, Illinois treats speeding as a petty offense when you are 25 or less mph over the limit. In many circuits the standard payment is commonly $164, but it varies by county (Illinois Secretary of State).

That $164 is a common reference point, not a statewide fixed price. The amount printed on your ticket or on the circuit clerk's payment page is the one that applies to you.

Above that tier, the cost is no longer a typical payment. Speeding 26–34 over is a Class B misdemeanor with a maximum of up to $1,500, and 35+ over is a Class A misdemeanor with a maximum of up to $2,500. Those are ceilings set by statute, not what everyone pays.

Bottom line: the same mph-over bracket can produce different totals in different counties. Treat $164 as a common figure for the lowest tier only.

What drives the total cost of an Illinois speeding ticket?

Several factors move the total, and only some of them are fixed by statute.

  • How far over you were. The three tiers (25 or less, 26–34, 35+) set the offense class and the maximum penalty.
  • Where it happened. A work zone carries a statutory minimum fine of $375 for a first offense.
  • Which county and court handle it. The standard payment and any added costs vary by county and court.
  • Your record. Points accumulate on the Secretary of State scale, and repeat convictions within 12 months trigger suspension.
  • Insurance. This is not part of the court total, but it is often the larger long-term cost.

We do not list individual court cost line items because they differ by county. Your clerk's office can tell you what is included in a given payment amount.

What changes at 26 mph over the limit?

At 26+ mph over, 'aggravated speeding' removes the option of a simple payable ticket. The offense moves from a petty offense to a misdemeanor under 625 ILCS 5/11-601 / 11-601.5 (state code via Justia).

  • 26–34 over: Class B misdemeanor, up to $1,500.
  • 35+ over: Class A misdemeanor, up to $2,500.

In practice this means you should read your citation carefully for court instructions rather than assuming you can mail in a standard payment. A misdemeanor is a more serious matter than a petty offense, so the court's process may differ from a routine ticket.

This is general information, not legal advice. If you are charged with a misdemeanor, a licensed Illinois attorney can explain your specific situation.

What is the fine for speeding in an Illinois work zone?

Work-zone speeding carries a statutory minimum fine of $375 for a first offense. That is a floor, which means the fine cannot be set below it for a first offense.

This minimum sits on top of the general structure described above. If the speed you were cited for also falls in the 26–34 or 35+ over tiers, the misdemeanor classifications and their maximums still matter.

We do not state work-zone amounts for repeat offenses here because they are outside the figures we have verified. Check the statute or your citation for details.

How many points does a speeding ticket add in Illinois?

Convictions carry 5–50 point values on the Secretary of State scale. Which value applies depends on the offense, and a more serious speeding tier is treated more seriously than a minor one.

Repeat convictions within 12 months trigger suspension. That makes timing important: two convictions close together carry far more risk than the same two spread over several years.

The Secretary of State maintains driver records and the point scale (Illinois Secretary of State). To see how your own record stands, check it with the Secretary of State rather than relying on a general table.

A conviction, not just a ticket, is what carries points. How a case ends is decided by the court.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance in Illinois?

It can. Published market analyses show a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal.

That is a national average, not an Illinois figure. Your own change depends on your insurer, your driving history, how fast you were going and when your policy renews.

Because the effect shows up at renewal, a ticket can cost more through premiums than through the court payment. We do not recommend any insurer. If you are concerned, ask your current insurer how a conviction would be treated.

Reminder: 20–25% is a national average from published analyses. It is not a prediction for Illinois drivers or for you.

Can an Illinois speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

That decision belongs to the court. We cannot say how likely a dismissal or reduction is, and we do not give legal advice.

What we can say is that the tier matters. A petty offense at 25 or less over is handled differently from a misdemeanor at 26+ over, where a simple payable ticket is not an option.

Practical steps people take include:

  1. Read the citation for the court date, the charge and any payment instructions.
  2. Contact the circuit clerk for the county named on the ticket to confirm the amount and options.
  3. For a misdemeanor charge, consider speaking with a licensed Illinois attorney.

Paying a ticket generally means accepting a conviction, which is when points and insurance effects can follow. Understand that before you pay.

What happens if I don't pay an Illinois speeding ticket?

Ignoring a ticket rarely makes it go away. The court can take further action, and the consequences depend on the court and the offense. We do not list specific penalties for non-payment because they vary and are outside our verified figures.

The risk is higher for 26+ over, where aggravated speeding removes the simple payable ticket option. Those cases are misdemeanors, so the citation's court instructions should be followed closely.

Also keep the 12-month rule in mind. Repeat convictions within 12 months trigger suspension, so an unresolved ticket combined with another conviction can add up quickly. Contact the circuit clerk if you are unsure of your status.
