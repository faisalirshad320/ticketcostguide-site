How much is a speeding ticket in Alabama?

The base fine for speeding in Alabama is typically $20–$200 base fine, plus court costs that vary by municipality (Alabama Law Enforcement Agency (ALEA)). That is the range before court costs, and it is the only base-fine range we can source.

The speeding provision is Ala. Code §32-5A-171, which you can read through Justia's Alabama code index. The statute sets the limits. The local court that handles your citation sets the actual fine within those limits.

That means two drivers cited for similar conduct in different places can end up with different totals. Totals vary widely by municipality, and we do not publish a single statewide figure because none exists.

Treat the base fine as a starting point, not the amount due. The total on your citation or court notice is what counts.

If you want a precise number, the court named on your citation is the best place to get it. Court clerks can usually tell you the amount owed for a specific citation and the payment methods the court accepts.

Why do court costs change the total so much?

Municipal and district court costs vary by jurisdiction and often exceed the base fine. For many drivers, the add-ons are the larger part of the bill.

This is why a search for one Alabama speeding fine amount rarely produces a reliable answer. The base fine is one piece. The court handling the case adds its own costs on top.

We do not list exact dollar amounts for court costs. The locked guidance we rely on says only that they vary by jurisdiction, and we will not guess at a figure for your town or county.

When you look at your citation or court notice, check these items:

  • Which court is handling the citation (municipal or district).
  • Whether the amount listed is the base fine alone or includes court costs.
  • Any instructions on how, where and by when to respond.
  • Whether the court lists payment options such as online, mail or in person.

If the notice is unclear, call the clerk's office listed on it. Asking what the total due is, and whether it includes all costs, avoids surprises.

How many points does a speeding ticket add in Alabama?

Alabama assigns points by how far over the limit you were. A speeding violation adds 2 points if you were 25 or less over, or 5 points if you were more than 25 over (Alabama Law Enforcement Agency (ALEA)).

  • 25 or less over the limit: 2 points.
  • More than 25 over the limit: 5 points.

The gap between the two tiers matters. A ticket in the higher tier adds more than twice the points of a lower-tier ticket, even if the base fine range is the same.

Points are tracked by the state licensing authority, not just the court. Even if you pay a low fine, the points can still be recorded on your driving record.

Points and fines are separate costs. A modest fine does not mean a modest effect on your record.

Can a speeding ticket get my license suspended?

A single speeding ticket in the lower tier is unlikely to reach the suspension threshold alone. The threshold is cumulative, though, and every point counts toward it.

Under the locked facts for Alabama, 12–14 points in 2 years = 60-day suspension. The 2-year window is why older, unrelated violations can still matter when you pick up a new ticket.

A speeding ticket for more than 25 over adds 5 points at once. Added to points from other violations in the same window, it can bring a driver closer to the threshold faster than a lower-tier ticket would.

Other violations carry their own point values. We do not list those here, because this page covers speeding only. Check your own record with the state licensing authority to see where you stand.

Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a general overview of how states handle traffic laws in its State Laws resource. It is useful context, but your Alabama record is what matters for any suspension.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance in Alabama?

It often does. Published market analyses indicate that a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal.

That is a national average, not an Alabama figure. We do not have a sourced Alabama-specific percentage, so do not read the national number as a prediction for your policy.

What you actually pay depends on several things that we cannot model:

  • Your insurer and its own rating rules.
  • Your driving history before the ticket.
  • How the violation is recorded on your record.
  • When your policy comes up for renewal.

Because the increase applies at renewal, the cost can show up months after the fine is paid. It can also continue over multiple renewals, so the long-run cost of a ticket may be larger than the fine itself.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any specific insurer. If you want to know how a ticket would affect your own rate, ask your insurer or an independent agent directly.

Can an Alabama speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Outcomes are decided by the local court handling your citation. Because fines are set within statutory limits by that court, what is available to you can depend on where you were cited.

We cannot tell you whether your ticket will be dismissed or reduced. We also do not give legal advice, and we are not a law firm.

What you can do is gather information before the response date on your notice:

  • Read the citation carefully for the court name, the charge and the response instructions.
  • Contact the court clerk to ask what options the court offers for your type of citation.
  • Keep a copy of everything you submit and any payment confirmation.
  • If the points or the cost are a concern, consider speaking with a licensed Alabama attorney of your own choosing.

Points and insurance are part of the decision. A lower fine does not necessarily mean fewer points, so ask how any option would affect your record as well as your bill.

What happens if I don't pay a speeding ticket in Alabama?

Ignoring a citation usually makes the situation worse. We do not have sourced figures for late penalties in Alabama, so we will not quote any. What follows is general guidance on what to check.

Because the local court sets the fine and handles the case, it is also the one that decides what happens if you miss a deadline. Consequences can include added costs and problems with your driver's license, depending on the court and the circumstances.

If you cannot pay in full or you missed the date on your notice, act early:

  1. Find the court name and contact details on your citation.
  2. Call or visit the clerk and ask what the current amount owed is.
  3. Ask whether the court offers any way to resolve the citation after a missed date, or a payment arrangement.
  4. Get any agreement in writing.

Responding on time is almost always cheaper and simpler than responding late. Check the due date on your notice first.
