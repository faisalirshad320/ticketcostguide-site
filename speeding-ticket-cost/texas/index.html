How much is a speeding ticket in Texas?

For ordinary speeding, the lead figure is a fine of up to $200 by statute, set by municipal and justice courts, plus state and local court costs. Typical totals run about $150–$300 and vary by city and county (Texas Department of Public Safety).

The statutory reference is Tex. Transp. Code §545.352 / §542.401, available through Justia's Texas code library.

The $200 figure is a ceiling on the fine for ordinary speeding, not a flat price. The amount on your ticket can be lower, and court costs are added on top of the fine.

We do not list an exact figure for any single city or county. That number depends on the court, and the court clerk or your citation is the reliable place to find it.

What drives the total on a Texas speeding ticket?

Two pieces make up what you pay: the fine and the court costs. The fine is capped by statute at up to $200 for ordinary speeding, and the court that hears your case sets it.

Court costs are the second piece. State court costs are uniform, while municipal add-ons vary by court.

That is why two drivers cited for the same thing can pay different totals. The typical range of about $150–$300 reflects that spread between courts.

  • The court: municipal and justice courts set their own fines within the statutory limit.
  • Local add-ons: these vary by court and are added to the uniform state costs.
  • How the case ends: paying the ticket, contesting it, or completing a Driving Safety Course leads to different outcomes.

This page covers ordinary speeding. Other offenses and unusual circumstances can be treated differently, so read your citation closely.

How many points does a speeding ticket add in Texas?

Texas abolished its driver-point and surcharge program in 2019. A speeding ticket is therefore not described here as adding a set number of points, and there is no annual surcharge program to budget for.

License action now follows specific offenses and repeat-conviction rules. In other words, what matters is the kind of offense and whether you have repeat convictions, not a running point total.

Older articles may still mention Texas points or surcharges. Check the publication date and compare it with the 2019 change before relying on them.

For the current status of your license, the Texas Department of Public Safety is the official source. For a cross-state view of traffic laws, the Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a state laws resource.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance in Texas?

It can. Published market analyses indicate that a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal.

That is a national average, not a Texas figure. Your own change could be smaller, larger, or nonexistent, depending on your insurer, your record and how the ticket is resolved.

The renewal timing matters. A change usually shows up when your policy is next rated, not the day you get the ticket.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend insurers or give insurance advice. If you want to know how a conviction would affect your rate, ask your insurer directly.

Can a Texas speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

For many drivers, yes. Driving Safety Course (defensive driving) dismissal is a statutory right for most eligible drivers once per 12 months.

Completing the course in the way the court requires can lead to dismissal instead of a conviction. That matters for your record and may matter for insurance, though we cannot say how your insurer will treat it.

  • Eligibility rules apply, so not every driver or ticket qualifies.
  • The once-per-12-months limit means a second ticket inside that window may not be eligible.
  • The court that handles your ticket explains its process and any fees.

We do not list a course fee because we have no sourced figure for one. Ask the court clerk what applies to you.

This is general information, not legal advice. For questions about your specific case, speak with a licensed attorney of your own choosing.

What happens if I don't pay a Texas speeding ticket?

Ignoring a ticket rarely makes it go away. The citation names the court that has your case, and that court controls the deadlines and next steps.

Because the fine and court costs are set by the court, an unresolved ticket stays with that court. We do not state specific penalties or late fees here, since we have no sourced figures for them.

If you cannot pay the full amount, or you are unsure of your options, contact the court clerk before your deadline. Courts handle these questions every day.

  1. Read the citation for the court name and the response deadline.
  2. Contact the clerk to confirm the amount you owe.
  3. Ask whether you are eligible for a Driving Safety Course dismissal.
  4. Choose how to resolve the ticket before the deadline passes.

What should I do after getting a speeding ticket in Texas?

Start with the citation. It tells you which court has the case, which decides the fine you face within the statutory cap of up to $200 for ordinary speeding.

Then compare your options. Paying the ticket usually means a conviction. A Driving Safety Course dismissal, where you are eligible, is available once per 12 months.

Keep the cost in perspective. The fine and court costs together typically land at about $150–$300, but insurance can add to the long-term cost. Nationally, premiums rise roughly 20–25% on average at renewal after a speeding conviction.

Finally, avoid relying on old point-system advice. Texas abolished its driver-point and surcharge program in 2019.
