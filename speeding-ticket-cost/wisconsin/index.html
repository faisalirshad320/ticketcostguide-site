How much is a speeding ticket in Wisconsin?

Wisconsin sets statutory forfeitures of roughly $30–$300 by tier, but mandatory surcharges and costs roughly double totals, commonly landing between $175–$300. Fines double at 20+ over and in work zones (Wisconsin Department of Transportation — DMV).

In Wisconsin a first-offense speeding ticket is usually called a forfeiture, not a fine. In practice it works like a fine: you pay it or contest it.

The number on the citation is not always the number you pay. The surcharge stack is added to every forfeiture, and the final total depends on the county and court handling your case.

Use the table above as a range, not a quote. We do not publish an exact local figure for any single county because our sources do not give one.

What drives the total cost of a Wisconsin speeding ticket?

Several things move the final amount. Some are in your control and some are not.

  • Speed bracket: the faster you were going over the limit, the higher the tier. Tiers are 10 or less over, 11–19 over, and 20+ over.
  • Location: a work zone doubles the fine.
  • Surcharges and costs: these are added to every forfeiture and roughly double the total.
  • County and court: the amount of each add-on can vary by county or court.

The rules come from Wis. Stat. §346.57 / §346.60 (Wis. Stat. §346.57 / §346.60 — state code via Justia). Section 346.57 covers speed restrictions and §346.60 covers how the speed rules are applied.

Because the add-ons are a stack, a small change in the base forfeiture does not translate into a small change in the total. Read the whole citation before you decide how to respond.

How many points does a speeding ticket add in Wisconsin?

Points depend on how far over the limit you were:

  • 10 or less over: 3 points
  • 11–19 over: 4 points
  • 20+ over: 6 points

Reaching 12 points in 12 months means suspension (Wisconsin Department of Transportation — DMV). A single 20+ over ticket puts you halfway there.

Points are separate from the money. You can pay the forfeiture in full and still carry the points on your driving record.

Two tickets in the top bracket within the same year would reach the suspension threshold on points alone. Count your recent tickets before you decide what to do.

Why do fines double at 20+ over and in work zones?

Wisconsin treats high-speed violations and work zone violations as more dangerous, so the forfeiture is doubled in both cases. This is set out in the speeding provisions of the state code, including Wis. Stat. §346.57 / §346.60 (Wis. Stat. §346.57 / §346.60 — state code via Justia).

The doubling makes the jump between brackets expensive. Going from the middle bracket to the 20+ over bracket changes both the base forfeiture and the points, and the doubled base then sits under the same surcharge stack.

If you were speeding in a work zone and also 20+ over, read your citation closely. We do not publish a combined figure for that case, and the court's total is what counts.

For a state-by-state look at how other states handle work zone penalties, the Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a reference (Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws).

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance in Wisconsin?

It can. Published market analyses show a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal.

That is a national average, not a Wisconsin figure. Your own change could be higher, lower or nothing, depending on your insurer, your record and your policy.

The premium effect often costs more over time than the ticket itself. A forfeiture is paid once, while a higher premium can show up at every renewal while the conviction counts against you.

We do not recommend any insurer and cannot predict your rate. If the premium is a concern, ask your insurer how a conviction would be treated.

Can a Wisconsin speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Whether a ticket can be dismissed or reduced depends on the facts and on the court. We cannot tell you whether yours qualifies, and TicketCostGuide does not give legal advice.

What we can say is how the cost structure affects the decision. Your options generally include paying the forfeiture, or contesting the citation in court. Paying usually means accepting the conviction and the points that come with the bracket.

  • Check the bracket on your citation, since it sets the points.
  • Check whether the work zone doubling is listed.
  • Look at the court information on the citation for how to respond.

If the stakes are high, such as a 20+ over ticket or a record that is already close to 12 points in 12 months, you may want to talk to a licensed attorney of your choosing.

What happens if I don't pay a Wisconsin speeding ticket?

Ignoring a citation does not make it go away. The deadlines and consequences are set by the court that issued it, and they are printed on or attached to your citation.

We do not publish specific late fees or timelines because they vary by county and court and are not in our sources. Check your citation and contact the court clerk directly.

Unpaid or ignored tickets can lead to further trouble with the court and with your driving privileges. Respond before the date on your citation.

For official guidance on licensing and driver records, start with the Wisconsin Department of Transportation — DMV.

How many points is a speeding ticket in Wisconsin?

Do fines double for speeding in a work zone in Wisconsin?

Yes. Fines double in work zones and also at 20+ over the limit. The surcharge stack is still added to every forfeiture, so the total can climb quickly. We do not publish a single combined figure, so check your citation.

Why is my Wisconsin speeding ticket more than the listed forfeiture?

Mandatory surcharges and costs are added on top of the forfeiture and roughly double the total, commonly $175–$300. The amounts can vary by county and court. That is why the amount you pay can be well above the base forfeiture.
