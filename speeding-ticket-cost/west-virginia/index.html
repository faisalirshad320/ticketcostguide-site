How much is a speeding ticket in West Virginia?

The lead figure is a statutory fine of up to $100 for a first offense, plus court costs that commonly add $160+ depending on the court (West Virginia Division of Motor Vehicles). The fine cap comes from W. Va. Code §17C-6-1, which you can find through the state code on Justia.

The fine is only part of what you pay. In West Virginia the court costs often make up the larger share of the bill.

Like several low-fine states, West Virginia has a costs column that is the real total. Read the whole citation, not just the fine line.

Your exact amount is set by the court that handles your ticket. We do not publish a single statewide total because the locked source figures give a cap on the fine and a typical range for costs, not a fixed price.

What drives the total cost of a West Virginia speeding ticket?

Three things matter most: the fine the court sets, the court costs that apply, and which court hears the case.

  • The fine. For a first offense it is capped at up to $100 by statute.
  • Court costs. These commonly add $160+, depending on the court.
  • The court itself. Municipal and magistrate court costs vary by county and court, and they usually exceed the fine.

Because costs vary, two drivers cited for the same thing can pay different totals. The location of the stop and the court named on your citation both matter.

Points are a separate cost. They do not appear as a dollar charge, but they can affect your license and your insurance later.

Why are court costs bigger than the fine?

In many low-fine states, the fine is small and the court adds fees that make up most of the bill. West Virginia fits that pattern. The fine is capped at up to $100 for a first offense, while court costs commonly add $160+.

This is why a quick look at the fine can understate what you will owe. Municipal and magistrate court costs vary and usually exceed the fine, so check the total on your citation or ask the court clerk for the full amount due.

We do not list a specific cost for any single county or city. The locked figures only support the range above, and any exact local number should come from the court that issued your ticket.

Tip: ask the clerk for the total due, including costs, before you decide how to handle the ticket.

How many points does a speeding ticket add in West Virginia?

A speeding conviction adds 2–5 points, depending on how many miles per hour you were over the limit. The faster you were going over the limit, the more points you can expect.

The important threshold is 12+ points = suspension. If your total points reach that level, your license is subject to suspension.

Points matter most when you already have other violations on your record. One ticket may not put you near the threshold, but several can add up.

For the current point schedule and your own record, contact the West Virginia Division of Motor Vehicles. For a broader look at how states handle traffic laws, the Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a state-by-state resource.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance rates?

It often does. Published market analyses show that a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal.

That is a national average, not a West Virginia figure. Your own change could be higher, lower or none at all, depending on your insurer, your driving record and the details of the ticket.

Because the effect shows up at renewal and can last, the insurance cost can be larger than the fine and court costs combined. That makes the outcome of the ticket matter more than the fine alone.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer. To learn how a ticket may affect your own policy, ask your insurer directly.

Can a West Virginia speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but that decision belongs to the court. Our locked source figures do not describe specific programs, deals or outcomes, so we do not list any.

What we can say is that the outcome matters in two ways. A conviction can add 2–5 points, and a conviction is what typically raises insurance premiums. A different outcome may change one or both effects.

If you are weighing options, start with the court named on your citation. The clerk can tell you what the court allows and what you would owe.

For advice on your specific situation, a licensed attorney is the right source. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice or recommend any attorney.

What happens if I don't pay a West Virginia speeding ticket?

We do not have a locked source figure for penalties on unpaid tickets, so we will not guess at them. Ignoring a ticket is risky because the court can take further action, and the total you owe may grow.

The 12+ point threshold is a separate risk. Points reaching that level mean suspension, so an unresolved ticket should not be left until the last minute.

The best next step is to read the due date and court details on your citation. If anything is unclear, call the clerk of the court listed on it.

The West Virginia Division of Motor Vehicles can confirm the status of your license and driving record.
