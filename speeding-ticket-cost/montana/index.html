How much is a speeding ticket in Montana?

Montana's statutory fines are commonly $20–$200 for ordinary speeding, depending on zone and speed, plus court surcharges (Montana Department of Justice — Motor Vehicle Division). That range is the base fine set under Mont. Code §61-8-303 / §61-8-725.

The number printed on your citation or the court's notice is what you actually owe. It can differ from the base fine because of surcharges and because the court that handles your case sets its own handling of the amount.

This page does not list exact fines for each mph bracket, because we only publish figures we can trace to a cited source. For an exact amount, read your citation or contact the court named on it.

Bottom line: expect a base fine in the $20–$200 range, then add court surcharges that vary by county and court.

What drives the total cost of a Montana speeding ticket?

Three things matter most: where you were stopped, how fast you were going compared with the limit, and which court handles the citation.

  • Zone. Daytime interstate fines in Montana are among the mildest. Town and zone tickets cost more.
  • Speed. The statutory range runs from the low end to the high end depending on how far over the limit the driver was.
  • Court. Surcharges are added to the statutory fine, and the amount varies by county and court.

Two drivers cited for the same speed can therefore pay different totals if one was on an open interstate and the other in a town or posted zone.

The fine is also only part of the cost. Points, a possible insurance increase and time spent dealing with the court all add to the real price of a conviction.

What are the court surcharges on a Montana speeding ticket?

Court surcharges are added to the statutory fine. They are separate from the fine itself, which is why the amount you pay can be higher than the range most people see quoted.

We do not publish a single surcharge amount for Montana, because it varies by county and court. Quoting one number would risk being wrong for your case.

To find your exact total:

  1. Read the citation for a listed fine or payment amount.
  2. Check the court's notice or payment instructions for surcharges and fees.
  3. Call the clerk of the court named on the citation and ask for the full amount due.

If the amount on the court's notice is different from what you expected, ask the clerk what each line item covers before you pay.

How many points does a speeding ticket add in Montana?

Convictions carry conviction points toward Montana's habitual-offender threshold: 30 points in 3 years equals revocation (Montana Department of Justice — Motor Vehicle Division).

That means a single ordinary speeding conviction is unlikely to threaten your license on its own. Points matter when they pile up alongside other moving-violation convictions inside the same 3-year window.

We do not list a per-violation point value here, since it is not part of the figures we have verified. Your driving record from the Motor Vehicle Division shows the points actually recorded against you.

Points apply to convictions. How your case ends, whether a conviction is entered or not, changes whether points are recorded.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance in Montana?

It can. Published market analyses show a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal. That is a national average, not a Montana figure.

Your own change could be smaller, larger or zero. It depends on your insurer, your driving history, how the ticket was resolved and when your policy renews.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer and cannot predict your rate. If you are worried about a renewal increase, ask your insurer how a conviction would be treated before you decide how to handle the ticket.

For context on how states treat violations generally, the Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a state-by-state reference (Governors Highway Safety Association — State Laws).

Can a Montana speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

You have the right to contest a citation instead of paying it. Whether a ticket is dismissed or reduced depends on the facts, the court and the prosecutor, and nobody can promise an outcome.

Common questions to raise with the court clerk include:

  • What are my options for responding to this citation?
  • Is there a deadline for responding, and what happens if I miss it?
  • Can I request a hearing?
  • What is the full amount due, including surcharges?

Paying the fine generally resolves the citation, but it can also mean a conviction and points on your record. That is worth weighing against the 30-points-in-3-years habitual-offender threshold.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice or recommend attorneys. If the ticket affects your license, job or insurance, consider speaking with a licensed Montana attorney.

What happens if I don't pay a Montana speeding ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts can add penalties, and unresolved tickets can lead to further action against your license or a warrant. The specifics vary by county and court.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court before the due date. Ask whether it offers any payment arrangement. Some courts do, but we have not verified a statewide rule, so we do not state one.

If you plan to contest the ticket, respond to the court in the way the citation instructs. Not responding is not the same as contesting.

Keep a record of anything you pay or send, including dates and confirmation numbers.
