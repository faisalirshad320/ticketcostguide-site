How much is a speeding ticket in Massachusetts?

The lead figure is about $105 for 1–10 mph over the limit, then $10 more for each mph above 10 over (Massachusetts Registry of Motor Vehicles). That total is built from a $50 fine floor, a $50 head-injury surcharge and a $5 fee, all set by statute.

The rule comes from Mass. Gen. Laws c.90 §20, which you can read through the state code on Justia. Because the amount follows a formula, your cost depends mostly on how far over the limit the citation says you were going.

Quick example: 30 over works out to roughly $305. The same driver at 1–10 over would owe about $105.

This page covers the statutory formula only. Your citation and the court notice are the final word on what you owe in your case.

What drives the total cost of a Massachusetts speeding ticket?

The biggest driver is speed over the limit. Up to 10 over, the amount is about $105. Beyond 10 over, each extra mph adds $10.

That per-mph step makes high-speed tickets expensive fast. A driver cited at 30 over faces about $305, nearly three times the bottom-tier amount.

  • Speed over the limit: sets where you land on the formula.
  • The head-injury surcharge: $50 on every speeding conviction.
  • The $5 fee: part of the statutory total.
  • Insurance: a separate, indirect cost that follows a conviction.

The speed written on the citation matters, so read it carefully. If it looks wrong, that is a question for the court, not for this guide.

What is the head-injury surcharge and the $5 fee?

Massachusetts adds a $50 head-injury surcharge to every speeding conviction. It is not optional and it does not change with your speed.

Together with the $50 fine floor and a $5 fee, it makes up the roughly $105 base total for 1–10 over. In other words, more than half of the lowest-tier ticket is the surcharge rather than the fine itself.

Above 10 over, the surcharge stays at $50. The extra cost comes from the $10 per mph step in the fine formula.

We list no other add-ons here, because none appear in our locked source data. If your paperwork shows other line items, check them against the notice from the court or the Registry.

How many points does a speeding ticket give in Massachusetts?

Massachusetts uses insurance surcharge points, known as SDIP, rather than license suspension points for ordinary speeding. These points are tied to how insurers rate you, not to a point total that triggers a suspension on its own.

There are still limits tied to repeat events. Three surchargeable events in 24 months require a driver retraining course. Seven in 3 years means suspension.

  • 3 surchargeable events in 24 months: driver retraining course required.
  • 7 surchargeable events in 3 years: suspension.

We do not list a point value per violation here. The Registry of Motor Vehicles is the source for how your own record is counted (Massachusetts Registry of Motor Vehicles).

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance in Massachusetts?

It often does. Published market analyses show a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal.

That is a national average, not a Massachusetts figure. Your own change depends on your insurer, your record and how your policy is rated.

Because Massachusetts uses SDIP surcharge points, the conviction is what feeds into rating, and the effect can outlast the fine. For many drivers the premium change is larger than the ticket itself over time.

We do not recommend any insurer or give advice on shopping for coverage. Ask your own insurer how a conviction would affect your renewal.

Can a speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

That is a decision for the court that hears your citation. We do not give legal advice, and we do not have locked figures on how often tickets are dismissed or reduced.

What we can say is why the question matters. A conviction brings the $50 head-injury surcharge, the fine under the formula and a possible insurance effect. Those costs follow the outcome of your case.

  • Read the speed and date on your citation first.
  • Check the response instructions on the notice.
  • Consider speaking with a licensed attorney if you want legal advice about your options.

The statute behind the fine formula is Mass. Gen. Laws c.90 §20, available via Justia.

What happens if I don't pay a Massachusetts speeding ticket?

We do not have locked figures for late fees or other penalties, so we will not guess at them. What happens next is set by the court and the Registry, and it is spelled out in your notice.

The risk is more than a bigger bill. Ignoring a citation can lead to licensing problems and can add to the cost of the original ticket.

Keep in mind the repeat-event rules too. Three surchargeable events in 24 months require a driver retraining course, and 7 in 3 years means suspension.

If you cannot pay or have a question about a deadline, contact the court listed on your citation. For licensing questions, start with the Registry of Motor Vehicles.
