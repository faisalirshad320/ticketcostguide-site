The exact amount varies by county and court, and points can add a separate assessment.

How much is a speeding ticket in New York?

New York sets speeding fines in bands based on how far over the limit you were going. Under N.Y. VTL §1180 / §1803, the statutory fine is $45–$150 for 1–10 over, $90–$300 for 11–30 over, and $180–$600 for 31+ over (New York State Department of Motor Vehicles).

A mandatory state surcharge of $88–$93 per conviction is added on top of the fine. The DMV's Driver Responsibility Assessment can add more, depending on your points.

The bands are ranges, not fixed prices. Where your ticket lands inside a band varies by county and court, so the number on your ticket or the court's notice is the one that counts.

Because the range inside each band is wide, two drivers going the same speed can pay different fines. Check the amount printed on your own paperwork or on the court's website.

What drives the total cost of a New York speeding ticket?

Several pieces make up the total. Some are set by statute and some depend on where and how the case is handled.

  • Speed over the limit: this decides which fine band applies and how many points you receive.
  • County and court: the exact fine inside a band varies by county/court.
  • Mandatory surcharge: $88–$93 per conviction, added to the fine.
  • Points: at 6+ points, the separate Driver Responsibility Assessment of $300+ applies.
  • Insurance: a conviction can raise premiums at renewal, which often costs more than the fine itself over time.

The court fine is only the first layer. The surcharge and any assessment come on top, so a ticket's real cost is higher than the fine alone.

How many points is a speeding ticket in New York?

Points depend on how far over the limit you were. The DMV's schedule is:

  • 3 points for 1–10 over
  • 4 points for 11–20 over
  • 6 points for 21–30 over
  • 8 points for 31–40 over
  • 11 points for 41+ over

Eleven points in 18 months leads to a license suspension. A single ticket for 41+ over reaches that number on its own.

Points also affect cost directly. At 6+ points, the separate Driver Responsibility Assessment of $300+ applies, which is why a ticket for 21–30 over costs more than the fine band suggests.

What are the state surcharge and the Driver Responsibility Assessment?

These are two separate charges that come on top of the court fine (New York State Department of Motor Vehicles).

The mandatory state surcharge is $88–$93 per conviction. It is added whenever you are convicted, whatever the fine.

The Driver Responsibility Assessment is billed by the DMV, not the court. It starts at $300+ and is triggered at 6+ points, so it mostly affects tickets for 21 or more over the limit.

Paying the court fine does not cover the assessment. It is a separate DMV charge, so watch for mail from the DMV after a conviction.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance in New York?

It often does. Published market analyses suggest a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal.

That is a national average, not a New York figure. Your actual change depends on your insurer, your record and how fast you were going.

Over several renewals, a higher premium can cost more than the fine and surcharge combined. TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer. Ask your own insurer or agent how a conviction would affect your policy.

Can a New York speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

It depends on where the case is heard. NYC and many other jurisdictions route cases through the Traffic Violations Bureau, where plea bargaining is not available.

In that setting, you generally either plead and accept the result or contest the charge at a hearing. In courts outside that system, how a case is handled can differ by court.

The fine and points depend on the conviction you end up with, so a reduction can change both. This site cannot tell you whether to contest a ticket. For advice on your case, speak with a licensed attorney.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore a speeding ticket?

Ignoring a ticket does not make it go away. Court notices set deadlines, and missing them can bring further consequences from the court and the DMV.

Points still matter after a conviction. Eleven points in 18 months leads to suspension, and the DMV's $300+ Driver Responsibility Assessment applies at 6+ points.

Read the ticket and any court notice carefully. Contact the court or the DMV listed on your paperwork about your options and deadlines.
