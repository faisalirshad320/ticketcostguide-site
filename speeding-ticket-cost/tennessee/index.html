How much is a speeding ticket in Tennessee?

Speeding in Tennessee is a Class C misdemeanor with a fine of up to $50. County court costs and litigation taxes raise typical totals to roughly $120–$250 depending on the county (Tennessee Department of Safety and Homeland Security).

That range is a typical total, not a guarantee. Your ticket or the court clerk will show the exact amount for your case.

Tennessee's $50 cap means you are mostly paying the court, not the fine.

The rest of this page explains where the money goes, how points work, and what to check before you pay.

Why is the total so much higher than the $50 fine?

The fine is only one piece of what you owe. Under Tenn. Code §55-8-152, speeding is a Class C misdemeanor, and the fine side of the bill is capped at $50.

County court costs and state litigation taxes make up most of the total. Those charges are added on top of the fine when the case is processed.

Because court costs are set at the county and court level, the same speed can produce a different bill in different places. This page gives only the typical range, roughly $120–$250 depending on the county, because we do not publish exact local figures we cannot source.

  • Fine: up to $50.
  • County court costs: vary by county and court.
  • State litigation taxes: vary by county and court.

What drives the total on a Tennessee speeding ticket?

Since the fine is capped, the main driver is where your case is handled. The county and court decide which costs apply, and those costs make up most of what you pay.

A few practical points follow from that:

  • The county matters more than the speed. A higher speed can add points, but the fine itself stays within the cap.
  • Read the citation and the court's notice. They show what that court charges for your case.
  • Ask the clerk if the breakdown is unclear. Clerks can usually explain which part is the fine and which part is costs and taxes.

If you want to compare how other states set penalties, the Governors Highway Safety Association publishes a state laws resource.

How many points does a speeding ticket add in Tennessee?

Tennessee assigns 1–8 points by how many miles per hour you were over the limit. For example, 3 points apply at 6–15 over, and 4 points apply at 16–25 over (Tennessee Department of Safety and Homeland Security).

Points matter because they accumulate. Reaching 12 points in 12 months results in a suspension notice.

  1. Find the mph over the limit on your citation.
  2. Match it to the points bracket for that speed.
  3. Add any other points you have gotten in the past 12 months.

We list only the brackets we can source. For brackets beyond the examples above, check the Department of Safety's published schedule or ask your court.

Points are separate from money. Paying the fine does not remove them, and a driver with several tickets in a short period can reach the suspension threshold faster than expected.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance in Tennessee?

It can. Published market analyses show a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal.

That is a national average, not a Tennessee figure. Your own change depends on your insurer, your driving record and how the ticket is resolved.

Over several years of higher premiums, the insurance effect can exceed the court bill. That is why many drivers look at the whole picture, not only the amount due at the clerk's office.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend insurers or predict what your company will charge. Your insurer or agent can tell you how a conviction would affect your policy.

Can a Tennessee speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but this depends on the court and the facts of your case. We do not have sourced figures on how often tickets are dismissed or reduced, so we do not estimate that.

What you can do without legal advice:

  • Read the citation for the court name, the listed speed and any instructions.
  • Contact the court clerk and ask what options that court offers for your type of ticket.
  • Ask what the total would be under each option, since costs and taxes make up most of the bill.

If the ticket could affect your license, your job or your record, you may want to speak with a licensed Tennessee attorney. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not recommend specific attorneys.

What happens if I don't pay a Tennessee speeding ticket?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. Because speeding is a misdemeanor offense under Tenn. Code §55-8-152, an unresolved citation is a court matter, not just a bill.

The exact consequences depend on the court. We do not list specific deadlines or penalty amounts because we cannot source them for every county.

The safest step is to check your citation for the court and any instructions, then contact the clerk before the date listed. If you are unsure what you owe, ask for the full amount in writing.
