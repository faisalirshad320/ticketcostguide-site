How much is a speeding ticket in Washington?

Washington speeding penalties are set by the Supreme Court's IRLJ 6.2 penalty schedule — commonly about $145–$500+ by mph over, with higher amounts in school and work zones (Washington State Department of Licensing). That is the headline range for a ticket in the state.

The schedule works alongside the speeding statute, RCW §46.61.400 / IRLJ 6.2 penalty schedule. The statute sets the speed rules, and the schedule turns the violation into a dollar amount.

The low end of the range applies to a driver only slightly over the limit. The "$500+" end is where larger overages land, and the plus sign means some tickets can go higher.

The figure on your ticket is the number that matters. This page explains the range, but your citation and the court that issued it control what you owe.

What determines the total on a Washington speeding ticket?

Two things drive the amount: how far over the limit you were, and where you were driving. The mph bracket is the main factor, and school and work zones carry higher amounts.

Because the schedule is statewide, the mph bracket fixes the ticket's face amount. A driver cited in one part of the state for a given overage faces the same scheduled amount as a driver cited in another part.

  • Speed over the limit: a higher bracket means a higher scheduled amount.
  • Location: school and work zones carry higher amounts than the standard range.
  • Assessments: statutory assessments are built into the IRLJ schedule amounts, so they are not stacked on top of the scheduled figure.

We do not publish a bracket-by-bracket dollar table because the figures we can verify are the overall range and the way the schedule is structured. Your citation lists the exact amount.

Are there surcharges or extra fees on top of the fine?

Statutory assessments are built into the IRLJ schedule amounts. In plain terms, the scheduled amount already accounts for them, so you should not expect a separate surcharge line added to the same figure.

That does not mean every cost connected to a ticket is zero. Other costs can arise from how a case is handled, such as a request for a payment arrangement or a missed deadline. We do not give figures for those because we have no verified amounts to cite.

If your ticket total looks different from what you expected, compare it with the amount printed on the citation and ask the court clerk to explain the difference.

How many points does a speeding ticket add in Washington?

None. Washington has no point system. There is no running point total on your driving record that you need to keep below a threshold.

Instead, suspensions follow repeat moving-violation convictions within set windows. The risk grows with the number of convictions, not with a points tally.

The Department of Licensing keeps driver records and handles suspensions (Washington State Department of Licensing). To see how other states treat points and violations, the Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a comparison of state laws (Governors Highway Safety Association).

No points does not mean no consequences. A conviction still goes on your record and counts toward the repeat-violation windows.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance in Washington?

It can. Published market analyses show that a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal.

That is a national average, not a Washington figure. Your own change could be smaller, larger or nonexistent depending on your insurer, your history and how the ticket is recorded.

The increase generally shows up at renewal rather than the day you are cited. Because the premium effect can add up over time, it is worth weighing alongside the fine itself.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend insurers or predict your premium. Your insurer can tell you how it treats a conviction.

Are school zone and work zone speeding tickets more expensive?

Yes. The IRLJ 6.2 schedule carries higher amounts in school and work zones than the standard range of about $145–$500+ (Washington State Department of Licensing).

The extra cost reflects the added risk in these areas, and the schedule treats them separately from ordinary roadways. We do not give a specific zone figure because the verified material we rely on gives only the range and the fact that amounts are higher.

If your ticket was issued in one of these zones, the citation should show the scheduled amount. Check whether the location is noted, because it affects what you owe.

Can a Washington speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Some tickets are contested, and the outcome depends on the facts and the court. We cannot say how likely a dismissal or reduction is, and this page is not legal advice.

The ticket itself explains your options and deadlines, such as paying, contesting or requesting a hearing. Read those instructions closely, because the choice you make can affect the amount and your record.

Because the mph bracket fixes the face amount, one practical question is whether the recorded speed matches what happened. If you have questions about your specific case, a licensed attorney can advise you. We do not recommend any particular one.

  • Check the cited speed and location on the citation.
  • Note the response deadline printed on it.
  • Contact the court clerk with procedural questions.

What happens if I don't pay a speeding ticket in Washington?

Ignoring a ticket is usually the costliest option. Courts expect a response by the deadline on the citation, and missing it can lead to further consequences beyond the scheduled amount.

Those consequences can involve your driving record and license status, and the Department of Licensing handles suspensions (Washington State Department of Licensing). We do not state specific penalty amounts for late payment because we have no verified figures for them.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court clerk before the deadline and ask what options exist.

Responding on time, even to contest the ticket, is generally better than letting the deadline pass.
