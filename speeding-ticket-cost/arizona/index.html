How much is a speeding ticket in Arizona?

For ordinary speeding, Arizona treats the ticket as a civil traffic violation. The penalty is set by the local court and is commonly $100–$300 with fees (Arizona Department of Transportation — Motor Vehicle Division).

That is a range, not a fixed price. The court that handles your citation decides the final amount, and its fees are included in the range above.

Lead figure: Civil penalty set by the local court (commonly $100–$300 with fees); 'excessive speed' — 20+ over, 85+ mph, or 35+ in some zones — is a class 3 misdemeanor under §28-701.02 — Arizona speeding penalty structure.

The structure comes from A.R.S. §28-701 and §28-701.02, which you can read in the state code via Justia. Two things matter most: whether your speed was ordinary or excessive, and which court hears the case.

What drives the total on an Arizona speeding ticket?

Several factors change what you owe and what the ticket does to your record. Some are in your control and some are not.

  • Ordinary or excessive speed. Ordinary speeding is civil. Excessive speed is a class 3 misdemeanor.
  • The court. Justice and municipal courts set the civil penalty and add their own surcharges and fees.
  • Points. A speeding conviction adds 3 points, which matters if you already have others.
  • Insurance. This is usually the largest cost over time, though it does not appear on the citation.

Because the court sets the penalty and fees, two drivers cited for similar speeds can owe different amounts. Read your citation and the court's notice before assuming any figure you find online applies to you.

What counts as excessive speed in Arizona?

Under A.R.S. §28-701.02 (A.R.S. §28-701 / §28-701.02 via Justia), 'excessive speed' means 20+ over the limit, 85+ mph, or 35+ in some zones. In those cases the offense is a class 3 misdemeanor.

The difference is significant. An ordinary speeding ticket is a civil matter. A misdemeanor carries a criminal record risk, and that is a different kind of consequence from a fine.

The 35+ threshold applies only in some zones. Check the statute and your citation to see which category you were cited under.

If your citation lists a misdemeanor charge, the stakes are higher than the fine alone. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot advise on your case. A licensed attorney can explain your options.

What surcharges and fees get added to the fine?

Court surcharges and fees are added by the justice or municipal court, and the amounts vary by court. We do not publish a single statewide surcharge figure because the sources we rely on do not give one.

The commonly cited $100–$300 range already includes fees. The final bill depends on the court handling your ticket.

To find your exact total:

  1. Read the citation for the court name and any listed amount.
  2. Check the court's notice or website for its fee schedule.
  3. Ask the court clerk to confirm the full amount before you pay.

Court staff can tell you what you owe. They cannot give legal advice, but they can explain how the court's payment process works.

How many points does a speeding ticket add in Arizona?

A speeding conviction adds 3 points to your Arizona driving record (Arizona Department of Transportation — Motor Vehicle Division).

Points matter because they accumulate. Reaching 8+ points in 12 months leads to a suspension or Traffic Survival School.

A speeding ticket alone does not reach that level, but it moves you closer. If you have other recent violations, the 3 points could be what pushes you to the threshold. The Motor Vehicle Division is the place to confirm your current point total.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance in Arizona?

It can. Published market analyses suggest a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal.

That is a national average, not an Arizona figure. Your own change depends on your insurer, your driving history and the type of violation.

Over time the insurance effect can outweigh the fine. Excessive speed, with its criminal record risk, may be treated more seriously than an ordinary civil ticket. TicketCostGuide does not recommend insurers. Ask your own insurer how a conviction would affect your renewal.

Can an Arizona speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome depends on the court and the facts of your case. We cannot predict results or give legal advice.

Practical steps include:

  • Check that the citation is accurate, including the location, the posted limit and the charge.
  • Find out from the court which options it offers, and what deadlines apply to your citation.
  • Consider whether the charge is ordinary or excessive speed, since a misdemeanor carries higher stakes.
  • Speak with a licensed attorney if you face a criminal charge or a possible suspension.

If you are near the 8+ points in 12 months threshold, the question is not only the fine. A suspension or Traffic Survival School may be at stake, so ask the Motor Vehicle Division where you stand.

What happens if I don't pay an Arizona speeding ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes things worse. Courts can add consequences when a ticket goes unpaid or a required appearance is missed. The details depend on the court and the charge.

If the offense is excessive speed, a class 3 misdemeanor, ignoring it carries more serious risk than a civil ticket.

If you cannot pay in full or cannot make the date, contact the court clerk before the deadline on your citation. Courts are often more willing to work with people who get in touch early.
