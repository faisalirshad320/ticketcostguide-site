How much is a speeding ticket in Florida?

Florida sets the base penalty by how far over the limit you were driving. Under Fla. Stat. §318.18(3) (Justia), the statutory tiers are:

  • $25 for 6–9 mph over
  • $100 for 10–14 mph over
  • $150 for 15–19 mph over
  • $175 for 20–29 mph over
  • $250 for 30+ mph over

These amounts are doubled in school and work zones, and county court costs raise real totals substantially (FLHSMV).

The base tiers are uniform statewide. The same speed carries the same base penalty in every county, so the difference you see between jurisdictions comes from the add-ons.

The statutory penalty is the floor of what you will pay, not the total. County court costs and fees are added on top, and they vary by county.

What drives the total cost of a Florida speeding ticket?

Three things move the final number: how far over the limit you were, where you were cited, and which county court handles the ticket.

The speed tier fixes the base penalty. A ticket at the low end of the scale starts at $25, while one at 30+ over starts at $250. That gap is set by statute and does not change by county.

The location matters in two ways. If the citation was issued in a school or work zone, the statutory amount is doubled. And the county where the ticket is processed decides which court costs and fees are added on top of the penalty.

Because those county amounts vary, we do not publish a single all-in figure for Florida. The citation or the county clerk's payment page shows the actual amount owed for your ticket.

Beyond the payment itself, points on your record and a possible rise in insurance premiums are separate costs that can outlast the fine.

Why do county court costs change what I pay?

County courts add costs and fees on top of the statutory penalty. Florida's base penalty tiers are uniform statewide, but these add-on costs vary by county.

That means two drivers cited for the same speed can owe different totals if their tickets are handled in different counties. The statute sets the starting point, and the county sets much of what gets stacked on top.

We do not list an exact add-on amount for any county, because the amounts depend on the court and can change. To see what applies to your citation, check the payment instructions on the ticket or contact the clerk of court for the county named on it.

Do not estimate your total from the base penalty alone. County court costs raise real totals substantially.

How much more does a school or work zone ticket cost?

School and work-zone amounts are doubled by statute. The doubling applies to the speed-based penalty tiers listed in Fla. Stat. §318.18(3) (Justia).

In practical terms, a ticket that would carry a $100 base penalty on an ordinary road carries a doubled amount when issued in a covered zone. The same doubling applies to each tier, so the higher tiers become the most expensive.

County court costs are added on top of the doubled amount and vary by county. Check the citation to confirm whether the zone designation was marked, since it changes the base penalty.

How many points does a speeding ticket add in Florida?

A speeding conviction adds 3 points if you were going 15 or less over the limit, or 4 points if you were going 16+ over.

Points matter because of the suspension threshold: 12 points in 12 months results in a 30-day suspension. A single ticket will not reach that level, but several convictions in a short period can.

  • 15 or less over: 3 points
  • 16+ over: 4 points
  • 12 points in 12 months: 30-day suspension

For your own record and point total, check with the Florida Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles department (FLHSMV). For a comparison with other states' rules, the Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a summary of state laws (GHSA).

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance in Florida?

It can. Published market analyses indicate that a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal.

That is a national average, not a Florida figure. Your actual change depends on your insurer, your driving history, the severity of the violation and how your policy is rated. Some drivers see little change and others see more.

Because the premium effect can last across renewals, it can cost more over time than the ticket itself. We do not recommend any insurer. If you are concerned, ask your own insurer how a conviction would be treated.

Can a Florida speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome depends on the facts and on the court. We do not give legal advice and cannot say whether your ticket qualifies.

The citation lists your options and the deadline for responding. Typically these include paying the amount, or contesting the ticket in court. Paying generally means accepting the conviction, which brings the points and any insurance consequences described above.

Because county costs and procedures vary, the clerk of court for your county is the right place to ask about what is available and what it would cost. If you want advice on contesting a ticket, a licensed Florida attorney can review your situation.

Before paying, compare the cost of the fine with the cost of points and a possible insurance increase.

What happens if I don't pay a Florida speeding ticket?

Ignoring a citation usually makes the situation worse. The court and FLHSMV can take further action, and the exact steps depend on your case and county.

Unpaid or unanswered tickets can add costs and can affect your driving privileges. We do not list specific late amounts because they vary by county and court.

Read the response deadline on the citation and act before it passes. If you cannot pay in full, contact the clerk of court for the county on your ticket to ask what options exist.
