How much is a speeding ticket in Connecticut?

The lead figure for Connecticut is this: set by the Superior Court violations schedule — commonly in the $150–$400 range with fees by mph over; traveling unreasonably fast or 85+ mph brings higher charges — Connecticut speeding fine range with fees (Connecticut Department of Motor Vehicles).

In plain terms, most speeding tickets fall somewhere inside that range. Where yours lands depends mainly on how far over the limit you were recorded.

The speeding provisions appear in Conn. Gen. Stat. §14-218a / §14-219 (Justia). The statutes define the offenses, and the court's violations schedule turns them into payable amounts.

The number printed on your ticket is the number that matters. Use the ranges on this page to set expectations, not to replace it.

What drives the total on a Connecticut speeding ticket?

Three things shape the final cost. The first is the number of mph over the posted limit, because the schedule is built by mph bracket. A small overage sits toward the lower end of the range, and a larger one moves toward the upper end.

The second is the type of charge. Traveling unreasonably fast, or going 85+ mph, brings higher charges than the standard bracketed amounts. We do not publish a single dollar figure for those tiers, since the locked sources describe them only as higher.

The third is how the fees are packaged. State fees and surcharges are built into the payable amount on the ticket, so the amount you see is generally the amount you pay if you resolve the ticket as written.

  • Speed over the limit (the mph bracket)
  • Charge type (standard, unreasonably fast, or 85+ mph)
  • Fees included in the payable amount
  • Follow-on costs such as insurance changes at renewal

Are there surcharges and fees on top of the fine?

Connecticut builds state fees and surcharges into the payable amount on the ticket. You should not expect to add a separate list of surcharges to the total you see.

This differs from some states, where a base fine is printed and court costs are added at payment. If you are comparing states, the Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a state-by-state overview of traffic laws, though it does not replace each state's own fine schedule.

Amounts are set statewide by the judicial branch's violation schedule for each mph bracket. That means the schedule, not the town where you were stopped, is the starting point for your cost.

If the amount you are asked to pay looks different from the ticket, ask the court clerk to explain the difference before paying.

How many points does a speeding ticket add in Connecticut?

Speeding adds points under the Connecticut DMV point system. We do not list a per-offense point value here, because the locked source figures do not include one. Your ticket and the DMV can tell you how your specific violation is treated.

The threshold that matters is this: 10 points = 30-day suspension. Points accumulate on your driving record, so one ticket on top of earlier violations can matter more than the same ticket on a clean record.

  • Check your driving record through the Connecticut DMV to see where you stand.
  • Keep track of any other recent violations, since they count toward the same total.
  • Remember that a suspension is a separate consequence from the fine.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance in Connecticut?

It can. Published market analyses show that a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal.

That is a national average, not a Connecticut figure. Your own change could be smaller or larger, depending on your insurer, your record and the type of violation.

Because of this, the long-term cost of a ticket can exceed the fine. A fine is paid once, while a premium increase can last for several renewals.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer. To learn how a ticket would affect your own policy, ask your insurer or an independent agent.

Can a Connecticut speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Some tickets are contested, and outcomes vary. We cannot say how likely a dismissal or reduction is, and nothing on this page is legal advice.

What we can say is that the ticket itself lists your options and the process for responding. Read it carefully, because choosing to pay it generally resolves the matter as charged.

If you are weighing whether to contest, compare the possible savings against the other costs. Those include points toward the 10-point suspension threshold and a possible premium increase of roughly 20–25% nationally.

  • Read the instructions printed on the ticket.
  • Contact the court clerk with procedural questions.
  • For advice about your specific case, consult a licensed Connecticut attorney of your own choosing.

What happens if I don't pay a Connecticut speeding ticket?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. Unresolved tickets can lead to additional court action and trouble with your driving privileges.

We do not list specific deadlines or penalty amounts here, because they are not part of our locked figures. Your ticket and the court are the authoritative sources for them.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court clerk before the deadline on the ticket rather than waiting. Courts can often explain what options exist for your situation.

Also keep the point system in mind. A violation that stays unresolved does not make the points issue go away, and the DMV treats a 10-point total as grounds for a 30-day suspension.

Frequently asked questions

How much is a speeding ticket in Connecticut?

Are surcharges added to a Connecticut speeding ticket?

State fees and surcharges are built into the payable amount shown on the ticket. You should not expect a separate surcharge bill on top of it. If the amount you are asked to pay differs from the ticket, ask the court clerk to explain.

How many points until a Connecticut license is suspended?

Speeding adds points under the Connecticut DMV point system, and 10 points = 30-day suspension. Points from other violations count toward the same total, so a speeding ticket can matter more if your record already has points.

Does the fine change by county in Connecticut?

No. Amounts are set statewide by the judicial branch's violation schedule for each mph bracket. The main factor is how many mph over the limit you were recorded going, along with whether the charge is for unreasonably fast driving or 85+ mph.

What is the penalty for going 85 mph or more in Connecticut?
