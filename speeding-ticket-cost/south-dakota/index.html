How much is a speeding ticket in South Dakota?

A Class 2 misdemeanor with scheduled fine-and-costs totals commonly about $85–$200 by mph over (South Dakota Department of Public Safety). That is the number to keep in mind when you first open the envelope or read the citation.

The range is a guide, not a quote. The low end generally applies to a small overage and the high end to a larger one. We do not list an exact dollar amount for each mph bracket because we could not source one, and we would rather leave a gap than guess.

Check the amount printed on your citation or ask the court clerk. That figure, not a general range, is what you will actually owe.

South Dakota speeding offenses fall under SDCL §32-25 (ch.), which you can read through Justia's state code.

What drives the total on a South Dakota speeding ticket?

The biggest driver is how many miles per hour you were over the posted limit. The scheduled total moves up as that number grows, which is why the published range is described "by mph over."

A few other things can matter in practice:

  • How the ticket is resolved. Most tickets are paid on the uniform schedule without a court appearance. Going to court can change the outcome.
  • Which court handles it. Always confirm the amount with the court named on your citation.
  • Your driving record. Points are assigned per conviction, so a ticket can affect your standing toward suspension thresholds.
  • Insurance later. This is not part of the fine, but it often costs more than the ticket itself over time.

We do not state separate amounts for school zones, work zones or commercial drivers. Our sources did not give us those figures, so check your citation if any of those apply.

Are court costs and surcharges added on top of the fine?

For standard scheduled tickets, court costs are included in the standard scheduled amounts. The $85–$200 range is a fine-and-costs total, so you should not expect to add a separate court-cost line to it.

That is useful when you are comparing numbers. If a website quotes only a base fine, its figure will look lower than what you actually pay. The scheduled total is the number to compare.

If your citation shows a higher amount than you expected, ask the clerk what it includes. We have no sourced figures for any other fee, so we do not list any.

How many points does a speeding ticket add in South Dakota?

Points are assigned per conviction toward suspension thresholds (15 points in 12 months). In plain terms, each speeding conviction adds points to your record, and reaching the threshold within the 12-month window can lead to suspension.

We do not give a points value for each speed bracket. Our sources did not include a verified table, and a wrong number could mislead you. Your citation, the court or the state driver licensing office can tell you the points tied to your specific conviction.

Points attach to a conviction. How you resolve the ticket can therefore affect your record as well as your wallet.

For a broader look at how states handle points and related rules, see the Governors Highway Safety Association's state laws pages.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance in South Dakota?

It often does. Published market analyses show that a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal. That is a national average, not a South Dakota figure.

Your own result could be higher, lower or nil. It depends on your insurer, your driving history, how fast you were going and whether the ticket becomes a conviction on your record.

Because that increase can last across renewals, the ticket's true cost can exceed the scheduled total. We do not recommend any insurer. If you are worried about your rate, ask your own insurer how it treats a single minor violation.

Can a speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

It can be, but there is no guarantee, and the options depend on the facts and the court. Most tickets are paid on the uniform schedule without a court appearance, which is the simplest path. Paying generally means accepting the conviction and the points that go with it.

Contesting the ticket means going through the court process. Some drivers consider that when points or insurance are a concern. Others find the scheduled total is lower than the time and risk involved.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot say what you should do. If the stakes are high, such as a commercial license or a record near the 15-point threshold, consider speaking with a licensed attorney of your own choosing.

What happens if I don't pay a South Dakota speeding ticket?

A speeding ticket here is a Class 2 misdemeanor under SDCL §32-25 (ch.), so ignoring it is risky. Missing a deadline or a required court date can lead to further consequences, which may include added penalties or action on your license.

We cannot give exact deadlines or penalty amounts because our sources did not provide them. Read your citation closely, because it should state how and when to respond.

  1. Find the court and the response instructions on the citation.
  2. Decide whether to pay on the schedule or contest it.
  3. If you are unsure about the deadline, call the clerk before it passes.

Contacting the court early is almost always better than waiting to see what happens.
