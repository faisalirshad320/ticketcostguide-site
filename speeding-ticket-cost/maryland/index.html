How much is a speeding ticket in Maryland?

Maryland District Court prepayable speeding fines run from roughly $80 to $530, depending on how many miles per hour over the limit you were driving (Maryland Motor Vehicle Administration). The schedule has higher tiers for 20+, 30+ and 40+ mph over.

The fine is set under Md. Transp. §21-801.1 and the District Court fine schedule (Justia, Maryland code). The citation you receive should show the amount that applies to your alleged speed.

The more you exceed the limit, the higher the tier and the larger the fine. A ticket near the bottom of the range costs far less than one in the 30+ or 40+ over tiers.

We publish only the range and tier thresholds. For the exact prepay amount, read your citation or check with the MVA or the District Court.

Does the cost change by county in Maryland?

No. Maryland publishes one statewide prepay schedule, so the prepayable amount for a given speed doesn't vary by county. A ticket in one county is priced the same way as a ticket in another.

That differs from some states, where each court sets its own amounts. You can compare how states handle traffic laws in the Governors Highway Safety Association state laws overview.

Your total can still differ from a neighbor's ticket because of the speed recorded on your citation, and because of whether you prepay or ask for a court date.

How many points does a speeding ticket add in Maryland?

Points depend on how far over the limit you were going:

  • 9 mph or less over: 1 point
  • 10–29 mph over: 2 points
  • 30+ mph over: 5 points

Points are added to your driving record with the Maryland Motor Vehicle Administration (MVA).

Eight points leads to suspension and 12 points leads to revocation. A single 30+ over ticket adds 5 points, which gets you much closer to the suspension line than a minor ticket does.

Points and fines are separate costs. Paying a fine doesn't erase the points, and the points can matter more than the fine if you already have other violations.

Should I prepay or request a court date?

Prepaying means paying the scheduled amount, within the roughly $80–$530 range, without appearing in court. It's usually the quicker route.

Requesting a court date changes the math. The prepay amount no longer applies, and a judge sets the fine, up to $500.

That ceiling is lower than the top of the prepay range, but a judge-set fine is not guaranteed to be lower than your prepay amount. We can't tell you which option is better for your situation.

  • Prepaying generally means you accept the violation and its points.
  • A court date gives you the chance to present your side to a judge.
  • The outcome of a hearing is up to the judge, not a published schedule.

If you're unsure about your rights or options, consider speaking with a licensed attorney or the court clerk. TicketCostGuide can't give legal advice.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance in Maryland?

It can. Published market analyses suggest a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal.

That is a national average, not a Maryland figure. Your actual change depends on your insurer, your driving history and how serious the violation was.

Because a premium increase can continue across several renewals, the long-term cost of a conviction may exceed the fine itself. We don't recommend any insurer and can't predict what your own company will do.

Treat 20–25% as a rough benchmark for planning only. Ask your insurer how it treats violations if you want a specific answer.

What drives the total cost of a Maryland speeding ticket?

Several factors shape what a ticket costs you overall:

  1. Speed over the limit. This sets the fine tier and the points: 1, 2 or 5.
  2. Prepay or court date. Prepaying uses the scheduled amount. A court date means a judge-set fine up to $500.
  3. Your point total. Eight points means suspension and 12 means revocation.
  4. Insurance at renewal. Nationally, roughly 20–25% on average.

The fine is the visible part. Points and insurance often add more over time.

Can a ticket be reduced, and what happens if I don't pay?

Whether a ticket is reduced or dismissed is decided by the court. Requesting a court date is how you ask a judge to consider your case. Results vary, and we can't predict them.

Ignoring a ticket is risky. An unresolved citation can lead to further consequences with the court and the MVA, and your license status may be affected. Check your citation and the MVA website for current instructions and deadlines.

If your point total is approaching 8, the stakes of how you resolve the ticket are higher. Consider getting individual advice from a licensed professional.
