How much is a speeding ticket in Maine?

Maine speeding tickets are priced from a statewide schedule. Totals commonly run about $135–$500+ depending on how many miles per hour over the limit you were clocked, and that range includes surcharges (Maine Bureau of Motor Vehicles).

The schedule is set out under 29-A M.R.S. §2074 and the Violations Bureau schedule, which you can read through the state code on Justia. The "500+" end of the range signals that the most serious tiers can go past the figure we show.

The lower end of the range applies to the smallest overages. The higher end applies as the speed over the limit climbs. Your ticket shows the exact amount for your bracket.

We do not list a separate dollar figure for each mph bracket because the published figure we rely on is the overall range. For the exact number, read the amount printed on your ticket or ask the Violations Bureau.

What drives the total on a Maine speeding ticket?

The main driver is the mph bracket recorded on your ticket. Because Maine uses a statewide schedule, the bracket fixes the amount. A few miles over costs toward the low end of the range, and a large overage costs toward the high end or beyond.

That makes the officer's recorded speed the most important number on the page. Check that it matches what you understood about the stop and the posted limit.

  • Speed over the limit: the bracket sets the scheduled amount.
  • Surcharges: statutory surcharges are already built into the scheduled amounts.
  • Points: they do not change the fine, but they affect your license and your insurance over time.

The ticket's fine is only part of the cost. A conviction can also bring points and, later, a higher insurance premium.

Are surcharges added on top of the fine?

Statutory surcharges are built into the scheduled amounts. The total you see on a Violations Bureau schedule already folds them in, so you should not expect to add them again on top of that figure (Maine Bureau of Motor Vehicles).

This is why we describe the range as "including surcharges." If you are comparing a figure from another website, check whether it shows the base fine alone or the full scheduled total. Those can look quite different.

We do not publish a dollar breakdown of individual surcharges because the sources we rely on do not give one. If you need the split, the Violations Bureau or the court handling your ticket can explain it.

How many points does a speeding ticket add in Maine?

Demerit points accrue per conviction. The key threshold is that 12 points in 12 months means a suspension (Maine Bureau of Motor Vehicles).

We do not state a point value for each mph bracket, because the figures we rely on do not list one. Your driving record through the Bureau of Motor Vehicles shows what has been added to your license.

Points follow a conviction. If you pay the ticket, that generally counts as accepting the conviction, so think about points before you pay.

If you already have points on your record, a new speeding conviction matters more. Count what you have within the last 12 months and compare it with the suspension threshold.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance in Maine?

It can. Published market analyses show that a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal. That is a national average, not a Maine figure.

Your own change could be smaller, larger or nonexistent. It depends on your insurer, your driving history, how far over the limit you were and when your policy renews.

  • The increase usually shows up at renewal, not right after the stop.
  • A conviction is what typically drives the change, so how the ticket is resolved matters.
  • TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer. Ask your own insurer how a conviction would affect your rate.

Over several years, the extra premium can exceed the fine itself. That is worth weighing when you decide how to respond to the ticket.

Can a Maine speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Some tickets are contested, and the outcome depends on the facts and on the court or Violations Bureau process. The scheduled amount applies if you are convicted in a given mph bracket, so the bracket on the ticket matters a great deal.

We cannot tell you whether your ticket should be contested. For that, read the instructions on your ticket and contact the court or Violations Bureau listed on it. Questions about your specific case belong with a licensed attorney.

  • Check that the recorded speed and posted limit on the ticket are correct.
  • Read the response options printed on the ticket before you pay.
  • Remember that paying generally resolves the ticket as a conviction, with points and possible insurance effects.

We do not list deadlines or reduction rules here because they are not part of the figures we sourced. The ticket and the court are the reliable place to confirm them.

What happens if I don't pay a Maine speeding ticket?

Ignoring a ticket rarely makes it go away. Unresolved tickets can lead to further court action and can put your driving privileges at risk. The ticket itself and the court will list the exact steps and timing.

We do not state specific late fees or deadlines, because they are not part of the figures we sourced. Do not assume the scheduled amount stays the same if you wait.

If you cannot pay in full or are unsure how to respond, contact the court or Violations Bureau named on your ticket before the due date passes.

The Bureau of Motor Vehicles can tell you about the status of your license and any points on your record.

How we sourced these numbers

Our lead figure, about $135–$500+ by mph over including surcharges, comes from the Uniform Violations Bureau fine schedule as reported by the Maine Bureau of Motor Vehicles, last reviewed Oct 2026. The points rule (12 points in 12 months means suspension) comes from the same body.

The legal basis is 29-A M.R.S. §2074 and the Violations Bureau schedule, available through the Justia state code. For comparison with other states' traffic laws, the Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a state laws index.

The 20–25% insurance figure is a national average from published market analyses, not a Maine-specific number. We do not use any figure that is not in these sources, and we do not give legal advice.
