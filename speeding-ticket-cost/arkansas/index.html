How much is a speeding ticket in Arkansas?

The fine is set by the district court within statutory limits — commonly $25–$500 depending on speed and prior offenses, plus court costs (Arkansas Department of Finance and Administration — Driver Services). That range covers the fine itself. Court costs are added on top.

The statute usually cited for speeding in Arkansas is Ark. Code §27-51-201. It frames the limits the court works within, which is why the amount is a range and not one fixed price.

We do not publish a mile-per-hour-by-fine schedule. Our sources give a range, and we will not invent exact figures for each speed bracket.

Your citation or court notice shows what applies to your case. Treat the range on this page as a guide, not a quote.

What drives the total cost of an Arkansas speeding ticket?

Three things move the number most: how fast you were going, whether you have prior offenses, and which county court handles the ticket.

  • Speed. Higher speeds push the fine toward the statutory maximum.
  • Prior offenses. Repeat offenses also move the fine toward the maximum.
  • County and court. Court costs are added by the district court and vary by county.

The fine and the court costs are separate pieces. Two drivers with the same fine can owe different totals if their cases are in different counties.

Points and insurance add costs that never appear on the ticket. A conviction puts points on your driving record, and a higher premium may follow at renewal. Both are covered below.

If you are budgeting, plan for a figure somewhere in the range, add court costs, and then confirm the actual amount with the court handling your case.

How many points does a speeding ticket add in Arkansas?

Speeding convictions carry 3–8 points by speed. The faster you were going, the more points you can expect, up to the top of that range.

Points matter because 14+ points can trigger suspension. A single ticket at the high end of the range is a large share of that threshold, and several smaller tickets can add up.

For the rules on how points are tracked and what happens to your license, check Arkansas Department of Finance and Administration — Driver Services at the agency's Driver Services page. That is the agency that handles driver records.

Our sources do not say how long points stay on a record. We will not guess. Ask Driver Services for the current rules.

Two points are worth separating: the fine ends when you pay it, but points sit on your record and can combine with future tickets.

What surcharges and court costs are added to the fine?

The surcharge our sources name is court costs. Court costs are added by the district court and vary by county.

Because they vary, we do not give a dollar amount for them. Any exact figure we printed would be wrong for some counties. The accurate answer is "the fine range, plus whatever court costs your county's district court adds."

To find the real number, you have a few options:

  • Read the citation and any court notice for an amount due.
  • Contact the clerk of the district court that has your case.
  • Ask whether the amount quoted includes court costs or only the fine.

Asking that last question avoids a common surprise, where a quoted fine is lower than the total you owe once costs are added.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance in Arkansas?

It can. Published market analyses indicate a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal.

That is a national average, not an Arkansas figure. We do not have a sourced Arkansas-specific number, so we do not give one.

What you actually pay depends on your insurer, your driving history, the speed involved and when your policy renews. Some drivers see little change, and others see more.

The insurance effect can outweigh the fine over time, because a higher premium can last across renewals. When you compare options for handling a ticket, include that possible cost along with the fine and court costs.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend insurers. For a real quote, ask your own insurer or compare quotes yourself.

Can an Arkansas speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes outcomes differ from the original citation, but that is decided by the court in your case. Our sources do not list specific dismissal or reduction programs, so we do not describe any.

What the sourced facts do tell you is what pushes costs up: higher speeds and repeat offenses move the fine toward the statutory maximum. A lower speed and a clean record sit toward the lower end of the range.

To learn what options your court offers, start with the district court clerk. The clerk can explain deadlines, how to respond to the citation and how to appear.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice. If you want advice on contesting a ticket, you can consult a licensed Arkansas attorney of your own choosing.

What happens if I don't pay an Arkansas speeding ticket?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse, not better. The court that issued the citation controls what happens next, and the details depend on that court.

Our sources do not give specific late fees, deadlines or enforcement steps, so we will not state any. Check your citation and court notice for dates, and contact the district court clerk if anything is unclear.

There is also a points angle. Since 14+ points can trigger suspension, an unresolved ticket that ends in a conviction can still affect your record. Ask Driver Services how a particular outcome would be recorded.

  • Read the citation for the response instructions.
  • Contact the court before any deadline passes.
  • Confirm the total owed, including court costs.

If you cannot pay in full, ask the clerk what the court allows. Do not assume an option exists until the court confirms it.
