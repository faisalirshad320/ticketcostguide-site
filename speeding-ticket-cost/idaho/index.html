How much is a speeding ticket in Idaho?

Idaho treats most speeding as an infraction with a fixed penalty. The commonly cited cost is about $90–$155 total including court costs, depending on the zone and mph over (Idaho Transportation Department — DMV).

That is a range, not a quote for your ticket. The amount printed on your citation, or set by the court that handles it, is the figure that applies to you.

Idaho's flat infraction amounts make totals more predictable than most states.

The penalty comes from Idaho Code §49-654, which covers speed limits and the basic rules of speeding. This page does not list a separate dollar figure for each mph bracket, because we only publish numbers we can source.

What drives the total cost of an Idaho speeding ticket?

Two factors move the fixed penalty within the range: the zone where you were stopped and how many mph over the limit you were going. Both are recorded on your citation.

  • Zone: the type of area where the violation happened affects where you land in the range.
  • Mph over: a larger gap above the limit generally points toward the higher end.
  • Court handling: the court listed on your citation sets how and where you pay.

Beyond the fine itself, the larger long-term costs usually come from points and your insurance renewal. Those are covered below.

Are there extra fees or surcharges on top of the fine?

The fixed penalty already includes court costs for most infractions. That means the range of about $90–$155 is meant to be the total, not a base fine you add fees to.

Any other local charge varies by county and court. We do not publish an exact local figure because we have no sourced number for it.

If your citation shows a total that looks different from the range, read the citation closely. The court listed on it can explain what the amount includes.

How many points does a speeding ticket add in Idaho?

A moving violation in Idaho carries 3–4 points. A speeding conviction is a moving violation, so expect points to be added to your driving record.

Points matter because they accumulate. Reaching 12–17 points in 12 months results in a 30-day suspension.

  1. Check the points listed for your violation.
  2. Add them to any other moving violations in the same period.
  3. Compare the total with the 12–17 point suspension range.

The Idaho Transportation Department — DMV is the agency that keeps driver records. Its site is the place to confirm your own status.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance in Idaho?

It can. Nationally, a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal, according to published market analyses.

That is a national average, not an Idaho figure. Your own change depends on your insurer, your driving history and how your policy is rated.

Over a policy term, a premium increase can outweigh the fine itself. It is worth weighing when you decide how to respond to a ticket.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer or give advice on your policy. Ask your insurer how a conviction would affect your renewal.

Can an Idaho speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

We do not have sourced figures on dismissal rates or reduction programs, so we will not guess at them. What we can say is that the citation and the court listed on it control your options.

Read the citation for the response instructions and any deadline. Contact the court clerk for the process that applies to your case.

This is not legal advice. If you are worried about points, a suspension or your record, a licensed Idaho attorney can advise you on your situation.

What happens if I don't pay an Idaho speeding ticket?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse, but we have no sourced figures on Idaho's specific late penalties, so we do not list any. The court listed on your citation can tell you the consequences in your case.

Because Idaho's infraction amounts are fixed, you can usually find out exactly what you owe by checking the citation or contacting the court.

  • Read the citation for the payment and response instructions.
  • Contact the court if the instructions are unclear.
  • Confirm how any conviction would affect your points before you decide.

A conviction adds points, and points can lead to a suspension at the 12–17 point level, so a missed ticket can have costs beyond the fine.
