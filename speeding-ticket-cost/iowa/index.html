How much is a speeding ticket in Iowa?

Iowa sets speeding fines by a schedule rather than leaving the amount to each judge. The lead figure is: scheduled fines under §805.8A — roughly $50–$200 for 1–20 over with a per-mph addition above 20 — plus a criminal surcharge and court costs on top (Iowa Department of Transportation — Motor Vehicle Division).

In plain terms, the fine printed for your speed bracket is only part of what you owe. The surcharge and court costs are added to it, so the amount you pay is higher than the base fine alone.

The statute behind the schedule is Iowa Code §805.8A / §321.285. The schedule ties the fine to how far over the limit you were driving.

Rule of thumb: for 1–20 over, expect the base fine to land somewhere in the roughly $50–$200 range, then add a surcharge and court costs. Above 20 over, the per-mph addition pushes the base higher.

What drives the total on an Iowa speeding ticket?

Three things shape the final bill: your speed bracket, the statutory surcharge, and court costs.

  • Speed over the limit. The scheduled fine rises with the number of miles per hour over. Above 20 over, a per-mph addition applies.
  • Criminal surcharge. A statutory surcharge is added to every scheduled fine, whatever the bracket.
  • Court costs. These are also added to every scheduled fine and can vary by county or court.

Because the schedule makes the base amount uniform statewide for each bracket, a driver in one county should see the same base fine as a driver in another for the same speed. Differences in the final total come mainly from the add-ons.

We do not publish an exact surcharge or court-cost figure here because our sourced data does not give one. Your citation or the clerk of court will show the actual amount owed.

Is the fine the same in every Iowa county?

The base fine is. The scheduled-fine system makes the base amount uniform statewide for each bracket, so a ticket for the same number of miles over carries the same scheduled fine regardless of where you were stopped.

What can differ is the add-on side. Court costs vary by county or court, so two people with identical base fines may not pay identical totals.

If you want the exact number, look at the amount printed on your citation, then confirm it with the clerk of court in the county where the ticket was issued. Do not rely on a figure from a different county.

How many points does a speeding ticket add in Iowa?

Iowa does not publish a running point total for ordinary speeding the way many states do. Instead, Iowa tracks convictions for its habitual-offender and suspension rules.

That means the practical question is not "how many points," but "how many convictions are on my record." A speeding conviction becomes part of the record the state uses when it applies those rules (Iowa Department of Transportation — Motor Vehicle Division).

For the exact thresholds that trigger a suspension or habitual-offender status, read the code text on Justia or ask the Motor Vehicle Division. We do not state thresholds that we have not sourced.

For how other states handle penalties and point systems, the Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a state-laws index.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance in Iowa?

It can. Published market analyses indicate that a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal.

That is a national average, not an Iowa figure. Your own change depends on your insurer, your driving record, how fast you were going and how the ticket is resolved.

Premium changes usually show up at renewal rather than immediately. The cost of a conviction can therefore exceed the fine itself, since the higher premium may last across more than one renewal.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend insurers or give insurance advice. To see how your own rate might change, ask your insurer or an independent agent.

Can an Iowa speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome depends on the facts and on the court. Because the base fine follows a schedule, a lower amount generally comes from a lower charge or a dismissal, not from a judge simply picking a smaller number for the same offense.

Options people commonly look at include contesting the ticket, asking the court about a different charge, or checking whether any program the court offers applies to them. We cannot say which of these is available in your case.

Whatever route you consider, remember the add-ons. If a conviction is entered, the criminal surcharge and court costs come with it. If you want advice on whether to contest, that is a question for a licensed attorney, not for this guide.

What happens if I don't pay an Iowa speeding ticket?

Ignoring a citation tends to make things worse. The ticket carries a response deadline, and missing it can lead to additional court action and added consequences for your license, as the state's suspension rules are built around convictions and compliance.

We have not sourced specific late fees or deadlines, so we do not state any here. Read the instructions on your citation, then contact the clerk of court if anything is unclear.

If you cannot pay the full amount at once, ask the clerk whether the court offers any payment arrangement. Doing that before the deadline is better than doing it after.
