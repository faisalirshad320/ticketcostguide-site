How much is a speeding ticket in Delaware?

For a first offense at lower speeds, the statutory fine is $25–$75. At higher speeds, per-mph additions apply, and repeat offenses carry larger fines. Court costs and fees come on top (Delaware Division of Motor Vehicles).

The fine structure comes from 21 Del. C. §4169, which you can read through the Justia state code library. The statute sets the fine. It does not set the full amount you will pay.

That is why the number printed on a ticket or court notice can be higher than the base fine. Court costs and state fees are added to the statutory fine.

The $25–$75 range is the fine for a first offense at lower speeds only. It is not the total cost of a ticket, and it does not cover higher speeds, repeat offenses, court costs or fees.

What drives the total cost of a Delaware speeding ticket?

Four things move the total: how fast you were going, whether it is a repeat offense, the court costs and fees, and the long-term effect on your record and insurance.

  • Speed over the limit. Lower speeds fall into the $25–$75 first-offense range. At higher speeds, per-mph additions raise the fine.
  • Offense history. Repeat offenses carry larger fines than a first offense.
  • Court costs and state fees. These are added to the statutory fine. The exact amounts can vary by county or court, and we do not publish a single figure for them.
  • Indirect costs. Points on your record and a possible change in your insurance premium can cost more over time than the fine itself.

To find your own total, check the amount on your citation or on the court's payment notice. The court that handles your ticket is the final authority on what you owe.

What court costs and fees are added to the fine?

Delaware adds court costs and state fees to the statutory fine. Our locked source data confirms that this happens. It does not give a single fixed amount for either one.

For that reason, we show these items in the table as "added to the statutory fine; varies by county/court." We do not estimate a local figure. A made-up number would be worse than none if you used it to budget.

Ask the court listed on your ticket for the exact amount due. The Delaware Division of Motor Vehicles also publishes general information at dmv.de.gov.

Think of the cost in two layers: the statutory fine under 21 Del. C. §4169, and then the court costs and fees stacked on top of it.

How many points does a speeding ticket carry in Delaware?

A speeding conviction carries 2–5 points, depending on how many mph over the limit you were going. Higher speeds over the limit mean more points.

Two thresholds matter on your record:

  • 12 points in 2 years leads to a behavior modification course.
  • 14 or more points leads to a suspension.

Delaware also halves point values after 12 months for calculation purposes. An older violation therefore counts for less toward those thresholds than a recent one.

Points are separate from the fine. You can pay a modest fine and still move closer to a course requirement or suspension if you have other recent violations.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance rates?

It often does. Published market analyses show that a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal.

That is a national average. It is not a Delaware figure, and your own change could be higher, lower or none at all. It depends on your insurer, your driving history and the details of the violation.

Because premiums can stay elevated across several renewals, the insurance effect can add up to more than the fine. We do not recommend any insurer. Your insurer or a licensed agent can tell you how a conviction would affect your own policy.

For general background on how states handle traffic safety law, see the Governors Highway Safety Association state laws page.

Can a Delaware speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome depends on the facts and on the court. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and cannot say whether your ticket qualifies. Our locked data does not list specific dismissal or reduction programs, so we do not describe any.

The ticket itself should say how to respond, whether by paying the fine or contesting it. Contesting means you do not simply pay. Paying is generally treated as accepting the violation, which brings the fine, the points and the record entry.

If you are weighing your options, compare the full cost of paying with the possible cost of points and higher premiums. Then contact the court clerk with procedural questions. For advice on your specific case, speak with a licensed Delaware attorney of your own choosing.

What happens if I don't pay a speeding ticket in Delaware?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. The fine does not go away, and the court or the DMV may take further action. Our locked data does not list specific late penalties or deadlines, so we do not state any.

Read the deadline and instructions on your citation, and respond before that date. If you cannot pay the full amount or have missed the date, contact the court that issued the ticket as soon as possible.

Also remember that points can add up. If you reach 12 points in 2 years, you face a behavior modification course. At 14 or more points, you face a suspension. Letting a ticket go unresolved does not help your record.
