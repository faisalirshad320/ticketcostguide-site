How much is a speeding ticket in Wyoming?

Wyoming speeding fines are set by the court bond/fine schedule — commonly about $25–$200 by mph over, with higher amounts in work zones and for 80+ speeds (Wyoming Department of Transportation, last reviewed Oct 2026). That range is the lead number for the fine itself.

The speeding statute is Wyo. Stat. §31-5-301 (Justia). The amount on your citation depends on how far over the limit you were and on where the stop happened.

The fine is not always the full bill. Court fees are added per the local schedule, so the total can differ from one court to the next.

We publish only the sourced range. The exact amount for your ticket is on your citation or available from the clerk of the court that handles it.

What drives the total cost of a Wyoming speeding ticket?

Several things move the number up or down. The main ones are listed below.

  • Miles per hour over the limit. The bond schedule is organized by mph over, so a few miles over costs less than a much larger overage.
  • Work zones. Higher amounts apply in work zones than on an ordinary stretch of road.
  • Very high speeds. Higher amounts also apply for 80+ speeds.
  • Court fees. These are added per the local schedule and vary by county and court.
  • Where you were stopped. Town tickets cost more than interstate ones.

Put together, two drivers going the same speed can pay different totals if their tickets are handled by different courts.

Why is a Wyoming ticket cheaper on the interstate than in town?

Wyoming's low schedule reflects its high rural limits. Because the posted limit on open highway is high, the schedule is built so that being over by a modest amount there does not carry a large fine.

Town tickets cost more than interstate ones. A driver cited inside a town limit should expect the schedule to be less forgiving than one cited on a rural interstate for a similar mph over.

This does not mean a rural ticket is trivial. Work zones and 80+ speeds carry higher amounts wherever they occur, and court fees are still added per the local schedule.

Check the posted limit and the road type on your citation. They help explain why your amount differs from a friend's.

How many points does a speeding ticket add in Wyoming?

None. Wyoming has no demerit-point system, so a speeding conviction does not add a set number of points to your driving record.

That does not mean convictions are ignored. Suspensions follow repeat convictions and court action, so a pattern of tickets can still put your license at risk.

If you are comparing states, note that many others do assign points. For a broad look at how state laws differ, see the Governors Highway Safety Association state laws page.

Are there court fees on top of the fine?

Yes. Court fees are added per the local schedule. We do not list an exact fee because it varies by county and court, and we will not guess at a figure.

To find the full amount you owe, look at the citation for a stated total, or contact the clerk of the court named on it. Ask whether the amount shown includes court fees or only the base fine.

  • Base fine: set by the bond/fine schedule.
  • Court fees: added per the local schedule.
  • Total: the sum, which differs by court.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance in Wyoming?

It can. A speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal, according to published market analyses.

That is a national average, not a Wyoming figure. Your own change depends on your insurer, your driving history and the rules that apply to your policy.

Because the premium effect can last longer than the fine itself, it is worth thinking about the long-term cost, not only the amount due to the court. TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer or give insurance advice.

Can a Wyoming speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes a court will dismiss or reduce a citation, but outcomes depend on the court and the facts. We do not predict results, and nothing here is legal advice.

The citation will explain how to respond. If you want to dispute it, the court that issued the citation is the place to ask what steps are available.

  • Read the citation carefully, including the court named on it.
  • Ask the clerk what options that court offers.
  • Confirm whether the amount you were quoted includes court fees.

If you need advice on your specific situation, a licensed attorney is the right source. We do not recommend any particular one.

What happens if I don't pay a Wyoming speeding ticket?

Ignoring a citation can lead to court action. Wyoming ties suspensions to repeat convictions and court action, so an unresolved ticket can make a minor matter into a larger one.

The citation lists how and where to respond. If you are unsure, contact the clerk of the court named on it before doing anything else.

Responding promptly, whether you pay or contest, is generally cheaper and simpler than letting a ticket go unresolved.
