How much is a speeding ticket in New Mexico?

The lead figure is penalty assessments of roughly $15–$200 by mph over, plus fees — with higher court-set fines if the case goes before a judge (New Mexico Motor Vehicle Division). The faster you were going compared with the limit, the higher the assessment.

Fees are added to the penalty assessment, so what you pay is more than the assessment alone. Our sources describe the fees only as an add-on, so we do not state an exact fee amount.

Court cases can exceed the assessment amounts. If a judge sets the fine, the total can be higher than the assessment printed for your speed.

This page gives ranges, not a quote for your ticket. Your citation and the court handling it decide the real number.

What is a penalty assessment and how does it work?

A penalty assessment is a set amount tied to the offense, in speeding cases to how many mph over the limit you were driving. It is the state's way of letting you resolve a ticket without appearing before a judge. The governing provisions are NMSA §66-7-301 / §66-8-116 (state code via Justia).

Paying the penalty assessment resolves most tickets without a court date. For many drivers that is the main practical difference between an assessment and a court case.

The assessment is not the whole bill. Fees are added to it, and the amount can vary by county or court, so read the paperwork you received for the total due.

  • The assessment scales with mph over the limit.
  • Fees are added on top.
  • Paying generally closes the matter without a hearing.

What drives the total cost of a New Mexico speeding ticket?

Several factors move the final number. Speed is the biggest, because the assessment range of roughly $15–$200 is set by mph over.

The second factor is the route your case takes. An assessment you pay is one thing; a fine set by a judge can exceed the assessment amounts.

The third is fees, which are added to the penalty assessment and can vary by county or court. We do not have a sourced figure for them, and we will not guess at one.

The fourth is the long-term cost. Points and insurance changes can matter more than the ticket itself, as the next sections explain.

  • Mph over the limit: sets the assessment and the points.
  • Payment vs. court: court-set fines can be higher.
  • Fees: added to the assessment.
  • Your record: points accumulate over time.

How many points does a speeding ticket carry in New Mexico?

A speeding violation carries 3–8 points by mph over. A minor overage sits at the low end and a large one at the high end.

Points add up. Collecting 7–10 points in 12 months risks suspension, and 12 points means revocation.

Because the points range is wide, a single high-speed ticket can put a driver close to the suspension zone. Add an earlier violation within the same 12 months and the risk grows quickly.

Check the points shown for your specific violation with the New Mexico Motor Vehicle Division. That is the authority for your driving record.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance in New Mexico?

It can. A speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal, according to published market analyses.

That is a national average, not a New Mexico figure. Your own change depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the ticket is recorded.

Because the increase is a percentage of your premium, it often outweighs the one-time penalty over the following renewals. A driver with a higher premium sees a larger dollar increase.

We do not recommend any insurer and cannot predict what yours will do. Ask your insurer how it treats violations.

Can a speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes outcomes change when a case goes to court, but we cannot say how often or by how much. Court cases can exceed the assessment amounts, so contesting a ticket can also carry a risk of a higher fine.

Paying the penalty assessment resolves most tickets without a court date. That route is simple, but it is also a decision about the record and the points that come with the violation.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice. If you are weighing whether to contest, consider speaking with a licensed professional who can look at your citation.

  • Compare the assessment with the possible court outcome.
  • Consider the points and the 12-month suspension thresholds.
  • Think about the insurance effect at renewal.

What happens if I don't pay a New Mexico speeding ticket?

We do not have a sourced figure for late penalties or deadlines, so we will not state one. The paperwork you received is the best place to find the due date and the instructions for your case.

Ignoring a ticket generally leaves the matter unresolved, and a case that goes before a judge can end with a higher court-set fine than the assessment. Unpaid matters can also affect your driving record and license status.

If you are unsure what is due, contact the court named on the citation or the New Mexico Motor Vehicle Division. Do this before the date on the ticket passes.

How we sourced these numbers

The penalty assessment range, points and suspension thresholds come from NMSA §66-7-301 / §66-8-116 and the New Mexico Motor Vehicle Division, last reviewed Oct 2026. The statute text is available through Justia, and the agency is at the New Mexico Motor Vehicle Division.

The insurance figure is a national average from published market analyses and is not specific to New Mexico. Where a figure varies by county or court, we say so rather than invent a local amount.

Frequently asked questions

How many points is a speeding ticket in New Mexico?

A speeding violation carries 3–8 points by mph over. Higher speeds over the limit carry more points. Check the New Mexico Motor Vehicle Division for the points that apply to your specific violation and your record.

How many points will suspend my license in New Mexico?

Collecting 7–10 points in 12 months risks suspension, and 12 points means revocation. Because a single speeding ticket can carry 3–8 points, a second violation within the same 12 months can bring a driver near those thresholds.

Do I have to go to court for a speeding ticket in New Mexico?

Not usually. Paying the penalty assessment resolves most tickets without a court date. If you contest the ticket and the case goes before a judge, the fine is set by the court and can exceed the assessment amounts.

Are there fees on top of the penalty assessment?

Which law sets New Mexico speeding penalties?
