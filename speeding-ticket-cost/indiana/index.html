How much is a speeding ticket in Indiana?

In Indiana, a speeding ticket is generally an infraction, not a crime. The locked figure to start from is this: a Class C infraction with a judgment of up to $500, plus court costs that vary by county (commonly $120+) (Indiana Bureau of Motor Vehicles).

The $500 is a ceiling set by statute. It is not a price list, and it does not tell you what a specific ticket will cost.

The court costs are a separate layer. They are added on top of the judgment and depend on the county where the ticket is handled.

Key point: Most ordinary tickets resolve well below the $500 statutory ceiling. The final bill is the judgment plus county court costs, so two drivers with the same ticket in different counties can pay different totals.

What drives the total cost of an Indiana speeding ticket?

Several pieces combine to make the total. Only some of them are fixed by the state.

  • The judgment. This is the amount the court enters against you, capped at $500 for a Class C infraction.
  • County court costs. These vary by county and are commonly $120 or more.
  • Points. These do not add to the bill directly, but they affect your driving record and what comes next.
  • Insurance. This is not part of the court total, but it is often the larger long-term cost.

County court costs are added to the judgment and often exceed the fine for low speeds. That means for a minor ticket, the court costs can be the bigger share of what you pay.

We do not publish an exact judgment for each speed bracket. The locked sources give only the ceiling, so any specific local amount should be confirmed with the court that issued your ticket.

What does Indiana law say about speeding penalties?

Speeding falls under Ind. Code §9-21-5, which covers speed restrictions. You can read the state code through Justia's Indiana code library.

Under that framework, the infraction carries a judgment of up to $500. The state code sets the ceiling, while the county where the case is filed determines the court costs.

The Indiana Bureau of Motor Vehicles is the agency that handles driver records and the point system. Its site is the best place to confirm what is on your record: Indiana BMV.

For a broader comparison of how states treat traffic offenses, the Governors Highway Safety Association keeps an overview at GHSA State Laws.

How many points does a speeding ticket add in Indiana?

A speeding conviction adds 2–8 points, depending on how many miles per hour over the limit you were. Faster speeds fall toward the higher end of that range.

Points matter because accumulating points triggers BMV probation and suspension. A single ticket may not cause a problem, but repeated tickets can add up.

We do not list the exact mph brackets or the point total at which probation or suspension begins, because those details are outside the figures we have verified. Check your own record and the current rules with the Indiana BMV.

Tip: Ask the BMV for a copy of your driving record so you know your current point total before you decide how to handle a new ticket.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance rates in Indiana?

It can. Published market analyses show that a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal.

That is a national average, not an Indiana figure. Your own change could be smaller, larger, or nonexistent, depending on your insurer, your record, and the details of the ticket.

Because insurance is priced individually, we cannot say what your premium will do. Your insurer or policy documents are the place to find out how it treats a moving violation.

Over several years of renewals, a premium increase can exceed the cost of the ticket itself. That is why many drivers look at the full cost, not only the court total.

Can an Indiana speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Some tickets are contested, and outcomes vary by court. Because a Class C infraction carries a judgment of up to $500, the amount actually entered can fall below the ceiling.

We do not have verified, court-by-court information on diversion programs, reductions, or dismissal practices, so we do not describe them here. Those options, if any, are set by the county and the court handling your case.

The ticket itself should list how to respond and where. The court clerk can explain the process and the court costs that would apply in that county.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice. If you are weighing whether to contest a ticket, consider speaking with a licensed attorney of your own choosing.

What happens if I don't pay an Indiana speeding ticket?

Ignoring a ticket tends to make the situation worse, not better. The judgment and county court costs remain owed, and a missed deadline can bring further consequences from the court.

Indiana's point system also links unpaid or accumulated violations to your driving privileges. Accumulating points triggers BMV probation and suspension.

We do not state exact deadlines, late fees, or penalties, because those depend on the court and are not in our verified figures. Your citation and the court clerk are the right sources for them.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court before the due date and ask what options exist in that county.
