How much is a speeding ticket in New Hampshire?

New Hampshire publishes a fine schedule for speeding. Scheduled fines run roughly $62–$434 including the statutory 24% penalty assessment, rising by mph over (New Hampshire Division of Motor Vehicles).

The speeding statute is RSA 265:60, available through Justia's New Hampshire code. The fine for your ticket depends mainly on how far over the limit you were recorded.

That range is wide. A driver a little over the limit sits near the low end, and a driver well over sits near the top.

The range already includes the 24% assessment. You do not add the assessment on top of the $62–$434 figure.

We give the full range rather than a mph-by-mph chart. The exact amount for your citation is the one printed on it or listed by the court that handles it.

What drives the total cost of a New Hampshire speeding ticket?

Speed over the limit is the biggest factor. The scheduled fine rises as the mph over rises, so the recorded speed on the citation largely sets your base fine.

The second factor is the penalty assessment. It is a fixed percentage, so it grows in step with the base fine.

The third is the offense itself. The type of violation affects how many points are attached, and points matter for your license and for what insurers see.

  • Mph over the limit: sets the base fine within the schedule.
  • 24% penalty assessment: added to every base fine.
  • Points: 3–4 by offense, which can affect your license status.
  • Insurance at renewal: a conviction can raise your premium, though the size varies by insurer.

Because the posted schedule and the fixed assessment work together, the court-imposed total is fairly predictable once you know the base fine.

What is the 24% penalty assessment?

New Hampshire adds a 24% penalty assessment to every base fine by statute. It is not optional and it is not set by the individual court.

In practice, the number on your citation or on the court's notice is usually the base fine plus the assessment. The $62–$434 range we cite already reflects that.

Because the percentage is fixed, you can estimate the assessment on any base fine you are given. A larger base fine means a larger assessment.

If you see two numbers, ask whether one is the base fine and the other includes the assessment. Compare the total, not just the base.

This page does not list other possible charges because we have not sourced them. Check your citation or the court notice for anything beyond the fine and assessment.

How many points does a speeding ticket add in New Hampshire?

A speeding conviction adds 3–4 points, depending on the offense. The point count follows the specific violation, so a more serious speeding offense can carry the higher figure.

Points matter because of the suspension threshold. Reaching 12 points in 12 months can lead to a suspension of up to 3 months.

That threshold is the practical risk for drivers who collect more than one violation in a short period. A single speeding ticket alone adds only 3–4 points, but several violations can add up quickly.

  • Speeding conviction: 3–4 points by offense.
  • 12 points in 12 months: up to a 3-month suspension.

For the current rules and your own record, check with the New Hampshire Division of Motor Vehicles. For a comparison of how states handle traffic laws, the Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a state-law resource.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance in New Hampshire?

It can. Published market analyses suggest a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal.

That is a national average, not a New Hampshire figure. Your own change could be lower or higher depending on your insurer, your record and the type of violation.

The increase usually shows up when your policy renews, not the day you pay the fine. It can also last for more than one renewal, though how long varies by company.

Treat the 20–25% number as a rough national benchmark. We do not recommend any specific insurer. Ask your own insurer how a conviction would affect your rate.

Because the insurance effect can outweigh the fine over time, many drivers weigh the whole cost, not just the amount due at the court.

Can a speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

You generally have the right to contest a ticket instead of simply paying it. Paying the fine is typically treated as accepting the violation, which is what leads to points and a conviction on your record.

Whether a ticket can be dismissed or reduced depends on the facts and on the court. We cannot say what would happen in your case, and TicketCostGuide does not give legal advice.

If you are weighing options, read the instructions on your citation first. They explain how to respond and where.

  • Read the citation and any court notice carefully.
  • Note the recorded speed and the offense charged, since both drive the fine and points.
  • Consider whether a licensed attorney could advise you on your own situation.

Reducing the offense can change the points and the fine, because both follow the specific violation and the mph over.

What happens if I don't pay a New Hampshire speeding ticket?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. Unpaid or unanswered citations can lead to added penalties and can put your license at risk.

We do not list specific late fees or deadlines here because we have not sourced them. Your citation and the court's notice show the dates and amounts that apply to you.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court that issued the notice before the deadline and ask what options exist.

Do not assume a missed ticket will go away. Respond by the date on the citation, even if you plan to contest it.

A license problem on top of a speeding ticket is costly. Stay current on any notices from the court and the New Hampshire Division of Motor Vehicles.

Frequently asked questions

How much is a speeding ticket in New Hampshire?

Is the 24% penalty assessment added to the fine?

Yes. New Hampshire adds a 24% penalty assessment to every base fine by statute. The $62–$434 range already includes it, so you should not add it again. If your notice shows a base fine and a total, the total is what you owe.

Does a higher speed mean a higher fine in New Hampshire?

Yes. The scheduled fines rise by mph over the limit, which is why the range is wide, roughly $62–$434 with the 24% assessment. A small overage lands near the low end and a large one near the high end.

Are New Hampshire speeding ticket totals predictable?

Largely, yes. The state posts a fine schedule and adds a fixed 24% assessment to every base fine, so once you know the recorded speed you can estimate the total. Check your citation for the exact amount and any other charges.
