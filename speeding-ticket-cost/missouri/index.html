How much is a speeding ticket in Missouri?

Missouri court fine-schedule totals are commonly about $85–$250 including costs, rising by mph over the limit (Missouri Department of Revenue — Driver License Bureau). That range comes from the speeding provisions of Mo. Rev. Stat. §304.010 and the court fine schedule (Mo. Rev. Stat. §304.010 / court fine schedule — state code via Justia).

It is a range, not a single price. The amount printed on your ticket or in the court's notice is the one that applies to you.

The scheduled amounts already include court costs. You should not need to add a separate costs line on top of the scheduled total.

We do not list an exact dollar figure for each mph bracket. Courts set their own schedules, and we will not publish a number we cannot source.

What drives the total on a Missouri speeding ticket?

Three things move the number most. They are how far over the limit you were, which court is handling the ticket, and whether the charge is municipal or state.

  • Mph over the limit. The scheduled fine rises as the speed goes up, so the low end of the range is for the smallest overages and the high end is for the larger ones.
  • County or court. The amount varies by county and court. Two drivers with the same speed can see different totals.
  • Municipal or state charge. Whether the ticket is municipal or state changes both the fine and the points.

The last point is easy to miss. A ticket from a city police officer and one from a state officer can be filed in different places and carry different consequences.

Look at the citation to see which court it names. That tells you where the fine will be set and how it will be handled.

What if I was going 26 or more over the limit?

Under the locked schedule information we rely on, tickets for 26+ mph over must be heard by a judge. That means a scheduled amount you simply pay online or by mail is not the usual route.

You would need to appear, or follow the court's instructions for how the case will be heard. The total for these cases is set through the court process rather than by the scheduled range, so the $85–$250 figure should not be treated as a ceiling or a promise for them.

If your ticket is in this category, read the court notice closely and contact the court clerk about what it requires. Questions about your rights or defenses are legal questions. TicketCostGuide cannot answer them, and a licensed attorney can.

How many points does a speeding ticket add in Missouri?

A municipal conviction adds 2 points. A state charge adds 3 points (Missouri Department of Revenue — Driver License Bureau).

Points matter because they accumulate. Reaching 8 points in 18 months means a suspension.

  • Municipal conviction: 2 points
  • State charge: 3 points
  • Suspension threshold: 8 points in 18 months

Because municipal and state tickets carry different points, a single speeding ticket can move you closer to the threshold by different amounts depending on how it was filed.

If you have other recent violations, add their points to see where you stand. The Department of Revenue's Driver License Bureau is the place to confirm the points on your record. For a comparison of how other states handle points, see the Governors Highway Safety Association's State Laws page.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance in Missouri?

It can. A speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal, according to published market analyses.

That is a national average, not a Missouri figure. Your own change could be smaller, larger or nonexistent, depending on your insurer, your driving record and the details of the violation.

We do not recommend any insurer and cannot predict your rate. If you want to know the effect on your policy, ask your insurer how it treats a conviction of this type.

It helps to think of the insurance effect as a second cost that arrives later. The fine is paid once, while a higher premium can last through several renewals.

Can a Missouri speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes courts handle tickets in ways other than a plain guilty plea. Whether that is possible for your ticket depends on the court, the charge and your record.

We do not list specific programs or amendment options here, because they vary by county and court and are not part of the sourced figures on this page. The court named on your citation can tell you what choices exist and what deadlines apply.

Since the outcome affects both the fine and the points, it is worth understanding the whole result before you decide. A lower fine does not always mean fewer points, and a municipal or state label can change the points on its own.

For advice about your particular case, speak with a licensed attorney. We do not give legal advice or recommend any attorney.

What happens if I don't pay a Missouri speeding ticket?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. Courts can add consequences when a ticket goes unanswered, and those can affect your driving privileges.

The specific steps depend on the court handling your ticket. We do not publish exact deadlines or added penalty amounts, because they are not in our sourced figures.

  • Read the citation for the court name and the instructions it gives.
  • Contact the court clerk if you are unsure what is owed or where to respond.
  • Use the Department of Revenue's Driver License Bureau to check your license status.

Tickets of 26+ mph over must be heard by a judge, so those cannot be handled as a simple scheduled payment. Pay attention to any required court date.
