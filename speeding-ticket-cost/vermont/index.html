How much is a speeding ticket in Vermont?

Vermont handles most speeding tickets as civil matters through the Judicial Bureau. The lead figure is waiver penalties that rise per mph over — commonly about $50–$300+ including surcharges, with higher amounts in school and work zones (Vermont Department of Motor Vehicles).

That is a range, not a single price. Where you land in it depends mainly on how far over the limit you were driving and where.

The waiver amount printed on the complaint is the full price of not contesting. If you pay it as written, you are not expected to add anything else on top.

The penalty schedule comes from 23 V.S.A. §1081 / Judicial Bureau schedule (state code via Justia). We do not list an exact dollar amount for each mph bracket here, because we only publish figures we can tie to the sources listed at the bottom of this page. Your complaint shows the figure that applies to your ticket.

Treat the range above as a guide to the likely scale of the cost, then check your own paperwork for the actual number.

What drives the total cost of a Vermont speeding ticket?

Several factors move a ticket toward the low or high end of the range. The main ones are listed below.

  • Miles per hour over the limit. Waiver penalties rise per mph over, so a small overage costs less than a large one.
  • Location. School zones and work zones carry higher amounts than ordinary roads.
  • Surcharges. Statutory surcharges are included in the waiver amounts, so the figure on the complaint already contains them.
  • Your record. Points from this ticket add to any already on your record, which matters for the suspension threshold.

The ticket price is only part of the real cost. Points and a possible insurance increase can matter more over time than the penalty itself.

Because the waiver penalty is set by the schedule rather than negotiated at the roadside, the officer cannot tell you the final amount on the spot. Read the complaint carefully once you receive it.

How many points does a speeding ticket add in Vermont?

A speeding conviction in Vermont adds 2–8 points by mph over. A minor overage sits near the low end, and a large overage sits near the high end.

Points matter because of the suspension threshold: 10 points in 2 years = suspension (Vermont Department of Motor Vehicles). One high-point ticket, or several smaller ones close together, can bring a driver near that line.

Check how many points the ticket carries before you decide whether to pay or contest. Paying the waiver is a decision about points as well as money.

We do not state how long points stay on a record, or any point total for a specific mph bracket, because those details are outside the figures we have sourced. The Vermont DMV is the place to confirm your own point total and status.

If you hold a commercial license or are a young driver, other rules may apply. We do not cover those here, so check with the DMV directly.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance in Vermont?

It can. Published market analyses indicate that a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal.

That is a national average, not a Vermont figure. Your own change could be smaller, larger or nonexistent, depending on your insurer, your record and how the ticket is treated.

For general comparison of how states treat traffic offenses, the Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a state law reference (Governors Highway Safety Association).

  • Insurers usually look at convictions on your driving record, and they typically apply any increase when your policy renews.
  • The insurance effect is separate from the waiver penalty and is not part of the amount on your complaint.
  • TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer. Ask your own insurer how it treats a speeding conviction.

When you weigh whether to pay or contest, include the possible premium increase alongside the penalty itself.

Can a Vermont speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

You generally have a choice between paying the waiver amount and contesting the ticket. Paying the waiver penalty means you are not contesting. Contesting means asking the Judicial Bureau to hear the matter.

The outcome of a hearing depends on the facts and the evidence. We cannot predict whether a given ticket would be dismissed or reduced, and we do not give legal advice.

The waiver amount is the price of not contesting. If you contest and lose, the outcome may differ from the waiver figure, so read the instructions on your complaint.

Questions worth considering before you decide:

  1. How many points does this ticket carry, and where does that leave you against the suspension threshold?
  2. Is the location a school or work zone, which carries a higher amount?
  3. Could a conviction affect your premiums at renewal?

For questions specific to your case, consult a licensed attorney or the Judicial Bureau. We do not recommend a specific attorney.

What happens if I don't pay a Vermont speeding ticket?

Ignoring a ticket rarely makes it go away. An unpaid or unanswered complaint can lead to further penalties, and the consequences can reach your driving privileges.

We do not list specific deadlines or added fees, because they are not among the figures we have sourced. The complaint itself explains how and when to respond, and the Judicial Bureau can confirm the status of your case.

  • Read the complaint as soon as you receive it.
  • Decide whether to pay the waiver amount or contest.
  • Keep a record of what you paid and when.

The 10 points in 2 years threshold is a separate matter from nonpayment, but both can affect your license. Check with the Vermont DMV if you are unsure where you stand (Vermont Department of Motor Vehicles).

Frequently asked questions

How much is a speeding ticket in Vermont?

Judicial Bureau waiver penalties rise per mph over and commonly run about $50–$300+ including surcharges, with higher amounts in school and work zones. The exact amount is printed on your complaint, and paying it is the full price of not contesting.

Are surcharges added on top of the Vermont waiver amount?

No. Statutory surcharges are included in the waiver amounts. The waiver amount printed on the complaint is the full price of not contesting, so you do not add surcharges to it when paying.

How many points is a speeding ticket in Vermont?

A speeding conviction carries 2–8 points by mph over. The more miles per hour over the limit, the more points. Your complaint and the Vermont DMV can confirm the points for your specific ticket.

How many points suspend your license in Vermont?

Ten points in 2 years leads to suspension. Because a single speeding ticket can add anywhere from 2 to 8 points, several tickets in a short period can bring a driver close to that threshold.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance in Vermont?

Possibly. Published market analyses show a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal. That is a national average, not a Vermont figure, so your own change may differ.

Is a speeding ticket more expensive in a school or work zone in Vermont?

Yes. The Judicial Bureau waiver penalties carry higher amounts in school and work zones than the general range of about $50–$300+ including surcharges. The exact figure for your ticket is printed on the complaint.

Should I pay or contest a Vermont speeding ticket?

That depends on your situation. Paying the waiver is the full price of not contesting, but it also means accepting the points. Consider points, the 10-points-in-2-years threshold and insurance, and consult a licensed attorney for advice.
