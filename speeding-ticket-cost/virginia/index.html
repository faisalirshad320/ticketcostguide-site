How Much Does a Speeding Ticket Cost in Virginia? Fines, Points and What Drives the Total

By Editorial Team, TicketCostGuide · Last reviewed 2026-10-15

A prepayable speeding ticket in Virginia costs $6 per mph over the limit plus $51 in fixed processing costs, with more per mph in school and work zones (Virginia Department of Motor Vehicles). At 20+ over or 86+ mph it becomes reckless driving, with court-set fines up to $2,500 that vary by court.

How much is a speeding ticket in Virginia?

For a ticket you can prepay, Virginia uses a formula instead of a flat fine. The cost is $6 per mph over the limit plus $51 in fixed processing costs (Virginia Department of Motor Vehicles). The per-mph rate is higher in school and work zones.

The fixed processing costs apply to every prepaid ticket, whether you were a little over or a lot over. So the total grows with each mph, but it never drops below that fixed amount.

To estimate your own total, take the number of mph you were over the posted limit, multiply by the per-mph rate, and add the fixed costs. Check the figure printed on your ticket or with the clerk of the court that issued it, because the court's schedule is what you will actually pay.

The formula applies to prepayable tickets only. Once speed reaches the reckless-driving threshold, the formula no longer applies and a court sets the outcome.

When does speeding become reckless driving in Virginia?

This is the part of Virginia law that catches the most drivers. Going 20+ over the limit or 86+ mph is reckless driving, a Class 1 misdemeanor. Penalties include fines up to $2,500 and possible jail under Va. Code §46.2-870 / §46.2-862.

The trap is on the interstate. 86 mph on a 70 interstate is a criminal charge, not a ticket. A driver who thinks of it as "just a few over the usual 80" can end up with a criminal record rather than a fine.

Reckless cases also add court-set fines and fees. These vary by court, so we do not give a single figure for them. The $2,500 figure is the upper limit on the fine, not what every driver pays.

  • Prepayable ticket: formula-based cost, fixed processing costs included.
  • Reckless driving: criminal charge, court-set fines and fees, possible jail.
  • Either threshold alone is enough: 20+ over, or 86+ mph.

Because this is a criminal charge, it is worth speaking with a licensed Virginia attorney about your own case. TicketCostGuide cannot give legal advice or recommend an attorney.

How many points does a speeding ticket add in Virginia?

Virginia assigns 3–6 demerit points by offense, and reckless driving carries 6. Where your speeding violation falls in that range depends on the specific offense on your record (Virginia Department of Motor Vehicles).

The DMV suspends licenses at point thresholds. Drivers also earn +1 safe point per clean year, which offsets some of the demerit accumulation over time.

Points matter beyond the fine. Several tickets over a short period can lead to a suspension even when each one was modest. A reckless conviction adds the most in one step.

Check your own point balance and the current thresholds directly with the Virginia DMV. We do not publish a threshold table here because only the DMV can confirm your record.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance in Virginia?

It often does. Published market analyses show a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal. That is a national average, not a Virginia figure.

Your own change could be higher or lower. It depends on your insurer, your driving history, how far over you were and whether the conviction is on your record at renewal.

For many drivers the insurance effect over several renewals can outweigh the ticket itself. That is one reason to weigh the full cost of a conviction rather than only the amount due at the clerk's window.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer. Ask your own insurer or a licensed agent how a conviction would affect your policy.

Can a Virginia speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but outcomes depend on the facts, the court and the charge. We do not publish odds or promise any result.

For a prepayable ticket, the cost follows the per-mph formula plus fixed costs. For a reckless-driving charge, the court sets fines and fees, so the final outcome can differ from case to case.

Whether to contest a ticket is a legal decision. A licensed Virginia attorney can explain your options, including what a reduction could mean for points and your record. Ask the clerk of the court on your ticket about how your court handles hearings.

  • Read the charge on your ticket carefully: speeding or reckless driving.
  • Note the court and any date or instructions printed on it.
  • Get legal advice before deciding, especially for a criminal charge.

What happens if I don't pay a Virginia speeding ticket?

Ignoring a ticket rarely makes it go away. Unpaid or unresolved tickets can lead to added court action and possible consequences for your driving privileges, depending on the court and the charge.

A reckless-driving charge is a criminal matter. Missing a court obligation on a Class 1 misdemeanor is more serious than missing payment on a prepayable ticket.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the clerk of the court shown on your ticket before anything is due. Courts set their own procedures, and we do not list payment-plan terms here because they vary by court.

What drives the total cost of a Virginia speeding ticket?

Several things move the number. The biggest is how far over the limit you were, because the prepayable cost is built per mph.

  • Speed over the limit: $6 for each mph over in a standard zone.
  • Location: school and work zones carry a higher per-mph rate.
  • Fixed processing costs: $51 on every prepaid ticket.
  • Reckless threshold: 20+ over or 86+ mph moves the case into court, with fines up to $2,500 and court-set fees.
  • Points: 3–6 by offense, with 6 for reckless driving.
  • Insurance: a national average of roughly 20–25% at renewal.
