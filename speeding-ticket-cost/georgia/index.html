How much is a speeding ticket in Georgia?

Georgia treats a speeding ticket as a misdemeanor. The fine range is $0–$1,000, set by the county or municipal court, and a $200 state ‘Super Speeder’ fee applies at 75+ mph on a two-lane road or 85+ mph anywhere (Georgia DDS).

The governing provisions are O.C.G.A. §40-6-181 and §40-6-189, which you can read through Justia’s Georgia code. The statute sets the ceiling. The court sets the actual number within it.

Your total therefore depends on three things:

  • The fine your county or municipal court assigns for your speed.
  • Whether the Super Speeder fee applies to your ticket.
  • Any other amount the court lists on your citation or in the clerk’s records.

There is no single statewide price for a Georgia speeding ticket. The only locked statewide figures are the $0–$1,000 range and the $200 Super Speeder fee.

What is the Georgia Super Speeder fee?

The Super Speeder fee is a $200 state fee. It applies when you are caught at 75+ mph on a two-lane road or 85+ mph on any road.

It is separate from the court fine. The court sets the fine, and the state adds the $200 on top of it, so a Super Speeder ticket costs more than the fine alone.

The state fee has its own payment clock. If you do not pay it within 120 days, your license is suspended (Georgia DDS).

That matters because paying the court fine does not settle the state fee. They are two separate obligations, and you should track both.

Super Speeder fee: $200, due within 120 days. Missing the deadline suspends the license.

How many points does a speeding ticket add in Georgia?

Points depend on how far over the limit you were. The schedule we cite runs from 2 to 6 points:

  • 15–18 mph over: 2 points
  • 19–23 mph over: 3 points
  • 24–33 mph over: 4 points
  • 34+ mph over: 6 points

A suspension follows when you reach 15 points in 24 months. A single 6-point ticket is a large share of that threshold, and several tickets in a row can add up quickly.

Points do not change the dollar fine. They affect your driving record and your suspension risk, and they may indirectly affect insurance cost.

For the official point record tied to your license, check with Georgia DDS.

Why does the same speeding ticket cost different amounts in different counties?

Fines below the statutory maximum are set by each county’s court schedule. Two drivers going the same speed can receive different fines if their tickets are handled by different courts.

Georgia’s statutory range for the fine is $0–$1,000. Where a given ticket lands in that range is a decision of the county or municipal court that hears it.

We do not publish an exact local figure for any county. A number we could not source from the locked references would be a guess, and a guess could mislead you.

To find your actual amount, check the citation itself. The court’s clerk or published fine schedule is the authoritative source for what your court charges.

Rule of thumb: the $200 Super Speeder fee is the same state-wide when it applies. The court fine is not.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance in Georgia?

It often does. Published market analyses show that a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal.

That is a national average, not a Georgia figure. Your own change could be higher, lower or nonexistent, depending on your insurer, your record and how the ticket is resolved.

The increase usually shows up at renewal rather than immediately. Because premiums recur, the insurance effect can exceed the fine and the Super Speeder fee over time.

Can a Georgia speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

That depends on the court and the facts of your case, and we cannot give legal advice. Our locked sources do not set out dismissal or reduction rules, so we will not describe any here.

What we can say is that the fine range is wide, $0–$1,000, and the court sets the amount within it. How your court handles a ticket is something to confirm directly with the clerk.

If the Super Speeder fee applies, it is a separate state fee and it is not the same thing as the court fine.

If you want help with your specific situation, a licensed Georgia attorney is the right person to ask. We do not recommend a particular one.

  • Read the citation for the court name and instructions.
  • Ask the clerk what options the court offers.
  • Keep track of every deadline, including the 120-day Super Speeder deadline.

What happens if I don’t pay a Georgia speeding ticket or Super Speeder fee?

For the Super Speeder fee, the consequence is clear. If you do not pay the $200 within 120 days, your license is suspended (Georgia DDS).

For the court fine, the process is run by the court that issued the citation. Our locked sources do not describe court-level penalties for non-payment, so we do not list any.

The safe approach is to treat every deadline on the citation as binding. Contact the court clerk before a deadline passes if you have questions.

Points are a separate risk. Reaching 15 points in 24 months also leads to suspension, so unpaid fees are not the only route to losing your license.

Two suspension triggers to watch: an unpaid Super Speeder fee after 120 days, and 15 points in 24 months.
