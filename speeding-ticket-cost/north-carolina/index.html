How much is a speeding ticket in North Carolina?

For ordinary speeds, the fine is usually modest, in the $10–$50 range. The bigger number is court costs: uniform court costs of roughly $190 apply to nearly every ticket (North Carolina Division of Motor Vehicles, last reviewed Oct 2026).

That means the real cost of an ordinary ticket is the small fine plus the court costs. On most tickets the court costs are the larger piece.

The offense is governed by N.C.G.S. §20-141 / §20-16.1 (Justia, North Carolina code). The first section covers speed limits and the second covers license suspension triggers.

  • Fine: $10–$50 range for ordinary speeds.
  • Court costs: roughly $190, applied to nearly every ticket.
  • License points: 2–3 for typical speeding.
  • Insurance: separate SDIP points can raise premiums 30%+ per point tier.

These are ranges, not a quote. The amount on your citation and the figure confirmed by the court clerk are what you actually owe.

Why are court costs bigger than the fine?

North Carolina speeding fines are low, but the state adds uniform statewide court costs to nearly every ticket. At roughly $190, those costs dwarf the fine on most tickets.

This surprises many drivers. A ticket with a fine in the $10–$50 range can still cost several times that once court costs are added.

Because the costs are uniform, they do not shrink much when the fine is small. They are the main reason a minor speeding ticket in the state is not cheap.

We do not list a fixed total for every ticket. Your citation, the court handling it and the outcome of your case decide the final bill, so confirm the exact amount with the clerk.

Rule of thumb: expect the court costs of roughly $190 to be the largest line item on an ordinary speeding ticket, not the fine.

How many points does a speeding ticket add in North Carolina?

License points for typical speeding are 2–3. These are recorded on your driving record by the North Carolina Division of Motor Vehicles (North Carolina Division of Motor Vehicles).

North Carolina also has a separate insurance point system, often called SDIP. Those insurance points are not the same as license points, and they work through your insurer rather than the DMV.

The insurance points matter because they can raise premiums 30%+ per point tier. A conviction can therefore cost more over time through insurance than it does on the day you pay the ticket.

  • License points: 2–3 for typical speeding.
  • SDIP points: separate, and they can raise premiums 30%+ per point tier.

We do not give point totals beyond these locked figures. Check your own record with the DMV if you are worried about a running total.

Can a speeding ticket suspend my license in North Carolina?

Yes, for faster speeds. Driving 15+ over (when driving over 55) or going 80+ mph risks a 30-day suspension under §20-16.1 (N.C.G.S. §20-141 / §20-16.1 via Justia).

There are two separate triggers. One is how far over the limit you were, and the other is an absolute speed of 80+ mph.

The word to note is "risks." A suspension is a possible consequence of these tickets, not an automatic outcome in every case. What applies to you depends on the charge and how the case is resolved.

  • 15+ over (when driving over 55): risks a 30-day suspension.
  • 80+ mph: risks a 30-day suspension.

If your ticket is near either threshold, read the charge on your citation closely. A licensed North Carolina attorney can explain how the suspension rules apply to your situation. We do not recommend specific attorneys.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance in North Carolina?

It can. North Carolina insurance points can raise premiums 30%+ per point tier, and that is the figure that applies to this state.

For context, published market analyses show that a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal. That is a national average, not a North Carolina figure, so do not treat it as your expected increase.

The two numbers measure different things. One is a national average across many states and drivers. The other is how the North Carolina point system can move a premium for each point tier.

Your actual change depends on your insurer, your record and the type of conviction. Ask your insurer how a conviction would affect your renewal before you decide how to handle the ticket.

Because insurance can outlast the ticket, the long-term cost of a conviction may exceed the fine and court costs combined. Avoiding the conviction itself can be worth a look.

Can a speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced in North Carolina?

One option is a Prayer for Judgment Continued (PJC). A PJC can spare insurance consequences once per household per 3 years.

The limit is per household, not per driver. If someone in your household has used a PJC within the past 3 years, it may not be available to you.

A PJC mainly addresses the insurance side of the cost. The court costs of roughly $190 are a separate matter, and we do not state whether they are affected.

Whether a PJC, a reduction or a dismissal is realistic for your ticket depends on the charge, your record and the court. We do not publish odds or recommend attorneys.

  • Check whether anyone in your household has used a PJC in the last 3 years.
  • Read your citation for the charge and any court date.
  • Ask the court clerk or a licensed North Carolina attorney about your options.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore a speeding ticket?

Ignoring a ticket does not make it go away. The fine and court costs of roughly $190 remain tied to the citation, and the court can take further action.

We do not publish late fees or deadlines because we have no sourced figures for them. Your citation lists your court date and instructions, and the clerk can confirm what you owe.

If your ticket is in the suspension-risk range (15+ over when driving over 55, or 80+ mph), missing the process can make a bad situation worse. Deal with the court before any date passes.
