How much is a speeding ticket in Kentucky?

The Kentucky statutory fine for speeding is $20–$100, and court costs are added on top. Totals commonly land around $140–$200 depending on the county (Kentucky Transportation Cabinet — Driver Licensing).

The fine itself comes from KRS §189.390, which you can read through Justia's Kentucky code. The statute sets the speeding rules, but the number on your citation also reflects what your local court adds.

We do not list an exact fine for each mph bracket, because the figures we can source give only the range. If your citation shows a specific amount, that is the number to rely on, and the court clerk can confirm it.

Quick math: a low statutory fine does not mean a low bill. Kentucky's low statutory fines are mostly court costs in practice.

Why are court costs bigger than the fine?

Court costs are set by the district court and usually exceed the fine itself. That is why the typical total of around $140–$200 sits well above the top of the $20–$100 fine range.

Because costs are set at the court level, two drivers with the same offense can pay different totals in different counties. We do not publish an exact per-county figure, since none is available in the sources we use.

When you read your citation, look for:

  • The fine amount listed for the offense.
  • Any court costs shown separately or rolled into a total.
  • The court and county that will collect payment.

If the citation shows only one total, ask the clerk whether it includes costs. Do not assume the printed fine is the full amount due.

How many points does a speeding ticket add in Kentucky?

Points depend on how far over the limit you were going. Driving 10–15 over adds 3 points, and driving 16–25 over adds 6 points.

At 26+ over, the matter triggers a hearing rather than a simple point entry. We do not describe what happens at that hearing, because the details are outside the figures we have sourced.

Points matter because they accumulate. Reaching 12 points in 2 years means suspension (Kentucky Transportation Cabinet — Driver Licensing).

  • 10–15 over: 3 points
  • 16–25 over: 6 points
  • 26+ over: hearing
  • 12 points in 2 years: suspension

Two tickets in the 16–25 over tier would reach the suspension threshold on points alone, so a second ticket can matter more than the first. Check your own record with the Transportation Cabinet for your current count.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance rates?

It often does. Published market analyses show a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal.

That is a national average, not a Kentucky figure. Your own change depends on your insurer, your driving history and how the conviction appears on your record.

The premium effect can last longer than the fine. A ticket that costs a few hundred dollars once may cost more over several renewals, so look at the whole cost, not just what you pay the court.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer or give insurance advice. If you are worried about your rate, ask your own insurer how a conviction would be treated.

Can a Kentucky speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but it depends on the court, the prosecutor and the facts of your case. We do not have sourced figures on how often tickets are dismissed or reduced in Kentucky, so we will not guess.

Options drivers commonly look into include:

  • Contesting the ticket in district court.
  • Asking whether the charge can be amended, which could change the points that apply.
  • Confirming your court date and the steps for responding before it passes.

Because points are tied to the mph-over tier, the tier on your final disposition matters. A change in tier could change the point total, but the court decides whether that is possible.

TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice or recommend attorneys. For questions about your case, contact the court clerk or a licensed Kentucky attorney of your choosing.

What happens if I don't pay a speeding ticket?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. An unresolved citation can lead to additional consequences set by the court or by licensing authorities, and the details depend on your case.

We do not state deadlines or added penalty amounts, because those figures are not in our sourced data. Your citation and the court clerk are the reliable places to confirm them.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court before the due date and ask what is available. Courts handle this differently by county, just as they set costs differently.

Also remember that points and the 12-points-in-2-years suspension threshold apply to convictions on your record, so how a ticket is resolved can matter beyond the money.

What drives the total cost of a Kentucky speeding ticket?

Four things move the total most: the fine within the $20–$100 range, the court costs for your county, the points added, and the insurance effect later.

  1. Fine: the statutory amount under KRS §189.390.
  2. Court costs: set by the district court, so they vary by county and court.
  3. Points: 3 points for 10–15 over and 6 points for 16–25 over.
  4. Insurance: nationally, roughly 20–25% higher on average at renewal.
