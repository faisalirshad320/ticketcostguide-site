How much is a speeding ticket in New Jersey?

The lead figure is court-schedule fines of roughly $85–$260 by mph-over tier. That range doubles in 65-mph zones and Safe Corridors, and court costs and assessments come on top (New Jersey Motor Vehicle Commission).

The fine is set by how far over the limit you were driving, under N.J.S.A. 39:4-99 / 39:4-98.6 (state code via Justia). Higher mph-over tiers sit toward the upper end of the range.

The number printed on your ticket or on the court's payment notice is the one that counts. We do not publish an exact per-tier or per-town amount, because court costs and assessments vary by county and court.

The fine is only part of the cost. Points, a possible MVC surcharge and a higher insurance premium can add more over time than the court payment itself.

How many points is a speeding ticket in New Jersey?

Points depend on how fast you were going over the limit:

  • 2 points for 1–14 mph over
  • 4 points for 15–29 mph over
  • 5 points for 30+ mph over

Reaching 12 points leads to a license suspension. Points matter for two reasons: they move you toward that threshold, and 6+ points within 3 years triggers an MVC insurance surcharge of $150+.

Because one 4-point or 5-point ticket gets you most of the way to 6 points, even a single higher-tier ticket can matter. Two tickets in the 2-point tier would not reach 6 points on their own, but a third would.

What is the MVC surcharge and how is it billed?

New Jersey is one of the few states that bills a separate state surcharge after the court case ends. The Motor Vehicle Commission handles it, not the municipal court.

MVC surcharges at 6+ points are billed annually for 3 years on top of court fines. The locked starting amount is $150+, so your actual bill may be higher than that figure.

  • The surcharge is separate from the fine you pay the court.
  • It arrives after the case is resolved, so it is easy to leave out of your budget.
  • It is tied to your point total, not to a single ticket's fine.

Check your MVC account and mailed notices for the exact amount that applies to you. We do not state an exact figure beyond $150+.

What drives the total cost of a New Jersey speeding ticket?

Four things move the number most:

  1. Mph over the limit. This sets your fine tier within the roughly $85–$260 range and your points (2, 4 or 5).
  2. Where you were caught. Fines are doubled in 65-mph zones and Safe Corridors under N.J.S.A. 39:4-99 / 39:4-98.6 (Justia).
  3. County and court. Court costs and assessments vary by county and court, so two drivers with the same tier can pay different totals.
  4. Your point total. At 6+ points, the MVC surcharge of $150+ is billed annually for 3 years.

Insurance is a fifth cost, but it comes from your insurer rather than the state. It is covered below.

Doubling applies to the court-schedule fine tiers. Check posted signage, since a Safe Corridor or 65-mph stretch changes the fine you face.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance in New Jersey?

It can. Published market analyses show a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal. That is a national average, not a New Jersey figure.

Your own change could be smaller or larger. Insurers set their own rules, and we do not recommend any insurer or predict what yours will do.

The effect is usually felt at renewal, not right away. That means the real cost of a ticket can appear months after you paid the court.

When you compare costs, add the fine, court costs, any MVC surcharge and a possible premium increase. For a higher-tier ticket, the premium effect may be the largest piece.

Can a New Jersey speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes a case ends differently than the original charge, but we cannot say whether yours will. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice.

Because points and the MVC surcharge depend on the outcome, the result of the case affects more than the fine. A different outcome could change your point total, and with it whether the 6+ point surcharge applies.

  • Read the ticket for the court, the contact details and any deadlines.
  • Ask the court clerk what options exist for your charge.
  • Consider speaking with a licensed New Jersey attorney if the stakes are high, such as a license at risk.

Court procedures vary by county and court, so what one court allows may differ from another.

What happens if I don't pay a New Jersey speeding ticket?

Ignoring a ticket rarely makes it cheaper. The court notice sets the process, and missing it can lead to further action on the case. We do not list specific penalties or deadlines here, so check your notice or ask the court clerk.

The MVC surcharge works separately. If you reach 6+ points, the surcharge of $150+ is billed annually for 3 years on top of court fines, so paying the court does not settle the whole bill.

Points also keep counting toward the 12-point suspension threshold. Staying on top of both the court payment and the MVC billing is the safest way to avoid added trouble.
