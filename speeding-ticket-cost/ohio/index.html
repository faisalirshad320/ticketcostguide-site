How much is a speeding ticket in Ohio?

A typical Ohio speeding ticket is a minor misdemeanor with a fine up to $150, plus court costs that vary by municipal court (commonly $100+). Higher degrees apply for repeat offenses and 30+ over (Ohio Bureau of Motor Vehicles).

The speed rules come from Ohio Rev. Code §4511.21, which you can read through Justia's Ohio code. The statute sets out the offense. What you pay is the fine the court imposes plus that court's costs.

Because costs vary by court and often exceed the fine, two drivers with the same ticket can pay different totals. We do not publish an exact local figure, because no single statewide number exists.

Short version: the fine has a ceiling, but the court costs depend on which municipal court handles your ticket.

What drives the total cost of an Ohio speeding ticket?

Three things move the total: the fine, the court costs, and the offense level.

  • The fine. For a minor misdemeanor it is up to $150. Ohio's $150 statutory cap keeps fines low.
  • Court costs. These vary by municipal court (commonly $100+) and often exceed the fine. The costs column decides the total.
  • The offense level. Higher degrees apply for repeat offenses and for 30+ over, so the minor-misdemeanor range may not describe your case.

This is why a quick search for "the" Ohio speeding fine can mislead. The fine alone is only part of what you pay.

If your ticket falls into a higher degree, the figures on this page are not a safe estimate. The citation and the court that issued it are the best guide to your exposure.

How many points does a speeding ticket add in Ohio?

Most speeding convictions carry 2 points. Some small overages carry 0 points in some circumstances, and a conviction at 30+ over carries 4 points (Ohio Bureau of Motor Vehicles).

Points matter because 12 points in 2 years means a suspension. A single ticket will not usually get you there. Several convictions in a short window can.

  • Most speeding convictions: 2 points
  • Small overages, in some circumstances: 0 points
  • 30+ over: 4 points
  • Suspension threshold: 12 points in 2 years

Points attach to a conviction. How your case ends therefore affects your record as well as your wallet.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance rates in Ohio?

It can. Published market analyses find that a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal. That is a national average, not an Ohio figure.

Your own change could be higher, lower, or nothing. It depends on your insurer, your driving history, and the conviction itself. We do not recommend or rank insurers, and we cannot say what yours will do.

A fine of up to $150 plus court costs is paid once. A premium increase at renewal can recur. When you compare the cost of a ticket, include the possible effect at renewal as well as the amount on the citation.

The 20–25% figure is a national average from published market analyses. It is not specific to Ohio or to any one insurer.

Can an Ohio speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome depends on the court and the facts of your case. We do not give legal advice and cannot predict what a particular court will do.

In general, a ticket can end in a conviction as charged, a conviction on a different charge, or no conviction. Those outcomes can differ in fine, costs and points. For example, small overages may carry 0 points in some circumstances, while most speeding convictions carry 2 points.

If you want to contest the ticket or ask about options, start with the court named on your citation. The clerk can explain the process and your deadlines. A licensed Ohio attorney can advise on your situation, and we do not recommend any specific one.

Keep in mind that court costs vary by court and often exceed the fine. Even a reduced outcome may leave a meaningful costs bill.

What happens if I don't pay an Ohio speeding ticket?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes the problem bigger. The citation tells you how to respond and where. The court that issued it controls the next steps.

Courts have tools to follow up on unpaid fines and missed responses, and the details vary by court. We do not list specific penalties or deadlines here, because we have not sourced a uniform statewide rule for them.

  • Read the citation for the response instructions.
  • Contact the clerk of the court named on it.
  • Ask the clerk for your total, including costs, before you pay.

Acting early also keeps your options open. That includes contesting the ticket if you choose to.

How do I find the exact total for my ticket?

Because court costs vary by municipal court (commonly $100+), the only reliable number is the one from your court. Check the citation first, then the court's clerk.

  1. Note the court named on your ticket.
  2. Check whether the offense is listed as a minor misdemeanor or a higher degree.
  3. Ask the clerk for the fine and court costs together.
  4. Ask whether the outcome you are considering changes the points.

Compare the answer with the range on this page. The fine should sit at or under $150 for a minor misdemeanor. The costs are the part that can surprise you.
