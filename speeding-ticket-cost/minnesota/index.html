How much is a speeding ticket in Minnesota?

Minnesota speeding fines are set by the statewide payables list. Fines are commonly $40–$150 plus a state surcharge of about $75, varying by mph over, according to the Minnesota Department of Public Safety — Driver and Vehicle Services.

That range is the number to plan around. Where you land in it depends mainly on how far over the limit you were driving.

The surcharge is added on top of the fine. When you compare a ticket to the fine range, remember the surcharge is a separate line.

The exact amount printed on your citation can differ by county or court. We give only the statewide range and do not list an exact local figure, because we cannot verify one for every court.

Your citation or the court's payment page is the final word on what you owe.

What drives the total cost of a Minnesota speeding ticket?

Three things shape the total: the fine, the surcharge and any consequence tied to speed.

  • Mph over the limit. The payables list varies the fine by how far over you were. Faster means a higher fine within the $40–$150 range.
  • The state surcharge. A statutory state surcharge, about $75, is added to every payable fine.
  • Doubled fines. Under Minn. Stat. §169.14, doubled fines apply at 20+ mph over the limit.

Because the surcharge applies to every payable fine, even a ticket at the low end of the range carries it. For a lower-speed ticket, the surcharge can make up a large share of what you pay.

County and court differences are the fourth factor. We do not publish a local total, so check the court listed on your citation.

How many points does a speeding ticket add in Minnesota?

None. Minnesota has no point system. License action follows the record of convictions and specific high-speed offenses.

You will not find a points chart for Minnesota, and a ticket does not add a number to a running total. What matters is whether you are convicted and how fast you were going.

The Driver and Vehicle Services division maintains driver records. Check your own record with them if you want to see what is on file.

No points does not mean no consequences. A conviction still goes on your record, and that record can matter for your license and for insurance.

What happens at very high speeds or 20+ mph over?

Two rules stand out in the locked facts.

  1. 20+ mph over the limit: doubled fines apply under Minn. Stat. §169.14.
  2. 100+ mph: this can bring a 6-month license revocation (Minnesota Department of Public Safety — Driver and Vehicle Services).

The first rule raises the money you owe. The second affects your ability to drive, which can cost far more than the fine through lost work and transport.

We do not list a doubled dollar amount, because the exact figure depends on the base fine for your mph over. Read the citation, and the statute itself, for the details of your case.

The word "can" matters in the revocation rule. It describes a possible outcome, not an automatic one in every case.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance in Minnesota?

It often does. Published market analyses show that a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal.

That is a national average, not a Minnesota figure. Your own change could be higher, lower or nil, depending on your insurer, your driving history and how your policy treats violations.

We do not recommend insurers and cannot tell you what yours will do. The practical step is to ask your insurer how a conviction would be treated at renewal.

Over several renewals, an insurance increase can add up to more than the fine and surcharge combined. Weigh that when deciding how to handle a ticket.

Can a Minnesota speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but the outcome depends on the facts and on the court. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice, so we do not tell you what to do with a specific ticket.

What we can say is how the money works. A payable fine on the statewide list is the amount you can pay without a court appearance. Contesting a ticket moves it into court, where the result can differ.

Because Minnesota has no point system, the main stakes are the fine, the surcharge, your record of convictions and insurance. Those are worth weighing before you decide.

  • Read the citation for the court and the response instructions.
  • Check the statute, Minn. Stat. §169.14, for how the speed rules apply.
  • If you want advice on options, speak to a licensed attorney of your own choosing.

What happens if I don't pay a speeding ticket in Minnesota?

Ignoring a ticket rarely makes it cheaper. The court can treat an unanswered citation as a failure to respond, and additional consequences can follow.

We do not give specific deadlines or added penalty amounts here, because we cannot verify them from our locked sources. Your citation and the court's notice set out the timing and the next steps.

License action in Minnesota follows the record of convictions and specific high-speed offenses. An unresolved ticket can complicate that record.

If you cannot pay the full amount, contact the court listed on the citation before any deadline passes and ask what options it offers. Policies vary by county and court.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Minnesota state surcharge on a speeding ticket?

A statutory state surcharge is added to every payable fine, and it is about $75. It is separate from the fine itself, so it increases what you pay even on a ticket at the low end of the $40–$150 range.

At 100+ mph, a speeding offense can bring a 6-month license revocation, according to the Minnesota Department of Public Safety. Because there is no point system, license action follows the record of convictions and specific high-speed offenses.
