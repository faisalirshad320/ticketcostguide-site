How much is a speeding ticket in Alaska?

Alaska speeding fines are set by the Alaska court system's bail forfeiture schedule, commonly in the $50–$300 range by mph over, and doubled in work zones (Alaska Division of Motor Vehicles). That range is the fine itself.

The schedule works by bracket. The faster you were going above the posted limit, the higher the bracket and the higher the amount. The underlying traffic rules sit in Alaska Stat. §28.35 / court bail schedule (state code via Justia).

Exact amounts come from the court bail schedule for the mph bracket cited on the ticket. We do not publish a dollar amount for each bracket, because we only state figures we can tie to a source.

Your citation is the best guide to your own cost. Find the mph over the limit that the officer wrote down, then confirm the amount with the court listed on the ticket.

What drives the total cost of an Alaska speeding ticket?

Three things move the number most. They are how far over the limit you were, where you were driving, and what the ticket says about the court's handling of it.

  • Mph over the limit. This decides your bracket on the bail schedule, and the bracket decides the base amount within the commonly cited $50–$300 range.
  • Location of the violation. Work zones and school zones carry doubled fines under the schedule.
  • The court and the ticket. The amount printed for your bracket is what matters, so always read the citation rather than relying on a general figure.

The fine is not the only cost. Points on your record and a possible rise in your insurance premium can add up to more than the fine over time.

Do work-zone and school-zone tickets cost more?

Yes. Work-zone and school-zone violations carry doubled fines under the schedule. If the base amount for your mph bracket would be one figure on an open road, the same bracket in one of these zones is doubled.

The lead range is stated as commonly $50–$300 "doubled in work zones" (Alaska Division of Motor Vehicles). School zones follow the same doubling rule under the schedule.

Because doubling applies to the bracket amount, a high-bracket ticket in a zone can cost far more than the same speed elsewhere. Check whether your ticket notes a zone, since that changes the amount you owe.

The doubling is the only surcharge we can state from the locked sources. If your paperwork shows any other add-on, ask the court what it covers rather than assuming.

How many points does a speeding ticket add in Alaska?

A speeding conviction adds 2–6 demerit points depending on speed. Faster speeds mean more points, in the same way they mean a higher bracket on the fine schedule.

The point threshold that matters most is 12 points in 12 months, which results in a suspension. A single high-speed ticket can add a large share of that total, and two tickets close together can add up quickly.

Points are tracked through the Alaska Division of Motor Vehicles. You can find licensing information at the Alaska Division of Motor Vehicles.

  • Points added: 2–6, by speed.
  • Suspension threshold: 12 points in 12 months.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance in Alaska?

It often does. Published market analyses show that a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal. That is a national average, not an Alaska figure.

Your own change could be higher or lower. It depends on your insurer, your driving history, how fast you were going and how long the conviction stays on your record.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer. If you are worried about your premium, ask your own insurer how a conviction would be rated at renewal.

Because the premium effect continues after the fine is paid, it is worth weighing when you decide how to respond to the ticket.

Can an Alaska speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but we cannot give legal advice and do not predict outcomes. Whether a ticket can be contested or changed depends on the facts, the court and the options listed on your citation.

Start by reading the ticket. It names the court and explains how to respond. If you want to dispute it, contact that court directly.

For questions about your particular case, a licensed attorney is the right person to ask. We do not recommend a specific attorney.

Keep in mind that paying the amount on the bail schedule usually means accepting the violation, which is when points and insurance effects can follow. Think about those costs before you pay.

What happens if I don't pay an Alaska speeding ticket?

Ignoring a ticket tends to make things worse. The court can treat an unanswered citation as a further problem, and your licensing status can be affected.

We do not state specific deadlines or added penalties here, since those are not among the figures we can source. The ticket lists the court and how to respond. Contact that court if you are unsure what is due or when.

You can also check your licensing status with the Alaska Division of Motor Vehicles. For a comparison of how states approach traffic law, the Governors Highway Safety Association keeps a state laws resource.
