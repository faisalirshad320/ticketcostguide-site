How much is a speeding ticket in Alaska?

Alaska speeding fines come from the court system's bail forfeiture schedule. The schedule is commonly in the $50–$300 range by mph over the limit, and it is doubled in work zones (Alaska Division of Motor Vehicles).

That range is a starting point, not a quote. The number you actually owe is the amount the schedule assigns to the mph bracket cited on your ticket.

The offense is governed by Alaska Stat. §28.35 and the court bail schedule (Alaska Stat. §28.35 on Justia). The statute sets the framework, and the schedule turns it into a dollar amount for each bracket.

Quick rule: the lower your recorded speed over the limit, the closer you will be to the low end of the $50–$300 range. A higher bracket, or a work zone, moves the total up.

What drives the total on an Alaska speeding ticket?

Three things matter most. Each one is printed on or tied to the citation itself.

  • The mph bracket. The schedule prices tickets by how far over the limit you were recorded.
  • The location. Work-zone and school-zone violations carry doubled fines under the schedule.
  • The court. Amounts are taken from the court bail schedule for the bracket on your ticket, so confirm the figure with the court named on the citation.

We do not publish an exact dollar amount for each mph bracket, because we only report figures we can tie to the cited sources. Read the bracket on your ticket and match it against the current schedule from the court.

Remember that the fine is not the only cost. Demerit points and any insurance increase can add up over time, as the sections below explain.

Are fines higher in work zones and school zones?

Yes. Work-zone and school-zone violations carry doubled fines under the schedule.

Doubling applies to the schedule amount for your bracket. Since the base range is commonly $50–$300, a ticket in one of these zones can cost meaningfully more than the same speed on an open road.

The zone should be noted on the citation. If you are unsure whether your ticket was classified as a work-zone or school-zone violation, check the citation and ask the court that handles it.

Tip: slowing down in marked work and school zones is the simplest way to avoid the doubled amount, because the multiplier applies on top of whatever bracket you were cited in.

How many points does a speeding ticket add in Alaska?

A speeding conviction adds 2–6 demerit points, depending on speed. Faster speeds fall toward the higher end of that range (Alaska Division of Motor Vehicles).

The important threshold is 12 points in 12 months, which results in a license suspension. One high-speed ticket will not reach that alone, but several violations in a year can.

  • Check how many points your ticket's bracket carries.
  • Add them to any other points on your record from the past year.
  • Compare the total with the 12-point suspension threshold.

For your own point total and record, the Division of Motor Vehicles is the authoritative source. We cannot see your record.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance in Alaska?

It can. Published market analyses indicate that a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal.

That is a national average, not an Alaska figure. Your actual change depends on your insurer, your driving history, and how the ticket is recorded.

Because insurance is often the largest long-term cost, it is worth comparing it with the fine itself. A ticket in the $50–$300 range can look small next to a premium increase that repeats at renewal.

TicketCostGuide does not recommend any insurer. Ask your own insurer how a conviction would affect your rate.

Can an Alaska speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

That depends on the facts and on the court. The court named on your citation can tell you what options exist and how to request a hearing.

Paying the scheduled amount is generally treated as resolving the ticket under the bail forfeiture approach, which can mean a conviction and points. Contesting it is a different path with different outcomes.

  • Read the citation for the response instructions.
  • Confirm your mph bracket and whether a work or school zone is noted.
  • Consider the points and insurance impact, not only the fine.

This page is not legal advice. For a specific case, consult a licensed Alaska attorney or contact the court directly.

What happens if I don't pay an Alaska speeding ticket?

Ignoring a ticket usually makes things worse. We do not list specific deadlines or penalty amounts, because those are not among the figures we can source for this page.

The citation and the court's instructions state what you need to do and by when. If you miss a deadline, ask the court what follows, and check with the Division of Motor Vehicles about the effect on your license (Alaska Division of Motor Vehicles).

If you are close to the 12 points in 12 months threshold, the stakes are higher. A suspension follows at that level, so check your total before deciding how to respond.

Frequently asked questions

How much is a speeding ticket in Alaska?

Are Alaska speeding fines doubled in work zones?

Yes. Under the court bail schedule, work-zone violations carry doubled fines. School-zone violations are doubled as well. The doubling applies to the schedule amount for the mph bracket on your ticket, so the same speed costs more in a marked zone.

How many points is a speeding ticket in Alaska?

A speeding conviction adds 2–6 demerit points, depending on how fast you were going. Higher speeds carry more points. Reaching 12 points in 12 months results in a license suspension, so add up any other recent violations on your record.

How many points suspend your license in Alaska?

The threshold is 12 points in 12 months, which leads to a suspension. A single speeding ticket adds 2–6 points depending on speed, so several violations within a year can get you there. The Division of Motor Vehicles can confirm your current total.

Will a speeding ticket raise my insurance in Alaska?

Does the fine depend on how fast I was going?

Yes. The court bail schedule prices tickets by mph over the limit, which is why the commonly cited range runs from $50 to $300. Your ticket lists the bracket, and the schedule assigns the amount for it. Work and school zones are doubled.
