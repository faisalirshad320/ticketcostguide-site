How much is a speeding ticket in Pennsylvania?

The lead number is a formula, not a flat price: $35 base plus $2 for every mph above 5 over the limit (so 20 over ≈ $65 base), with fines doubled in active work zones (PennDOT Driver and Vehicle Services). That figure is the base fine only.

What you actually pay is higher. Court costs, EMS surcharge and other statutory fees are added, and these commonly push totals past $150.

Bottom line: the base fine is only the starting point. Plan for the total on your citation or court notice, not the formula alone.

Because court costs and several surcharges vary by county and court, we do not publish one exact statewide total. The amount printed on your ticket or court paperwork is the figure that counts.

How is a Pennsylvania speeding fine calculated?

The fine is set by the speeding statute, 75 Pa.C.S. §3362 / §3326 (state code via Justia). The base starts at $35. Then $2 is added for every mph above 5 over the limit.

The speed that counts is the gap between the posted limit and the speed recorded for your citation. The first few mph of that gap are treated differently from the rest, because the per-mph amount applies only to mph above 5 over.

Our one worked example: at 20 mph over, the base comes to about $65. We show only that example because it is the one we can tie directly to the published formula and our sources.

  • Start with the $35 base.
  • Add $2 for each mph above 5 over the limit.
  • Double the fine if the violation happened in an active work zone.
  • Then add court costs, EMS surcharge and other statutory fees.

Are fines higher in a work zone?

Yes. Fines are doubled in active work zones. The rule sits in the same part of the code cited above, 75 Pa.C.S. §3362 / §3326 (Justia).

The word "active" matters. The doubling is tied to an active work zone, so the specific facts of where and when you were stopped can matter to the fine. Your citation should indicate how the violation was charged.

Doubling applies to the fine. Court costs, EMS and other statutory fees are separate items, and their amounts vary by county and court.

Example for scale only: a base of about $65 at 20 over would be doubled in an active work zone, before court costs and surcharges are added.

Why is the total more than the base fine?

Most of the gap comes from add-ons. Court costs, EMS surcharge and other statutory fees typically exceed the base fine. That is why totals commonly end up past $150, even when the base looks modest.

These items are not set by the speeding formula. They depend on the court that handles the citation, so they vary by county and court. We do not list exact dollar amounts for them because our sources do not give a single statewide figure.

  • Court costs: varies by county/court.
  • EMS surcharge: varies by county/court.
  • Other statutory fees: varies by county/court.

If you are comparing options, ask the court office for the total due, not just the fine. The total is what you would pay if you resolve the ticket as charged.

How many points does a speeding ticket add in Pennsylvania?

It depends on how far over the limit you were. A speeding conviction at 11+ mph over carries 2–5 points (PennDOT Driver and Vehicle Services). Under 11 mph over carries no points.

Points matter because of what they trigger. Reaching 6 points triggers a departmental exam/hearing. That is a driver-record process, separate from paying the fine.

High-speed convictions add a 15-day suspension at 31+ over. That is in addition to the fine and the points.

  • Under 11 mph over: no points.
  • 11+ mph over: 2–5 points.
  • 6 points: departmental exam/hearing.
  • 31+ mph over: 15-day suspension added.

Does a speeding ticket raise insurance in Pennsylvania?

It can. Published market analyses indicate a speeding conviction typically raises US auto premiums by roughly 20–25% on average at renewal. That is a national average, not a Pennsylvania figure.

Your own change could be higher, lower or nil. It depends on your insurer, your record and how the ticket is resolved. We do not recommend any insurer and cannot predict your rate.

The points rule can interact with this. A result under 11 mph over carries no points, which is one reason people often look at that outcome. Whether it changes your premium is a question for your insurer.

Treat the 20–25% as a rough national benchmark, not a quote. Over time, the insurance effect can outweigh the ticket itself.

Can a speeding ticket be dismissed or reduced?

Sometimes, but we cannot tell you whether yours can. TicketCostGuide is not a law firm and does not give legal advice or recommend attorneys.

What we can say from the locked facts: under 11 mph over carries no points, and that threshold is a common plea-bargain target. A reduction to a lower tier can change the points outcome, not only the fine.

If your ticket falls near a threshold, compare outcomes before you decide:

  • Would the result carry points (11+ mph over) or none (under 11 mph over)?
  • Was the violation charged as an active work zone, which doubles the fine?
  • What is the total due once court costs, EMS and other fees are added?

For advice on your specific case, speak with a licensed professional or the court that issued the citation.

What happens if I don't pay or ignore the ticket?

Ignoring a ticket generally makes things worse, not better. We do not list specific late penalties here, because our sources do not give statewide figures for them.

What we can say is that a conviction can lead to points on your record, a departmental exam/hearing at 6 points, and a 15-day suspension at 31+ over. The citation and court notice will state your deadline and options. Read them closely and contact the court office if anything is unclear.
